# Evidence pass for the group `town-churches`: churches of the borough, their belfries and their use as hospitals

Written for OA10 (`GOVERNANCE/requirements.json`; handling `decision_log.md` DEC-OA10-12) following `evidence/PLAN.md`. The file is `evidence/town-churches.jsonl`. Tier letters in the tables: **D** documented, **I** inferred, **P** plausible-procedural (a generic rule, never a fact), **G** open gap; the number is the row id (`town-churches.NNNN`).

The report covers two passes of the same group. The **first pass** (2026-11-14 to 2026-11-16) wrote 288 rows. The **second pass** (2026-11-16, this version of the file and of the report) read what the first pass left, acquired the sources that the open gaps pointed to (nine texts and one set of photographs, section 0), closed six gaps, narrowed three, added six pairs of conflicting rows (four topics: the year the Presbyterian church was built, the year Christ Church was built, the corps of the Presbyterian hospital and a third footprint scenario) and rewrote the register entries. Nothing finished in the first pass was redone; the rows it wrote stand except where a row below says it is superseded.

**Validator runs of the second pass:** `python3 -I tools/bt/town/validate_evidence.py town-churches`, the same with `--complete`, with `--check-ledger` (169 sources with local text checked), with `--all --allow-missing` (every group that has an evidence file: 1042 rows on the last run) and with `--self-test` (the synthetic cases, the examples, the real ledger and the robustness run) were run on 2026-11-16 after the last change to the file and pass; the file has 349 rows (building 12, field 287, search 50).

## 2. What the pass found, target by target

### Presbyterian Church (`bld-presbyterian-church`)

- **The site is settled within about 15 m**, and the congregation's own history now states it: "In December 1841 a lot was purchased on High and South Baltimore streets" (row 0307, documented). The Hopkins inset of 1858 (row 0058), the Sanborn plan of 1886 (row 0059) and two advertisements of the Compiler (row 0057) agree; the basemap bound of 40 m (confidence Contested) is replaced by 15 m. The 2023 outline (1662 square metres, 3.8 times the church of the plan of 1886) holds later work and is **not** the 1863 footprint (row 0060).
- **When it was built (conflict).** The history of 1886 says the new and present church was completed in 1840 (row 0053, inferred); the anniversary volume of 1915 says the lot was bought in December 1841 and "this building ... was erected in 1842", with the pews of the old building used in it, and its table of dates says the present structure was built in 1842 (row 0306, documented). A lot bought in December 1841 cannot hold a church completed in 1840, so the 1840 may speak of another building (the first church of 1816 stood on North Washington street). Both are carried; neither is chosen.
- **Form.** Brick; a west part of about 73 ft with one storey and 20 ft to the eaves and an east part of about 31 ft with two storeys on the plan of 1886 (rows 0061, 0062, 0063, 0065, 0066, 0067); shingle roof; no cupola drawn (row 0069); a basement (row 0310, inferred: the Sunday School met in it before the Sunday School rooms were added). **Later work (new):** Sunday School rooms were added in 1877-78 and the interior was renovated in 1884 (row 0308); if the two-storey east part of the plan of 1886 is the Sunday School addition, the church of 1863 was the west part, about 45 by 73 ft (row 0309, inferred): that is a third scenario beside the Hopkins symbol (about 29 by 47 ft, row 0064) and the plan as drawn (about 45 by 104 ft, row 0063); the three are in conflict and none is chosen (EG-02). The volume names no steeple, tower or cupola of the Presbyterian church.
- **Hospital use (documented, from five sources).** Sanitary Commission 1863: the Cavalry Corps Hospital under Dr. Rulison occupied the church and the two school-houses near it and held 300 wounded (row 0072). The congregation's own history of 1915 (new) says that during the pastorate of Mr. Finney "the church building was used as a Cavalry Hospital" (row 0304), which agrees. Christian Commission 1863 (new): the First Corps hospitals in the town, where many had been wounded on the first day, occupied the Presbyterian church, the Catholic church and the Court-house (row 0303): **the Christian Commission gives a different corps from the Sanitary Commission and the congregation** and the rows are carried as a conflict (they can all be true if the church changed hands, which none says). A neighbour's statement (new): two neighbours "came here on the afternoon of the first of July ... with food for the wounded" (row 0302, documented): the church held wounded on the afternoon of 1 July, which the first pass could only infer (row 0073); **the owner plan's "set up June 30" and "fell to Confederates evening Jul 1" are stated by no non-lead source** (their source, HMDB 17939, is a lead). Mrs. Stewart (new): the church "was occupied during the greater part, if not the whole, of the month of July, until the tent hospital — Camp Letterman — was established" (row 0301). The date it ended is still an open gap (row 0077, bounded). The church was a church again by 19 November 1863 (row 0074).
- **The interior (new, documented).** "Some of the men lay in the pews, and others on boards placed on top of the backs of the seats" (row 0076): the pews stood and boards bridged their tops. The Christian Commission found straw bedding in this church and made **no bunks** here (row 0300); the same work as in the Catholic church (wet musty straw cleaned out, fresh bedding, the house swept, the windows fixed to lower from the top) was done by two ladies of Michigan. The owner plan's "alternate pews removed" has no support (EG-04). **Damage (new):** "the church was much damaged"; help was raised at Pittsburgh and Philadelphia and the church was thoroughly renovated; the writer knows of no compensation asked or received (row 0305).
- **Photographs (new).** The volume of 1915 prints a halftone captioned *Presbyterian Church 1863* (printed p. 7; row 0345, documented as a photograph: the caption names the church, the month is not stated; a halftone of 100 ppi from the PDF of the archive.org item): the church seen from the corner at a slant, a brick wall with a row of tall narrow windows and two smaller windows above at the near end (row 0346, inferred), a stone base with four white posts, **a low stone retaining wall in front, a large tree between the wall and the building and a picket fence at the right** (row 0347, inferred; they agree with the description of 1885, row 0311), the front hidden by the tree. The cover photograph of the volume (before 1915) shows the west front toward Baltimore street with three round-arched openings, a round window in the gable and two chimneys on the ridge (row 0348, a **later** row: the arches may date from the renovation of 1884).
- **The pastor of July 1863 (new, row 0055, documented):** Rev. Henry Graham Finney, 1861-1864 (the table of pastors).
- **Gaps:** outbuildings (row 0071; the manse on Baltimore street was secured only in 1867), the day the hospital ended (row 0077). A stone retaining wall around the front lawn and trees before the entrance are recorded for 1885 only (row 0311, inferred, later: not drawn in July 1863 without a source that dates them).

### Christ Lutheran Church (`bld-poorhouse-christ-lutheran-wills#christ_lutheran_church`)

- **Documented (first pass):** built on the lot of 1886 in 1835 (row 0080); a hospital "occupied since the first day's battle" by the wounded of the 2nd Division of the First Corps (Hospital Scenes, row 0098) and "the Lutheran Church Hospital" of the retreat, where a sergeant of the 97th New York had his wound dressed and Chaplain Howell was shot on the steps (rows 0099); forty patients in the basement and a hundred more above it (rows 0100, 0101); steps up to a landing before the front door and a modest portico (row 0094); pews in tiers with the western tier set apart for the students (row 0095); alleys on each side (row 0083); the order to move the hospital came on a Monday, no date (row 0102).
- **New (documented; quoted at second hand from Dr. C. A. Hay's Historical Discourse of 18 February 1877 by the article of 1938).** "Our church was struck but once, and was not materially damaged" (row 0328); it was "the first one opened as a hospital ... occupied at once, and for nearly six weeks afterwards, by the Second Division of the First Corps" (row 0329); "Forty wounded men were crowded into the lecture room and one hundred into the body of the church, stretched upon boards that were laid across the tops of the pews" (row 0330): **the pews stood and boards bridged their tops**, which answers the question the first pass left open at row 0104. Some weeks after the battle the surviving patients were taken to the seminary (row 0331). The damage of the hospital use called for extensive renovation: the congregation appointed a survey committee on 23 November 1863 and a committee of ladies solicited subscriptions at Philadelphia, and on 8 January 1864 an officer was authorized to close the claim for damages with the Government; no compensation has been received (row 0332). **Built:** the same article says an organization was effected in 1835 and a church built in 1836 (row 0327): a conflict with the 1835 of row 0080, carried.
- **A photograph that names it (new).** LOC 2024640778, a Tyson stereograph (no. 578), is captioned *Chambersburg Street, from the east corner of the diamond, showing the cupola of the College Church, occupied as a hospital after the battle* (row 0337 documented: what it shows; row 0339: the caption says the church was a hospital). The cupola is an **open round lantern of tall arched openings between slender piers under a projecting cornice ring, with a low hemispherical dome and a small finial, on a square stage above the roofs** (row 0338, inferred: the card is dated between 1861 and 1865, after the battle by its own caption, and no text says the cupola is unchanged since 1863). It fits the round symbol of 10 ft of the plan of 1886 (row 0093).
- **Form (inferred, `describes` later):** brick, two storeys, 22 ft to the eaves, about 50 by 103 ft with a rear projection (rows 0086, 0089, 0090, 0093, 0091); the Hopkins symbol is half the size in both directions (25 by 52 ft): a conflict is carried (rows 0087, 0088). The 2023 outline is not the 1863 footprint.
- **Gaps:** the date the hospital closed (row 0105, bounded), additions and damage beyond the above (row 0106), outbuildings (row 0097); the street number (30 or 44 Chambersburg Street) is stated by no local text (search row 0034).

### St. Francis Xavier Catholic church (`bld-st-francis-xavier`)

- **Documented (first pass; Reily 1885, Barton 1898):** the brick church of 1852-53, 48 ft wide, 90 ft long, about 40 ft high, a Roman cupola of about 60 ft, inside walls about 26 ft, 64 pews, a gallery (rows 0117, 0119, 0120, 0125, 0127); a hospital "before noon of July 1, 1863" for several weeks, Mass said meanwhile at the house of Nicholas Codori (rows 0132, 0133, 0135); the damage repaired from 4 January 1864 (row 0134). Barton: the soldiers "lay on the pew seats, under them and in every aisle ... in the sanctuary and in the gallery" (row 0137).
- **New.** The Christian Commission report says that **bunks were made for all the patients, raising them from the floor**, the wet, musty straw was cleaned out and fresh sweet bedding put in, the house was swept and the windows fixed so that they could be lowered from the top, and the Sisters of Charity washed and fed the men (row 0333); it also lists the church among the First Corps hospitals of the town, where many had been wounded on the first day (row 0334). Mrs. Stewart says the Catholic church, "opposite" the United Presbyterian church on West High street, was "thrown open and filled to its utmost capacity" (rows 0335, 0336). The accounts of Barton (pews, floors) and of the Commission (straw, then bunks) describe different times or parts and neither is dated; **neither says that pews were removed or boards laid** (EG-04).
- **Form from the plan of 1886 (inferred):** the footprint is about 49 by 89 ft, the size of the description of 1853 (rows 0118); the label gives 22 ft to the eaves against 26 ft for the inside walls: carried as a conflict (rows 0120, 0121). The Hopkins inset has the label Cath. Ch. and no hatched symbol (row 0114).
- **The Sisters of Charity:** Barton says Father Burlando with twelve Sisters left Emmitsburg on the Sunday after the third day; the owner plan says fourteen, from a web article (a lead): a matter for the hospital pass.

### Town church belfry (`bld-town-belfry`)

- **No source names the church.** Six accounts speak of a steeple or of steeples and belfries of the churches (Wiedrich's battery on East Cemetery Hill, a dispatch and a Times special of Thursday 2 July, the Times account of 2 July, Willis, and Col. Morrow in "a church steeple" until 3 July, who is not said to be the sharpshooters' steeple): rows 0150, 0151, 0152, 0138, 0139. The identity is an open gap (row 0141, bounded below); storeys, footprint, walls, roof and outbuildings are open gaps (no source gives any dimension).
- **The churches whose steeples or lanterns the sources document (new list; none is chosen):** St. James (its pastor "watched the progress of the great battle ... from the steeple of this church" on 1 July 1863: row 0320, documented, second-hand); the Catholic church (a Roman cupola of about 60 ft: row 0125); the German Reformed church (an open lantern of four slender posts under a low dome: rows 0188, 0341); Christ Lutheran Church (an open arcaded lantern under a low dome: row 0338). The Sanborn plan draws the same round symbol at four churches (EG-06). That the sharpshooters used any one of them is stated by no source (EG-01).
- **Photographs.** LOC 2014646001 (O'Sullivan, from Cemetery Hill; the same plate as 2006685383) shows two small towers with domed caps beside a taller tower with a clock face (row 0153). LOC 2012647703 (the second plate of the charge of the Louisiana Tigers, from Cemetery Hill; read this pass from the master TIFF) shows at the left edge of the town a church-like building end-on with a narrow drum, a bulbous cap and a needle finial (row 0344, inferred: **which church is not told**; the caption names none and no camera position is documented). LOC 2012647712, "from Cemetery Hill at about position of right gun of Wiedrich's Battery", shows the German Reformed Church (rows 0190, 0188): an observation, not an identification of the sharpshooters' steeple. The photographs viewed with nothing to tell are in the search rows (`0295` to `0298`).

### Other churches standing in July 1863 (the list is a finding)

Rupp (1846, p. 527) lists eight churches of the borough in 1844 (row 0239). The pass finds **eight churches in July 1863**: the Presbyterian, Christ Lutheran, St. James' Lutheran, the German Reformed (the Union Brick Church of 1814, enlarged 1862), the Methodist Episcopal of 1822, the Catholic, the United Presbyterian (the Associate Reformed, remodelled 1859) and the Coloured Church. **The Christian Commission report says that on Saturday to Tuesday, 4 to 7 July, "every church and public building, and in fact every private house, was filled with wounded men"** (page 68; the report prints Tuesday as July 7): applied to the churches that no source names it is an inference, recorded as `state` rows of tier inferred with the window 4 to 7 July (rows 0318, 0317, 0250). Documented hospitals by name: the Presbyterian, Christ Lutheran, the Catholic, St. James' (five weeks), the German Reformed, the United Presbyterian and the Methodist (no date).

- **Methodist Episcopal Church of 1822 (TWN-CHU-0001):** built 1822 on Middle Street, bought by the G. A. R. post in 1880 (rows 0156, 0159); a hospital (Barton: a Sister of Charity nursed the sick in it; row 0169, no date; window 4 to 7 July inferred: row 0318). The Hopkins symbol (about 38 by 67 ft) and the hall of 1886 remodelled in 1880 (about 36 by 40 ft) are a conflict (rows 0163, 0164). Tower, outbuildings: gaps. **No photograph found.**
- **German Reformed Church (TWN-CHU-0002):** the Union Brick Church of 1814 at High and Stratton Streets, enlarged and re-dedicated June 1862 (rows 0172, 0173, 0175), brick; about 45 by 70 ft on the plan of 1886 and 51 by 72 ft on the inset (they agree). **A hospital, documented twice (new):** "The Reformed Church and the Public School building next to it ... were filled with wounded men" (Mrs. Stewart, row 0194) and the printed caption of the Tyson card LOC 2024640779: *Stratton Street ... and German Reformed Church on the hill, used as a hospital after the battle* (row 0343). The card (Tyson no. 561, catalogued [between 1863 and 1880]; rows 0340, 0341, 0342) shows the church from Stratton Street: an **open lantern of four slender posts under a plain cornice with a low hemispherical dome and a small finial, on a square stage on a tall light-coloured wall** (the colour read as a wash or plaster on the brick: inferred), behind a stone house with a shingle roof; it agrees with the lantern of LOC 2012647712 (row 0188). Outbuildings: a gap.
- **United Presbyterian Church (TWN-CHU-0003):** remodelled in 1859 (a vestibule and a gallery at the east end; the outside painted the colour of the new court house; rows 0198, 0202, 0201); services on 25 October and 26 November 1863. **New:** a church of the congregation stood by June 1813 as the Associate Reformed Church (row 0197: a bound, not the year of building); in July 1863 it was a hospital "filled to their utmost capacity", on West High Street opposite the Catholic church, standing some distance from the street, on the site where the United Brethren church stood in 1915 (rows 0312, 0313, 0314); the grounds were enclosed by a fence and, between the church and the fence, **two long trenches** were dug and the dead, wrapped only in blankets, were buried as close together as they could be laid; heavy boards were laid over them after rain (rows 0315, 0316); the Christian Commission's "church opposite", where the same work as in the Catholic church (bunks, straw, bedding, windows) was done by two ladies of Michigan, is read as this church, which stood across West High street from the Catholic church (row 0313). About 49 by 53 ft on the plan of 1886 and 39 by 69 ft on the inset: a conflict (rows 0206, 0207). Tower: a gap.
- **St. James' Lutheran Church (TWN-CHU-0004):** "a very large finely constructed brick church about 40 by 70 feet, with a basement and gallery" (1912 list, row 0225); a hospital after 3 July 1863 "for a considerable period", damaged, the damages paid in December 1863 (rows 0234, 0235); services on 6 August 1863 (row 0236). **New (second-hand through the article of 1938; the church record of 1864 of the pastor):** the pastor was Rev. Abraham Essick, 1861-1864 (row 0319); he "watched the progress of the great battle ... from the steeple of this church" (row 0320: **a steeple**); the parsonage "hard by" hid him while shells fell (row 0321); "even this sacred place was not exempt from hostile bullets" (row 0323); "for five weeks this house was used as a hospital ... these floors and these pews were stained with patriot blood ... from this altar ... were dispensed the soothing cordial and healing ointment" (rows 0322, 0325: the pews stood); the repair bill was $2,024.17 and the Government paid $120 in May 1915 (row 0324). Five weeks from 1 to 3 July end on 5 to 7 August, which agrees with the service held in the church on 6 August (row 0236). The congregation withdrew in 1848 from the old union church to the site at York and Stratton streets where a new church was built (row 0218: inferred, the year of building is not stated); Buehler places the church "at the corner of York and Stratton streets" on 26 June 1863 (row 0326). Plan of 1886: three levels, 35 ft to the eaves, about 45 by 72 ft, a cupola symbol (rows 0224, 0228, 0231).
- **Coloured Church (TWN-CHU-0005):** labelled on the inset of 1858 and, as an African church, in the list of 1844; read as one church (row 0239, inferred); standing in July 1863 is **inferred** from continuity (row 0240); in the hospital use by the general statement only (row 0250, inferred); no form, no other source (search rows: five hits, all of other places). Not drawn beyond a marked point unless the owner decides (EG-05).
- **Not drawn as 1863 (and why):** the Methodist church of **1872** (TWN-CHU-0006) and the Episcopal chapel of **1876** (TWN-CHU-0007) are dated by the History of 1886 and are `not_yet_built` (rows 0252, 0264); the frame **chapel of the plan of 1886** (TWN-CHU-0008) has no date in any source and the Hopkins inset of 1858 shows nothing there: its existence in July 1863 is an open gap (row 0275) and it is not drawn until a source dates it. The **White Church** of the First Corps hospital (USSC p. 23; hospitals.json H-04) is St. Mark's German Reformed Church on the Baltimore Pike, **not a church of the borough** (the Commission's Mrs. Spencer worked there: Brockett; a request note goes to the passes concerned).

## 8. Method and limits

- **Positions.** Hopkins 1858: the symbol centre was read in scan pixels (gridded 6x views) and carried to the ground by the affine of `docs/basemap1863/maps/transforms/hopkins-1858-gettysburg-inset.json` (leave-one-out 2.85 m inside the borough); the symbols are schematic and differ from the Sanborn footprints by 10 to 18 m, so the Hopkins readings carry `error_m` 15 (25 for St. James, 60 for the Catholic label). Sanborn 1886: each sheet was registered by a similarity through two street crossings of the Hopkins control at the nominal scale of the plan (288 px = 1 inch = 50 ft at full size); the plan is **continuity evidence only**, so every value read from it is `inferred` with `describes` later. The second pass added no map reading; the third footprint scenario of the Presbyterian church subtracts a part of the first pass's reading (row 0309).
- **Text layers.** Damaged words are quoted as they stand (verbatim mode); no OCR correction touches a digit. The new texts have no page marks: the printed page is read from the page heads (USCC-1863: a head such as "THE PENNSYLVANIA INVASION. 73" stands before the text of its page) or from the bare number line at the head of each page (PRESBY-175TH-1915); the magazine of 1938 has two running heads inside the article, so its rows give page 8 or 9 by their position before or after the head "THE LUTHERAN 9"; BUEHLER-1900 has no page numbers (page null with a note). A quotation that crosses a page break uses `[...]` where the page number stands in the text.
- **Second-hand quotations.** The article of 1938 prints passages of three texts that are not otherwise available (the church record of 1864 of the St. James pastor Abraham Essick, the Historical Discourse of Dr. C. A. Hay of 1877, Dr. J. B. Baker's History of St. James). The rows that rest on it carry a `note` saying so and are cited as what the article says the text says; the Law's rule is kept (a non-lead source states exactly the claim), but the tier does not claim the originals were read. Rights of the 1938 article were not verified: facts and short quotations only, nothing of its text is shipped.
- **Photographs.** Every reading was made from an image that was opened and looked at (the 4096 px copy and, for the Tyson cards and for LOC 2012647703, native crops of the master TIFF: `tmp/library/town/LOC-PHOTO-CHURCHES-2/`, with the hashes of the masters). The captions of the Tyson cards name the buildings: **the card says which church**. The College Church card (no. 578) is catalogued [between 1861 and 1865] and its form rows say `after_battle_to_1869`; the German Reformed card (no. 561) is catalogued [between 1863 and 1880], so its form rows say `later`; the captions put both after the battle. A form read from them is `inferred`, not documented, because no text says the form is unchanged since 1863 (PLAN 3.4). No camera position is claimed. A tower in a photograph whose caption names no church is never given a name here.
- **Scale of the work.** The machine was loaded (load average above 30 on 4 vCPUs) during the second pass, so the work was reading, searching and a few light downloads; no job was run through the benchmark lock and no browser was started.
