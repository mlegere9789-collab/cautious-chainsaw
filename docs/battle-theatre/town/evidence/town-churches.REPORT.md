## 2. What the pass found, target by target

### Presbyterian Church (`bld-presbyterian-church`)

- **The site is settled within about 15 m.** The Hopkins inset of 1858 (the black rectangle beside the label Presb'n Ch., row 0058) and the Sanborn plan of 1886 (the footprint, row 0059) agree within 16 m, two advertisements of the Compiler place the church at Baltimore and High streets (row 0057), and the 2023 outline holds the Sanborn centre. The basemap bound of 40 m (confidence Contested) is replaced by 15 m. The 2023 outline STR-TOWN-PRESBYTERIAN_CHURCH covers 1662 square metres, 3.8 times the church of the plan of 1886 (about 45 by 104 ft, 435 square metres): it holds later work and is **not** the 1863 footprint (identity row 0060).
- **Hospital use (documented).** The Sanitary Commission report of 1863 (p. 24): the Cavalry Corps Hospital "occupied the Presbyterian Church on Baltimore street, and the two school-houses in the immediate vicinity. It was under the charge of Dr. Rulison, and the three buildings contained 300 of our wounded" (row 0072, `hospital_id` H-01). The first day is the earliest day a source supports (row 0073, inferred); **the owner plan's "set up June 30" and "fell to Confederates evening Jul 1" are not stated by any non-lead source of this pass** (the plan's source is HMDB 17939, class lead in the ledger): they stay unproved (EG-03). The church was a church again by 19 November 1863, when the address of Col. Anderson was given in it before a full house (row 0074, day inferred from the account). The date the hospital left is an open gap (row 0077).
- **Form (all inferred, `describes` later: the Sanborn plan is 23 years after the battle).** Brick; a west part of about 73 ft with one storey and 20 ft to the eaves and an east part of about 31 ft with two storeys; footprint about 45 by 104 ft; shingle roof; no cupola drawn (rows 0061, 0062, 0065, 0066, 0067). The Hopkins symbol of 1858 is a black rectangle of about 29 by 47 ft: the two footprints are carried as a **conflict** (rows 0063, 0064). The roof form is a generic gable (rule GR-ROOF-GABLE-30, row 0068).
- **Gaps:** the pastor of July 1863 (row 0055; only a former pastor, Dr. Watson, is named), how the interior was changed (row 0076), the date the hospital use ended, outbuildings. No damage is drawn (rule GR-NO-DAMAGE-WITHOUT-SOURCE, row 0075).

### Christ Lutheran Church (`bld-poorhouse-christ-lutheran-wills#christ_lutheran_church`)

- **Documented:** built 1835 on the lot of 1886 (row 0080); a hospital "occupied since the first day's battle" by the wounded of the 2nd Division of the First Corps (Hospital Scenes, row 0098) and "the Lutheran Church Hospital" of the retreat through the town, where a sergeant of the 97th New York had his wound dressed and Chaplain Howell was shot on the steps (New York at Gettysburg vol. I, rows 0099); forty patients in the basement and a hundred more above it on the first day of the nursing (rows 0100, 0101); steps up to a landing before the front door and a modest portico (row 0094); pews in tiers with the western tier set apart for the students (row 0095); alleys on each side of the church (row 0083); the order to move the hospital came on a Monday (row 0102, no date).
- **Form (inferred, `describes` later):** brick, two storeys, 22 ft to the eaves, about 50 by 103 ft with a rear projection of about 9.5 by 18 ft, a round yellow symbol of about 10 ft at the street end read as a cupola, shingle roof (rows 0086, 0089, 0090, 0093, 0091). The Hopkins symbol is **half the size in both directions** (25 by 52 ft): a conflict is carried (rows 0087, 0088). The 2023 outline (1777 square metres) is not the 1863 footprint (the plan of 1886 covers about 480).
- **Gaps:** the date the hospital closed (row 0105), damage and additions (rows 0106), outbuildings (row 0097); the street number (30 or 44 Chambersburg Street) is stated by no local text (search row, no row can carry it). The owner plan's "pews removed, boards laid" is not repeated by any non-lead source: Hospital Scenes says the wounded lay on "the hard boards" (floor or pews not stated; row 0104).

### St. Francis Xavier Catholic church (`bld-st-francis-xavier`)

- **Documented (Reily 1885, Barton 1898):** the brick church of 1852-53, 48 ft wide, 90 ft long, about 40 ft high, a Roman cupola of about 60 ft, inside walls about 26 ft, 64 pews, a gallery over the entrance (rows 0117, 0119, 0120, 0125, 0127); a hospital "before noon of July 1, 1863" for several weeks, Mass said meanwhile at the house of Nicholas Codori (rows 0132, 0133, 0135); the damage done during the battle repaired from 4 January 1864 (row 0134). Barton: the soldiers "lay on the pew seats, under them and in every aisle ... in the sanctuary and in the gallery" (row 0137): **the pews stood**; the owner plan's "alternate pews removed, boards laid on the rest" has no non-lead source (EG-04).
- **Form from the plan of 1886 (inferred):** the footprint is about 49 by 89 ft, **the size of the description of 1853** (rows 0118); the label gives 22 ft to the eaves against 26 ft for the inside walls: carried as a conflict (rows 0120, 0121). The Hopkins inset has the label Cath. Ch. and no hatched symbol: the place is carried at the label with 60 m and the nearest mark is named in the row (row 0114).
- **The Sisters of Charity:** Barton says that Father Burlando with twelve Sisters left Emmitsburg on the Sunday after the third day (5 July by the order of the text; more may have come later); the owner plan says fourteen, from a web article (a lead): the number is a matter for the hospital pass.

### Town church belfry (`bld-town-belfry`)

- **No source names the church.** Six accounts speak of a steeple or of steeples and belfries of the churches: Wiedrich's battery on East Cemetery Hill (sharpshooters "posted in a steeple in the town" on the first day; "the sharpshooters in the church steeple were very annoying", two officers and several men hit, horses killed, a shell fired at the steeple), a dispatch of Thursday 2 July (sharpshooters "fire at us from the steeples of churches"), a Times special of Thursday 3:30 p.m. (they are "shooting at our men from the steeples of churches"), the Times account of 2 July ("the steeples and belfreys of the churches"), Willis (during the night of 1-2 July "houses and steeples of public buildings"), the Park Service study of the McPherson farm (Col. Morrow in "a church steeple" until 3 July: not said to be the sharpshooters' steeple) (rows 0150, 0151, 0152, 0138, 0139). The identity is an open gap (row 0141); storeys, footprint, walls, roof and outbuildings are open gaps (no source gives any dimension).
- **Photographs.** LOC 2014646001 (O'Sullivan, from Cemetery Hill) shows two small towers with domed caps beside a taller tower with a clock face (row 0153, inferred: the caption names no church); LOC 2014646002 (from the north-west) shows the town as a low band and no church can be told (search row). LOC 2012647712, taken "from Cemetery Hill at about position of right gun of Wiedrich's Battery", shows the German Reformed Church in the background with an open lantern on its roof (rows 0190, 0188): that is an **observation, not an identification** of the sharpshooters' steeple (EG-01).

### Other churches standing in July 1863 (the list is a finding)

Rupp (1846, p. 527) lists eight churches of the borough in 1844: Presbyterian, Lutheran, Union, Methodist, German Reformed, Independent, Catholic and African (row 0239). The pass finds **eight churches in July 1863**: the Presbyterian, Christ Lutheran, St. James' Lutheran, the German Reformed (the Union Brick Church of 1814, enlarged 1862), the Methodist Episcopal of 1822, the Catholic, the United Presbyterian (the Associate Reformed, remodelled 1859) and the Coloured Church. Five are documented hospitals (the Presbyterian, Christ Lutheran, the Catholic, the Methodist and St. James'); no source says the German Reformed, the United Presbyterian or the Coloured Church served. The 1844 "Union" and "Independent" churches could not be matched with one of these (EG-08).
- **Methodist Episcopal Church of 1822 (TWN-CHU-0001):** built 1822 on Middle Street, bought by the G. A. R. post in 1880 (rows 0156, 0159); a hospital (Barton: a Sister of Charity nursed the sick in it; row 0169, no date). The Hopkins symbol (about 38 by 67 ft) and the hall of 1886 remodelled in 1880 (about 36 by 40 ft) are a conflict (rows 0163, 0164). Tower, outbuildings: gaps.
- **German Reformed Church (TWN-CHU-0002):** the Union Brick Church of 1814 at High and Stratton Streets, enlarged and re-dedicated June 1862, services in June and December 1863 (rows 0172, 0173, 0175); brick; about 45 by 70 ft on the plan of 1886 and about 51 by 72 ft on the Hopkins inset (they agree: no conflict); LOC 2012647712 shows the church with **an open lantern with a domed cap on the roof ridge** (rows 0190, 0188, 0189). Hospital use or damage: no source found (gap).
- **United Presbyterian Church (TWN-CHU-0003):** remodelled in 1859 (a vestibule and a gallery at the east end; the outside painted the colour of the new court house; rows 0198, 0202, 0201); services on 25 October and 26 November 1863; two soldiers buried in its graveyard after the battle (row 0214). The year it was built is a gap. About 49 by 53 ft on the plan of 1886 and 39 by 69 ft on the inset: a conflict (rows 0206, 0207).
- **St. James' Lutheran Church (TWN-CHU-0004):** "a very large finely constructed brick church about 40 by 70 feet, with a basement and gallery" (1912 list of claims, row 0225); a hospital after 3 July 1863 "for a considerable period", damaged, the damages paid in December 1863 (rows 0234, 0235); services on 6 August 1863 (row 0236). Plan of 1886: three levels, 35 ft to the eaves, about 45 by 72 ft, a cupola symbol (rows 0224, 0228, 0231). The year it was built is a gap.
- **Coloured Church (TWN-CHU-0005):** labelled on the inset of 1858 and, as an African church, in the list of 1844; read as one church (identity row 0239, inferred); standing in July 1863 is **inferred** from continuity (row 0240); no form, no other source (search rows: five hits, all of other places). Not drawn beyond a marked point unless the owner decides (EG-05).
- **Not drawn as 1863 (and why):** the Methodist church of **1872** (TWN-CHU-0006) and the Episcopal chapel of **1876** (TWN-CHU-0007) are dated by the History of 1886 and are `not_yet_built` (rows 0252, 0264); the frame **chapel of the plan of 1886** (TWN-CHU-0008) has no date in any source and the Hopkins inset of 1858 shows nothing there: its existence in July 1863 is an open gap (row 0275) and it is not drawn until a source dates it. The **White Church** of the First Corps hospital (USSC p. 23; hospitals.json H-04) is St. Mark's German Reformed Church on the Baltimore Pike, **not a church of the borough**: a request note goes to the passes concerned.

## 6. Generic rules used (a procedure, never a fact)

- Rows by rule: `GR-NO-DAMAGE-WITHOUT-SOURCE` 7, `GR-ROOF-GABLE-30` 7.
- `GR-ROOF-GABLE-30` fills the roof form where the plan of 1886 gives only the material (a ridge along the long side, 30 degrees); `GR-NO-DAMAGE-WITHOUT-SOURCE` is the intact state before the first dated use or damage of each building. No other rule is used: the footprint rule `GR-FOOTPRINT-2023` is **not** used for any church because the outlines of 2023 are of later buildings.

## 8. Method and limits

- **Positions.** Hopkins 1858: the symbol centre was read in scan pixels (gridded 6x views) and carried to the ground by the affine of `docs/basemap1863/maps/transforms/hopkins-1858-gettysburg-inset.json` (leave-one-out 2.85 m inside the borough); the symbols are schematic and differ from the Sanborn footprints by 10 to 18 m (Presbyterian 15.8 m, Christ Lutheran 17.7 m, Methodist 15.3 m, Reformed 17.3 m, United Presbyterian 10.6 m, St. James 10.5 m), so the Hopkins readings carry `error_m` 15 (25 for St. James, whose mark is not stated, and for the Coloured Church, which lies outside the control; 60 for the Catholic label) and the Sanborn readings 15 (20 for the Methodist church of 1872). Sanborn 1886: each sheet was registered by a similarity through two street crossings of the Hopkins control (Baltimore x Middle and Baltimore x High for sheet 1; Baltimore x Middle and Stratton x York for sheet 2; Washington x Chambersburg and Washington x Middle for sheet 3) at the nominal scale of the plan (288 px = 1 inch = 50 ft at full size); the plan is **continuity evidence only**, so every value read from it is `inferred` with `describes` later.
- **Sizes.** The full-size crops were measured with scripts (`tmp/town-town-churches/w/measure.py`, `outline.py`, `pct25blobs.py`): fill and outline agree to within a foot; the Catholic footprint (49 by 89 ft) equals the 48 by 90 ft that Reily gives for 1853, which checks the scale. Values are given to the nearest foot and within 2 ft.
- **Text layers.** Damaged words are quoted as they stand (verbatim mode); no OCR correction touches a digit. Printed pages of the Conewago book follow the page marks of its PDF text (printed page = PDF page minus 14); the weeklies have no page marks and say so.
- **Photographs.** Every reading was made from an image that was opened and looked at; no camera position is claimed; the lantern of the German Reformed Church is `documented` only as what the record of LOC 2012647712 names; which building of the print is the church rests on the record and on position (inferred, row 0188).
