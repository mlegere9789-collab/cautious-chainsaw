# 1863 ground surface: Warren contours, error model, de-modernization (PDF B.9.2 steps 2 and 3)

Run 2026-10-19/20. Everything here is a candidate surface and a set of measurements. **It is not a verified 1863 ground.** The Warren sheet is a survey of 1868-69 (revised 1873); the modern surface is the 2017 lidar. Nothing in this folder claims the Law item 1 or Gate Q16 is met. All figures are read from the JSON files named next to them; scripts are `tools/bt/elev1863_*.py` (run order below). No network request was made: the existing scan, DEM and layers in `tmp/b1863` and `docs/basemap1863` were reused read-only; `docs/basemap1863` and `tools/b1863` were not edited.

## 1. Source used, and what was not used

* Warren sheet: LOC 99448794 (call G3824.G3 187- W3), the 1:12,000 reduction, 8,878 x 9,845 px (`tmp/b1863/maps/scans/cw0353500.jp2`; the gray copy `landcover/warren_full_gray.png` is the same pixels). It covers the **whole field**, not "portions of sheets 2 and 3". The PDF's LOC 99446453 (the 1886 tracing, 1:2,400) and the NARA full sheets were **not obtained** (earlier probe: LOC 403, NARA robots); all 4-ft detail below is the 1:12,000 reduction, where 4 ft of relief on a 10 percent slope is one pixel.
* Elevation reference: 3DEP 1 m bare earth, lidar project PA_South_Central_B1_2017 (`tmp/b1863/maps/ref/dem_1m.tif`, EPSG:32618).
* Warren legend (read on the scan): contours 4 ft apart, datum "500 feet below the cemetery", 1:12,000.

| 6 road-line points | 13.9 m | 19.6 m |
| b1863 check 2, 45 road runs | 17.1 m | 11.1 m (in-sample) |
| level-free contour coherence: modern DEM variance along each extracted contour, 7,560 fragments | 8.15 m2, better in 38.5 percent of fragments | **7.04 m2**, better in 61.5 percent |
| regional shift still needed for contours to lie level on the modern relief (25 cells of 2,000 px) | median 49 m, p90 68 m | **median 30 m, p90 55 m** |

**Disagreement of the two frames over the sheet** (30,744 grid points): median **61 m**, p95 161 m, max 203 m, mean vector (E,N) = (-17, -42) m; by region: NE 30 m, SE 39 m, NW and SW 80 m medians. This is larger than b1863 check 2's 24.5 m median because that was measured on road runs, which both frames were tuned to; away from roads the frames diverge more. Residual map: `frames_residual_map.png`.

**Choice: the FFD frame for all elevation work, by evidence, not averaged.** For a terrain product the question is how well Warren relief lies on the modern relief; three independent relief measures (contour coherence, residual shift, label fit) favour the FFD, while point controls and the 6 road-line points favour the affine. The conflict b1863 check 2 reported is therefore confirmed, not resolved. Caveat: the FFD was fitted to modern roads, which in valleys follow valley floors, so part of its relief advantage may be that; it cannot be separated here. The affine run is kept as a full sensitivity case (`error_model.json` > `by_frame.affine`: errors are 10 to 30 percent larger). Whichever frame, horizontal uncertainty is about 16 to 30 m, and every elevation residual below contains slope times that displacement (on a 10 percent slope, 1.6 to 3 m).

## 3. Task 2: contour extraction (`warren_contours_extracted.geojson.gz`, `extraction_qa.json`, `anchors_read_labels.json`)

**Method** (scripts s1 to s5): ink = darker than the local median by 40 grey levels; thick blobs (hachures, tree glyphs, buildings, lettering) removed by a 5 px opening; skeleton (scikit-image, `skan` graph, spurs under 12 px pruned); branches paired at junctions by straight-through angle; chains linked across gaps up to 32 px if within 35 degrees; rejected: chains under 40 px, long straight chains (roads, straight fences, neatline) unless they come as a parallel ladder of two or more parallel neighbours (steep slopes), the more fence-like of two chains that overlap. Result: **28,161 chains, 11,548 of at least 40 px, 9,750 kept** (1,705,110 px of line; geometry in the FFD frame, UTM 18N). Adjacent fragments were found by perpendicular ray casting (22,264 raw edges; 10,248 reliable edges after requiring at least 5 hits each way, 16 in all, median gap at most 60 px).

**Labels.** Every printed contour value was read by eye from crops of the scan (3x zoom). 91 labels were read: 64 margin labels (value at the neatline, contour found by extrapolating fragment ends to the neatline) and 27 interior labels (hilltop rings; several are spot-height style, not a multiple of 4: 404, 425, 444, 448, 452, 456, 464, 484, 498, 508, 604 etc.). Not read: any label I could not read; none invented. The Culp's Hill ring shows 508 (the b1863 control point note says 408; digits are obscured by hachures: **a reading conflict, unresolved**; 508 is what I read at 3x zoom; the DEM was not used to choose it). **48 labels were accepted as anchors** (margin: extrapolated end within 10 px of the label's neatline crossing; interior: label inside a ring closed within 60 px, or within 15 px of the line); 43 were rejected (no clean fragment, fragment lost in woods or hachures).

**Levels.** Warren ink gives order but not height. A contour fragment's level (feet above Warren's datum) is set by walking the adjacency graph from the anchors: neighbour = +4 or -4 ft, the direction (uphill or downhill) taken from the sign of the **modern 3DEP surface smoothed over 50 m** (orientation only; no DEM height is copied; this is the "use the DEM only as a sanity check" rule stretched to its limit and it is stated here because the sign of the slope is the one thing the DEM supplies). Levels are kept only within 6 graph hops of an anchor and, if several anchors vote, only if they agree within 8 ft. **Validation against Warren-only truth** (leave-one-anchor-out on the printed labels, `cv_label_holdout.json`): of 48 anchors only 17 can be predicted from the others; **76 percent are within one contour (4 ft) and 88 percent within 8 ft** (hops 0 to 2: 75 percent within 4 ft, n = 8; 3 to 4: 60 percent, n = 5; 5 to 6: 100 percent, n = 4). n is small: these are indicative, not tight.

**Absolute height.** The sheet's datum is a reference "500 ft below the cemetery"; the NAVD88 height of that reference is not recorded anywhere I have. One constant converts feet to metres: `z_m = K + 0.3048 * z_ft`, **K = 36.54 m** (bootstrap 95 percent interval 35.8 to 37.3 m, scatter 2.2 m over 35 margin labels; the 13 interior labels give 36.35 m independently). K is calibrated against the modern DEM, which makes the mean residual near zero by construction: **the error model therefore has no absolute-height information; it is a relative one** (shape of Warren against shape of the lidar). The one independent absolute check is the 13 hilltop rings: DEM summit minus ring level has mean +0.95 m (Warren says 0 to 1.2 m, expected +0.6 m); rms about +0.6 m is 2.1 m.

**Coverage, measured.** Sheet inner neatline (marginalia and legend outside it excluded), FFD frame: **69.83 km2**; the Battle Theatre prototype box (6.16 x 6.12 km, `terrain.json`) lies **100 percent inside the sheet** (37.64 km2); the b1863 box (235.4 km2) is 29.7 percent covered by the sheet, and East Cavalry Field and everything outside the sheet has **no Warren data**. Within the sheet:

| area | contour lines extracted within 30 m | **trusted Warren surface** (a level assigned within 6 hops of a printed label, TIN valid) |
|---|---|---|
| Warren sheet 69.83 km2 | 93.7 percent | **14.2 percent (9.92 km2)** |
| Battle Theatre prototype box 37.64 km2 | 95.5 percent | **10.0 percent (3.77 km2)** |

So the extraction reaches nearly the whole sheet geometrically, but **a surface with a level can be built over only about one tenth to one seventh of the battlefield**: the limit is the number of readable anchors (48), not line tracing. The remainder is marked uncovered (provenance 2); no level is guessed there. Extending the coverage needs more printed labels (the 1:2,400 sheets, which print more) or a second independent level source.

**Hand check, 40 random selected fragments plus 30 random trusted fragments** (`extraction_qa.json`; one reviewer, me; judged by eye on the fragment geometry): **28 of 40 clean (70 percent)**; 12 fail: 8 wander through tree glyphs and hatching in dense woods, 2 follow a road or fence line, 1 follows town buildings, 1 follows the neatline frame. Among the 30 trusted fragments **26 are clean (87 percent)**, 4 fail (a wavy fence or road edge, a field boundary, a neatline-adjacent line). Woods and the borough are the weak areas and are not masked separately.

## 4. Task 3: the elevation error model (`error_model.json`, `warren_tin_1863.tif`, `warren_grade.tif`, `warren_minus_dem.tif`, `residual_map.png`)

TIN: Delaunay over 102,335 vertices on the 1,265 trusted fragments (3 px spacing, FFD frame), triangles with edges over 90 m or more than 60 m from a vertex invalid, 1 m grid on the DEM grid. Residual = Warren TIN minus modern DEM (positive: Warren higher). Terrain class from the **modern** DEM: hilltop TPI(150 m) at least +3 m; stream within 20 m of an NHD flowline and TPI at most -1.5 m; valley floor TPI at most -3 m and slope at most 8 degrees; slope at least 3 degrees; flat otherwise (class shares of the DEM: flat 55.5 percent, slope 33.4, hilltop 5.1, stream 4.0, valley floor 1.9).

**Measured vertical differences, Warren-derived minus modern, FFD frame, metres. These replace "about 50 ft" (15.2 m) as the working error model for relative height; the numbers are 4 px (about 4 m) samples along contours, so neighbouring samples are strongly correlated and the count of independent contours ("frag") is the honest sample size.**

| terrain class | hop 0 (labelled contour itself): n pts / frag, mean, RMS, p95 abs | hops 1-3: n pts / frag, mean, RMS, p95 abs | hops 4-6: n pts / frag, mean, RMS, p95 abs |
|---|---|---|---|
| flat | 1,097 / 36, +0.50, 2.45, 6.46 | 11,544 / 327, +0.90, 2.98, 6.12 | 21,344 / 657, +0.76, 2.74, 5.76 |
| slope | 837 / 39, +0.88, 3.05, 7.03 | 9,572 / 329, +1.13, 3.06, 6.30 | 22,898 / 705, +1.07, 3.27, 6.40 |
| hilltop | 212 / 13, -1.61, 2.54, 4.50 | 1,642 / 86, -1.40, 2.84, 5.11 | 4,661 / 249, -1.51, 3.30, 6.59 |
| valley floor | 11 / 3, +2.44, 2.94, 3.79 (too few) | 420 / 52, **+3.48, 4.45**, 7.08 | 1,186 / 112, **+3.17, 5.36**, 9.56 |
| stream | 103 / 5, -0.04, 1.64, 3.10 (too few) | 872 / 51, **+2.94, 4.57**, 10.62 | 2,065 / 131, **+3.13, 4.39**, 7.76 |
| all | 2,260 / 48, +0.42, 2.67, 6.46 | 24,050 / 387, +0.96, 3.10, 6.28 | 52,154 / 853, +0.84, 3.19, 6.34 |

* **Hop 0** measures Warren-versus-lidar with almost no level-assignment error; **hops 1-6 add the level-walk error** (the leave-one-out above: about one contour interval, 1.2 m, typical). The all-class RMS grows only from 2.7 to 3.2 m, so the level walk is a small term next to the frame and survey terms.
* **Reading in terrain classes.** Flat and sloping ground: RMS about 2.5 to 3.3 m (8 to 11 ft), p95 about 6 to 7 m (20 to 23 ft). Hilltops: Warren is **1.4 to 1.6 m lower** than the modern DEM, RMS 2.5 to 3.3 m; the expected bias for a ring below a summit is only -0.6 m, so about 1 m of the bias is a real difference (hilltop quarrying and road and monument work are modern lowerings in the other direction, so the cause is not settled; it may be the 1868 survey or the frame). **Valley floors and streams: Warren is 3.0 to 3.5 m higher than the lidar and the RMS is 4.4 to 5.4 m**: the lidar surface lies in the stream channels and incised bottoms that 4-ft contours smooth over (or channel incision since 1868, which is the PDF's "creek beds worse" caveat). Samples there are fewer (about 50 to 130 independent contours).
* **Level-free check (no label involved):** the along-contour spread of the DEM about each contour's own mean: flat 1.06 m rms (p95 2.0), slope 2.50 (p95 3.1), stream 1.45 (3.3), valley floor 7.3 (dominated by contours crossing valley cross-sections), hilltop 6.2 (rings on steep sides). Values greater than the mapped contour interval mean the frame places the line 16 to 30 m from where its elevation is constant.
* **Comparison with the PDF.** The PDF's Knowles figure ("hilltops and ridgelines within about 50 ft", both an elevation and a displacement) is not confirmed or refuted: our relative vertical RMS (8 to 11 ft on slope and flat ground, 8 to 11 ft on hilltops) is well inside 50 ft, and the 95th percentile (about 22 ft) is too. But the datum was calibrated against the DEM (K), and the horizontal residual (16 to 30 m) is the same order as 50 ft (15 m). The honest bound for the Battle Theatre: **relative height of the trusted Warren surface 3.2 m RMS (about 10 ft), p95 6.3 m (21 ft), on ground the lidar still represents; valley floors and streams 4.4 to 5.4 m RMS (14 to 18 ft) and biased about +3 m (Warren higher); hilltops biased about -1.5 m.** Absolute elevation is not independently checked.
* **Caveats.** (1) Warren is 1868-69, 5 years after the battle; the lidar is 2017, 154 years later. Differences include real change since 1868 (quarries, roads, avenues, grading, channel incision, monument bases) and survey error, which cannot be separated except in the polygons of section 5. (2) Residuals include horizontal frame error times slope. (3) The orientation of the level walk uses the modern surface. (4) Only 14 percent of the sheet has trusted levels; samples sit where anchors, labels and clean contours are, so the figures describe those places (open slopes with printed margin labels) rather than woods and the town. (5) 12 hilltop rings and 48 anchors are small samples. (6) The frame (section 2) is the largest uncontrolled term.

Residual map: `residual_map.png` (read visually: coherent patches of 5 to 8 m at the NW ridges and the west edge, stripe patterns on steep west-facing slopes at the top centre where one contour step of level error or a 20 m shift shows as 4 to 8 m, a quarry-adjacent patch near Round Top; no sign of gross whole-sheet error such as mirrored or rotated contours). The prototype box has only 10 percent coverage, so most of the field has no Warren-derived value at all.

## 5. Task 4: de-modernization (`demodernization_polygons.json`, `unexplained_residuals.json`, `placebo_test.json`, `dem_1863_candidate.tif`, `dem_1863_provenance.tif`, `disturbance_unedited.tif`, `provenance_map.png`)

**Candidate polygons (371), all from layers already in the repository, none new**: 34 NPS memorial-avenue edges (15 m buffer; reason: avenue built 1882-1920, source the NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory pages quoted in `docs/basemap1863/SOURCES-roads.md` section 4), 102 segments of the US 15 bypass (opened 28 June 1963; 45 m buffer; the only source is the AARoads wiki, a **lead**, so tier inferred), 24 modern quarry/water polygons (`terrain_changes.json`), 94 road cut/fill candidates on roads the roads layer classes as post-1863, 117 parking lots (class rule), 0 visitor-centre pads found. 141 other road cut/fill candidates lie on 1863-era roads and are **not attributed to post-1863 work** (the project's own caveat: many 1863 roads run in cuts). Evidence tier of every polygon: **inferred** at most: avenue dates are documented (NPS, type B), but that the ground surface changed by the amount the lidar shows is reasoning, and no source states a profile. Not covered: the Round Top branch railroad (1884-1942), the Electric Railway (1893-1916) and the Little Round Top road work have no geometry/profile in the layers; the **unfinished railroad grade stays** (not touched); no earthwork was pre-placed or removed.

**Edit rule (local contrast, because regional bias is common to a polygon and its surroundings):** contrast = median(Warren - modern inside) - median(same in the ring 50 to 150 m outside, trusted cells only); an edit needs at least 300 valid cells inside and in the ring, |contrast| at least **5.96 m** and at least 65 percent of inside cells on the same side. The threshold is the **95th percentile of a placebo test** (`placebo_test.json`: the same rule on 158 polygons shifted 250 to 450 m off the documented disturbance). **That test is the key finding: at a 2.5 m threshold 33.5 percent of ordinary ground passes, and the real candidates' contrast distribution is almost the same as the placebo's** (median |contrast| 1.85 m versus 1.43 m; 90th percentile 5.2 versus 4.9): **Warren at its measured accuracy cannot confirm the cuts, fills and pads of the documented disturbances at their typical depths (1 to 4 m).** At the 5 percent level, of 57 candidates with coverage **4 edited** (US15 segments DM-0051 and DM-0052, 600 cells each at the edge of the trusted area; road cut candidate DM-0408 on w488502359; parking lot DM-0526) and about 3 would be expected by chance: **the 4 edits are statistically no better than false positives. They total 4,291 cells (0.43 ha); each is marked provenance 1 and a consumer who wants only evidence-supported ground should treat class 1 as lidar.** Edit method: low-pass (sigma 15 m, masked) difference Warren TIN minus lidar minus the ring median, cosine feather 8 m inside the polygon edge; lidar fine detail kept.

Result by polygon: **4 edited; 314 not edited because they lie outside the trusted Warren surface (fewer than 300 valid cells); 53 not edited because Warren cannot resolve them (contrast below threshold, sign not consistent, or no trusted ring).** Each record has id, geometry (WKT, UTM 18N), reason, source, tier, residual statistics, and the decision. `disturbance_unedited.tif` flags all 367 unedited polygons' cells (known post-1863 disturbance, **still modern ground**; do not present it as 1863).

**Unexplained residuals (12 regions, 35.8 ha, `unexplained_residuals.json`, new runs write a numbered file instead of overwriting):** contiguous regions of at least 1 ha where the 25 m-smoothed |Warren - modern| is at least 6 m (about the p95 of the measured error) and no candidate polygon lies within 15 m. All 12 are near-uniform offsets of 6.4 to 10.8 m (or -7.5 and -8.3 m for two, in the south-east) over 1 to 10 ha. They are **not** explained by a plausible horizontal shift of the sheet and are **not** edited. Hypotheses stated, none established: a contour-level assignment error of about 5 to 7 contour intervals in that stretch (the offsets are 5 to 6.8 intervals; a missed or mis-oriented ladder step), or a real change. They need a second reader of the contours (or the 1:2,400 sheets) before any cause is assigned.

**Candidate surface:** `dem_1863_candidate.tif` = modern DEM (EPSG:32618, 1 m, 11,250 x 10,750) edited only inside the 4 polygons above; stored as uint16 in 5 cm steps (metres = DN x 0.05; 0 = nodata; the 5 cm rounding is the only change elsewhere). **Provenance raster** `dem_1863_provenance.tif`: 0 = unmodified lidar inside the trusted Warren surface (9,945,781 cells, 9.9 km2), 1 = blended toward Warren (4,291), **2 = outside the trusted Warren surface (110,987,428; this includes the rest of the Warren sheet, which has contours but no assigned level, and everything outside the sheet)**, plus the extra raster `disturbance_unedited.tif` (1 = known disturbance polygon, unedited). Visual check: `provenance_map.png` and `residual_map.png` were rendered and read; the orange known-disturbance areas sit where the avenues, the bypass and the quarry are, the blue trusted patches lie on open ground at the sheet's west and east flanks, red edits are pin-points. Over 99 percent of the candidate surface is the modern lidar.

## 6. Tier of Warren-derived elevation under the PDF definitions

PDF tiers: documented = a specific source states it; inferred = reasoned from documented facts; plausible-procedural = a generated fill for a gap. The printed labels are documented statements of Warren's survey (A1/A2) **for 1868-69**, not for July 1863. Everything built here is therefore **inferred**: (1) the tracing and level walk are my reasoning from the sheet, (2) the datum constant K is calibrated against the lidar, (3) the 1868-69 surface is taken as the 1863 surface with unmeasured change in between, (4) Warren is a single root; Gate Q19 wants two independent roots for any visible-result datum, so every Warren-derived height must carry a flag. The few labelled contours themselves (48 anchors, hop 0, 0.1 percent of the area) are the closest to documented for 1868-69. The Warren surface should **not** be tiered "documented" for 1863 under the Law item 4 (non-lead source supporting exactly the claim). Untrusted area (86 percent of the sheet and all of the field outside it) remains modern lidar presented only as "modern ground": **plausible-procedural at best if used as a surface**, and the product must show it as such (PDF B.9.2 step 8).

## 7. Gates and inputs (Q16, Q03, Q08, Q02, Q19, Q23)

* **Q16 (photo-match at documented camera positions): inputs prepared, gate not met.** Check 5 (`docs/basemap1863/verification/check5-rephotography.md`) draped the modern DEM; a surface for it now exists only on 14 percent of the sheet and the relative accuracies are in section 4. Camera positions are still estimates (tiers B to D); this work adds no camera position. `dem_1863_candidate.tif` plus `dem_1863_provenance.tif` let a Q16 renderer drape the 4 edits (negligible) and mark the rest; no photograph has been matched against the Warren TIN. The pass criteria and the 24 to 30 m horizontal uncertainty of the frame mean a ridge-line match of better than 25 m cannot be expected.
* **Q03 (no anachronisms):** the avenue, bypass, parking, quarry polygons are the terrain-side inventory; none is edited with evidence, so modern ground (avenues, bypass embankments) remains in the surface in `disturbance_unedited.tif` areas. Q03 is **not met for terrain**.
* **Q08 (no pre-placed earthworks):** no earthwork was placed or removed; the unfinished railroad grade is untouched.
* **Q02 / Q23:** every product states its tier (inferred), source and error; the render flag for non-documented ground is class 2 and `disturbance_unedited`.
* **Q19:** one root only (Warren).

## 9. Unfinished, and what would change the result

1. **Coverage** is 14 percent of the sheet with a level. More printed labels (the 1:2,400 NARA and LOC 99446453 sheets, not retrieved), a second reading of the contour labels by another reader, and a better association of margin labels to contours in woods would raise it.
2. **Label reading** is one reviewer at 3x zoom; digits can be wrong (Culp's Hill 508 or 408 stays open); 43 of 91 labels were rejected for association, not for reading.
3. **Extraction quality** is 70 percent clean fragments overall; woods, hatching and the borough need a second pass (a tree-glyph mask, a hachure filter).
4. **The edit rule has a 33 percent false-positive rate at a 2.5 m threshold and Warren cannot confirm documented disturbances at 1 to 4 m depths**: any de-modernization of avenues, roads and parking needs another source (HABS/NPS drawings, 1930s aerials, CLR profiles), not Warren alone.
5. **12 unexplained regions** (35.8 ha) are logged and open.
6. **Datum**: K is not an independent measurement; the true Warren datum offset needs the sheet's own datum statement or a benchmark.
7. **Frame**: the FFD-versus-affine conflict (61 m median between them) is not resolved; a per-region choice or a joint fit to controls AND relief might do better. Not tried: using the lidar summits as controls inside the frame fit (circular for an error model).
8. **Not done**: the 2006-08 PAMAP epoch and two-epoch difference; the 1:2,400 sheets; stream channels and drainage from Warren (PDF step 4); Knowles viewshed and Codori-Trostle thicket tests (step 7); modern railroad profile, quarry floor, monument bases and the Little Round Top road (no geometry/profile in the layers); areas outside the Warren sheet (East Cavalry Field: no Warren data at all, the surface there is modern lidar only).
