## 7. Defects found

1. The text layer of the Official Records scan misreads the figure 2 as 3 (and 12 as 13, 22 as 23) in places, and some hours and figures are damaged. Those that changed a record are listed in section 3 (p. 882, p. 884, p. 886, p. 897, p. 261, p. 155, p. 880, p. 889); p. 151, the abstract of returns, is scrambled (labels separated from the columns, "l,78r" for 1,787, "67" cells) and was read on the page image. Damage that stays: Weir's "until 1.3( o'clock" on p. 880 is cut by the right edge of the page itself (the last glyph is half off the scan), so no clock is entered for it; Tyler's "li miles" is 1½ miles (no record uses it); Meade's "extreme l^f t" is "extreme left" in the second reading (no change). Two entries of the second version's list are resolved: Milton's "10 ^. m." is 10 a. m. (0196, 0197) and Newton's heading "July 3" is July 2 on the page (0300); Dow's "7.80" is 7.30 (0237).
2. The first version's request about Company C (assigned to two groups) was answered by the plan; the note stays as the record of it. The footnotes of p. 168 of the organization table (stars of the two Connecticut heavy batteries, dagger of Battery K) are lost in the text layer of both copies read.
3. Tyler's "about eight batteries" of 1 July does not match the batteries of the two brigades he names; the roster draws Batteries F and K, 3d U. S., C and F Pa., and F and G, 1st Pa., as six units while the table prints three consolidated batteries (the records on both ids say so).
4. State-volume index: section `PA2-S036` (heading BATTERY "H"*) holds the inscriptions of Ricketts' and Thompson's batteries; `NY3-S024` (heading Battery G) holds the inscription of Battery K; `ME-S021` holds the roster of the Sixth Maine under another heading. The index is not changed.
5. Hunt (p. 232) puts Osborn's batteries "in the cemetery grounds, to the north of the Baltimore road" and (p. 233) "on the south of the road": the records use "Cemetery Hill" and carry neither side.
6. The Itinerary (R007) is a compilation of the 1880s by Kirkley, so it is a `compiler` source (tier inferred); Thompson's report names Battery K, 4th U. S. on his right where Hunt, McGilvery and Phillips name Thomas (carried as a conflict of neighbours).
7. Hunt's account of 1887 (Battles and Leaders) is a staff officer's later writing; it is entered with that date in each note and its tier follows the claim (documented where it names its own act or the order of an hour, inferred where the place is a reading).

## 8. Checks of the readings

The quote of every record is found by code in the text layer; the checks below look at what code cannot see, a digit that the text layer prints wrongly and a statement that says more than its passage. The scripts and the second readings are scratch files in `tmp/units-us-hq-artres/audit/` and `ocr/` (untracked, not shipped).

1. Second reading of the scan pages. All 94 Official Records pages on which a passage rests (88 in part 1, 6 in part 3) were read a second time from the scan with tesseract (300 dpi, gray, one thread), one page after the other (`audit/ocr_one.py`), in commands of at most six pages; the commands whose time was taken used 18 to 25 CPU seconds each, at low priority, about six CPU minutes in all. They were not run through `tools/horde/bench.sh`: a queued `bench.sh` job for the same work had waited about an hour for a slot with some 30 other jobs queued, and was withdrawn; an earlier direct attempt with tesseract's default threads (outside the lock, and the reason for the one-thread commands) had spent 28 minutes of wall time on one page and was stopped. `audit/digitdiff.py` aligns the words of every passage with the second reading of its page and lists each place where either side carries a digit and the two differ: 109 differences over the 94 pages (13 on p. 151, 22 on the organization-table pages 145, 155, 156, 167 and 168, 74 elsewhere). Each was looked at, and the ones that could change a record were read on the page image; they are the corrections of section 3. The others are noise of the second reading (1 read as "i", U. S. read as 8, 1½ read as 14, 4½-inch read as 44), words glued or lost, the edge of a passage (a sentence that begins just past its end), the table of ammunition issued on p. 879 (no record uses its figures) and the table pages, where the order of the columns differs between the two readings.
2. `audit/printedcheck.py` looked for each printed clock, distance and strength string of the records (159 strings of the Official Records records) in the second reading of its pages: 146 were found; the 13 not found were each looked at and are explained: "about 6.30 o'clock" twice (the second reading garbles "o'clock"), "1st" read as "ist", "Toward 5 o'clock" twice (the first letter is lost), the 11 o'clock of Ames (read on the page image: 11 o'clock on the morning of the 2d), the two records of Milton's 10 a. m. (the scan reading that the text layer lacks), "3a instant" twice (the text layer's own misprint of 3d), the dateline of p. 1086, the total 1,131 of p. 151 (read on the page image, the columns add up) and the 8.35 p. m. of Meade's dispatch (the second reading prints 8.85; the page image prints 8.35).
3. Statements against passages. `audit/supportcheck.py` lists, for each statement, the capitalized words that do not stand in its passage even by a near match (OCR damage allowed); `audit/numcheck.py` does the same for numbers. The numbers: 134 of 353 records have one that is not in the passage as digits, and each was read: the years 1863 and 1887, days of July that the passage prints as "2d", numbers printed in words ("thirty-four", "thirty-nine", "one hundred and forty"), "4.5" for the damaged "4i", and the scan readings of section 3. The names: 132 records on the first run, 38 after the unit-naming words were set aside; the repairs are in section 3 (22 earlier records). Twenty records still name a person or unit that their passage does not and were left: the brigade commanders in the records of their brigades (0003, 0004, 0006, 0007, 0026, 0027), Thompson in the strength records of the consolidated Batteries C and F (0022, 0023, 0276, 0277, 0308, 0309), McGilvery as the addressee of Phillips' and Milton's reports (0184, 0188, 0194), Osborn where the text layer prints "osbom" (0135, 0166), the Chief Signal Officer's title (0251), Gettysburg in the sentence that follows the headquarters' arrival (0301) and the row label of the abstract (0321).

What agrees: Phillips' footnote (5 officers and 100 men) and the Pennsylvania inscription (105 officers and men) for Thompson; Toombs' two rows of the 4th New Jersey add to the commission's total of 24 officers and 362 men.
