# C-003. When the Fifth Corps reached Gettysburg, morning of 2 July 1863

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window from about 07:00 to about 08:00, drawn at 07:00. Class: weighted (the evidence favours one reading but does not settle it). Tier: inferred (the lower bound is stated; the upper bound reads Sykes's "latter date" as 2 July).

## The dispute

General Sykes's Fifth Corps marched all night of 1-2 July from Hanover and Bonaughtown. The plan lists 05:00 and "08:00 (head of column)", recommends "head at 08:00 near Powers Hill", and does not print its sources. The first delegated pass found the Official Records at odds (Slocum 5, the corps about 7, Sykes 8) and set a window from 07:00 to 08:00.

## What the witnesses say

**Seth Williams, assistant adjutant general at army headquarters, dispatch timed 6.15 a.m. on 2 July:** "The Second Corps is up and the Fifth is momentarily expected." The sender's own clock; written on the spot.

**General Slocum, report of 23 August 1863:** "The Fifth Corps arrived at 5 a. m. on July 2" and was placed on the right of the Twelfth Corps; at about 8 the Fifth and part of the Twelfth moved left across Rock Creek. He stood on that right flank. General A. S. Williams (Twelfth Corps) says the Fifth "arrived, and took position on our right" before orders at 8 a.m., with no hour.

**Colonel Chamberlain, 20th Maine, report of 6 July:** after sleeping by the roadside just before daybreak, the regiment "reached the heights southeasterly of Gettysburg at about 7 a. m., July 2". **General Barnes, First Division, 24 August:** the division halted after midnight about 2 miles out and "reached Gettysburg at about 7 o'clock in the morning" (the day digit is garbled in the scan and is not used). **General Meade, 1 October:** "By 7 a. m. the Second and Fifth Corps ... had reached the ground." Colonel Rice's itinerary (44th New York, same brigade) has the regiment 3 miles out at 1 a.m., starting at 4, going about 2 miles and "getting into position": no hour, but an early arrival fits.

**General Sykes, report of 31 July:** he reached "the field of Gettysburg ... about 8 a. m. on the latter date" (2 July); his troops took position on the right and were later massed near the Rock Creek bridge on the Baltimore pike. A battery commander (Gibbs) had his guns in position about 8. Books of 1900 and 1913 repeat Sykes's 8 without a source. General Crawford's Third Division was near McSherrystown at daylight and was at the others' position "by noon". Bachelder (1873) has the corps halted near Powers' Hill, Slocum's headquarters.

## The weighing

The 6.15 dispatch is the best class of writing and says the corps was not yet up at headquarters. Against it stand Slocum's single 5 a.m., written 51 days later, and the corps' own reports of about 7 and 8. The reports may be describing different moments of one march (first division, corps commander, tail), but the sources do not say so; the window shows the spread rather than choosing one. Slocum stood on the right where the corps first went in and headquarters sat on Cemetery Hill; the records do not say whether headquarters would have known of troops on the far right at 5.

## The decision

Default: **07:00 to 08:00, drawn at 07:00**. The plan's 05:00 and 08:00 and the 6.15 and noon readings stay listed. The plan's default holds in part (08:00 is the upper end, but nothing makes it the head of the column or places it at Powers Hill). The first delegated default holds.

## What would change it

A diary, log or report of the corps' first troops with an hour for the first arrival on the right; a page image of Barnes's report; the sources behind the plan's two hours.

## Searches that failed

The Epperson page and Freeman's book were not read. No other hour was found in the other Fifth Corps and Twelfth Corps reports, in the Joint Committee testimony by pattern, or in the Gettysburg weeklies (not read page by page).
