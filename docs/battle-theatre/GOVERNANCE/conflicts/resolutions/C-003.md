# C-003. When the Fifth Corps reached Gettysburg, morning of 2 July 1863

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window from about 07:00 to about 08:00, drawn at 07:00. Class: weighted (the evidence favours one reading but does not settle it). Tier: inferred (the lower bound is stated; the upper bound reads Sykes's "latter date" as 2 July).

## The dispute

General Sykes's Fifth Corps marched all night of 1-2 July from Hanover and Bonaughtown. The plan lists 05:00 and "08:00 (head of column)", recommends "head at 08:00 near Powers Hill", and does not print its sources. The first delegated pass found the Official Records at odds (Slocum 5, the corps about 7, Sykes 8) and set a window from 07:00 to 08:00.

## What the witnesses say

**Seth Williams, assistant adjutant general at army headquarters, dispatch timed 6.15 a.m. on 2 July:** "The Second Corps is up and the Fifth is momentarily expected." The sender's own clock; written on the spot.

**General Slocum, report of 23 August 1863:** "The Fifth Corps arrived at 5 a. m. on July 2" and was placed on the right of the Twelfth Corps; at about 8 the Fifth and part of the Twelfth moved left across Rock Creek. He stood on that right flank. General A. S. Williams (Twelfth Corps) says the Fifth "arrived, and took position on our right" before orders at 8 a.m., with no hour.

**Colonel Chamberlain, 20th Maine, report of 6 July:** after sleeping by the roadside just before daybreak, the regiment "reached the heights southeasterly of Gettysburg at about 7 a. m., July 2". **General Barnes, First Division, 24 August:** the division halted after midnight about 2 miles out and "reached Gettysburg at about 7 o'clock in the morning" (the day digit is garbled in the scan and is not used). **General Meade, 1 October:** "By 7 a. m. the Second and Fifth Corps ... had reached the ground" (an upper bound for the whole corps: it is as true of 5 a.m. as of 7, so it is not used for the lower end of the window; Everett's oration of November 1863 repeats it). Colonel Rice's itinerary (44th New York, same brigade) has the regiment 3 miles out at 1 a.m., starting at 4, going about 2 miles and "getting into position": no hour, but an early arrival fits.

**General Sykes, report of 31 July:** he reached "the field of Gettysburg ... about 8 a. m. on the latter date" (2 July); his troops took position on the right and were later massed near the Rock Creek bridge on the Baltimore pike. A battery commander (Gibbs) had his guns in position about 8. Books of 1900 and 1913 repeat Sykes's 8 without a source. General Crawford's Third Division was near McSherrystown at daylight and was at the others' position "by noon" (at noon at the latest). Bachelder (1873) has the corps halted near Powers' Hill, Slocum's headquarters.

**Other hours in the library.** A Sanitary Commission journal of Thursday 2 July, printed in the Commission's report of 1863 (Major Bush): "The 5th corps arrived two miles from town, on the Baltimore Pike about ten A. M.", a visitor on the road, who does not say which part of the corps he saw. Powell, history of the Fifth Corps (1896): "reached Gettysburg about 5 A.M. July 2d", the same hour as Slocum and no source named. The New York volume of 1902, repeated by Norton in 1913: the corps "reached the Gettysburg field about 6 a. m." and stood near the Rock Creek bridge, a later memory with no source. The county history of 1886: the Second and Fifth Corps and the rest of the Third "reached the grounds a little after sunrise", with no clock reading.

## The weighing

The 6.15 dispatch is the best class of writing and says the corps had not yet reported at army headquarters; it does not show that no troops stood on the far right. Against it stand Slocum's single 5 a.m. (with Powell, who gives the same hour and no source), written 51 days later, and the corps' own reports of about 7 and 8. The reports may be describing different moments of one march (first division, corps commander, tail), but the sources do not say so; the window shows the spread rather than choosing one. Slocum stood on the right where the corps first went in and headquarters sat on Cemetery Hill; the records do not say whether headquarters would have known of troops on the far right at 5. The journal's 10 a.m., the New York 6 a.m. and the county history's "after sunrise" are a visitor's glimpse, a later memory and a loose phrase; none overturns the corps' own reports.

## The decision

Default: **07:00 to 08:00, drawn at 07:00**. The picture is drawn at 07:00 as the best-supported hour, not as the earliest documented one: Slocum's 5 a.m. is earlier and stays selectable. The plan's 05:00 and 08:00, the 6.15 dispatch, the Third Division's "by noon" and the other hours above stay listed. The plan's default holds in part (08:00 is the upper end, but nothing makes it the head of the column or places it at Powers Hill). The first delegated default holds.

## What would change it

A diary, log or report of the corps' first troops with an hour for the first arrival on the right; a page image of Barnes's report; the sources behind the plan's two hours.

## Searches that failed

The Epperson page and Freeman's book were not read. No other hour was found in the other Fifth Corps and Twelfth Corps reports of the Official Records (the rest of the library was searched by a regular expression, which found the hours listed above), in the Joint Committee testimony by pattern, or in the Gettysburg weeklies (not read page by page).
