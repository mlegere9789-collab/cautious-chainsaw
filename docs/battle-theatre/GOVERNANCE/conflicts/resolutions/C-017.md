# C-017: Who took over the Third Corps when Sickles fell?

## The dispute

Major General Daniel Sickles was wounded on the afternoon of 2 July (Birney puts it at 6 o'clock). The plan lists two answers, Birney and Hancock, and recommends "Birney (corps), Hancock overseeing the sector". The first delegated default kept that. Two questions are mixed in the one row: who commanded the Third Corps, and who was placed in command over it.

## What each source says

**Birney** (First Division, Third Corps), report of 7 August 1863: "At 6 o'clock I found Major-General Sickles seriously wounded, and, at his request, took command of the troops." He then ordered Humphreys' division to change front. About 7.30 Hancock reached him with a brigade and "at his request" he assigned it a position.

**Humphreys** (Second Division), sworn 21 March 1864: "I received an order from General Birney informing me that General Sickles was wounded, and that he had succeeded to the command of the corps." **Sickles**, sworn 26 February 1864: "The command of the 3d corps then devolved on Major General Birney", who "continued in command of the corps". **Meade's report** (1 October 1863): "the stubborn resistance of the Third Corps, under Major-General Birney".

**Hancock** (Second Corps), report written between July and 24 October 1863: Meade, told that Sickles had been wounded, "directed me to assume command of the Third Corps in addition to that of my own. By this arrangement, the immediate command of the Second Corps devolved again upon General Gibbon, and that of the Third upon General Birney." Sworn 22 March 1864 he says the same, and that Birney was "then in command of General Sickles's corps". **Gibbon** (7 August 1863): he took the Second Corps "when General Hancock was ordered to take command of the Third Corps". The note to the order of battle in the Official Records says Hancock "was put in command of the Third Corps, in addition to that of his own."

**French** (a later commander): his report of 1 October 1863 says he joined the Third Corps "by virtue of orders received from headquarters Army of the Potomac, dated July 7"; Birney signs as commanding the Third Corps on 7 July and French on 12 July.

## Weighing

The sources do not conflict. Birney held the corps from about 6 p.m. (Birney, Humphreys, Sickles, Meade, and Hancock himself); Meade put Hancock over it with the Second Corps (Hancock, Gibbon, the order of battle). Hancock's own sentence gives both at once. "Overseeing the sector" is the plan's phrase; the sources say "command of the Third Corps in addition to that of my own". French is a later successor, not a rival answer. Clock: not applicable.

## Decision

**Default: "Birney (corps), Hancock overseeing the sector"**: Birney in immediate command of the Third Corps from about 6 p.m. on 2 July; Hancock placed over it by Meade. Class settled, tier documented. The PDF default and the first delegated default both hold. Four values are listed (Birney, Hancock, the plan's combined wording, and French, added).

## What would change it

Meade's written order of 2 July placing Hancock over the Third Corps, with its hour, or a report that Birney was relieved of the immediate command that evening. Neither was found.

## Searches that failed

No text of Meade's order and no hour for it in the dispatches of 2 and 3 July; no statement in the Third Corps reports that Hancock, not Birney, gave the corps its orders that evening; later narratives repeat the two-level account without an hour.
