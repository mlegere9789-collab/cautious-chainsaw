# C-025 Geary back to Culp's Hill

**The dispute.** On the evening of 2 July the Twelfth Corps sent most of its force to help the left. Geary's division (Kane's and Candy's brigades; Greene's brigade stayed) took the wrong road and was away when Confederates entered the empty works on Culp's Hill. The plan lists three times for Geary's return: overnight, 23:00 and 04:00, and recommends a window of 02:00 to 04:00.

**Who says what.**
- *Geary* (division commander, report of 29 July 1863): "At 9 p. m. I ordered Kane's brigade to return to its original position and Candy's to follow it"; Kane's brigade was fired on within 200 yards of the breastworks; "At 1 a. m. on the 3d, the First Brigade ... was placed in position on the right of Kane's brigade" (Official Records 27/1 p. 827).
- *Kane* (6 July): "At twilight we were removed to the batteries on the turnpike, and shortly after ordered to return"; fired on in the woods "on Thursday night" (p. 847).
- *Greene* (12 July), in the works: "About 10 o'clock I was informed that General Kane, with his brigade, was returning to his position"; "The First Brigade returned to our support at 1.30 a. m. on the 3d" (p. 857).
- *Candy* (6 July): the brigade stayed east of Rock Creek until "about 12 p. m.", then was ordered up (p. 836). *Slocum* (corps commander, August): the return was "nearly midnight before this movement was fully completed" (p. 761). *Williams* (22 August) says he did not learn the works were held until "nearly 12 o'clock at night"; the first scan of the volume is damaged at the hour and only the second scan reads it, so he is not cited.
- *Meade*: Geary "having returned during the night, attacked at early dawn"; Slocum has the movement complete "nearly midnight" and orders issued for an attack at daybreak. The Park Service handbook says "under cover of darkness". The county history (1886) puts Geary there "At 4 o'clock on the morning of the 3d"; Greene says the artillery opened at 4 a. m.

**Weighing.** Kane says he was ordered back shortly after twilight, earlier than Geary's 9 p. m., and Geary's own report puts the end of the enemy's attack about 10 p. m. just before that order; the default's lower end is Geary's printed hour, not the earliest time implied. Geary has Candy's brigade held on the turnpike from 9, Candy has it east of Rock Creek until about midnight: they disagree on when and where it was ordered, and the span covers both. The writers in the line give 9 p. m., about 10, about midnight, 1 a. m. and 1.30 a. m.: different acts (the order, Kane's arrival, Candy ordered up, placed in line, back at Greene's support) and different brigades, not rival values. Slocum's "nearly midnight" is the corps view of the whole movement. No report gives 02:00 to 04:00 for the return; 04:00 is the artillery opening for the dawn attack. No writer gives 23:00. The reports do not carry a month, so the day is inferred from the context.

**Decision.** Default: the night of 2 to 3 July from the order at 21:00 to the First Brigade in line at 01:00 to 01:30 (class weighted, tier inferred); Kane's brigade is reported returning about 22:00 and arrives soon after. The plan's default does not hold (it lies after every report); the first delegated default holds in substance, with its "evening" lower end read as the 21:00 order. All values stay selectable.

**Searches that failed.** The pages behind the plan's 23:00 and 04:00 (Epperson, Civil War News) could not be read. No report gives a return between 2 and 4 a. m. or at 11 p. m.

**What would change it.** A regimental report or diary of 2 or 3 July with the hour Kane's or Candy's brigade reached the works.
