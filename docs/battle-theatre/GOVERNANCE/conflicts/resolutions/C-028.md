# C-028 Hunt's guns open Jul 3

**The dispute.** The plan lists 04:30 and 05:30 for the Union artillery opening on Culp's Hill on 3 July, recommends 05:30, and offers 04:30 as the alternative. Both hours come from Hunt's report, and they are two different events of the same guns, not two answers to one question.

**What Hunt and Muhlenberg say.** Hunt (chief of artillery, report of 27 September 1863, Official Records 27/1 pp. 237-238): at 1 a. m. on the 3d Muhlenberg's and Kinzie's batteries were posted opposite the centre of the Twelfth Corps; "At 4.30 a. m. these batteries opened, and fired without intermission for fifteen minutes into the wood"; soon after daylight Rigby's battery was added and "at 5.30 a. m. all the batteries opened" and fired at intervals until 10. Lieutenant Muhlenberg, who commanded the corps's artillery brigade (August 1863, p. 870), has the same: at 4.30 a. m. twenty guns opened and fired fifteen minutes, then ceased to let the infantry take part, and "Commenced at 5.30 a. m." again until 10. Hunt gives the same hour; his two batteries are part of Muhlenberg's twenty guns, so they count as one witness.

**Others.** Packer (Fifth Connecticut, 21 July 1863): "4 a. m. July 3, the battle opened upon our side by twenty pieces of artillery". Greene (12 July): "At 4 a. m. our artillery ... opened". Slocum (August): the guns "opened the battle at 4 a. m." Geary (29 July) has a simultaneous artillery and infantry attack at 3.30 a. m. (early dawn), his artillery fire lasting ten minutes; Candy (6 July) has a battery open on the enemy at 3.45 a. m. They describe the same bombardment. Steuart, in the works the guns hit, says "about daylight"; Williams says the artillery opened at daylight for fifteen minutes. Sunrise was 04:36.

**Weighing.** The guns' commander and the chief of artillery say 4.30; Packer and Greene on the line and Slocum's corps summary say 4, and Geary and Candy say 3.30 and 3.45. They write alike (weeks after, no diary), and an hour in the dark is an estimate; the 20-minute difference between the armies' clocks, which is not applied, is not the cause as all are Union. Nothing separates them, so the first fire is a window of 03:30 to 04:30 (class irreducible, tier inferred: the day is not in Hunt's or Muhlenberg's sentences). 05:30 is the general fire, carried by Hunt and Muhlenberg alone.

**Decision.** Shown: first fire 03:30 to 04:30 (drawn at 04:30, the guns' own commander's hour, the end of the window); all batteries at 05:30. The plan's default 05:30 holds only as the second event; the first delegated default holds as two events but its first hour is 04:30 alone. Every reading stays selectable.

**Searches that failed.** No battery report gives a different first hour; the Confederate reports give none; the web pages the earlier notes cite were not read.

**What would change it.** An hour for the first round from a gun detachment or Best's staff, or the Confederate defenders' report of when the Union guns began.
