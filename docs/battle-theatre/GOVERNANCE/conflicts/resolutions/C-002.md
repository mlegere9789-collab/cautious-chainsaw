# C-002. When Humphreys' division reached the field, night of 1-2 July 1863

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window from about 00:00 to 02:00 on 2 July. Class: irreducible (the record itself is divided). Tier: inferred.

## The dispute

Brigadier General Humphreys commanded the Second Division of Sickles's Third Corps. It marched from Emmitsburg on the afternoon of 1 July, was led toward the Black Horse Tavern, found Confederate troops near it, turned back, and reached the Union lines in the night. The plan lists 00:00 and 02:00 and recommends 02:00. The first delegated pass kept 01:00 from Humphreys' report. The plan's row prints no sources; the timeline rows behind it name Epperson (midnight) and Stone Sentinels (02:00), two web pages that could not be read here. Reports of 1863 from inside the division give all three hours.

## What the witnesses say

**Humphreys, report of 16 August 1863:** after retracing his steps he marched on, "bivouacking at 1 a. m. on July 2 about 1 mile from Gettysburg and eastward of the Emmitsburg road". He had two of his three brigades and one battery; the Third Brigade and Smith's battery had been left on the Waynesboro pike. He commanded the division and gave the orders. This is the hour of settling into bivouac, the end of the march. A round hour; Union writer; no clock named.

**Humphreys, private letter to Fitz Lee, printed in 1878:** "My division (Second division Third corps) reached the ground towards midnight of July 1st". Written some fifteen years after, a recollection. He says "reached the ground", not "bivouacked": it may be the head of the column, but he does not say so.

**Bachelder, 1873 and 1878.** In his guide of 1873 Humphreys' division "arrived at midnight". In a letter printed in 1878 it "reached the field at 1 o'clock the next morning". He was a historian, not a witness, and names no source either time. The New York monuments volume (1900: "arrived at Gettysburg in the night at 1 a. m.") and the 1913 Meade volume ("did not reach the ridge until one o'clock in the morning") agree with the report and also name no source.

**Burling, Third Brigade, 20 August 1863, and Humphreys:** the brigade left at Emmitsburg could not march until nearly 4 a.m. and "joined the corps and division at 9 a. m., July 2". That is a different event from the main body's arrival.

**Brewster, Second (Excelsior) Brigade, report of 15 August 1863:** "Marched to a point near Gettysburg, passing through Emmitsburg, reaching there about midnight." He commanded the brigade and writes of it. "There" is not defined; it is read as the point near Gettysburg (Burling has the Third Brigade reaching Emmitsburg at noon), and that is a reading, not a statement.

**Tripp, 11th Massachusetts, report of 27 July 1863, and Bodine, 26th Pennsylvania, report of 28 July 1863** (both regiments of Carr's First Brigade): Tripp, "marched through Emmitsburg to Gettysburg, Pa., where we arrived at 2 a. m. on the 2d instant"; Bodine, "Marched, via Emmitsburg, to Gettysburg ... where we arrived at 2 a. m. of July 2". Each reports his own regiment. These are the plan's 02:00, found in the reports.

## The weighing

Four officers of one division wrote within 45 days of the night: Brewster (about midnight), Humphreys (bivouacking at 1 a.m.), Tripp and Bodine (2 a.m.). All are the best class of writing, all were with their units, all are Union. Humphreys' own later letter says "towards midnight". The three hours probably mark different moments or different units of one march (a brigade reaching a point, the division settling into bivouac, two regiments of another brigade arriving), but no source says which regiment led or what each hour marks, so none can be preferred on the rubric. The later books that say 1 a.m. name no source and are counted with Humphreys' report. The record is divided, and the honest result is a window. The Union and Confederate clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes; this is not applied and does not matter among Union writers.

## The decision

Irreducible, tier inferred. Default: a window from about 00:00 (Brewster; Humphreys' letter) to 02:00 (Tripp and Bodine), with Humphreys' 01:00 inside it. The animation starts no unit before about midnight, the earliest source-supported time, and does not draw the whole division at one hour; which unit arrives when is not documented. The Third Brigade and the battery arrive at 09:00. The plan's midnight and 02:00 both hold as ends of the window; the first delegated 01:00 holds in part: it is Humphreys' own hour and stays selectable. All values stay listed.

## What would change it

A note, letter, diary or brigade log of 1-2 July saying which regiment or brigade led the column and when the head reached the lines; a look at the page image of Brewster's report (to confirm that "there" is the point near Gettysburg); the sources behind Epperson's and Stone Sentinels' times.

## Searches that failed

The Epperson and Stone Sentinels pages could not be read. No other hour for the division was found in the Official Records reports of Carr, Birney and Burling (Brewster, Tripp and Bodine do give hours and are cited above), nor in the other regiments of the First Brigade, in the Joint Committee testimony by regex, or in the local books by pattern. The life of Humphreys by his son (listed in the ledger) was not fetched.
