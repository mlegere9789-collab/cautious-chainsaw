# C-002. When Humphreys' division reached the field, night of 1-2 July 1863

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: 01:00 on 2 July. Class: weighted (the evidence favours one value but does not settle it). Tier: documented, flagged as resting on one line of knowledge.

## The dispute

Brigadier General Humphreys commanded the Second Division of Sickles's Third Corps. It marched from Emmitsburg on the afternoon of 1 July, was led toward the Black Horse Tavern, found Confederate troops near it, turned back, and reached the Union lines in the night. The plan lists 00:00 and 02:00 and recommends 02:00. The first delegated pass kept 01:00 from Humphreys' report. The plan's row prints no sources; the timeline rows behind it name Epperson (midnight) and Stone Sentinels (02:00), two web pages that could not be read here.

## What the witnesses say

**Humphreys, report of 16 August 1863:** after retracing his steps he marched on, "bivouacking at 1 a. m. on July 2 about 1 mile from Gettysburg and eastward of the Emmitsburg road". He had two of his three brigades and one battery; the Third Brigade and Smith's battery had been left on the Waynesboro pike. He commanded the division and gave the orders. This is the hour of settling into bivouac, the end of the march. A round hour; Union writer; no clock named.

**Humphreys, private letter to Fitz Lee, printed in 1878:** "My division (Second division Third corps) reached the ground towards midnight of July 1st". Written some fifteen years after, a recollection. He says "reached the ground", not "bivouacked": it may be the head of the column, but he does not say so.

**Bachelder, 1873 and 1878.** In his guide of 1873 Humphreys' division "arrived at midnight". In a letter printed in 1878 it "reached the field at 1 o'clock the next morning". He was a historian, not a witness, and names no source either time. The New York monuments volume (1900: "arrived at Gettysburg in the night at 1 a. m.") and the 1913 Meade volume ("did not reach the ridge until one o'clock in the morning") agree with the report and also name no source.

**Burling, Third Brigade, 20 August 1863, and Humphreys:** the brigade left at Emmitsburg could not march until nearly 4 a.m. and "joined the corps and division at 9 a. m., July 2". That is a different event from the main body's arrival.

**02:00.** No report, letter or book found gives it. The reports of Carr, Brewster and Birney give no hour for the night.

## The weighing

Humphreys' report of 1863 is the best class of writing, by the man who led the column. His own later letter gives a different hour; by the rule that the writing of the time beats the memory, the report wins. The two may be two moments of one march (head reaches the ground, rear bivouacks), but no source says so, so that is left as a possibility. The later books that say 1 a.m. echo it and are counted with it, which is why the default is flagged as a single source. Nothing supports 02:00.

## The decision

Default: **01:00**, drawn as the end of the night march; the head of the column is drawn arriving before it, never earlier than "towards midnight". The Third Brigade and the battery arrive at 09:00. The plan's recommended 02:00 does not hold (no source). The first delegated default holds. Midnight stays listed (inferred, later memory); 02:00 stays listed as a marked stand-in.

## What would change it

A note, letter or diary of 1-2 July from Humphreys' staff or brigades giving the hour the head reached the lines; the sources behind Epperson's and Stone Sentinels' times.

## Searches that failed

The Epperson and Stone Sentinels pages could not be read. No other hour for the division was found in the Official Records reports of Carr, Brewster, Birney and Burling, in the Joint Committee testimony by regex, or in the local books by pattern. The life of Humphreys by his son (listed in the ledger) was not fetched.
