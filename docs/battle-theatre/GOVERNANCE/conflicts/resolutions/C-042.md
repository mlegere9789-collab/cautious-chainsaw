# C-042. Lee on the field, 1 July 1863

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: about 14:00. Class: weighted (the evidence favours one value but does not settle it). Tier: documented (a report of 1863 by a man who was there gives the hour, hedged).

## The dispute

General Lee rode from Cashtown to the battle west of Gettysburg on 1 July. The plan lists two hours for his arrival on the field: 13:30 (one timeline web page) and 14:30 (one encyclopaedia page), recommends 13:30, and says the earlier time is kept only "for coherence". The first pass found no hour in the Official Records and kept 13:30 as a marked stand-in. This pass read the reports and the staff accounts for an hour.

## What the witnesses say

**General William N. Pendleton, Lee's chief of artillery: "perhaps, two o'clock".** He rode with Lee from Cashtown and took position beside him on an eminence "more than a mile west of the town", where "we, on foot, took position overlooking the field. It was, perhaps, two o'clock, and the battle was raging with considerable violence." The report is dated 12 September 1863, ten weeks after the day. The Southern Historical Society Papers (vol. 5, p. 196) print it from the manuscript; the Official Records print (vol. 27 part 2, p. 348) loses the digit in its text layer, and a second scan of that volume reads 2. He does not say whose clock he used and he says "perhaps": an estimate, not a reading.

**General Early, 1878: "about two P. M."** Early (Southern Historical Society Papers, vol. 6, p. 32) cites Pendleton for the point, so this is the same line of knowledge, not a second witness. **Colonel Alexander, 1907: "Soon after two o'clock, Lee had arrived on Seminary Ridge."** Alexander was with Longstreet's corps and not on the field that afternoon; he gives no source.

**General Heth, 1877.** Rodes was "very heavily engaged", Heth found Lee, asked leave to attack and was refused ("Longstreet is not up"); a little later Lee gave permission. No hour, but it shows Lee on the field while Rodes's division was hard pressed. Hill's report says "About 2.30 o'clock" the right wing of Ewell's corps appeared on his left. General Anderson reports that his division reached Cashtown "early in the afternoon" and waited about an hour there.

**General Long (Lee's military secretary), 1876: Lee arrived "near the close of the battle on the afternoon of July 1st".** **Colonel Walter Taylor (Lee's adjutant), 1877: Lee "witnessed the flight of the Federals through Gettysburg".** Both were with Lee, neither gives a clock, and both speak of the end of the fight. **A history of 1913 (Life and Letters of General Meade, p. 56)** says Lee "reached Seminary Ridge about half-past four in the afternoon", as the Union line was retreating through the town. It names no source for the hour. A Park Service memo of 1940 repeats it.

**13:30 and 14:30.** Neither web page could be read here, and no report, letter or book found gives either hour for Lee. The plan also cites a Park Service seminar paper ("NPS Gettysburg Seminar, first day") for 13:30; no paper, page or quote is named and it could not be identified. The seminar papers read (Harman 2018 on Lee's whereabouts; the A. P. Hill essay) give no hour.

## The weighing

- The only first-hand hour is Pendleton's, written ten weeks after, by a general officer who was beside Lee. It carries "perhaps", so it is good to about a quarter of an hour either way.
- Early and Alexander repeat about two but do not add a witness. The values 13:30, 14:30 and 16:30 each rest on a web page, a timeline or a late history.
- The 16:30 reading is probably a later stage: Pendleton stood on the first crest west of the town with Lee, who suggested positions for guns to rake the valley while the fight was still going, whereas the 1913 book and Long describe Lee watching the retreat. The sources do not say whether Lee moved to Seminary Ridge in between.
- The two armies' clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes (an unsourced estimate). Pendleton, Hill and Anderson are all on the Confederate clock, so no correction is applied among them.

## The decision

Default: **about 14:00**, Lee reaching the crest west of Gettysburg and taking position overlooking the battle, from Pendleton's report. Class weighted, tier documented, with the flag that one line of knowledge carries it. The other values stay listed: 13:30 (the plan's value and recommended default; no source, kept as a marked stand-in), 14:30 (the plan's second value; no source; it is the latest time that Heth's and Hill's accounts allow) and about 16:30 (Lee on Seminary Ridge at the end of the fight, a later stage).

The plan's recommended default (13:30) does not hold: no source gives it, and the animation does not draw Lee on the field before the earliest source-supported time. The first delegated default (13:30 kept as a marked stand-in) holds only in part: it was the right treatment when no hour was known, and it is replaced now that one is.

## What would change it

An hour written on 1 July or soon after by someone with Lee (a dispatch or letter of Lee, Marshall, Venable or Taylor) or by Hill, Heth or Pender would replace Pendleton's estimate. A source behind the plan's 13:30 or 14:30 would move it. A page image of the Official Records print of Pendleton's report reading a digit other than 2 would change the hour.

## Searches that failed

No hour for Lee's arrival other than Pendleton's was found in the Official Records (Lee's, Hill's, Heth's, Anderson's and the other reports), in the Southern Historical Society Papers (Heth, Long, Fitz. Lee and Early), in Long's and Taylor's books, in Hoke, Longstreet, Fremantle, or in the Park Service texts. The Stone Sentinels and Wikipedia pages the plan names could not be read (hosts not reachable for this research).
