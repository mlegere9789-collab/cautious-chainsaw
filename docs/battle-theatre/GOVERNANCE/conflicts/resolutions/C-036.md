# C-036: the date of the fight at Williamsport

## The dispute
After Gettysburg the Confederate wagon trains and wounded waited at Williamsport, Maryland, for the flooded Potomac to fall. A Union cavalry division under Buford attacked the town and was beaten off by Imboden's force, which included wagoners armed with muskets of the wounded. The plan gives the date as 6 July or 7 July and recommends 6 July. The first delegated pass kept 6 July and showed 7 July as contradicted.

## 6 July
- **Buford**, commanding the attacking division, report of 29 Aug 1863 (p. 928): "July 6, the whole division ... marched at 4 a. m. toward Williamsport", and "At about 5 p. m., when near Saint James' College, the enemy's pickets were discovered, driven in, and preparations made to capture the town". He fought until dark.
- **Buford**, dispatch dated 7 July (p. 925, received 11:45 p.m.): "I attempted to take Williamsport yesterday". **Meade** wired Halleck on 7 July at 3:10 p.m. (p. 81) that Buford "attacked Williamsport yesterday". Both were written on the 7th about the 6th; Meade relays Buford.
- **Merritt**, who led one brigade of the division, report of 18 Jul (p. 943): "On the 6th instant we were engaged with the rest of the division fighting the enemy near Williamsport."
- **Murchison**, colonel of the 54th North Carolina in the defence, report of 5 Aug (p. 488): "On Monday [July 6], the enemy advanced on Williamsport". The bracket is the volume's; 6 July 1863 was a Monday.
- **Pendleton**, chief of artillery, report of 12 Sep (p. 353): the batteries with Imboden arrived "late on the 5th, and, on the 6th, did excellent service in repelling an attack of the enemy".
- **Imboden**, who commanded the defence, in 1887 (Battles and Leaders): "Early on the morning of the 6th I received intelligence of the approach from Frederick of a large body of cavalry".
- Lee's report of 31 Jul 1863 (p. 309) also says the trains were attacked "on the 6th", but the first scan prints the digit as "Gth", the second scan as "6th", and the page image could not be fetched. It is not cited.

## 7 July
No source found dates the Williamsport fight on 7 July. The plan took it from web pages (a lead). The 7th is the day Buford and Meade wrote about the fight, the day the army reached Hagerstown (Pendleton p. 353), the day of fighting at Boonsborough (Merritt p. 944), and the evening the 6th U.S. Cavalry met the 7th Virginia near Funkstown (W. E. Jones, p. 754). Whether one of these is the origin of the plan's date is not known.

## Weighing
The dispatches of the day, the reports of the weeks after and the memoir all agree, from both sides; four independent lines of knowledge (Buford, Merritt, Murchison, Imboden) were in the action. The hours differ greatly (Imboden: the enemy in front about half past one; Buford: first contact about 5 p.m.; Murchison: two hours of fighting until night), but that is a question of the hour, not decided here, and no clock difference moves an afternoon fight to another day.

## Decision
Class **settled**, tier **documented**. Default: **6 July 1863**. The plan's recommendation and the first delegated default both hold. 7 July stays selectable, marked unsourced and contradicted.

## What would change it
A report or letter of 1863 stating that the attack fell on 7 July. None was found.

## Searches that failed
The Official Records (all three parts) and the local books and papers for an attack at Williamsport on the 7th: nothing. The web pages the plan names were not read (they are leads).
