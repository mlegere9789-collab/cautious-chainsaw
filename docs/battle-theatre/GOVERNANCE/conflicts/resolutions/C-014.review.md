# Review of C-014 (Prisoners taken, 20th Maine front)

Reviewer: independent max-effort pass, 2026-11-24. Validator: 0 errors, 0 warnings. All quotes re-found and read in context (Chamberlain p. 625, Barnes p. 603, Rice p. 618, Fisher p. 658, Oates p. 216 and OR27-2 p. 393, Melcher, Gerrish, Minnigh, monument p. 249, Norton p. 113). Datelines checked (Chamberlain 6 Jul, Barnes 24 Aug, Rice 31 Jul, Fisher 9 Jul, Oates 8 Aug all correct).

**Verdict: DEFECTS (5 to fix)**

## What holds
- 368 and "Four hundred" are quoted exactly and both really refer to the prisoners sent to the rear after the counter-charge; 25 (night advance), over 500 (brigade to 9 p. m.) and over 30 (Fisher, 10 p. m. ascent) are correctly kept apart from them; 218 is correctly a no-source gap with three recorded failed lookups; 400+25 is carried and explained; the first pass's 368 to 425 is carried with the reason; no sum of unlike counts is made. Barnes's "drawn principally from the report" of the brigade commander is real (p. 602). "Nine other values" is right.

## Defects and exact fixes
1. **Class and mode disagree.** The class is "weighted" but the default is `mode: range` with the note "neither explains the other". PLAN section 6: weighted means one value is better weighted and is the default; a range with no headline, resting on two best-class witnesses nothing separates, is irreducible. Chamberlain (present, 4 days) outranks Barnes (elsewhere, relays, 53 days) on the rubric, so a weighted resolution would default to 400 with 368 selectable. Fix one way or the other: (a) class `irreducible`, tier unchanged, and word the weighing "the record is divided between two figures the evidence cannot separate"; or (b) keep weighted and make the default `mode: position` on 400. Do not leave weighted with a range.
2. **A missed explanation (PLAN section 6 step 3): rounding.** Chamberlain's "Four hundred" is a round figure; Barnes's 368 is exact and was drawn from Rice's report. A round 400 for 368 is a common reading and is within 9 percent. The weighing says "neither explains the other" without having tested rounding. Add a sentence to `weighing.summary` and the .md that 368 may be the count and 400 its rounding (or why not), and set `hedge: "about"` on Chamberlain's cite or explain why exact.
3. **Norton (cite 2 of C-014-P3) does not support the position.** Norton writes that Prince's four hundred was "mostly" from the 15th and 47th Alabama, "but many prisoners belonging to the Fourth and Forty-eighth Alabama and the Fourth and Fifth Texas were also taken. Vincent's other regiments took their full share, and a total of four hundred for the brigade does not seem excessive." He reads 400 as a brigade total, which is the opposite of "400 in front of the 20th Maine" and conflicts with Rice's over 500. Remove it from C-014-P3's support. If kept, put it on its own research position ("400 as a brigade total, Norton 1913"), flagged memoir_only, reason `later_memory`.
4. **C-014-P10's label names "Crawford" but cites only Fisher.** Either add the Crawford citation or delete "Crawford" from the label. Also, Fisher's own words are "I took two regiments of my brigade ... and the Twentieth Maine ... and at 10 p. m. ascended the hill ... taking over 30 prisoners in our ascent": his 30 therefore includes the 20th Maine's ascent, so Chamberlain's 25 very probably lies inside Fisher's 30 and the two should not be added. The reason text ("Chamberlain's own part of it is the 25") is an unstated inference; add "probably inside" and the same hedge in the .md.
5. **C-014-P9 range {84, 238} mixes units.** 84 is the 15th Alabama's unwounded captured; 238 is Oates's ceiling if all 154 of the 47th's seven companies are added to those 84. Presenting 84 to 238 as a count range suggests 84 is a low total for both regiments. Fix: value `{"n": 238}` with hedge upper ("at most"), and name 84 as a component in the label and in `inference.step`. The reason_not_chosen already notes Oates counts unwounded men only; keep it.

## Not defects, noted
- Melcher's "with over three hundred prisoners and all the wounded" and Gerrish's "over three hundred" both include more than the charge (Company B's separate 100+, the night advance); the weighing calls them "round counts"; acceptable as memoir-only.
- Both extremes of the range are flagged single_source honestly.
