# C-009. Berdan's reconnaissance start

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: about 11:00 on 2 July 1863. Class: weighted (the evidence favours one value but does not settle it). Tier: documented (an exact statement by the officer who received the order, in a report written 27 days after).

## The dispute

On the morning of 2 July 1863 Colonel Hiram Berdan of the 1st US Sharpshooters took a detachment of 100 sharpshooters, with the 3rd Maine in support, out of the Third Corps line on the Emmitsburg road to find out what the Confederates were doing in the woods to the west (Pitzer's Woods). They met Wilcox's Alabama brigade and found three columns of infantry in motion beyond the woods. The plan gives three hours for the start: 11:00, 11:15 and noon, and recommends 11:15. The first pass of the research found 11:00 in Berdan's report and noon in two others, and found nothing for 11:15.

"Start" is taken here as the order that sent the detachment out. Nobody gives the minute the men actually moved off.

## What the witnesses say

**Colonel Berdan, report of 29 July 1863 (27 days after): about 11 a.m.** He had put a first detachment of 100 on the crest beyond the Emmitsburg road at about 7:30 a.m. It could not see enough, so he reported to General Birney, and "about 11 a. m." received Birney's order for a second detachment of 100 with the Third Maine in support (Official Records, p. 515). He led the detachment himself and says he reported its results to Birney "about 2 o'clock".

**General Birney, who gave the order, gave two hours.** His report of 7 August 1863 says "At 12 m." he received General Sickles's permission to send 100 of Berdan's sharpshooters with the Third Maine (p. 482). In sworn testimony to the Joint Committee on 7 March 1864 he says that "About 11 o'clock", with the firing on his skirmish line growing sharp, he asked Sickles for leave to send a regiment and a battalion of sharpshooters to reconnoitre, and received it (printed p. 366). The later account agrees with Berdan.

**General Hunt, report of 27 September 1863: about noon.** "About 12 m. a detachment of Berdan's Sharpshooters was sent into the woods" (p. 234). Hunt says in the next paragraph that he reached the ground about 2 p.m., so he did not see it sent; he names no source. In his testimony of April 1864 he says he was at headquarters about 11 and then rode out with Sickles, who said he would send in a couple of companies of Berdan's sharpshooters to examine the wood (printed p. 449): that puts Sickles's decision after about 11, with no later hour.

**The signal station on Round Top, messages of the day.** Lieutenant Jerome sent Butterfield at 11.45 a.m. "Enemy's skirmishers are advancing from the west, 1 mile from here" and at 11.55 "The rebels are in force, and our skirmishers give way" (Official Records, vol. 27 part 3, pp. 487-488). These are the best class of writing there is (orders and messages of the day). They do not name Berdan's men, and the corps' picket line was also out, so they bound the start rather than state it: if they describe the reconnaissance, it began before about 11:55, and a noon start is too late.

**Two sharpshooters' diaries, quoted in 1889.** Captain Marble's entry headed 12 o'clock says the sharpshooters and the Third Maine are advancing in line of skirmishers on his left; F. E. Garrett's entry headed noon says "We have just come out of the fight". Lieutenant Buchanan printed them in an oration of 2 July 1889 (New York at Gettysburg, 1902, p. 1071). The original diaries were not seen, and the heading of an entry is not necessarily the hour of the events.

**General Wilcox, Confederate, report of 17 July 1863: before 9 a.m.** He describes the 10th Alabama's fight of fifteen or twenty minutes with "the Third Maine and the First New York Sharpshooters", then says the brigade "now (9 a. m.) took its position in line of battle" (OR27-2, p. 617). That dates his line at 9, not the fight, and no Union writer puts the fight before about 11. It stands alone and is shown as a separate value.

**Later writers.** Stevens (history of the sharpshooters, 1892) says "about noon" with no source, which matches Birney's report. The Maine volume (1898) says it was "between 11 and 12 o'clock" when the Third Maine advanced into the wood after crossing three-quarters of a mile of open field. The New York commission's narrative of 1900 says the detachment "advanced about 11:30 a. m." and names no source. The two commission writers wrote 35 and 37 years later and name no witness for their hours.

**11:15.** No report, diary or book read gives it. The plan took it from a terse web timeline it calls weak.

## The weighing

- Berdan received the order and wrote within a month; Birney gave it and, under oath, agrees with him. Two men on both ends of the order, neither copying the other.
- Birney's report of 1863 says noon, but his testimony of 1864 says about 11. A man's two accounts cannot both be right; the one that agrees with the officer who got the order and with the signal messages is preferred. Hunt's noon is second-hand and Stevens is counted with Birney.
- If the signal messages and the diary heading do refer to the reconnaissance, noon is too late as a start. The messages do not say so, so this is a bound, not a proof.
- The reported 20-minute difference between the two armies' clocks is not applied. It would not turn Wilcox's 9 into 11 in any case.
- "About 11" is read as 11:00 plus or minus a quarter hour, so 11:15 lies inside it; nobody states it.

## The decision

The default is about 11:00 on 2 July: Berdan is ordered to send the detachment out. It is weighted (not settled, because Birney's report and Hunt say noon and the minute of stepping off is not known) and documented (Berdan's report states the hour exactly; Birney's testimony agrees). The animation never draws the detachment leaving before 11:00; the signal messages show skirmishing by 11:45 to 11:55. The other seven values stay selectable: 11:15 (no source), noon (Birney's report, Hunt, Stevens), about 11:30 (New York, 1900), 11:45 to 11:55 (the signal messages), before 9:00 (Wilcox), about noon in the diaries, and between 11 and 12 (Maine, 1898), each with its reason.

The plan's recommended default, 11:15, does not hold: no source states it. The first delegated default, 11:00, holds, and now also rests on Birney's sworn account of 1864 and the signal messages.

## What would change it

A report, letter or diary of 1863 that gives the hour the detachment moved off (Captain Briscoe of Birney's staff, Colonel Lakeman's papers, the original diaries of Marble or Garrett, the signal station log) would replace the order hour. Evidence that the 11.45 and 11.55 messages describe another skirmish, or an original source for Birney's "12 m.", would move the default toward noon.

## Searches that failed

No report of Briscoe, Forney, Sanders or Herbert exists in the Official Records, and no clock reading between 11:00 and 11:30 appears there for the order or the start. No hour was found in the Joint Committee volume other than Birney's and Hunt's. The only 11.15 near the subject (Colonel Craig of the 105th Pennsylvania) is a different movement. Not read: the Bachelder papers, Berdan's or Birney's letters, the original diaries, and the signal station log.
