# C-022. When the Sixth Corps reached Gettysburg, afternoon of 2 July 1863

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window from 14:00 to 17:00, the head drawn at 15:00. Class: irreducible (the record itself is divided about the head's hour). Tier: documented (both ends are stated in documents).

## The dispute

Sedgwick's Sixth Corps left Manchester, Maryland, about 9 p.m. on 1 July and marched some 35 miles. The plan gives "head about 14:00; main body about 17:00" and recommends "head 14:00, main body 17:00"; it prints no sources. The first delegated pass found no Official Records support for 14:00 and set the head at 15:00 to 16:00, the first brigades in action about 17:00. The first pass did not read the Joint Committee's testimony of 1864, in which both commanders give 2 p.m.

## What the witnesses say

**General Meade, dispatch to Halleck headed "Headquarters near Gettysburg, July 2, 1863, 3 p. m.":** "The Sixth Corps is just coming in, very much worn out." His report (1 October) says "At 3 p. m. the Sixth Corps arrived", after a march of 33 miles. Major Kohler (98th Pennsylvania, report of 1 August) has his regiment in the vicinity of the field, to the left of Gettysburg, about 3 p.m.

**General Meade, sworn testimony of 5 March 1864 (Joint Committee on the Conduct of the War, p. 331):** the Sixth Corps, "leaving at nine o'clock at night, did not reach the scene until about two o'clock in the afternoon"; and "it having been reported to me about two o'clock that the 6th corps had arrived", he rode out to the left about three or half past three. (The heading over his testimony reads 1863 in the scan; the committee's own minute in the same volume gives 5 March 1864.) The second sentence relays a report made to him; the first does not say whether he means the head or the whole corps.

**General Sedgwick, sworn testimony of 8 April 1864:** "I arrived at Gettysburg about 2 o'clock on the afternoon of the 2d of July", reported to headquarters, found Meade out at the Third Corps' position, waited a few minutes and received his orders. His own report of 8 August 1863 says only "on the afternoon of July 2".

**General Wright, First Division, 7 August:** the corps "arrived about 4 p. m." at Gettysburg, halted about two hours, then was ordered up to support the left center. A battery commander of the corps also has 4 p.m. Everett's oration (November 1863) says Sedgwick arrived "not till two o'clock in the afternoon"; the New Jersey narrative of 1888 has the advance brigade on the field at two and the whole corps reported at 6.

**Colonel Grant (Vermont brigade, 3 August):** the brigade arrived "near the scene of action at about 5 p. m. July 2". General Bartlett (6 August): his brigade "arrived on the field a little after 5 p. m." and formed near the left. General Howe (7 August): the Second Division reached the field at 5 p.m. (the day digit is garbled in the scan and is not used). The First New York Battery came up about 10 p.m., behind the leading brigade of the Third Division.

## The weighing

A dispatch of the time is the best class of writing, and it has the corps "just coming in" at 3; Meade's report of 1863 says it arrived at 3. But the two men who were there, the army commander (who wrote the dispatch) and the corps commander, each swore in 1864 to about 2 p.m., and Meade adds that he was told about two that the corps had arrived. So 14:00 is not one man's memory: it is two sworn statements by the two commanders, with Everett's oration and a later New Jersey book in the same direction. Both can be true of a column 35 miles long (the head reported to headquarters about 2, the corps coming in about 3), but no source says so, and Meade's report says flatly that the corps arrived at 3. Nothing in rank, nearness or independence separates them, so the head's hour is a divided record. Sedgwick's own order of events (Meade out at the front, back a few minutes later) would fit a later hour for his reporting at headquarters; that is an inference about when he reported, not when the head arrived, and Sedgwick says separately that he arrived about 2. Wright's 4 p.m. and the 5 p.m. of Grant, Bartlett and Howe are different places and moments: the corps at Gettysburg, then the brigades moving to the left. All Union clocks; no correction applied.

## The decision

Irreducible, tier documented. Default: a window from **14:00 to 17:00**: the head between 14:00 and 15:00 (divided), the corps at Gettysburg about 16:00, brigades on the left about 17:00. The picture draws the head at 15:00, the hour of the only dispatch of the day; 14:00 is the earliest source-supported hour, stays selectable and is the floor for any drawing of the corps. The plan's pair (14:00 and 17:00) holds as the two ends of the window. The first delegated default lies inside it.

## What would change it

A log or report of the corps' leading brigade or of Sedgwick's staff giving the hour the head reached Gettysburg; a page image of Meade's testimony (to confirm the year); page images of Howe's and Neill's reports; the source behind the plan's 14:00.

## Searches that failed

The Stone Sentinels page the seed row names could not be read. No dispatch of Sedgwick or Butterfield in the correspondence dates the head's arrival, and the other Sixth Corps reports give no earlier hour for it. In the Joint Committee volume only Meade's and Sedgwick's testimony give an hour; Butterfield, Hunt and the others give none.
