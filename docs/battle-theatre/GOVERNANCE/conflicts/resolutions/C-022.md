# C-022. When the Sixth Corps reached Gettysburg, afternoon of 2 July 1863

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window from 15:00 to 17:00. Class: weighted (the evidence favours one reading but does not settle it). Tier: documented (both ends are stated in reports and a dispatch of the time).

## The dispute

Sedgwick's Sixth Corps left Manchester, Maryland, about 9 p.m. on 1 July and marched some 35 miles. The plan gives "head about 14:00; main body about 17:00" and recommends "head 14:00, main body 17:00"; it prints no sources. The first delegated pass found no Official Records support for 14:00 and set the head at 15:00 to 16:00, the first brigades in action about 17:00. The first pass did not read the Joint Committee's testimony of 1864.

## What the witnesses say

**General Meade, dispatch to Halleck headed "Headquarters near Gettysburg, July 2, 1863, 3 p. m.":** "The Sixth Corps is just coming in, very much worn out." His report (1 October) says "At 3 p. m. the Sixth Corps arrived", after a march of 33 miles. Major Kohler (98th Pennsylvania, report of 1 August) has his regiment in the vicinity of the field, to the left of Gettysburg, about 3 p.m.

**General Sedgwick, sworn testimony of 8 April 1864:** "I arrived at Gettysburg about 2 o'clock on the afternoon of the 2d of July", reported to headquarters, found Meade out at the Third Corps' position, waited a few minutes and received his orders. His own report of 8 August 1863 says only "on the afternoon of July 2".

**General Wright, First Division, 7 August:** the corps "arrived about 4 p. m." at Gettysburg, halted about two hours, then was ordered up to support the left center. A battery commander of the corps also has 4 p.m.

**Colonel Grant (Vermont brigade, 3 August):** the brigade arrived "near the scene of action at about 5 p. m. July 2". General Bartlett (6 August): his brigade "arrived on the field a little after 5 p. m." and formed near the left. General Howe (7 August): the Second Division reached the field at 5 p.m. (the day digit is garbled in the scan and is not used). The First New York Battery came up about 10 p.m., behind the leading brigade of the Third Division.

## The weighing

A dispatch of the time is the best class of writing, and it has the head coming in at 3. Sedgwick's 2 p.m. is one memory 281 days later, his own report says only "afternoon", and the order of his own story (Meade out at the front, back a few minutes later) fits the later hour. Wright's 4 p.m. and the 5 p.m. of Grant, Bartlett and Howe are different places and moments: the corps at Gettysburg, then the brigades moving to the left. The window shows the head, the corps at Gettysburg and the brigades on the left. All Union clocks; no correction applied.

## The decision

Default: **15:00 to 17:00**, the head drawn at 15:00. The plan's 14:00 is kept as a selectable, documented value (outweighed); 17:00 holds. The first delegated default holds. The earliest hour in the record, 14:00, is the floor for any drawing of the corps.

## What would change it

A log or report of the corps' leading brigade or of Sedgwick's staff giving the hour the head reached Gettysburg; page images of Howe's and Neill's reports; the source behind the plan's 14:00.

## Searches that failed

The Stone Sentinels page the seed row names could not be read. No dispatch of Sedgwick or Butterfield in the correspondence dates the head's arrival, and the other Sixth Corps reports and the Joint Committee testimony of Meade and others give no earlier or later hour for it.
