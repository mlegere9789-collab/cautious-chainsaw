# C-033: when Imboden left Cashtown with the wounded (July 4 or 5)

**The dispute.** The plan lists Jul 4 and Jul 5 and recommends "Jul 4 afternoon". The first delegated default said Jul 4, evening start. A timeline row of the plan adds a Jul 5 20:00 departure "per one account".

**Who says what.**

- *Imboden* (Brigadier General; he commanded the brigade and the whole train of wounded; article "The Confederate Retreat from Gettysburg", Battles and Leaders, 1887). It was clear by 9 o'clock on July 4 that the wagons could not be ready before late afternoon. The rain began shortly after noon. "About 4 p. M. the head of the column was put in motion near Cashtown, and began the ascent of the mountain in the direction of Chambersburg." He himself "remained at Cashtown giving directions" and "After dark I set out from Cashtown to gain the head of the column during the night." The rear of the column, Young's cavalry and Hart's battery, began the ascent "at dawn of the 5th": the column was seventeen miles long.
- *Guild* (Medical Director; report of 20 July 1863, 16 days after): "on the evening of the 4th, our ambulance trains took up their line of march by two routes", one by Cashtown, one by Fairfield. *Lee* (detailed report, January 1864): the trains with the wounded "were ordered to Williamsport on July 4, part moving through Cashtown and Greencastle, escorted by General Imboden". *Pendleton* (report of 12 September 1863): batteries were detailed on the 4th to report to Imboden at Cashtown, and "arrived with the train at Williamsport late on the 5th". *Hoke* (Chambersburg, 1887): "about four o'clock in the afternoon of Saturday, July 4th" the head of the train left in a terrific rain storm; no source named, possibly Imboden's article.
- *Jul 5.* Nothing found says Imboden or the head of the column left Cashtown on the 5th. The 5th is the day the tail left at dawn and the day the train arrived at Williamsport. The timeline row's "about 20:00 on the 5th" has no source here; Imboden's own departure "after dark" on the 4th (sunset 19:32) is in the same neighbourhood of the clock and may be what a later summary misdated, but that is a guess and is not used.

**Weighing.** Imboden alone gives an hour, and he is the man who gave the order, but he wrote 24 years later; a memoir supports an inferred value only. Guild, writing at the time, gives the evening of the 4th for the ambulance trains and no hour; whether that is the same start as Imboden's 4 p.m., or the trains leaving Gettysburg before the head reached Cashtown, the sources do not say. Lee and Pendleton give the day. All agree on July 4; none puts a departure on the 5th. Confederate clock throughout; the reported difference of about 20 minutes is not applied and is not at issue.

**Decision.** Class settled, tier inferred: the head of the column leaves Cashtown about 16:00 on July 4; Imboden himself leaves after dark on July 4; the rear leaves at dawn on July 5. The plan's "Jul 4 afternoon" holds. "Jul 4" holds as the day. "Jul 5" stays selectable as the rear's departure and the arrival, not as Imboden's departure; the first delegated default (Jul 4, evening start) holds as the day and the evening, with no hour (it may be the same start or the trains leaving Gettysburg).

**What would change it.** A contemporary letter or diary of an officer at Cashtown that gives an hour, or Imboden's own report of 1863, if it exists in the archives.

**Searches that failed.** Imboden's report of the retreat is not in the Official Records volumes read. No local text states a departure of Imboden on July 5 or at 20:00: a search of the Official Records, Battles and Leaders, the Southern Historical Society Papers and the state volumes found none.
