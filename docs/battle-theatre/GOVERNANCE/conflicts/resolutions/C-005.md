# C-005: Hood at Seminary Ridge

Researched 2026-11-24. Result: the head of Hood's division and Hood himself were on the ridge about 05:00 (between sunrise and about 05:30); the whole column could be up by about 07:30. Evidence weighed, not settled; the value is inferred, not documented.

## The dispute

The plan gives three clocks for when Hood's division stood behind Seminary Ridge on the morning of 2 July 1863: 05:30, 06:30 and 07:30. It takes 06:30 as its default. The three values come from a timeline page (EPP), from Freeman's appendix on times (FREE) and from a blog (PADRE). The first delegated pass found no hour for Hood in the Official Records, kept 06:30 as a marked stand-in and added Longstreet's "at sunrise" (about 04:35). The question here: what do the people who were there say, and what do the three clocks actually measure?

## What each writer says

1. **Fremantle**, a British officer with Longstreet's staff. His journal entry for Thursday, 2 July (printed 1864, p. 257): he "arrived at 5 A. M." at the observation point, and "Just below us were seated Generals Lee, Hill, Longstreet, and Hood, in consultation". He saw it himself and wrote it at the time. It places Hood, the man, on the ridge at 5 a.m.; it says nothing of the division.
2. **Shepherd**, Second Georgia, Benning's brigade of Hood's division. Report of 27 July 1863 (Official Records 27/2, p. 420): "At 3 a. m. it resumed the march, and again halted, after proceeding some 3 miles." No hour for the halt. He marched in the column. A second scan of the volume reads the same figures. His neighbour Hodges (Seventeenth Georgia) prints "3 p. m." for the hour the march resumed; that is unclear and is not used.
3. **Hood**, letter to Longstreet of 28 June 1875, printed in his book (1880, pp. 56-57), "from memory", 12 years later: his troops reached Gettysburg "before or at sunrise"; he arrived with his staff "shortly after daybreak"; "My division soon commenced filing into an open field near me."
4. **Longstreet**, book of 1896 (pp. 362, 378), 33 years later: McLaws and Hood rode up "and at sunrise their commands filed off the road"; the troops reached Lee's headquarters "about sunrise". In a letter of 20 April 1875 (quoted by Taylor, 1906, p. 198) he had written that his "two divisions nor myself did not reach General Lee until 8 a.m. on the 2d". Taylor notes the two statements cannot be reconciled. The diary of the day contradicts the 8 a.m. for Longstreet himself.
5. **Polley**, a soldier of the Fourth Texas, 1910 (p. 154): the Texas Brigade reached the ridge "not later than an hour after sunrise", about 05:35 at the latest. His account of the night before has errors, so he is used only for that bound.
6. **Longstreet's report** of 27 July 1863 (Official Records 27/2, p. 358): Hood's division "got within nearly the same distance of the town" as Marsh Creek, 4 miles, "about 12 o'clock at night". It gives no morning hour for Hood. **Law**, Battles and Leaders (1888): his brigade left New Guilford at 3 a.m. and marched 24 miles, arriving shortly before noon (a pace of nearly 3 miles an hour); he found the other brigades of the division resting "about a mile from the town, on the Chambersburg road". That fits Shepherd's 3 miles from a bivouac 4 miles out.
7. **Freeman**, historian, 1935 (appendix to volume III): "Hood's march began about 3 a.m., and as the distance his troops had to cover was an average of five miles, the head of his column should have been at the ridge by 5:30 a.m. The whole of it could have been there by 7 or 7:30." These are the plan's 05:30 and 07:30. They are computed, from an assumed five miles; Shepherd's report says about three. Freeman also writes that Hood and McLaws "rode ahead of their troops and probably confused the time of their own arrival with that of the appearance of their troops".
8. **Meade's Life and Letters** (1913, p. 69), a later narrative with no named source: Longstreet's two divisions "left at daylight" and "about eight o'clock halted in the fields in the rear of the seminary".

Not read: the Epperson timeline and the Padre Steve blog, whose hosts could not be reached for this research. Kershaw's report (McLaws's division, 1 October 1863) says only that his command "did not leave camp until about sunrise"; it does not concern Hood but shows that Longstreet's "at sunrise" cannot be right for McLaws's division.

## Weighing

Two witnesses of the time outrank the rest: Fremantle's diary of the day and Shepherd's report of 27 July 1863, 25 days after the event. Both were there and both say how they knew. They do not contradict each other or the later writers; together they show that a regiment left at 3 a.m. for about 3 miles and that Hood was on the ridge by 5. Hood, Longstreet and Polley are reminiscences from 12 to 47 years later. Freeman and Meade's narrative are later histories, one computed. Clocks: the Confederate clock for Shepherd, Hood, Longstreet and Polley; Fremantle's watch is unspecified. The 20-minute difference between the armies' clocks is not applied; it is smaller than the half-hour margin of the default.

The three plan values are three points of one march. The head of the column came up first (sunrise to 05:30); Freeman's 07:30 is when the tail could be up; 06:30 is the plan's midpoint and no source gives it. The memoirs' "sunrise" probably dates the generals, who rode ahead; Longstreet's own statements differ (8 a.m. in 1875, sunrise in 1896). The 08:00 of Longstreet's letter and Meade's narrative dates the closing up of both divisions.

No one gives a clock for the division's own arrival, so the value is an inference: Fremantle's 5 a.m., Hood's "soon", a 3 a.m. start and 3 miles at Law's pace (about 70 minutes, so up from about 04:10) and the late bounds of Freeman (05:30) and Polley (05:35).

## Decision

Class **weighted**, tier **inferred**. Default: **about 05:00 (sunrise to 05:30)**, the head of the division and Hood on the ridge, with the tail up by about 07:30 (Law's brigade was not with the column; it rejoined about noon). 05:00 is the one clock reading a writer of the day gives for Hood, and the earliest time the record documents; it is the instant for the picture, never earlier. All other values stay selectable with their reasons: 05:30 (an upper bound from five miles of road), 06:30 (no source), 07:30 (the tail of the column), sunrise about 04:35 (later memory), about 08:00 (later memory; both divisions closed up).

The plan's default (06:30) does not hold: no source states it and the head came up earlier. The first delegated default (a stand-in) does not hold: the point is not a gap.

## What would change it

A report, diary or letter of 1863 giving the hour at which the first or the last brigade of Hood's division reached the ridge, the hour at which the Second Georgia halted after its 3 miles, or the page image of Shepherd's report showing a figure other than 3 a.m.

## Searches that failed

No report by Hood, McLaws or Law on Gettysburg is in the Official Records index; no brigade or regiment report of Hood's division gives an arrival hour. A search of all local texts for 5.30, 6.30 and 7.30 beside Hood found none; the Park Service texts, Sorrel's memoir and McLaws's address of 1879 give no hour for Hood. The Thayer page of Freeman and the page images of the Official Records were refused by the network; Freeman's book and a second scan were read instead.
