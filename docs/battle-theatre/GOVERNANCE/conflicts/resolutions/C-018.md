# C-018: Who stopped Wright's brigade on 2 July, and did it reach the crest?

## The dispute

Late on 2 July 1863 Brig. Gen. A. R. Wright's Georgia brigade (3rd, 22nd and 48th Georgia and the 2nd Georgia Battalion) crossed the Emmitsburg road, took Union guns and was driven back. The plan's row lists three values: Webb, Stannard, and "reached the crest or not". Its recommended default is "stopped short of the crest, attribution unresolved". The first default of 11 November marked the attribution as a gap and Wright's crest as his own claim.

## What each side wrote

**Wright (report of 28 September 1863, 88 days later; Confederate clock).** He was ill on 1 July and back with the brigade at 7 a.m. on the 2nd. He says his men reached a point "within less than 100 yards of the crest of the heights", then "charged up to the top of the crest", took over twenty guns, and had to give them up because the brigade on his right "had actually given way" and the brigade ordered to support his left "failed to advance" (pp. 623-624). He names no Union unit he met there. Lee's report (20 January 1864) says "Wright gained the crest" and Anderson's (7 August 1863) says the brigades "possessed themselves of the ridge"; both repeat reports of the brigades and are counted with Wright.

**Wright's four regiments (17-18 July 1863).** Walker (3rd Georgia), McCurry (22nd), M. R. Hall (48th) and Moffett (2nd Battalion) were in the line. All say they fell back because the brigade on their right gave way or no support came. None uses the word "crest": Walker speaks of the enemy's "first line of battle" and "reserves on the height", Hall of going "beyond their first line of" batteries.

**Union witnesses of the time.** None names Wright's brigade as the one it stopped. Webb (13 July; Union clock) says an enemy line "advanced beyond one gun of Brown's battery", halted, wavered and fell back, pursued by the 106th and 72nd Pennsylvania. Curry (106th Pennsylvania, 11 July) says his charge took about 250 prisoners "among whom were 1 colonel ([William] Gibson, Forty-eighth Georgia)". Darrow (82nd New York, 3 August) says the regiment retook its ground "capturing the battle-flags of the Forty-eighth Georgia", and Hall's list of flags gives the 48th Georgia flag to the 59th New York. Heath (19th Maine, 4 July) says his regiment advanced and retook four guns, and Hancock credits the 19th Maine at Brown's battery. Gibbon says the head of the enemy column "came quite through a vacancy in our line to the left of my division" and was "checked and driven back" by the nearest troops and the Twelfth Corps. Stannard (4 July) and Randall (13th Vermont, 10 July) say the 13th Vermont retook guns at dusk and took about 80 prisoners; neither names the enemy brigade.

**Later writers.** Wilcox (1878) says only "I did not see Wright's brigade during the battle". Alexander reports Wright saying on 3 July "nearly there with my brigade yesterday" (1887) and, in 1907, "I went there with my brigade yesterday"; the hedge changes between his two versions. The Park Service handbook (1954) has Wright piercing "just south of the copse of trees", the Park Service study of 1981 and the Vermont book of 1914 put the 13th Vermont against Wright's men, and the book of 1914 calls the prisoners it took an Alabama regiment's (Alabamians were not in Wright's brigade).

## Weighing

*Who stopped him.* The reports of the time do not give one stopper. They show regiments of three brigades of Gibbon's division (Webb's, Harrow's, Hall's) taking the colonel and flags of Wright's left regiment, with the 19th Maine and others at Brown's battery. That does not make Webb the answer: the 82nd New York and 59th New York are not Webb's, and Webb's own report names no enemy brigade. The case for Stannard rests on books of 1914 and 1981; the reports of 1863 do not put the 13th Vermont against Wright, and its prisoners are called Alabamians. Wright and his regiments agree on why he withdrew: the neighbouring brigades gave way or never came.

*The crest.* Only Wright (repeated by Lee and Anderson) says the top was reached. His colonels and Webb give lesser places, but none contradicts him, and no witness says which crest or landmark is meant. Webb's account puts the enemy halted at one gun of Brown's battery in front of the ridge, which fits "short of the crest" on Webb's front only. The two readings cannot be told apart by rank, position or independence.

*The clock.* Not a question of time. The 20-minute difference between the armies' clocks is not applied. Webb's 6.30 p.m. assault is printed "of the 3d" in one text of the Official Records and "of the 2d" in a second scan; the page image could not be fetched. Curry's report fixes the event on 2 July.

## Decision

Class **irreducible**, tier **inferred**. The default is three claims shown side by side: (1) Wright says he reached the top of the crest, and no other witness confirms or denies it; (2) regiments of Webb's, Harrow's and Hall's brigades counter-charged and took the 48th Georgia's colonel and flags; (3) Wright and his regiments say they withdrew for want of support. Webb, Stannard and "stopped short of the crest" stay selectable, each with its reason.

The plan's default holds in part: "attribution unresolved" holds; "stopped short of the crest" is one of two readings. The first delegated default holds in part: Wright's crest remains his claim, but the attribution is not a gap, since the reports name who took the 48th Georgia's colonel and flags.

## What would change it

A report or letter of 1863 naming the Union unit Wright's brigade met at its farthest point, or a Georgia officer placing his regiment at a named landmark of the ridge, would let one position replace the set. The page image of Webb p. 427 would fix the day of his 6.30 p.m. assault.

## Searches that found nothing

No Union report names Wright's brigade as the one it stopped (full-text search of Part 1 of the Official Records). No regimental report of Wright's brigade uses "crest". The page image of Webb p. 427 could not be fetched (archive.org image host refused). The Stone Sentinels and Wikipedia pages the plan cites, Walker's history of the Second Corps, and the reports of Humphreys, Carr, Brewster, Caldwell and Lockwood were not read to the end.
