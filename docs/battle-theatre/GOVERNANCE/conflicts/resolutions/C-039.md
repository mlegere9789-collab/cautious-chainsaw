# C-039: When did Hancock reach the field on 1 July?

## The dispute

General Hancock was sent forward by Meade from Taneytown after Reynolds fell, to take command of the First, Third and Eleventh Corps. The plan lists about 15:00, before 16:00, 16:00 and 16:30, and recommends about 15:00 to 16:00. The first delegated default kept that band because all four hours "occur" in the Official Records. Read in context, they do not carry equal weight.

## What each side says

**Hancock, on his own arrival.** His report (undated in the print; written between July and 24 October 1863) says he was on the road at 1.10 p.m. and "At 3 p. m. I arrived at Gettysburg and assumed the command", with the First and Eleventh Corps "retiring through the town". In a letter of 17 January 1878 he wrote "about 3 P. M., or between that and 3:30". The Narrative of the Second Corps in his widow's book (1887) says "About 3:30 he reached Cemetery Hill", and Walker (1894) writes that "Hancock gives the hour of his arrival as half-past three". These are one line of knowledge: Hancock, years later. But the dispatch Hancock wrote from the hill, headed 5.25 p.m., says "When I arrived here an hour since" (about 16:25). Meade told the Joint Committee he received it about 7.

**Howard, on the hill.** His note to Meade, headed 5 p.m. on 1 July: "General Hancock arrived at 4 p. m., and communicated his instructions." His report of 31 August 1863 has the order to fall back at 4.10, the columns on Cemetery Hill at 4.30 and Hancock coming "about this time". His book (1907) says "between 4 and 5 P.M."

**Doubleday**, who commanded the First Corps and stood on the hill, prints "About 4.30 P.M." in 1882 (page image read); his report of December 1863 gives no hour. Walker (1894) quotes the same sentence as "About half-past three": the two cannot both be right and the discrepancy is not resolved here; Walker's version is not used.

**A. S. Williams** (Twelfth Corps) wrote in December 1863 that Hancock relieved Howard "at 4 p. m."; he was not on the hill and gives no basis. The first pass and the dossier call this Slocum's; the heading of the print shows it is Williams's letter. **An unnamed writer** of the Philadelphia Evening Bulletin (1864, quoted by a magazine) has "about two p.m."; Hancock's own report has him leaving Taneytown, 13 miles away, at 1.10, and no named witness agrees.

## Weighing

A writing of the day outranks writings of months and years later. Two such writings agree: Howard's note (4 p.m.) and Hancock's own dispatch (an hour before 5.25). Howard's report and Doubleday say about 4.30. Hancock's 3 to 3:30 is carried by one man, in the report and in later recollection; it also sits badly with his own words that the corps were retiring through the town (Howard puts the order to fall back at 4.10) and with Walker's statement (1886) that the staff first saw Gettysburg "at half-past three o'clock". Hancock's own account of 1864 (quoted in his letter of 1878) puts his message to Meade by Major Mitchell "About 4 o'clock P. M.", which fits 3 to 3:30 better than 4.25; it is the same single line of knowledge and not a reading of a clock of the day. It is a counter-point to the default, not a reversal. Both hours given on the day are round expressions ("4 p. m.", "an hour since"), not clock readings. The two armies' clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes; that is not applied and is far smaller than the hour at issue.

## Decision

**Default: a window of about 16:00 to 16:30**, class weighted, tier documented. The ends are Howard's note (16:00) and Howard's report with Doubleday (about 16:30); Hancock's dispatch (about 16:25) lies inside. Hancock is drawn arriving at 16:00. The earlier values (15:00, 15:30, Hancock's later words) stay selectable and the earliest source-supported time stays 15:00; nothing earlier is drawn. The PDF default (15:00 to 16:00) and the first delegated default do not hold: their early end rests on Hancock's later writings alone. Eight values are listed in all; "before 16:00" is a bound no writer states.

## What would change it

A report, diary, letter or telegram of 1 or 2 July with a clock reading for Hancock's arrival (Captain Parker, Major Mitchell, Warren, Howard's staff, a Second Corps headquarters journal), or the original of the 5.25 dispatch showing a different hour.

## Searches that failed

No other hour for the arrival in the Official Records (Buford, Doubleday and Meade give none); no hour in the newspapers of 1863 read; monument addresses that give "between 3 and 4", "about 4.30" and "3.30" are by speakers not named in the passages read and were not cited; the volume of the Joint Committee that holds Hancock's testimony of 22 March 1864 is not local (his words are quoted in his letter of 1878).
