## Defects and exact fixes
1. **A hedge in the same source is omitted from the weighing and the md.** In the same 1878 letter (SHSP-05 p. 168-169, quoting his testimony of 1864) Hancock says: "About 4 o'clock P. M. I sent word by Maj. Mitchell, aide-de-camp, to General Meade, that I would hold the ground until dark". That is Hancock's own hour for a message sent after he had taken the field; it is hard to square with an arrival at 16:00 to 16:25 and tends to support his earlier arrival. The JSON carries it only in a citation note and in a failed-search note. Add to the md "Weighing" and to `weighing.how_knew`: "Hancock's own account of 1864 puts his message to Meade 'about 4 o'clock', which fits 3 to 3:30 better than 4.25; it is the same single line of knowledge and is not a reading of a clock of the day." Keep the default; this is a disclosed counter-point, not a reversal.
2. **Context citation flagged exact.** P1 includes "On the morning of July 1, the command marched to Taneytown, going into bivouac about 11 a. m." with `exact: true`; it does not state the value (15:00). Set `exact: false` and `note: "context: time of the march"`. Same for P4's second cite "At 3.30 a. m. July 1, orders were received" (Howard, p. 701), which is about the 11th Corps march, not Hancock.
3. P8's `written.kind` is `newspaper_1863` for an item of March 1864 (quoted from the Evening Bulletin in a magazine). Use `reference_table`-free class: set `kind: later_history` with `note: "newspaper text of 1864 quoted second-hand in a magazine"`. Tier stays inferred and flags stay.

## Advisories
Howard's "4 p.m." in his note is a round hour; the resolution already says so. The "sits badly with ... retiring through the town" argument rests on Howard's 4.10 order and is an argument, not a source; it is labelled as such in the md. Keep.
