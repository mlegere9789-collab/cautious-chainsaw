# C-032: when the fight at East Cavalry Field began (July 3)

**The dispute.** The plan lists 13:00 and 15:00 and recommends 13:00. The Union reports speak of noon and of 1 p.m.; Custer's report speaks of 10 a.m.; a Confederate and two Union participants say 2 p.m.; a later narrator says about 3 for the great charge. Several of these date different things.

**Who says what.**

- *About 14:00 (the default).* Green (Captain, Louisiana Guard Artillery, two guns with Hampton; report of 8 July 1863, Confederate): "The engagement commenced at 2 p. m. by the dismounted sharpshooters on both sides." (The first scan prints a letter for the digit; the second scan prints 2.) Rawle (Captain, Third Pennsylvania Cavalry, in McIntosh's brigade; pamphlet of 1878): McIntosh, having relieved Custer shortly before one, ordered a strong skirmish line of the First New Jersey forward "about two o'clock", which drew out the Confederate skirmishers from the Rummel barn. Storrs (Major, Sixth Michigan, a squadron in front of Custer; recollection of 1889 given by Kidd): the first shot was not fired "earlier than 2 o'clock". A history of 1900 says the same without a source.
- *13:00 (the plan's default).* McIntosh (brigade commander; report of 4 August 1863): he moved at 1 p.m. to relieve Custer and, hearing the enemy were coming, placed the First New Jersey, which was "immediately engaged". Rawle puts his arrival shortly before one. That is an arrival and a deployment; the first fire comes after it by every other account.
- *Noon (the first delegated default).* Pleasonton (corps commander, at headquarters; 31 August 1863): "About noon the enemy threw a heavy force of cavalry against this position." Gregg (division commander, on the field; 25 July 1863): "At 12 m." he received the Eleventh Corps dispatch that large columns of cavalry were moving toward the right; the enemy's skirmishers then showed themselves and the attack followed. Noon is the warning.
- *10:00.* Custer (Michigan Brigade; his report as quoted by Kidd in 1889): "Everything remained quiet until 10 A. M., when the enemy appeared on my right flank and opened upon me with a battery of six guns"; the Fifth Michigan's entry in the same report: "At 10 a. m. moved out, and met the enemy on the right". Rawle and Kidd do not believe a fight so early; Kidd guessed that the report was misread from 1 o'clock, but the Fifth Michigan entry also says 10, so the guess is not proved.
- *15:00 (the plan's other value).* Rawle (1878, and his 1884 address citing his diary of 1863): the First Virginia fell back about three o'clock, as Stuart's last reserve appeared. That is the main mounted clash, an hour into the fight. Hampton (report of 13 August 1863) says an order to report to Stuart came "about 4.30 o'clock, I should think", while the sharpshooters were still engaged.

**Weighing.** The one report of the time that dates the start by the clock on the Confederate side is Green's, a participant writing five days after; it gives 2 p.m. exactly. Union participants of 1878 and 1889 agree on about two, with an hour explained: McIntosh was waiting, and noon is Gregg's warning. Custer's 10 a.m. is contradicted by his own squadron commander Storrs and by Green, and the others come after it by hours. Union and Confederate clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes; this is not applied and does not bridge 12:00 to 14:00.

**Decision.** Class weighted, tier inferred: about 14:00 for the start of the engagement. 10:00, noon, 13:00 and 15:00 stay selectable, each labelled for the event it dates: first contact on Custer's front, the warning, McIntosh's arrival, the mounted clash. The plan's 13:00 and the first delegated default (about noon) do not hold as the start.

**What would change it.** Fitz Lee's, Chambliss's, Ferguson's or W. H. F. Lee's reports of the fight (none is printed in the Official Records), Custer's report in full with its date, a page image of Green's report, or a Union signal message timed on the field.

**Searches that failed.** Stuart's report gives no clock reading for the fight; Kidd says Stuart claims to have left Gettysburg about noon, but that sentence was not found in Stuart's printed report. No local text states 15:00 as the start of the fight.
