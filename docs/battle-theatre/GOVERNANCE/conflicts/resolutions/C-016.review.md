# Independent review of C-016 (48th Alabama position)

Reviewer run of 2026-11-24. Read: PLAN.md, `resolutions/C-016.json` and `.md`, `dossiers/C-016.md`. Nothing outside the two review files and `tmp/conflicts/review-C-016/` was written.

## Verdict: DEFECTS (4, none touches the default)

The default (Little Round Top, western foot and slope, beside the 4th Texas), class weighted and tier inferred survive every attempt to refute them. The defects are in the weighing text, the citations and the list of sources used; they leave out evidence that mostly strengthens the default and they misstate one independence claim.

## 2 Defects

**D1 (moderate): the account relies on a quote that no citation carries.** `C-016.md` line 11 says that Oates, "fifteen years later (Southern Historical Society Papers, vol. 6, 1878, p. 174) names them, the 44th and 48th, and says Law sent them 'to the support of Robertson's Texas brigade'". `C-016.json` has no SHSP-06 citation (`grep -c SHSP-06` gives 0); only `P1-c2.note` mentions "Oates, 1878 and 1905", and `P1-c2` (Oates's report of 8 Aug 1863, p. 392) names no regiment, so the link from "the two regiments on my right" to the 44th and 48th rests on that uncited text. PLAN 11 item 12 asks that the quotes of the account agree with the JSON. The passage exists (`find_passage.py SHSP-06 --quote "General Law marched the Forty-fourth and Forty-eighth regiments by the left flank across my rear to the support of Robertson's Texas brigade"` finds offset 525804, p. 174) and belongs in `support[]` of P2 (and P1).

**D2 (moderate): `weighing.independence` and the 44th/48th contrast are wrong or incomplete; three non-lead sources in the local ledger are neither cited, weighed nor listed.**
- The weighing says "Norton and the Maine commission both work from Bane's report ... one contemporary link and three later readings of it" (also `.md`, "Independence"). The Maine volume cites Bane only once, in footnote (a) of p. 162, for the splitting of Robertson's brigade into two parts (`ME-GB-1898` offset 438945: "these skirmishers were in fact one of the causes of the separation of Robertson's brigade into two parts, (a)"; the footnote reads "(a) Official report of Major Bane, 4th Tex., Rebellion Records, serial no. 44, p. 401", OCR "Ofiicisl"). The sentence about the 48th (p. 163, "advancing northerly along the sloping side of Little Round Top, parallel with Plum Run ... a duel with the 40th New York") has no source note and has nothing in Bane's report (OR27-2 p. 411).
- Not cited, not weighed, not in `failed_searches`: (a) H. S. Melcher, 20th Maine, Battles and Leaders vol. 3 pp. 314-315 (condensed from the Lincoln County News, Waldoboro, 13 March 1885): Robertson "detached the 44th, 48th, and 4th Alabama from Law's brigade ... and connected them with Robertson's line, then well in front of Little Round Top" and Vincent's brigade was engaged "with Robertson's Texans and the three regiments of Law's brigade that had been assigned to his command, who were trying to get possession from the front" (`BL-3` offsets 1322095 and 1323385); (b) Alexander, Military Memoirs 1907 p. 396: "Law, on the right, had the 4th, 44th, and 48th Ala., and parts of the 4th and 5th Tex. Robertson, on the left, had only the 1st Tex. and 3d Ark." (`ALEXANDER-1907` offset 1043221); (c) the Park Service Slaughter Pen study (`NPS-SLAUGHTER-PEN`, PDF p. 32, printed 21, offset 58739): "the 48th Alabama, on Perry's right flank ... continuing its advance on the wooded slopes above the 44th Regiment and toward the open ground just east of Plum Run at the foot of Little Round Top", and its Appendix A (PDF p. 50, offset 93063) which lists the 44th and 48th Alabama among the Confederate regiments engaged in the Houck's Ridge / Slaughter Pen action.
- Effect: (a) and (b) are independent of Norton (1913) and of Maine (1898) and support the default; there are at least five later lines, not three. The same sources, and Bane's own sentence ("re-enforced by the Forty-eighth Alabama ... and the Forty-fourth Alabama ... we again charged") and Norton p. 172 and p. 179 ("a possibility that the Forty-fourth Alabama joined on the left of the Forty-eighth Alabama in the last assault on Little Round Top"), put the 44th beside the 48th on the Little Round Top side. `default.note` ("the 44th Alabama, not the 48th, is the regiment at the Devil's Den battery") and the panel text ("Devil's Den is where its sister regiment, the 44th, fought") state the split as settled; it is Perry's, Maine's and the Park Service's reading, contested by Bane, Melcher, Alexander and Norton's own caveat. The weighing never meets that evidence. The default is not weakened by it, but P1's reason (`different_event`: the 44th went to Devil's Den, the 48th did not) is only partly supported.

**D3 (minor): Norton's own hedge is dropped.** `P2-c4` (Norton pp. 171-172) is `hedge: "exact"`, `exact: true`. The quote starts after the lead-in, "from other indications I think I have been able to locate them correctly in stating that [the attack on the Devil's Den was made by ...] and that the attack on Little Round Top was made by ...", and the sentence ends "with a possibility that the Forty-fourth Alabama joined on the left of the Forty-eighth Alabama in the last assault on Little Round Top, as shown on the Government map". PLAN 5: a hedge keeps its tolerance, a quote is not stretched. `knew_by: computed` is right; `hedge` and `exact` are not.

**D4 (minor): P4 states an identification as fact.** `P4-c2` note and the P4 label say "the two companies of the 48th were A and H (Sheffield names them as the pickets)". Oates's footnote (p. 207) names only Company A (Lt. A. O. Dickson); Sheffield's "where Companies A and H were ordered on picket" belongs to the line of battle in the open field before the half-hour wait and the advance ("placed in line of battle in the open field, where Companies A and H were ordered on picket. After lying in line of battle a half hour, we were ordered forward"), and he later has pickets posted after night. That A and H are Oates's two skirmish companies is probable, not shown; the tier is inferred so the marking is right, the word "were" is not. The `place_of_observation` reason is also not the closest kind (`different_event` or `elsewhere`); not a defect on its own.

## 3 What I tried to refute and could not

- **Bane (OR27-2 p. 411) read whole.** It says the 4th Texas first met the enemy near "cliffs, upon which the enemy had a battery", was ordered by Robertson "to move by the right flank", drove the enemy "to the heights beyond, in our front and in close proximity to the mountain", was "enfiladed by the batteries on the left", and was then "re-enforced by the Forty-eighth Alabama, commanded by the gallant Colonel [James L.] Sheffield, and the Forty-fourth Alabama, whose commander I did not learn". The quotes `P2-c1` and `P2-c2` say what the resolution says (the 48th joined the 4th Texas; no place named).
- **Sheffield (pp. 395-396), Perry (p. 393), Oates (p. 392), Oates 1905 (pp. 207-208), Maine (pp. 162-165), Norton (pp. 18-19, 171-174, 179), NPS (pp. 31-32, 40, 43)** all read in context. Every quote is real, on the page given, about the unit and day claimed, and the `[...]` fragments do not join unrelated sentences. Norton's reading of Bane's "heights" as Vincent's line (p. 179) and "They must have gone north in the Plum Run valley" (p. 173, with "May it not be possible", "If the above explanation is not correct, a better one will be gladly received") are correctly described as reasoning, not record (`knew_by: computed`, note on p. 172).
- **No source of any date puts the 48th on the Houck's Ridge / Devil's Den crest.** The nearest are Law's order (Maine p. 163: attack "the battery in reverse", halting "about two hundred yards from Devil's Den on its easterly approach", then the 48th moving north along the slope of Little Round Top), Oates 1905 ("the Forty-eighth continued as a reserve or second line"), the Park Service appendix (Slaughter Pen action, D2) and a reprinted Texas narrative in Polley (1910, offset 432767: "a little cove called the 'Devil's Den,' which was covered by the Fourth and Fifth Texas, Law's Alabama and Anderson's Georgia brigades"), a confused account that never names the 48th. The P1/P3 positions keep all of them selectable.
- **Class and tier.** No report of the time names a place (Norton p. 171), the place name comes from later writers, so inferred is right; settled is excluded, irreducible is not needed because no source puts the 48th on the other side of Plum Run. Weighted stands.
- **Reasons, flags, groups.** Group labels are honest (the 44th/48th pair in P1 and P3 rests on different writers); no flag is needed (P2 has five groups). P1-P4 reasons are true. `holds` is right (PDF default holds; first delegated default holds as a place, not as a gap).
- **Source panel (333 characters)** is accurate to the record, plain, says nothing of how the site is made; "Three other values stay listed" matches P1, P3, P4.
- **Readers.** T2-139 (`must_change` true, row text puts the 44th and 48th together toward Devil's Den with "Wikipedia"; verified record says "48th Alabama target conflict ... not resolved by OR") and T2-166 ("West slope, Little Round Top ... 48th Alabama and 4th Texas", agrees) are the only timeline rows (normalised grep of `timeline_seed`, `timeline_verified`, `timeline_windows`). No `arrivals.json` row carries C-016; `tools/bt/gates` has no C-016 or 48th Alabama value; `app/data/conflict-defaults.json` and `decisions/conflict_defaults.json` are listed. Other `C-016` string hits in the app data are ids like `POS-A-11C-016`. Complete.
- **Animation, numbers.** Not applicable (a place). Strength 275 and loss 102 (Sheffield) match the report; the Park Service appendix prints 374 engaged beside the same 102 casualties, a difference the resolution does not use.
