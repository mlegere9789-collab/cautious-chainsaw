# C-047: strength and casualty totals

## The dispute
The plan's table B.1 and its notes give several totals for the strength and the losses of each army and fix none: Union 93,500 to 104,256, 93,921 engaged, 91,699 present equipped, casualties 23,049 to 23,055 or 22,822; Confederate 65,000 to 75,000, 71,699 engaged, 68,612 present equipped, casualties 22,709, 23,231 or 28,063. Its rule is a range with a declared basis. The sources count different things (aggregate, present for duty, effectives, engaged; returned, compiled, estimated) and are kept apart here, army by army. The plan's web figures have no source in the record.

## Union
- **Strength.** Meade's report (1 Oct 1863, p. 114): his army had "an aggregate of a little over 100,000" on the latest return before 28 June; an aggregate counts every man on the roll. Livermore (1900) computes 83,289 effectives engaged from the returns of 30 June (87,312 infantry and artillery, less 17 regiments, plus 12,369 cavalry, at 93 and 85 per cent). The Comte de Paris (1877) deducts stragglers and cavalry: about 75,000, or 81,000 without the deduction. Taylor put the army at 105,000 effective (1877, no return named). Meade's council memorandum of 2 July gives "total, 58,000" for seven corps after two days' loss. The Park Service (2010) gives 93,693 effectives, no source.
- **Casualties.** The revised return of the Official Records: 3,155 killed, 14,529 wounded, 5,365 missing, **23,049** (repeated by Livermore and Alexander). Meade's first return of 1863 said 23,186 (2,834, 13,709, 6,643); the volume itself says "but see revised statement". The plan's 23,055 and 22,822, and the Park Service's 22,807, have no return behind them.

## Confederate
- **Strength.** Taylor, Lee's adjutant, copied the return of 31 May (68,352 effectives) and reasoned to **62,000** at Gettysburg with the cavalry (1877); Early read the same return and wrote "under 60,000" (he leaves out two cavalry brigades left in the passes); Taylor later wrote 67,000 including all the cavalry (1906). Allan (1877) recalls Lee's own statement of 60,000 effective before the march, and Taylor's 61,000 and Venable's 55,000 sent to Lee in 1865. Oates relays Longstreet's 75,000. Livermore computes 75,992 effectives (returns of 31 May) or 77,707 engaged. The Comte de Paris: about 54,000 net of stragglers and cavalry.
- **Casualties.** The Official Records' return, **20,451** (2,592 killed, 12,709 wounded, 5,150 missing), compiled in 1889 from commanders' reports and called "approximative" by the editors; it leaves out the slightly wounded (Lee's order of 14 May) and has no return for one brigade. Guild's own table is 14,278, killed and wounded only. The Comte de Paris gives 22,728; the Park Service 22,557; Livermore's estimate, **28,063**, adds to the return the 6,026 wounded the Union army captured above the 770 reported, 275 more prisoners, and 1,311 killed assumed from a ratio. Busey and Martin's 23,231 is quoted by the plan; the book is not in the library.

## Weighing
For both armies the best witnesses disagree and what each counts differs; the Confederate returns are known to be short and every Confederate strength is a reading or an estimate. A return of the time outranks a table compiled later, a table a history, a history a memoir, but the returns of 31 May and 30 June are not in the volumes in a readable form, and the 1877 writers who copied them disagree by a few thousand. Each end rests on one line of knowledge.

## Decision
Class **irreducible**, tier **inferred** (the strengths and the larger casualty total are computed estimates). Default: the claims **side by side** with what each counts, no single figure: Union 83,289 effectives to a little over 100,000 aggregate, 23,049 lost; Confederate 62,000 to 75,992 effectives, 20,451 to 28,063 lost. The plan's rule holds; the first pass's basis does not (the Union return is read, 2,592 is the editors', and the strength figures exist in Taylor, Early and Livermore).

## DEC-008 consequence (reported, not edited)
The soldiers follow printed unit strengths (about 160,000 equipped in the plan's tables). That total lies inside the sum of the ranges above (about 145,000 to 176,000), but no source here confirms a printed unit figure, and the Confederate printed total (68,612) is above Taylor's 62,000 and Early's under 60,000. No count is changed.
