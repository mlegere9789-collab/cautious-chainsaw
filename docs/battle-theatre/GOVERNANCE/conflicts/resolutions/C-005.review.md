A negative that the lookup log cannot support is shown to the visitor (Law 4: a failed lookup is recorded as failed). Change the panel, `holds` and `.md` to "no source found".

**D2 (moderate): the gate-floor change and the animation rule are not licensed by the evidence they cite.**
- `readers[Q04]` and `readers[cs-1c-hood]` move the Q04 floor of Hood's division from 05:30 to 05:00, and `owner_note` justifies it: "which a diary of the day documents for Hood himself". PLAN 8: "A floor moves only to an earlier time that the record documents". Gate Q04 governs the entities of the unit (the soldiers). The resolution itself says of Fremantle "It places Hood, the man, on the ridge at 5 a.m.; it says nothing of the division" (`.md`, item 1), and its tier for 05:00 is inferred, not documented.
- The diary line does not even state 05:00 for Hood. `P5-c1`: "Colonel Sorrell, the Austrian, and I arrived at 5 A. M. at the same commanding position ... and I climbed up a tree ... Just below us were seated Generals Lee, Hill, Longstreet, and Hood" (Fremantle p. 257). 05:00 is the arrival of Fremantle's party; Hood is seen afterwards. The plan reads the same line as "Fremantle after 05:00" (`plan-clean.txt` lines 6433-6436, row T2-058) and Freeman as "found Lee and Longstreet in conference after 5 a.m." (appendix, offset 1263308).
- `default.animation.rule` is `earliest_documented`; its own note says 05:00 is "the earliest time a writer of the day places Hood". For the head of the division, nothing is documented; the accurate rule word is `best_supported`. Either the floor stays 05:30 (and 05:00 is the instant for Hood himself and the head, tier inferred) or the move is put to the owner as a decision; it cannot be reconciled as "documented".

**D3 (minor): P4's reason is not shown.** `P4.reason_not_chosen` is `later_memory` ("Written 12 and 33 years later ... the memoirs probably date the generals, not the column"). `later_memory` means "outweighed by the record written at the time", and no record of the time contradicts the memoir about the troops. Hood's letter (p. 56) speaks of his troops: "My troops, together with McLaws's Division, were put in motion ... which, after a hard march, we reached before or at sunrise", and "My division soon commenced filing into an open field near me". Fremantle (Hood seen after 5) and Shepherd (3 a.m., about 3 miles) are consistent with an arrival at sunrise, and the resolution's own arithmetic (`P5.inference`: "the head could be up from about 04:10") puts it inside. The Kershaw report named in the reason is about McLaws's division, and "Freeman finds that Hood rode ahead" is a historian's inference (rank 1, computed) that Freeman states beside "Hood and McLaws were trustworthy in their statements". The default's own label and tolerance ("sunrise to 05:30", 05:00 plus or minus 30) contain 04:35, so `within_range` or `tolerance` is the closer kind, and an `outweighed` comparison is not written in `weighing`.

**D4 (minor): an independent eyewitness for the closing-up bound is missed.** SHSP vol. 4 p. 114, "Letter from General C. M. Wilcox", Baltimore, March 1877 ("such facts as I have personal knowledge of"): "Wilcox's brigade was recalled from picket duty, leaving its post after sunrise, moved back to the Chambersburg pike, then on it towards Gettysburg for about a mile, bore off then to the right, passed through troops whose arms were stacked, was informed they were McLaws' and Hood's divisions"; the sharp fight in the woods that follows is dated "about 9 A. M." (`SHSP-04` offset 286790). His report of 17 July 1863 (OR27-2-R537, p. 617, offsets 2290425 and 2293483) gives "At 7 a. m. the following morning, the brigade rejoined the division" and "The brigade now (9 a. m.) took its position in line of battle". Both divisions were therefore resting with stacked arms between about 7 and 9 a.m., observed by a brigade commander of another corps. It does not contradict the default; it bears on P3 (`single_source`, Freeman only) and P6 (`memoir_only`), and it is absent from `failed_searches` (the resolution's own search was for 5.30, 6.30, 7.30 beside "Hood"). Also uncited: Fremantle p. 257, "At 7 A. M. I rode over part of the ground with General Longstreet, and saw him disposing of M'Laws's division", a diary clock for McLaws's division that helps the reason for P6.

## 3 Test of the default (item 5)

What states a time, by writer:

| writer | what is stated | about |
|---|---|---|
| Fremantle, diary of the day | arrived 5 A. M.; Hood seen with Lee, Hill, Longstreet afterwards | Hood the man |

| Freeman 1935 | head "by 5:30" (computed on 5 miles); whole "by 7 or 7:30"; Hood and McLaws "rode ahead of their troops" | head / tail, computed |
| Polley 1910 | Texas Brigade "not later than an hour after sunrise" | head, upper bound |
| Longstreet letter 1875, Meade 1913, Wilcox 1877/1863 | 8 a.m. halted; arms stacked before about 9 | closing up |

No source of the time states an hour for the arrival of the division. The default 05:00 for "the head" is an inference from one clock reading for the general, a 3 a.m. start with 3 miles, two memoirs and two upper bounds; every source about the head lies between 04:10 (the pace estimate) and 05:35 (Polley), overlapping the window 04:30-05:30, and every later time (07:00-08:00) is about the tail or the closing up (Freeman's own summary: "one division, less one brigade, in rear of Seminary Ridge by 7 or 7:30"). The record is not divided on the head, so weighted/inferred stands and irreducible is not required. A `window` default (about 04:35 to 05:30 for the head, tail by 07:30) would carry the same facts; the label already shows them, so I do not call `position` a defect. The only thing the label should not do is claim a documented instant for the soldiers (D2).

06:30 is fairly weighed: it is the plan's midpoint ("Freeman computes the head about 05:30 and the whole division 07:00-07:30", T2-064), no source found, EPP unread (D1 is about the words, not the rank). 07:30 is fairly weighed as the tail; it is also the figure of Freeman's summary, and D4 gives it an observed bound.
