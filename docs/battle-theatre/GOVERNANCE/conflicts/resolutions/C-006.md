# C-006: Longstreet at Lee's headquarters

Researched 2026-11-24. Result: by about 05:00 on 2 July 1863 Longstreet was with Lee on Seminary Ridge. The hour he came is earlier and unrecorded. Evidence weighed, not settled; the value is inferred, not documented.

## The dispute

The plan gives three values for when Longstreet reported to Lee on the morning of 2 July: 05:15, 08:00 and "stars bright" (pre-dawn). It takes 08:00 as its default. The first pass over the conflict kept the pre-dawn "stars" from Longstreet's book of 1896 and said that 05:15 and 08:00 had no source. Both do have one, and the man behind two of the three values is Longstreet himself.

## What each writer says

1. **Fremantle**, a British officer riding with Longstreet's staff. Diary of Thursday 2 July 1863 (printed 1863, American edition 1864, p. 257): he and a companion "arrived at 5 A.M. at the same commanding position we were on yesterday", and "Just below us were seated Generals Lee, Hill, Longstreet, and Hood, in consultation". He saw it and wrote it that day. 5 a.m. is when he arrived; the generals are seen after he climbed a tree. It is the only writing of the day, and it gives an upper bound: Longstreet with Lee by about 5.
2. **Longstreet, letter of April 1875** (printed by Walter Taylor, 1906, p. 198; Taylor dates it 20 April, Freeman 25 April): "nor myself did not reach General Lee until 8 a.m. on the 2d". Written in the quarrel over whether Lee ordered an attack at sunrise, twelve years on. The sentence speaks of his two divisions and himself together.
3. **Longstreet, article of 3 November 1877** (Annals of the War, 1879, p. 422): "I went to General Lee's headquarters at daylight". No hour.
4. **Longstreet, book of 1896** (p. 362): "The stars were shining brightly on the morning of the 2d when I reported at General Lee's head-quarters". No hour, 33 years on.
5. **Hood**, letter to Longstreet of 28 June 1875 (1880, p. 56): he arrived in front of the heights "shortly after daybreak"; early that morning he and Longstreet were with Lee and Hill. **Cullen**, Longstreet's medical director, letter of 18 May 1875 (printed in Longstreet's article, SHSP vol. 5, p. 78): Longstreet left camp "About 3 o'clock in the morning, while the stars were shining"; "I found you sitting with him after sunrise". The stars are those of the departure, not of the arrival. **Alexander** (1907, p. 389): Longstreet "met Lee upon Seminary Ridge about dawn". He does not say how he knew.
6. **McLaws**, address of 1879 (SHSP vol. 7, p. 68): when his division's head reached the hill "early in the morning" and he reported to Lee, Longstreet was already there, "walking back and forth some little distance from General Lee". **Ross**, an Austrian officer in Fremantle's party (1865, p. 49): woke at dusk, breakfasted with Longstreet's staff and rode "some five miles"; Lee was at the front. He gives no hour and does not name Longstreet on the hill.
7. **Freeman** (1935, appendix, "The Hour of Longstreet's Arrival"): calls the stars and the 8 a.m. "wide of the mark"; with the sun rising at 4:32, "it probably is approximately correct to say that Longstreet joined Lee on Seminary Ridge at 5:15 or about that time". A computation, not a sighting.

## The weighing

Only the diary is a writing of the time, and by the owner's rule it outranks the recollections. It contradicts 8 a.m. (Longstreet with Lee at 5), as do McLaws and Longstreet's own two later accounts. Taylor, who was on Lee's staff, endorses the 8 a.m. letter, but names no hour and no evidence of his own, so he repeats Longstreet. The 8 a.m. may date the closing up of the two divisions, which is a different event (see C-005 and C-007). Longstreet's three statements are one witness three times, with three different answers.

"Daylight", "shortly after daybreak", "after sunrise" and "about dawn" put him with Lee between first light and soon after sunrise (about 04:35), which the diary's 5 a.m. contains. Freeman's 5:15 is the same moment, estimated. "Stars" (before about 04:20) cannot be ruled out: a ride of several miles from a 3 a.m. start could end in the dark. But one recollection stands alone for it, and Cullen found Longstreet with Lee only after sunrise. The clocks of the two armies differ by about 20 minutes; that is not applied and does not touch the conflict with 08:00.

## Decision

Default: **by about 05:00 (first light to 05:15): Longstreet with Lee on Seminary Ridge.** Class weighted, tier inferred (no writer of the day gives the hour of the arrival; the diary gives the latest moment). The animation instant is 05:00, the moment by which he is certainly there; the sun is up, so the scene is in dawn light. No gate floor is moved. 05:15, 08:00 (which is also the plan's default), "stars" and the plain "daylight" reading all stay selectable.

The plan's default 08:00 does not hold as the hour Longstreet reached Lee; it is a real statement of Longstreet's, but a diary of the day contradicts it. The first delegated default ("stars bright", one memoir) does not hold as the default either; its tier (inferred) is kept.

## What would change it

A letter or diary of 2 July 1863 or the weeks after that gives the hour Longstreet reached Lee; the manuscript of Longstreet's April 1875 letter, to see what its 8 a.m. refers to; or a source that places him with Lee before sunrise, which would make "stars bright" the better-supported value.

## Searches that found nothing

No report in the Official Records (Lee's, Longstreet's and the commanders') gives the hour; nor do the Park Service texts, Sorrel, Long or Taylor's book of 1877. Longstreet's sentence in Battles and Leaders ("On the morning of the 2d I joined General Lee") gives none. Staff letters of 1863 and the Taylor manuscript were not read. A web page that annotates Freeman, noted by the first pass, was not reached and nothing is claimed from it. Newspapers of 1863 were not searched.
