# C-023: Stuart's arrival

Researched 2026-11-24. Result: Stuart reached Gettysburg on the afternoon of 2 July 1863; no record gives a clock reading. The value is documented as a part of the day only. The plan's "early afternoon for Stuart, evening for the brigades" mixes three different events.

## The dispute

The plan lists four values for when Stuart arrived: noon to 13:00, mid-afternoon, afternoon, evening. Its default is "Stuart himself early afternoon, brigades in the evening". The first delegated pass found the date in the Official Records, no hour, and kept the plan's pair as a marked stand-in. The question here: who says what, and are the four values about the same moment?

## What each writer says

1. **Stuart**, report of 20 August 1863 (Official Records 27/2, p. 697). He left Carlisle in the night of 1 July: "My advance reached Gettysburg July 2, just in time to thwart a move of the enemy's cavalry upon our rear by way of Hunterstown". He gives the day, no hour. "My advance" was Hampton's brigade. On 3 July his command "was increased by the addition of Jenkins' brigade" (p. 697), whose commander had been "wounded the day previous, before reporting to me" (p. 698).
2. **Lee**, report of 20 January 1864 (27/2, p. 322): Stuart "reached Carlisle on July 1 ... He arrived in the afternoon of the following day, and took position on General Ewell's left." Lee commanded the army; he does not say how he fixed the hour. His shorter report of 31 July 1863 gives no hour. Lee also writes that Jenkins's cavalry "accompanied General Ewell" (p. 306 of the same volume).
3. **Hampton**, report of 13 August 1863 (p. 724): the brigade "was stationed on July 2, at Hunterstown, 5 miles to the east of Gettysburg, when orders came from General Stuart" to move up to the left. No hour.
4. **Kilpatrick** (Union), report of 10 August 1863 (27/1, p. 992): he reached the field at 2 p. m. and "Was attacked by Stuart, Hampton, and Lee at sundown near Hunterstown". **Green** (Confederate gunner), report of 8 July 1863 (27/2, p. 497): ordered to Hampton at Hunterstown "just before sunset", arriving at dusk. Sunset was 19:32. Both date the fight, not Stuart's arrival; Hampton was there before it.
5. **McClellan**, Stuart's chief of staff, 1885 (pp. 331-332): Hampton "reached Hunterstown on the morning of the 2d of July"; "Stuart himself, with Fitz Lee's and Chambliss' commands, reached Gettysburg on the afternoon of the 2d". His words about taking position follow Lee's report, so the two count as one line. The volume on Meade (1913) also has Stuart with Fitz Lee's and Chambliss's brigades on the left "toward the afternoon of July 2", from no named source.
6. **Alexander**, Longstreet's artillery chief, 1907 (p. 376): Stuart "reported in person, late in the afternoon of the 2d"; Lee said "Well, General, you are here at last". He was on the field but not at Lee's side.
7. **Taylor**, Lee's adjutant, 1877 (p. 113): Stuart "only joined General Lee on the evening of the second day". It stands in a sentence on the strength of the armies. The same sentence says Jenkins's and Imboden's commands "accompanied General Ewell". **Kidd**, a Union cavalryman, 1889: Stuart "finally reached the main army" on "the evening of July 2", and Hampton came to Hunterstown "late in the afternoon". Neither says how he knew.
8. **Freeman**, 1935: Stuart "had arrived on the left, with two of his brigades, before sunset", with a footnote; an upper bound only.

## The weighing

The best class of writing is the reports of 1863 and Lee's of 1864. None has a clock reading. Lee, the only one to give a period, says afternoon. Noon to 13:00 and mid-afternoon (web timelines) are in no source found; early afternoon is in none either, and Alexander has Stuart reporting late in the afternoon. The evening of Taylor and Kidd is told 14 and 26 years later without a clock, and Taylor's "joined General Lee" may mean the call on Lee, which can come hours after Stuart took position on the left. The two fit together only if Stuart reached the left in the afternoon and reported late; no record says so. Clocks do not matter: no one gives a reading, and the 20-minute difference between the armies' clocks is not applied.

The plan's pair mixes events. Hampton's brigade was at Hunterstown before the fight at sundown (morning, says McClellan; late afternoon, says Kidd; no report gives an hour). Fitz Lee and Chambliss came with Stuart in the afternoon (McClellan, Meade; one line with Lee). Jenkins had been with Ewell since the Potomac and came under Stuart on 3 July, so he did not arrive on 2 July with Stuart.

## The decision

**Class: weighted. Tier: documented (the part of the day).** Default: **afternoon of 2 July; no hour is recorded.** Lee's report carries the period and Stuart's the day. Lee, McClellan and Meade are one line; Alexander and Freeman are separate, so the afternoon has three lines against two later memoirs for the evening. Every other value stays selectable with its reason: noon to 13:00 and mid-afternoon (no source), evening (outweighed; may date the call on Lee), the plan's pair (unsourced; early afternoon unsupported), late afternoon (Alexander; inside the afternoon), the Hunterstown fight at sundown (a different event), Hampton at Hunterstown in the morning or late afternoon (two memoirs, side by side), and Jenkins (a different event). No instant is chosen for the animation.

**The plan's default holds partly.** Afternoon holds for Stuart himself; "early" has no source. Evening holds only as the hour of Hampton's fight. **The first delegated default holds partly:** the stand-in is replaced by a documented afternoon.

**The 19:32 gate (Q04).** The result bears on it. The arrivals gate has Stuart's brigades, Hampton's, Fitz Lee's and Chambliss's, and Jenkins's, not before 19:32. The record puts Hampton at Hunterstown before 19:32 (Hampton, Green), Fitz Lee and Chambliss with Stuart in the afternoon (Lee, McClellan; only Taylor and Kidd say evening), and Jenkins on the field already. The gate was not changed; the owner decides whether the floors move.

## What would change it

A report, letter or diary of 2-3 July 1863 or soon after, by Lee or his staff, Stuart or his staff, Fitz Lee, or Chambliss, giving a clock reading for Stuart's arrival on the left or his call on Lee. A reading before 15:00 would make early afternoon the value; one at dusk would favour Taylor's evening.

## Failed searches

No clock reading in the Official Records (Lee, Stuart, Hampton, Early, Hoffman, Green, Kilpatrick; parts 1 to 3). No Gettysburg report of Fitz Lee, Chambliss or Jenkins is printed in part 2. No source in the local library gives noon, 1 p.m., early afternoon or mid-afternoon for Stuart. Long (1886), Taylor (1906), Sorrel and the Weekly Times annals do not tell the hour. The web pages the plan cites were not read: they are leads. The page images of Taylor (1877) and Kidd (1889) could not be fetched; their printed pages (113 and 140) are read from the running heads of the text.
