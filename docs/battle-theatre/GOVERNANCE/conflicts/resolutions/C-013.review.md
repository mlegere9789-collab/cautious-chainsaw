## 2 Defects

**D1 (moderate): irreducible is not the class the rubric gives.** PLAN.md (how to choose, step 6): "if the best-classed witnesses disagree with each other and nothing in section 5 separates them, the conflict is irreducible"; step 7: "if no source states the point, it is a gap". No two witnesses of one class give different hours: one end of the window (17:30) is the researcher's computation, the other (18:30) a single unsourced reading of 1898 (flagged `single_source`, `weak`). That is a gap with a bounded inference (bounds from the reports) or, with the 1898 hour as the only stated reading, weighted; "the record itself is divided" (rubric) is not shown. Reclassify (gap or weighted) or show two witnesses of the same rank in conflict. The window can stay as the mode of a weighted/gap default only if the class matrix allows it; otherwise it is owner's choice.

**D2 (moderate): the Maine volume's 18:30 is given the late end of the window on no better ground than being the only reading; a Confederate eyewitness of the charge is passed over as "no hour".** `.md` "Searches that failed" and `failed_searches[...]`: "Oates 1878 and 1905 ... no hour for the charge ... the account of 1878 has 'less than ten minutes' and 'five minutes' of delays and no clock". SHSP-06 (Oates, "The Battle on the Right", 1878; offset 538055, printed pp. 178-179), on his regiment's retreat from the 20th Maine's front after "Federals closing in on our rear": "On reflection ... I did order a retreat ... we ran like a herd of wild cattle right through the line of dismounted cavalrymen ... **This was just about sunset**, and the fighting all along our line had pretty well ceased"; and "When the battle commenced, four hours previously ... " (the roll call that night). Oates was the officer opposite Chamberlain. "Just about sunset" (19:32 by the resolution's own figure) is a clock statement, hedged, by a participant, 15 years later, and it contradicts the window's upper end 18:30 (and, with "four hours" from about 15:30, points to about 19:00). It must be a position (or at least weighed against the 1898 volume), and the claim "Oates 1878 ... no clock" is wrong.

**D3 (minor): P4's label overstates what Norton says.** P4 label "a clock its writer disowned": Norton (p. 246) says "Captain Woodward is mistaken about the time of the engagement ... The attack ... must have commenced ... between 4:30 and 5", and that Woodward "admits his mistake in regard to the time"; this concerns the start (Woodward's 2.30 and 3.15), not "nearly 6 p. m.". The note admits "the 'nearly 6 p.m.' is on the same clock" as an inference. If Woodward's start was about 1.5 hours early, "nearly 6" could be later than 18:00; this is not said. Say "his starting hours were corrected; 'nearly 6' stands uncorrected by Norton".

**D4 (minor): the lower bound 17:30 uses Chamberlain's "hour" and ignores Rice's shorter figure.** P1 inference: "16:30 plus about an hour is 17:30: the earliest". Rice (p. 617, same fight) has "above half an hour" and Conner "about one hour" for the 44th; with the attack on the brigade at 16:30 (Norton, between 4:30 and 5) and Rice's half hour the earliest is about 17:00-17:15. The "earliest 17:30" needs both figures or the lower one. The text acknowledges both lengths but fixes the end on the larger.
