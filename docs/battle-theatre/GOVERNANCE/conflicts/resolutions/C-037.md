# C-037: Barksdale's death

Researched 2026-11-24. Class: weighted. Tier: inferred. Default: wounded the evening of Jul 2; died early on Jul 3, dead by about 08:00; the hour is unknown.

## The dispute

The plan lists "Jul 2" and "Jul 3" for the death of Brigadier General William Barksdale (Mississippi brigade, McLaws's division) and recommends "Jul 2 (wounded), death Jul 2-3 shown as a range". The first delegated pass marked the death date an open gap. The question has two parts: when he was wounded, and when he died. They are kept apart.

## The wound (not in dispute)

- Longstreet, commanding Barksdale's corps, report of 27 Jul 1863 (Official Records 27/2, p. 362): Barksdale "was mortally wounded in the attack on the evening of the 2d". He wrote from his own corps; he relays rather than describes the fall.
- Col. Norman Hall, whose brigade held the line there, report of 17 Jul 1863 (27/1, p. 436): Barksdale was "mortally wounded" within 20 yards of the Seventh Michigan. No day in the sentence, but the report is of the fighting of the 2d.
- McLaws (address of 1878) counts him "killed" among the losses of the night of the 2d; Michael Jacobs (1864) has him fall on the 2d. Four independent groups: the wound is documented.

## The death

- Meade, dispatch of Jul 3, 8 a.m. (27/1, p. 74): "Barksdale's (Mississippi) dead body is within our lines." He relays what was reported to him (the dispatch opens "Prisoners report"); he gives no hour.
- A New York Times special, Gettysburg, Jul 3, entry at 7:45 a.m., printed in the Worcester Daily Spy of 4 Jul: Barksdale "wounded yesterday, is lying dead within our lines". Unnamed correspondent; no hour.
- Doubleday, report of 14 Dec 1863 (27/1, p. 260): among the events "which occurred on the third day was the death of the rebel General Barksdale"; his staff officer Lt. Col. Livingston brought him in. Doubleday relays; he does not say he was present.
- Later books: the 149th Pennsylvania (1914) says "the next day he died"; the Vermont volume (1914) says word came "during the night", eight men brought him in, and his body lay in the rear "during the forenoon"; the New York volume (1900) says "died that night". The Minnesota history (1916) says "dying on the third" and copies Doubleday.
- Not found: any account that says he died on Jul 2, before midnight, or that gives an hour. The Confederate Military History (Mississippi, 1899) says he "fell mortally wounded" and gives no date of death.

## Weighing

The two earliest writings (Meade, the Times) are hours old and agree that he was dead by about 08:00 on Jul 3. Doubleday, a report written within a year, names the third day. These three groups beat the later books, which say "that night" or "the next day" and do not conflict with them. Nothing supports a death on Jul 2 before midnight, and all the death evidence is relayed, none from the bedside. The Union and Confederate clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes; this is not applied and does not matter for a day-level date.

## Decision

Weighted, tier inferred. The default is the wound on the evening of Jul 2 and a death early on Jul 3 (before about 08:00); no hour is shown. It is inferred because the calendar day rests on Doubleday's day name joined to the morning dispatches. All values stay selectable: Jul 2 (no source found for the death; the plan's own source page was not read), the plan's range Jul 2-3, the wound on Jul 2, and a window from the evening of Jul 2 to about 08:00 on Jul 3 that leaves the day open.

The plan's default holds in part: the wound is right; the range is true but wider than the record. The first delegated "open gap" does not hold, because dated sources exist.

## What would change it

An account by Livingston, a surgeon at the Hummelbaugh house, a member of the Vermont detail that carried him in, or Barksdale's staff giving the hour; a Mississippi letter or regimental history of 1863 giving the day. A time before midnight would move the default to Jul 2.

## Searches that found nothing

Official Records (all three parts): no report gives a day or hour of death. Confederate Military History (Mississippi): none. Park Service Hummelbaugh record: "died at the house", no date. Not read: the plan's source pages (Stone Sentinels) for both values; Livingston's own account; the Mississippi regimental histories.
