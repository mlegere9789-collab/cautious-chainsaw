# C-037: Barksdale's death

Researched 2026-11-24. Class: irreducible. Tier: inferred. Default: wounded the evening of Jul 2; died between that night and about 08:00 on Jul 3, a window; the day and the hour are not settled.

## The dispute

The plan lists "Jul 2" and "Jul 3" for the death of Brigadier General William Barksdale (Mississippi brigade, McLaws's division) and recommends "Jul 2 (wounded), death Jul 2-3 shown as a range". The first delegated pass marked the death date an open gap. The question has two parts: when he was wounded, and when he died. They are kept apart.

## The wound (not in dispute)

- Longstreet, commanding Barksdale's corps, report of 27 Jul 1863 (Official Records 27/2, p. 362): Barksdale "was mortally wounded in the attack on the evening of the 2d". He wrote from his own corps; he relays rather than describes the fall.
- Col. Norman Hall, whose brigade held the line there, report of 17 Jul 1863 (27/1, p. 436): Barksdale was "mortally wounded" within 20 yards of the Seventh Michigan. No day in the sentence, but the report is of the fighting of the 2d.
- McLaws (address of 1878) counts him "killed" among the losses of the night of the 2d; Michael Jacobs (1864) has him fall on the 2d. Four independent groups: the wound is documented.

## The death

- Butterfield, Meade's chief of staff, sworn testimony of 29 Mar 1864 (Joint Committee on the Conduct of the War, p. 434): "Before the battle was over (on the night of the 2d) it was reported that General Barksdale was on the field wounded, and desired to be brought in; but he died before we reached him. I sent for him, but he was dead when found." He sent for him himself, so he writes of his own errand, 270 days later. The scan of the book is damaged at some of these words; the text above is read from the clean words around them. He gives no hour and does not say whether midnight had passed.
- Meade, dispatch of Jul 3, 8 a.m. (27/1, p. 74): "Barksdale's (Mississippi) dead body is within our lines." The sentence before it begins "Prisoners report ..."; it is not clear whether prisoners reported the body or Meade states it himself. He gives no hour.
- A New York Times special, Gettysburg, Jul 3, entry at 7:45 a.m., printed in the Worcester Daily Spy of 4 Jul: Barksdale "wounded yesterday, is lying dead within our lines". Unnamed correspondent; no hour.
- Doubleday, report of 14 Dec 1863 (27/1, p. 260): among the events "which occurred on the third day was the death of the rebel General Barksdale"; his staff officer Lt. Col. Livingston brought him in. Doubleday relays; he does not say he was present.
- Doubleday again, in his book of 1882: Barksdale "soon after was brought into my lines and died like a brave man"; no day or hour.
- Later books: the 149th Pennsylvania (1914) says "the next day he died"; the Vermont volume (1914) says "during the night word was brought" that he lay mortally wounded in front of the 13th Vermont's line, eight men brought him in, and his body lay in the rear "during the forenoon"; the New York volume (1900) says "died that night". The Minnesota history (1916) writes "dying on the third" in its own words (it cites Hall's report, which has no such words) and quotes Doubleday for the last messages. Also read, with no day: Hancock (27/1, p. 371) "left on the field, mortally wounded"; a medical history "died in a Union hospital"; a New York address of 1902 "in the presence of a Union general".
- Not found: any account that gives an hour. The Confederate Military History (Mississippi, 1899) says he "fell mortally wounded" and gives no date of death.

## Weighing

The two earliest writings (Meade, the Times) are hours old and agree that he was dead by about 08:00 on Jul 3. Inside that bound the witnesses disagree. Butterfield, who sent for him, says he was dead when found on the night of the 2d. Doubleday, in a report written within a year, names the third day; the Vermont volume and the 149th Pennsylvania have him found alive and dying later, and Doubleday in 1882 has him brought in and dying "soon after". Doubleday relays what his staff officer did; Butterfield's passage is a recollection after 270 days and "the night of the 2d" does not clearly mean before midnight. Neither side outranks the other on rank, nearness and independence, and none gives an hour, so the record is divided and the death is shown as a window. All the death evidence is relayed or recalled, none from the bedside. The Union and Confederate clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes; this is not applied and does not matter for a date.

## Decision

Irreducible, tier inferred. The default is a window: wounded on the evening of Jul 2 and dead between that night and about 08:00 on Jul 3; no hour is shown. It is inferred because no source states the day and hour. Both single days stay selectable with their witnesses: Jul 2 (Butterfield, the night of the 2d, dead when found) and Jul 3 (Doubleday, the third day, with the Vermont and Pennsylvania accounts of finding him alive), together with the plan's range Jul 2-3 and the wound on Jul 2.

The plan's default holds: the wound is right and the range is true; the record does not narrow it to one day. The first delegated "open gap" does not hold, because sources that bear on the death exist.

## What would change it

An account by Livingston, a surgeon at the Hummelbaugh house, a member of the Vermont detail that carried him in, or Barksdale's staff giving the hour; a Mississippi letter or regimental history of 1863 giving the day. A time before midnight would move the window to the night of Jul 2; a time after would settle Jul 3. A look at the page image of Butterfield's testimony (it was not available here) would confirm the words.

## Searches that found nothing

Official Records (all three parts): no report gives an hour of death. Joint Committee testimony: only Butterfield's passage. Confederate Military History (Mississippi): none. Park Service Hummelbaugh record: "died at the house", no date. Not read: the plan's source pages (Stone Sentinels) for both values; Livingston's own account; the Mississippi regimental histories.
