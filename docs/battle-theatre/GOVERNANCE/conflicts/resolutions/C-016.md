# C-016: where the 48th Alabama fought on 2 July 1863

## The dispute

The plan gives two places for the 48th Alabama (Law's brigade, Hood's division): Devil's Den and Little Round Top, and recommends Little Round Top. Its own timeline sends "Law's 44th and 48th Alabama" toward Devil's Den (a web encyclopaedia) and later puts the 48th with the 4th Texas on the west slope of Little Round Top (a web page of the Stone Sentinels). Both are web pages; neither is a source of the time. The first draft of the default kept Little Round Top as a marked gap because the 48th's own report names neither place. This research reads every report of Law's brigade and of the Texas brigade beside it, the later accounts that name the ground, and three books fetched for the purpose.

## What each writer says

**Col. James L. Sheffield, 48th Alabama** (report of 7 Aug 1863, Official Records 27/2, pp. 395-396). He commanded the regiment in the fight and wrote 36 days later, on the Confederate side. He names no place. He advanced "a distance of 1 mile" over rugged ground to about 20 paces from the enemy; his left, from "their exposed position to a fire from the front and from the mountain on the right", fell back. Companies A and H "were ordered on picket"; 275 men were engaged and 102 lost.

**Col. William C. Oates, 15th Alabama, next in line** (report of 8 Aug 1863, p. 392): "the two regiments on my right were moved by the left flank across my rear". Fifteen years later (Southern Historical Society Papers, vol. 6, 1878, p. 174) he names them, the 44th and 48th, and says Law sent them "to the support of Robertson's Texas brigade". In his book of 1905 (pp. 207-208) the 44th "was directed to the left to attack the Devil's Den", the 48th "continued as a reserve or second line" and was then "ordered across the rear to the left early in the advance". A footnote (p. 207) says the two skirmish companies of the 48th went round the eastern side of Great Round Top and "were not in the battle of July 2d".

**Col. William F. Perry, 44th Alabama** (8 Aug 1863, p. 393). Law told him to take a battery standing on a rugged cliff; his regiment went to the extreme left of the brigade. He does not mention the 48th. The two regiments that went left therefore parted: the 44th to the Devil's Den battery.

**Maj. John P. Bane, 4th Texas** (9 July 1863, p. 411). He commanded the regiment next to the Alabamians and wrote a week after. After two failed assaults on "the heights", which lay "in close proximity to the mountain", he was "re-enforced by the Forty-eighth Alabama, commanded by the gallant Colonel [James L.] Sheffield, and the Forty-fourth Alabama", and "we again charged" the works. This is the only report of the time that says what the 48th joined, and the first draft had not used it. It names no place either.

**Later writers who name the ground.** The Maine commission (1898, p. 163) says Law sent the 44th and 48th to attack the battery in reverse; they halted about 200 yards from Devil's Den; the 44th met the 4th Maine, while "the 48th Alabama, advancing northerly along the sloping side of Little Round Top, parallel with Plum Run", fought the 40th New York. Oliver Norton (1913), a private of Vincent's brigade on Little Round Top, notes that the reports of 1863 name neither place because their writers did not know the names (p. 171). He puts the Devil's Den attack with the 1st Texas, 3rd Arkansas, Benning's four regiments and "Law's Forty-fourth Alabama", and the Little Round Top attack with the 4th and 5th Texas and the 4th, 15th, 47th and 48th Alabama (pp. 171-172, 174); he reads Bane's "heights" as Vincent's line (p. 179) and found no record of the order that moved the 48th (p. 172). The Park Service study of 1984 (Kathleen George, pp. 40 and 43) has the 6th New Jersey and the 40th New York meeting the 44th and 48th Alabama in the valley of Plum Run. Hood (1880) and Law ("The Struggle for Round Top", 1888) say nothing of the 48th.

## The weighing

The reports of the time agree on three things: the 48th was moved to the left behind the line (Oates), it joined the 4th Texas in a charge on the heights near the mountain (Bane), and its own left was hit from the front and from the mountain on its right (Sheffield). None names a place. The 44th is the regiment tied to the Devil's Den battery (Perry, Oates 1905, Norton, Maine). The writers who name the ground put the 48th on the Little Round Top side of Plum Run, with the 4th Texas, and the Maine text and the Park Service study keep it in the valley below the hill. No source of any date puts the 48th on the Devil's Den ridge itself. The pairing in the plan comes from the order to the two regiments, not from where the 48th ended.

Clocks do not matter: this is a place, and the 20-minute difference between the two armies' clocks is not used. Independence is limited: Norton and the Maine text both lean on Bane's report and the Park Service study cites the Maine volume, so there is one contemporary link and three later readings of it.

## Decision

Default: **Little Round Top, at its western foot and slope, beside the 4th Texas.** Class: weighted (the evidence favours it but the reports never name it). Tier: inferred (the name is reasoned from Bane's report and the later accounts; no report of the time uses it).

Kept selectable, with reasons: Devil's Den (where Law's order pointed and where the 44th fought, not where the 48th ended); the Plum Run valley at the foot of the hill (the same ground, seen from the valley floor); and Companies A and H as skirmishers round the eastern side of Great Round Top (two companies only, not in the fight).

The plan's recommended default holds. The first draft's place holds, but not its marking as a gap.

## What would change it

A report, letter or map of 1863-64 that puts the 48th on the Houck's Ridge ground itself: a report by Law if one is found, Perry's article "At Devil's Den", or the Bachelder troop-movement map of the evening; or a Union report that names the 48th in front of Ward's brigade rather than Vincent's.

## Searches that found nothing

Union reports of the Official Records (only the 15th, 16th and 47th Alabama are named); Law's report (none filed); Law's essay in Battles and Leaders; Hood's book; the Bachelder guides (the brigade only); the appendix on the 48th in Oates's book; one search of archive.org for Perry's article (nothing usable); the Bachelder maps (images, not read).
