# C-010. Sickles' advance to the Peach Orchard

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window of about 14:00 to 16:00 on 2 July 1863. Class: irreducible (the record itself is divided on the hour, so the default is a window, not one time). Tier: documented (both ends are stated exactly by reports of the time).

## The dispute

On 2 July 1863 General Sickles moved his Third Corps forward from the line General Meade had meant him to hold, along the ridge toward Round Top, to the Emmitsburg road and the Peach Orchard, half a mile or more ahead. The plan gives three hours: noon, 14:00 and 16:00, and recommends 14:00. The first pass added 15:00 and 15:30 from the Official Records. The witnesses disagree because "the advance" was a sequence of moves over about two hours, and each wrote down the hour he saw or ordered.

## What the witnesses say

**About 2 p.m.** Colonel Craig (105th Pennsylvania, Graham's brigade, which held the Peach Orchard), report of 11 July 1863: the regiment stayed in column until 2 p.m., then "moved forward with the brigade to a point near the brick house on the Emmitsburg road" (p. 500). General Hunt, report of 27 September: "About 2 p. m. General Sickles formed his corps in line", the right on the Emmitsburg road in a peach orchard; he says he reached the ground at that time (p. 234). Colonel Austin (72nd New York) was ordered to advance across an open field at 2 p.m.; Colonel de Trobriand has a line of battle formed about 2. Captain Randolph, the corps's chief of artillery, wrote on 2 September that "Between 1 and 2 p. m." Sickles told him he was about to change his line, throwing his right forward from Round Top to a peach orchard on the road (p. 581-582): the notice, not the move.

**About 3 p.m.** Meade's report of 1 October 1863: about 3 p.m. he rode out to the left and found Sickles "had advanced, or rather was in the act of advancing" half a mile or more in front of the Second Corps (pp. 115-116). General Hancock (report written by October 1863): about 3 p.m. "the Third Corps advanced from its position toward the Emmitsburg road" (p. 369). Birney, in sworn testimony of March 1864, had Clark's battery open at three o'clock, when Meade rode down with Sickles (p. 366).

**About 4 p.m.** General Gibbon, report of 7 August 1863, from the Second Corps beside the Third: "At 4 o'clock the Third Corps advanced, and, swinging round its left flank, took up a position along the Emmitsburg road" (p. 416). Captain Hazard (Second Corps artillery): about 4. General Humphreys, whose division formed the right of the Third Corps's line: "About 4 p. m., in compliance with General Sickles' orders, I moved my division forward, so that the first line ran along the Emmitsburg road" (p. 532). Craig has a second forward move of his regiment at 4 p.m., to the front line a little beyond the road.

**Noon.** Humphreys, in sworn testimony of 21 March 1864: "About 12 o'clock, or a little after, by direction of General Sickles, my division was formed some 400 or 500 yards in front of the position in which it had remained during the night", and then, "perhaps a little after 4 o'clock", orders to move up to the road (pp. 390-391). His report of 1863 has "shortly after midday" for the first and about 4 for the second. Colonels Brewster and Hugo of his division date the orders for that formation about 1 p.m. Noon is the first move of one division, short of the road.

**Two later accounts that do not fit.** Meade, sworn on 5 March 1864, says he arrived "a few minutes before 4 o'clock" and found Sickles already far in advance (p. 332); his report has about 3 for the same arrival. General Longstreet (1896) writes that the signal messages show the Third Corps in position at one o'clock; he prints the second message as 12.55, where the Official Records have 11.55 a.m., and both messages report skirmishing a mile west of Round Top, not the corps's advance. General Birney's report of 1863 has his guns opening "say at 3.30 p. m."; the opening of fire came after the move. Sickles gave no hour in his testimony of 26 February 1864 and wrote no report.

## The weighing

- All these writers were officers who were there; the best class of writing is the reports of 1863 and the sworn testimony of 1864. None outranks another by rank or date alone.
- Two witnesses describe two moves themselves: Craig (2 and 4) and Humphreys (noon and a little after 4). With Randolph's notice between 1 and 2 and Meade and Hancock seeing the corps in motion about 3, the evidence shows a movement that began about 14:00 on the left and in the Peach Orchard and closed up to the road about 16:00. No witness states the sequence as such, so it is an inference from several, not a statement.
- Hancock's and Gibbon's reports, written beside each other, differ by an hour (3 and 4). Meade's two accounts differ by about an hour (3 and a few minutes before 4). Birney's two differ by half an hour. The differences are the hour each saw or counted, and the record cannot be reduced to one.
- The reported 20-minute difference between the two armies' clocks is not applied; it bears on none of these Union reports.

## The decision

The default is a window of about 14:00 to 16:00, the advance to the Emmitsburg road and the Peach Orchard. The picture starts the move at 14:00 and has Humphreys's division close up to the road about 16:00; nothing is drawn on the road before 14:00. Noon stays selectable as the first, shorter move of Humphreys's division; 15:00, 15:30, 13:00 and 16:00 stay as values with their reasons. The plan's 14:00 holds as the lower bound only, not as the single hour. The first delegated default (14:00, window to 16:00) holds and is now supported by Craig, Austin, de Trobriand and Randolph.

## What would change it

An order, diary or log of 1863 that dates the order to advance (Sickles's staff, Meade's headquarters) or the movement of Graham's brigade and Humphreys's division by the clock would narrow the window. Evidence that Humphreys's noon move was already an advance to the road would move the lower bound.

## Searches that failed

Sickles's testimony gives no hour. No report by Sickles or Graham exists in the Official Records. The regimental reports of Graham's and Ward's brigades give no hour for the move except Craig and Austin. No writer of the time gives noon for the advance to the road itself. Not read: the Bachelder papers, Phifer, the Stone Sentinels page, Hessler and Sickles's letters of 1891.
