# C-019. Johnson's attack on Culp's Hill

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: about 19:00 on 2 July 1863. Class: weighted (the evidence favours one reading but does not settle it). Tier: documented (an official report of ten days after states it), flagged single-source.

## The dispute

On the evening of 2 July 1863 General Edward Johnson's Confederate division (the brigades of Steuart, Jones and Nicholls, with the Stonewall Brigade held back) attacked Culp's Hill, where General Greene's single New York brigade was holding the whole Twelfth Corps line after the rest of the corps had been called to the left. The plan gives three hours for the attack: 19:00, 19:30 and 20:00, and recommends 19:00.

## What the witnesses say

**General Greene, report of 12 July 1863 (ten days after): a few minutes before 7 p.m.** The First Division and two brigades of the Second Division were ordered from his right at 6.30 p.m. and he began to occupy their empty works; "Before any further movements could be made, we were attacked on the whole of our front by a large force a few minutes before 7 p. m. The enemy made four distinct charges between 7 and 9.30 p. m." (Official Records, p. 856). He was the officer attacked, and his is the only clock reading for the first charge. In Battles and Leaders (1887) he writes that the rest of the corps "moved off just before dusk (about 7 P. M.)" and the attack came as he extended; that dates the corps leaving, not the attack, and runs a little later than his report. It is not counted as support for 19:00.

**General Geary, report of 29 July 1863: 7.30 as a sequence.** At 7 p.m. he was ordered to follow the First Division; at 7.30 he was ordered to hold the creek; "In the meantime" Greene had barely occupied Kane's position "when a vigorous attack was made" (p. 826-827). He was elsewhere and gives no hour for the attack; he says Greene's brigade sustained "an incessant attack of two and a half hours".

**The Confederate reports give an approach and periods, not a clock reading for the contact.** Captain Moseley (21st Virginia, 21 July): the regiment "remained in that position until about 6 p. m. of the 2d, when it advanced"; it fell back about 8 o'clock (p. 535). General Steuart (2 September): his pickets were relieved at about 5 o'clock and "shortly afterward the brigade was formed in line of battle and moved forward". General Ewell's report (the print gives only the year 1863): the artillery fire ceased "just before sundown", Johnson ordered the division forward "and about dusk the attack was made" (p. 447). General Jones (25 September) blames the confusion in the climb on "the lateness of the hour at which the advance was made, the darkness in the woods". Sunset was at 19:32 by the plan's computed value.

**20:00.** No report gives it for the start. The only 8 o'clock readings are the 21st Virginia falling back and Greene's report that about 8 p.m. the enemy appeared on his right flank; both belong to later phases. The plan took 20:00 from a Park Service page that could not be identified.

## The weighing

- Greene is the only witness who was attacked and gives a clock reading; his report is of the best class. He is the default on that ground, and flagged single-source because nobody gives the same reading independently.
- The Confederate hours are an advance across the valley about 6 and the climb after it, then "dusk" and "darkness in the woods", which are periods. By the computed sunset they begin about half an hour after Greene's reading. The two armies' clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes (not applied) and the Confederate advance took about an hour from line of battle to the works; between them these explain why the Confederate accounts read later than Greene's.
- Geary, a Union witness who was not attacked, has orders to leave at 7 p.m. (the First Division had gone nearly half an hour earlier), his column shelled on the way, the order to hold the creek at 7.30, and Greene attacked "in the meantime". That order of events puts the attack after 19:00 and perhaps after 19:30, and is the strongest ground for keeping 19:30 selectable. Geary's 7.30 is a sequence from a man who was marching away; Greene's account of 1887 has the same sequence a few minutes later than his report. So the attack could have been somewhat later than 19:00, which is why 19:30 stays selectable as an inference.

## The decision

The default is about 19:00: Greene's brigade is attacked a few minutes before 7 p.m. 19:30 stays as an inferred value (Geary's sequence and Ewell's dusk); 20:00 has no source for a start and is shown as a gap; the advance about 18:00, the periods "dusk" and "dark", and the 20:00 fall-back and flank attack are listed apart. The plan's recommended default and the first delegated default both hold.

## What would change it

A report, diary or signal message of 1863 giving a clock reading for the first charge, or a Confederate regiment's report with an hour for crossing Rock Creek, would narrow or move the default. Evidence that Greene's watch ran ahead, or an hour for the end of Latimer's artillery fire, would move it toward 19:30.

## Searches that failed

No clock reading for the start of the attack was found in the reports of Johnson, Steuart, Nicholls, Jones, Dungan, Walker, Greene, Kane, Candy or Geary beyond those above; none in the Joint Committee's volume; none in the Park Service texts read. Not read: Greene's papers, Geary's letters, the Twelfth Corps headquarters log, and the Park Service page the plan names.
