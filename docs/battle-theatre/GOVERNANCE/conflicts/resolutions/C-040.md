# C-040: When was Reynolds killed on 1 July?

## The dispute

Major General John F. Reynolds, commanding the left wing, was killed while placing the First Corps' leading brigades west of Gettysburg. The plan lists 10:15, 10:30 and 10:40-10:50 and recommends 10:30. The first delegated default was a window of 10:15 to 11:15, with 11:15 and 11:30 added from the Official Records.

## Who says what

**Wadsworth** (First Division, on the field), report of 4 July 1863: the division came up the Cashtown road "at about 10 a. m."; the right "became sharply engaged before the line was formed, and at this time (about 10. 15 a. m.) our gallant leader fell, mortally wounded". He does not say whether he saw it. **Doubleday** (took the corps; he was elsewhere when it happened), report of 14 December 1863: he "learned" that Reynolds "had just been shot", an event "in the beginning of the attack referred to, about 10.15 a. m." The two give the same hour with the same hedge; whether Doubleday had Wadsworth's report in front of him is not shown, so they count as one line.

**Weld**, Reynolds' aide, diary of July 1863 (printed 1912): Reynolds sent him to Meade at Taneytown; "I delivered the message to General Meade at 11.20, having been an hour and twenty minutes on my way". He therefore left Reynolds about 10:00 with Reynolds alive. He gives no hour for the death.

**Howard** (Eleventh Corps, in the town, looking from a building when told), note of 1 July headed 5 p.m.: "General Reynolds was killed at 11.15 a. m." In his report of 31 August he writes that Major Riddle brought word of the death "about 11.30 a. m."; **Schurz** (report of 20 August 1863) reached Howard about 11.30 and was told Reynolds "had just been killed". These date the news, not the fall.

**No writer found gives 10:30 or 10:40-10:50.** The only 10.45 readings near the event are Howard's "Reynolds attacked the enemy ... about 10.45 a. m." and Hall's "We opened ... at 10.45 a. m." (Hall's battery had been ordered into position by Reynolds), which date other events. **Heth** (Confederate) says one of Pegram's first shells wounded Reynolds; he gives no clock for it. A speech of 1913 (Indiana) has the troops "about 10 a. m." and Reynolds falling at the first volley.

## Weighing

A dispatch of the day normally outranks a report of three days later, but Howard was not at the fighting and was told about 11.30; his 11.15 is a reckoning from news. Wadsworth was on the field; Doubleday learned minutes later; both write about 10.15, and Weld's ride shows Reynolds alive at about 10:00 and Wadsworth's brigades coming up about 10, which all fits. Not settled: Hall says Reynolds placed his guns, and Hall opened fire at 10.45; if that hour is exact the death is later than 10.15. Hall's hour is for his own first fire, not the death. The two armies' clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes; this is not applied and does not touch an hour of difference.

## Decision

**Default: about 10:15**, class weighted, tier documented, flagged single-source (Wadsworth and Doubleday are one line). The PDF default (10:30) does not hold: no writer gives it. The first delegated window holds only partly: its late end is Howard's reckoning. All eight values stay selectable; 11:30 is marked as a different event (the news).

## What would change it

A witness at the place with a clock or watch reading for the fall (Reynolds' staff, an Iron Brigade colonel, Hall's battery log), or a dated note of 1 or 2 July that fixes it.

## Searches that failed

No hour other than those above in the Official Records or the ledger texts; Meade's own record gives only 11.20 for Reynolds' message and 1.10 p.m. for the order to Hancock; the shards' Beveridge (about 10) and Cheney (10:15) were not found or not fetched and are not cited.
