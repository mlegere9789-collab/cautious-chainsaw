# C-035: prisoners taken at Falling Waters, 14 July 1863

## The dispute
On the morning of 14 July the Union cavalry caught the Confederate rear guard (Heth's division, with Pettigrew's and Archer's brigades) near the pontoon bridge at Falling Waters. The plan lists 500 and 1,000 to 2,000, recommends 500, and the first delegated pass chose 500 to 511 from two reports, calling the larger figures "secondary only". The Official Records themselves carry the larger figures.

## The figures
- **Over 500.** Brig. Gen. John Buford, whose division was on the field, in his campaign report of 29 Aug 1863 (p. 929): "Our spoils on this occasion were one 10-pounder Parrott gun, one caisson, over 500 prisoners, and about 300 muskets." It counts what his division took, and "over" makes it a lower bound.
- **About 511.** Col. William Gamble, commanding Buford's First Brigade, report of 24 Aug (p. 937): "about 511 prisoners, 61 of whom ... were turned over to an officer of Kilpatrick's division by mistake", so his brigade kept about 450. Gamble and Buford are one division and are not counted as two witnesses.
- **1,500 or 1,600.** Brig. Gen. Judson Kilpatrick, who led the charge, letter of 7 Aug (p. 990): "upward of 1,600 prisoners" in the first scan of the volume; the second scan prints 1,500, as do McClellan (1885) and Kidd (1908). The page image could not be fetched, so the digit is not settled and both are kept.
- **Over 2,000.** Meade wired Halleck at 3 p.m. that his cavalry had "captured a brigade of infantry 1,500 strong", at 8:30 p.m. "500 prisoners, in addition to those previously reported" (pp. 93-94), and told Couch at 9 p.m. "we captured over 2,000 prisoners" (part 3, p. 698). A New York paper of 16 July prints "about 2,000". Meade was not on the field; Heth says he reported "on the statement of General Kilpatrick".
- **The Confederates give no count.** Lee, 21 July (p. 991): no organized body was taken, "only stragglers", and "The number ... I am unable to state with accuracy, but it is greatly exaggerated". Heth, 13 Sep (p. 642): his staff alone drove "several hundred stragglers" from roadside buildings; "I think I state correctly" that 3 out of 4 of the men captured that day were taken between the works near Hagerstown and where he engaged.
- **Partial counts.** McKeen's brigade about 50 (p. 383), Williams's skirmishers 200 to 300 (p. 772), the 7th Michigan 400 (p. 1000); each is one command.

## Weighing
A dispatch of the day normally outranks a report of weeks later, and that a memoir. Here the dispatches and Kilpatrick's report are one claim told several times, and the Confederate commanders contradict it in kind (no brigade) without offering a count. Buford and Gamble were present and counted their own men, but they count one division and Kilpatrick counts another command; they may be counting different sets (a day total from Williamsport to the river against the men taken at the ford). Nothing in the record separates a division commander's count from the cavalry's claim, and no Confederate return of men lost that day was found.

## Decision
Class **irreducible**, tier **documented** (each end is stated by a document written in 1863). Default: a **range**, over 500 (Buford) to over 2,000 (Meade's wire), with no single figure; the animation draws no more than the lowest documented count unless a larger one is selected. Every other value stays selectable with its reason. The plan's 500 holds only as the low end; the first delegated 500 to 511 holds as the two division counts, but its statement that the larger figures are secondary only does not. The plan's 1,000 has no source.

## What would change it
A Confederate count of the rear guard's missing on 14 July, the provost marshal's receipt rolls, or the page image of Kilpatrick's report.

## Searches that failed
Confederate reports and returns (no count of men lost); Battles and Leaders vol. 3; the Gettysburg weeklies and saved newspaper pages (no Confederate count); Gutenberg for Kidd (down for maintenance; the archive.org scan was used); the page image of p. 990 (image host blocked); Livermore and Phisterer (not fetched).
