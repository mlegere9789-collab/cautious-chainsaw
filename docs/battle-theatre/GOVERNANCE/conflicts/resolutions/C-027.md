# C-027: Where did Buford go from Gettysburg on 2 July?

## The dispute

Buford's cavalry division (Gamble's and Devin's brigades, with Merritt's Reserve Brigade) left the Union left on 2 July. The plan names three places, Taneytown, Westminster and Emmitsburg, and recommends "Westminster, marked unresolved". The first delegated default kept Westminster for 3 July and left the night of 2 July unresolved. The three places answer three different questions.

## What each source says

**Buford** (division commander), report of 27 August 1863: his division, on 2 July engaged with sharpshooters on the left, "held its own until relieved by General Sickles' corps, after which it moved to Taneytown, and bivouacked for the night. The next day, July 3, it moved to Westminster, to guard the trains of the army at that point." The date before the Taneytown sentence is garbled in the first scan of the Official Records; a second scan, read separately, prints "July 2". **Devin** (Second Brigade), 6 August 1863: after the Third Corps came into position, "we were ordered to march to Taneytown, where we bivouacked, and marched the next morning, July 3, to Westminster." **Calef** (battery with Gamble's brigade), 27 July 1863: on the morning of the 2d Buford ordered the battery to follow the First Brigade "to Taneytown ... for supplies and forage"; "Reached Taneytown about 4 p. m."; on 3 July to Westminster.

**Butterfield** for Meade, 2 July: "General Buford collect all the trains in the vicinity of Taneytown and take them down to Westminster"; a note at 12.50 p.m. speaks of "the permission to Buford to go to Westminster". **Pleasonton** (Cavalry Corps), 31 August 1863: Buford's division, relieved by the Third Corps on 2 July, "was directed to take post at Westminster, to assist in guarding the army trains".

**Merritt** (Reserve Brigade), 18 July 1863: from Mechanicstown "On the 2d instant, we marched to Emmitsburg"; on the 3d, "in compliance with orders received from corps headquarters", he marched toward the field.

**Patterson** (102d Pennsylvania, guarding a train), 4 August 1863, says he reached Westminster "on the morning of the 2d" and "Reported to General Buford". Buford was at Gettysburg that morning, so his date or the officer is wrong or loose; it stands alone.

## Weighing

Each commander states where his own unit went, on his own day, and the order and the corps commander's report explain the two stages: collect the trains at Taneytown, take them down to Westminster. Taneytown is the night of 2 July for Gamble's and Devin's brigades; Westminster is the destination and the place of 3 July; Emmitsburg is the Reserve Brigade only. The Itinerary of the Army of the Potomac repeats the same three movements from the reports. Calef's "about 4 p. m." is a Union clock reading; the 20-minute difference between the armies' clocks is not applied.

## Decision

**Default: Taneytown on the night of 2 July, then Westminster on 3 July** (Gamble's and Devin's brigades; the Reserve Brigade went to Emmitsburg). Class settled, tier documented. The PDF default holds only partly ("Westminster" for 3 July and as the destination; "unresolved" is no longer needed); the first delegated default holds partly for the same reason. Seven values are listed.

## What would change it

Gamble's or Buford's own order of 2 July naming the destination with its hour, or a journal of Gamble's brigade for that night.

## Searches that failed

Gamble's report gives no place for the night of 2 July; no order sending Buford to Taneytown as such was found (only the order to take the trains from Taneytown down to Westminster); later histories repeat the Official Records without an hour for the move.
