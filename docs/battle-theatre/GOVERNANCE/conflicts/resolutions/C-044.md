# C-044. Camp Letterman opening

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: 22 July 1863. Class: weighted (the evidence favours one value but does not settle it). Tier: inferred (two later writers give the day; the papers of 1863 bracket it but do not state it).

## The dispute

After the battle the Army of the Potomac left a general hospital at Gettysburg: Camp Letterman, a tent hospital on George Wolf's farm beside the York Pike, east of the town. The plan lists two opening days, 20 July and 22 July, recommends 20 July in its register, and in its own table on page 55 opens the camp on 22 July with 20 July flagged. The first delegated default kept 20 July, resting on a Park Service ranger page that gives "July 20 to November 19".

## What the sources say

**20 July.** No writing found gives it. The Park Service ranger page, fetched again for this research, does not mention Letterman at all in the text as served; the first pass had it only from a search summary. The Trust and Wikipedia pages the plan cites could not be reached (the connection was refused), so what stands behind them is unknown.

**22 July, two later writers.** Captain Louis C. Duncan of the Medical Corps, writing in the 1910s in a medical history of the war (undated reprint; 1916 is only the latest it can have been written), says of Camp Letterman: "It was opened on July 22nd and not closed until November 20, 1863". He was not a witness and gives no source. Sallie Myers, a Gettysburg teacher who nursed the wounded in the town and at the camp, wrote in 1909 that the people cared for the soldiers "until Camp Letterman, the hospital camp, was opened on July 22". We have her only as quoted by a Park Service seminar paper of 2010 (Atkinson), which itself says "the exact date that Camp Letterman began operation is hard to ascertain". The paper cites both writers and adds nothing to them.

**The papers of 1863 do not state the opening but bracket it.** On 5 July army headquarters ordered a general hospital established at Gettysburg. On 9 July Surgeon Henry Janes, in charge of the hospitals, wrote that he could "begin the permanent hospital soon, if I can get the hospital tents". On 14 July he telegraphed: "Have selected a fine site for the camp. Am anxiously awaiting the tents." On 15 July the depot quartermaster telegraphed: "The hospital tents arrived to-day, and are being put up" (he does not say where or for whom; the reading that they are the hospital's is an inference). The Park Service paper reports that a surgeon with the First Corps hospitals, William F. Norris, received orders to move his patients on 22 July (the paper is inconsistent: it says they were not moved until the next day, then quotes his letter of 26 July describing the move; the dates 22, 23 and 26 are recorded as given, not reconciled); that Chaplain William Way of the 24th Michigan wrote on 24 July of "the general hospital that is being established here"; and that the nurse Emily Souder saw the Third and Fifth Corps hospitals being brought into the General Hospital (her letter is headed 27 July, which was a Monday, but it is labelled Wednesday evening and follows an entry of Tuesday the 28th, so it is read, as the researcher's inference, as about 29 July). The Medical and Surgical History (1870) gives case histories of men sent to Camp Letterman on 24 July (two cases) and 27 July; it also puts other men there on 3 and 6 July, which cannot be literal (Janes was still awaiting tents on 14 July) and is read as the later name of the general hospital. The Christian Commission moved its tents to the General Hospital about 28 July. The Adams Sentinel of 28 July (the column is badly garbled) reports that a general hospital "has been established on the elevated grounds" east of the town with the wounded being removed to it; the Compiler of 3 August says 761 wounded had been removed to it by the Friday before (31 July); a Massachusetts committee in early August found the hospital still "being established". These fit 22 July as a process but give no day.

## The weighing

- The rule is that a letter or dispatch of the time beats a later history, which beats a reminiscence. Here the writings of the time do not state the opening; they only fix a window. The tents were on the ground from 15 July, the camp was still being established on 24 July, and the corps hospitals were merging into it by the end of the month.
- 22 July fits that window; the move orders of 22 July come on that very day. It is also the only day any writer gives. 20 July has no writer and sits two days before the day on which move orders were given, so it is the weaker reading even as the day the tents stood ready.
- The two writers of 22 July are not shown to be independent; neither gives a source. That is why the tier is inferred, not documented.
- "Opened" can mean the tents were ready, the first patients arrived, or the camp took over from the corps hospitals. These are different days. This resolution takes the two later writers' word (opened to patients on 22 July) and notes that the earliest dated transfers found are 24 July.
- Dates, not hours: no clock correction applies.

## The decision

Default: **Jul 22**, weighted, inferred. Jul 20 stays selectable, marked as unsourced. The plan's recommended default (20 July) does not hold: no source found gives it. The first delegated default (20 July, inferred, institutional) does not hold either; it rested on a page that does not say it. The plan's own table on page 55 (open 22 July) agrees with the default.

## What would change it

A writing of 1863 that says the general hospital opened or received its first patients on a named day: Janes's report to Letterman, a Sanitary or Christian Commission diary, the Detroit paper of 30 July 1863 (Way), the Norris letters, or Myers's own article of 1909. A source behind the plan's 20 July (the Trust page, the ranger page or a Wikipedia source) that states it would move the default.

## Searches that failed

No opening day in the Official Records (Letterman's report, Cuyler, Vollum, Janes's telegrams), in Letterman's Medical Recollections, in the Gettysburg Compiler and Adams Sentinel of July to December 1863, or in Moore, Brockett, Bucklin and the Christian Commission report. The Trust, Wikipedia and Civil War News pages were blocked; the Library of Congress search failed; the Detroit paper, the Myers article and the Norris letters were not read.
