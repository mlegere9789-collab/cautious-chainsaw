# C-001. When Meade reached Gettysburg, night of 1-2 July 1863

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window from about 01:00 to about 02:00 on 2 July, drawn at 01:00. Class: irreducible (the record is divided). Tier: documented (both ends are in reports of 1863).

## The dispute

General Meade left Taneytown on the night of 1 July, after Hancock reported the ground at Gettysburg good, and rode to the field. The plan lists two values: "after midnight" (a timeline page and an encyclopaedia page) and "about 04:00" (one terse timeline page), recommends "about 01:00, then a pre-dawn inspection of the line", and says it picked the earliest for coherence. The first pass found Meade's 1 a.m. and kept it. This pass read the other eyewitnesses.

## What the witnesses say

**General Meade, report of 1 October 1863:** he broke up headquarters at 10 p.m. and went "to the field, arriving there at 1 a. m. of the 2d. So soon as it was light, I proceeded to inspect the position." He repeated "one o'clock" in sworn testimony on 4 April 1864. Union clock; his own movements.

**General Hunt, chief of artillery, report of 27 September 1863:** the commanding general "reached Gettysburg about 2 a. m. of the 2d", and soon after his arrival told Hunt to look to the artillery. Hunt was at headquarters; he hedges with "about". A second scan of the volume reads both digits the same way. In 1887 he wrote only "after midnight".

**Generals Howard and Stannard, reports of 31 August and 4 July 1863:** at about 3 a.m. Meade, "who had previously arrived", met Howard at the cemetery gate and rode over the position (Howard); Stannard met Meade with Howard about 3 a.m. Hunt adds that after Meade arrived he looked at Cemetery Hill "so far as the darkness would permit" and rode the west front with Meade and Howard. So a night inspection is documented; Meade's own words put his inspection at first light.

**Lieutenant Haskell (Gibbon's staff), a letter of July 1863:** "At midnight Gen. Meade and staff rode by Gen. Gibbon's Head Quarters, on their way to the field." Hancock reports the Second Corps' bivouac about three miles behind the town. Meade was therefore still on the road at midnight.

**General Pleasonton, 1879:** the party reached Howard's headquarters on Cemetery Hill "after midnight"; at daylight Meade asked him to ride the position, until ten o'clock.

**Later books, no sources named:** Bachelder (1864 and 1873), the editors of Battles and Leaders (1887), Toombs's New Jersey volume (1888, "about one o'clock") and Fitz Lee in 1878 say one o'clock, as Meade does; Minnigh (1892), the 1913 Life and Letters ("just before midnight") and the Vermont volume (1914) say midnight. The county history of 1886 says one o'clock ("arrived here at one o'clock A. M.") in its summary chapter and two ("at 2 o'clock in the morning ... came on the field") in its chapter on the battle; both are cited, and each is counted with the report whose hour it repeats, an assumption, since nothing shows copying. Meade's sworn testimony of 1864 is testimony, not an after-action report.

**About 04:00:** no report, letter or book gives it. Howard and Stannard have Meade riding the line an hour earlier. The Epperson page could not be read here.

## The weighing

Meade and Hunt are of the same rank: official reports written about three months after, by men at headquarters, on the Union clock. Hunt's "about 2" is hedged, Meade's "1" is a round hour; neither tolerance closes an hour. Nothing in the record says they mean different places or acts. The later books that agree with Meade have no source of their own and are counted with him; the "midnight" books conflict with Haskell. Howard, Stannard and Haskell bracket the window from outside.

## The decision

The record is divided by an hour, so the default is the window about 01:00 to about 02:00, drawn at 01:00 (the earliest hour any first-hand writer gives). The plan's "pre-dawn inspection" is partly right: a night ride at about 03:00 is documented, Meade's own at first light too. Hunt's 02:00, "after midnight", the 03:00 meeting, first light, the midnight books and the midnight passage at Gibbon's camp all stay listed, with the 04:00 marked as unsourced and contradicted. The plan's default holds in part (01:00 is the lower end); the first delegated default holds in part (it left out Hunt).

## What would change it

An hour written on 1-2 July 1863 by someone with Meade (Butterfield, Williams, Biddle, Meade's letter of 2 July), a source behind Epperson's 04:00 or behind the midnight of the later books, or a page image reading Hunt's digit differently.

## Searches that failed

The Epperson, Stone Sentinels and Wikipedia pages could not be read. No hour for the arrival was found in the other Official Records reports, in the Joint Committee testimony other than Meade's, or in the Gettysburg weeklies of July 1863; the pages of the national newspapers of 1863 were not searched page by page.
