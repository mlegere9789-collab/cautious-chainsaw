# C-024: When did the Union council of war meet on 2 July 1863?

**Default:** the evening of 2 July, from dusk (about 19:32) to nearly midnight, with no start hour. Class gap (no source states the start hour; Meade and Gibbon bound it), tier plausible-procedural. Every value of the plan's row stays selectable.

## The dispute

The plan lists three times for Meade's council with his corps commanders in the Leister house: 21:00, midnight and "late", and recommends a window of 21:00 to 24:00. A first pass kept that window and added "two sittings possible". No source in the record states an hour for the start. The question is what the witnesses allow.

## What each source says

- **Meade**, who called the council, in sworn testimony of March 1864 (nine months later): after the battle of that day "had ceased, and darkness had set in" he sent for his corps commanders. He gives no hour; he calls it "the evening of the 2d".
- **Gibbon**, who sat in it (letter of 14 March 1864, printed in Meade's report, p. 127): "during a portion of the sitting of the council, which continued up to nearly 12 o'clock, fighting was going on on the right". So "nearly 12" is where the sitting ended. In his testimony (April 1864) he was summoned "at night, after the fighting was over" and the members sat "for several hours".
- **Hancock**, who sat in it (testimony of 22 March 1864): the fighting on the right "was going on at twilight, and after we had assembled in council". Howard's report puts the attack on East Cemetery Hill between 7 and 8 p.m. and the end of the fight at 9.30 p.m.; Hancock does not tie his sentence to that fight.
- **Haskell**, Gibbon's aide, in a long letter written soon after the battle: "After evening came on" Meade summoned his commanders; the council dissolved "after some two hours", and it was almost midnight when he walked back. He says all was going well on the right, which Gibbon's letter contradicts for part of the sitting.
- **Sedgwick, Sykes**, 1864: the council was in the "evening" (Sedgwick's letter), "at night" (his testimony), "on the night of the 2d and 3d" (Sykes). The minutes in the Official Records are indorsed "held Thursday p. m., July 2" and give no hour.
- **Pleasonton**, in 1883: "early in the evening". He says he was away from the field from about 5 to 10 p.m. and missed it, so he did not see it.
- **National Park Service Handbook No. 9** (1954, 1962): "At midnight Meade sought the advice of his Council of War". It cites nothing, and one sentence elsewhere in it says only "On the night of July 2".
- **21:00** comes from a secondary book the plan names (SS2) that is not in the record. No non-lead source states it.

A separate meeting of the corps commanders, about 3 p.m., ended when the attack opened on Sickles's front (Butterfield's circular of 3 p.m.; Sickles). It is a different event, not a first sitting of the night council.

## The weighing

All the good witnesses are Union staff and corps commanders who were in the room or called it; they wrote or testified eight to nine months later, when Meade was accused of wanting to retreat. None read a clock. They agree on the order of events: the fight on the left ended, darkness came, the commanders were summoned, the council sat while fighting went on on the right, and it ended nearly at midnight. The Park Service's midnight, read as a start, is contradicted by Gibbon, who was there; midnight is the end. The plan's 21:00 start is not in any source; Haskell's two hours before an end just under midnight would give about 21:45, Gibbon's "several hours" something earlier, and Hancock dates the fighting at twilight, not the assembly. No source fixes the start, so the default is a marked stand-in: the window the two bounds allow. Its start is the computed sunset, 19:32 (Meade's call came after darkness); its end is Gibbon's "nearly 12". The union and Confederate clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes; this is not applied, and no pair of values here needs it.

## Decision

Default: from dusk (about 19:32) to nearly midnight on 2 July; no start hour is given by any source. The animation uses no single instant; the council is drawn seated for the part of the window the witnesses agree on, with its start marked uncertain, never before 19:32. The plan's 21:00-24:00 window holds only for its end: its start has no source but stays selectable. The first delegated default (the same window, two sittings) holds in its evening date and is replaced for its start; "two sittings" was looked for and not found. Selectable: 21:00, midnight, late, the plan's window, the first delegated window, early in the evening (Pleasonton) and about 15:00 (the earlier meeting, a different event).

## What would change it

A timed order, log, diary entry or letter of the evening of 2 July at Meade's headquarters (Butterfield's, an aide's, or a corps commander's note that night) that gives the hour at which the commanders were called or the council opened.

## Searches that failed

The Official Records parts 1 to 3, the Meade biography, Battles and Leaders, the New York, Pennsylvania and Maine volumes, Haskell, Minnigh and the Park Service texts were searched for a start hour (nine, ten, eleven o'clock and the reverse order): the only hours found for a council that evening are the 3 p.m. meeting and Milroy's councils at Winchester. The Joint Committee testimony of Meade, Hancock, Gibbon, Sedgwick, Birney, Hunt, Warren and Butterfield gave no hour; the "8 or 9 o'clock" is Wadsworth's council of 12 July. Doubleday's book of 1882 (fetched, read) says only "At night". No testimony heading for Howard, Slocum or Newton was found in the volume, so none was read.
