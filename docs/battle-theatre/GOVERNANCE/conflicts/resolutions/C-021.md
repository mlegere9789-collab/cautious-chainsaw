# C-021 Pickett's arrival

**The dispute.** The plan lists four readings: Pickett's division came on the night of 2 July, on 3 July, marched from 06:00 on 2 July, or had no order to be up by daylight on 3 July. Two questions are mixed: the date (the night of the 2d against the 3d) and what "on the field" means.

**Date.** Pickett's division left Chambersburg on the morning of 2 July (Alexander, writing in 1907, says at 2 a.m.; no one gives 06:00). It marched about 22 to 25 miles and came within three or four miles of Gettysburg in the afternoon: Hoke (a Chambersburg civilian, 1887) says about 3 o'clock, Alexander 4 p.m., Colonel Aylett of Armistead's brigade (12 July 1863) that the brigade "bivouacked about 4 miles from Gettysburg, on the Chambersburg turnpike", and Lee's detailed report (January 1864) that the brigades "arrived near the battle-field during the afternoon of the 2d". That is the neighbourhood, not the field.

**On the field.** Aylett: the brigade "moved at 3 a. m. on the 3d" from there to the right of the town. Peyton (Garnett's brigade, 9 July 1863): "they reached the field about 9 a. m." Lee's earlier report (31 July 1863): Pickett "joined Longstreet the following morning". Major Dearing, whose artillery battalion was attached to the division (16 August 1863), is the one witness for the night: orders came "about dusk" on the 2d to move to the field, and the guns bivouacked in rear of the First Corps line; at daybreak they marched to the field. Fremantle (diary: "just come up"), Dickert (1899: "now come up") and Owen (1885: "just arrived") saw the division on the morning of the 3d, and General Wilcox (1877) saw its three brigades "arrive and form in line" about 10 a.m. Hunt (1887), Toombs (1888) and the Park Service handbook say night or evening of the 2d without a source; one passage printed by Hoke has the division in position soon after six, a second-hand quotation of Owen that is not in Owen's own book.

**Orders.** Longstreet (1896) says Lee gave no order for a morning attack and sent no Pickett; Lee (1864) says Longstreet was ordered to attack the next morning; Taylor (1906) answers Longstreet. None of this dates the arrival and no order to Pickett survives.

**Decision.** Default: on the field the morning of 3 July, not before dawn (class weighted, tier documented). The record gives brigade times, not a division time: Armistead's brigade starts at 3 a.m. (the earliest documented time, drawn as a march) and Garnett's reaches the field about 9 a.m. The plan's default and the first delegated default hold. The afternoon and night readings are kept as the neighbourhood and the guns. **Gate Q04** keeps the division off the scene until 04:36: nothing found contradicts that for the infantry (the 3 a.m. start is a march from four miles out, and no report times its arrival); Dearing's guns on the field at dusk of the 2d are earlier, if the model draws them with the division. No gate is changed.

**Searches that failed.** The web pages behind the plan's readings could not be read. No report by Pickett, Kemper, Garnett or Armistead exists; Kemper's brigade has no hour. Hoke's quotation of Owen is not in Owen's book.

**What would change it.** A report of Pickett, Kemper or a staff officer giving the hour, or one placing a brigade on the field before 04:36.
