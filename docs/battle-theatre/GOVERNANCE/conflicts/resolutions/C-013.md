# C-013. Chamberlain's charge

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window, about 17:30 to 18:30 on 2 July 1863, drawn at 18:00. Class: irreducible (the record is divided and nothing in it separates the readings). Tier: inferred (reasoned from the reports; no writer of the time states an hour).

## The dispute

Late on 2 July 1863 Colonel Joshua Chamberlain, with the 20th Maine at the left end of Vincent's brigade on Little Round Top, ordered a bayonet charge that swept the 15th and 47th Alabama from his front. The plan gives the hour as "17:30; no clock time in the sources" and recommends 17:30, labelled inferred. The first pass kept "about 17:30, window 16:00 to dusk". This pass read every report of the fight again, the Confederate reports, and the later books, and asked one question: does anyone give the hour?

## What the witnesses say

**Colonel Rice, 44th New York, commanding the brigade after Vincent fell, report of 31 July 1863.** The brigade "was detached from the division and ordered into position at about 4 p. m." (p. 616). The struggle against the 20th Maine was desperate "for above half an hour", after which Chamberlain "ordered a charge" (p. 617). The order to take Round Top was "8 o'clock in the evening" (p. 618), a later act. Union clock; he was on the hill. No hour for the charge.

**Colonel Chamberlain, 20th Maine, report of 6 July 1863.** His "formation was scarcely complete" when the first infantry attack came on the brigade's centre (p. 623); the flanking column came later, and "for an hour the fighting was severe" (p. 624). Then "At that crisis, I ordered the bayonet" (p. 624). "At dusk" Rice told him of Vincent's fall, and the advance on Round Top was at "9 p. m." (p. 625). Union clock; the colonel who gave the order. His hour is a length of time, not a reading; he gives no hour for the charge.

**Others of the brigade, July and August 1863.** Lieutenant Colonel Welch (16th Michigan): "About 4 p. m." the brigade moved to the left (p. 628). Lieutenant Colonel Conner (44th New York): "engaged about one hour" when Vincent fell (p. 630). Captain Woodward (83rd Pennsylvania, on Chamberlain's right): the contest "continued lively until nearly 6 p. m." when the enemy fell back (p. 632). Woodward's earlier hours (2.30 and 3.15 p.m.) were later called mistaken, by Norton, who says Woodward admitted it in 1912. General Barnes, the division commander (report of 24 August), tells the charge, then "It was now nearly dark" (p. 604); he does not say he stood at the ledge.

**Major Bane, 4th Texas (Confederate), report of 9 July 1863.** "About 4.30 p. m." the regiment was ordered forward (p. 410). His Texans attacked the brigade's centre, not the 20th Maine's ledge, so this dates the first attack, not the charge. Confederate clock.

**Norton, a private of the 83rd Pennsylvania, 1913.** Computes that the attack on the brigade "must have commenced at some time between 4:30 and 5 p. M." (p. 246). A reasoned figure, 50 years later.

**Colonel Oates, 15th Alabama, 1905.** Guns opened "about 3.30" and the line advanced within minutes (p. 207). That is the whole line; his regiment still had to climb Big Round Top. No hour for the repulse. Gerrish, a private of the 20th Maine (1882), has none either.

**The Maine commission's volume, 1898.** "At the hour when the Twentieth Maine dashed upon the 15th and 47th Ala. regiments ... It was about 6 : 30 p. m. when the Confederates were driven" (p. 258). The only reading of an hour tied to the charge. No source is named; the same chapter starts the action "at about 4 o'clock p. m." (p. 253). A Pennsylvania volume (1914) prints a monument inscription with the brigade arriving "about 5 p. m.", an hour later than the reports, which shows how later hours drift.

## The weighing

- The best class of writing, the reports of July and August 1863 by men on the hill, gives lengths of time and starting hours but no hour for the charge. Nothing in them contradicts anything else in them.
- Reasoning from them, the charge cannot be earlier than the first attack (16:30 to 17:00) plus Chamberlain's hour or Rice's half hour: about 17:30 at the earliest, before dusk (sunset was 19:32).
- The one stated hour, 18:30, comes from a book 35 years later that names no source. It cannot beat the reports, but the reports do not date the charge, so it is not beaten either. The two ends are an hour apart and nothing separates them: irreducible, so a window, drawn at its midpoint, 18:00.
- The Union and Confederate clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes; this is not applied and is much smaller than the window.
- Every figure is an "about". 17:30 is a computation, not a statement; it is flagged weak.

## The decision

Default: about 17:30 to 18:30, drawn at 18:00; class irreducible, tier inferred. Selectable: 17:30 (the plan's value; the early end), "no clock time in the sources" (true of 1863, shown as the visible gap), about 18:30 (Maine, 1898), about 18:00 (Woodward's "nearly 6 p. m.", inside the window, a clock its writer disowned), and 16:00 to dusk (the first pass, which dates other acts at its ends).

The plan's default 17:30 holds in part: as the early end of an inferred window, not as the point. The first delegated default holds in part: "about 17:30, inferred" stands as the early end, but 16:00 is the order into position, not the charge.

## What would change it

A report, letter or diary of 1863 to 1864 with a clock reading for the order or the repulse (a 20th Maine officer such as Spear, Morrill or Prince; Oates or Law; the battlefield commission or Bachelder papers), the Maine commission's source for 6:30, or the plan's key SS2 for 17:30. Two independent writers of the time for one hour would make it weighted.

## Searches that failed

No hour for the charge in the Official Records (reports of the Third Brigade, Barnes, Sykes, Ayres, Crawford, Fisher; Confederate reports of Oates, Perry, Sheffield, Robertson, Bane, Benning), Oates 1878 and 1905, Gerrish, Powell, Lossing, Chamberlain's book of 1915, or the Park Service texts. Not read: the Bachelder papers, the park archive, Spear's and Oates's letters, Chamberlain's magazine article of 1913 (not found among the volumes listed), Wikipedia and the key SS2.
