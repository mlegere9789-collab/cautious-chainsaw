# C-013. Chamberlain's charge

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window, about 18:30 to 19:30 on 2 July 1863, drawn at 19:00. Class: weighted (the evidence favours the later hours but does not settle them). Tier: inferred (no writer of the time states an hour).

## The dispute

Late on 2 July 1863 Colonel Joshua Chamberlain, with the 20th Maine at the left end of Vincent's brigade on Little Round Top, ordered a bayonet charge that swept the 15th and 47th Alabama from his front. The plan gives the hour as "17:30; no clock time in the sources" and recommends 17:30, labelled inferred. The first pass kept "about 17:30, window 16:00 to dusk". This pass read every report of the fight again, the Confederate reports, and the later books, and asked one question: does anyone give the hour?

## What the witnesses say

**Colonel Rice, 44th New York, commanding the brigade after Vincent fell, report of 31 July 1863.** The brigade "was detached from the division and ordered into position at about 4 p. m." (p. 616). The struggle against the 20th Maine was desperate "for above half an hour", after which Chamberlain "ordered a charge" (p. 617). The order to take Round Top was "8 o'clock in the evening" (p. 618), a later act. Union clock; he was on the hill. No hour for the charge.

**Colonel Chamberlain, 20th Maine, report of 6 July 1863.** His "formation was scarcely complete" when the first infantry attack came on the brigade's centre (p. 623); the flanking column came later, and "for an hour the fighting was severe" (p. 624). Then "At that crisis, I ordered the bayonet" (p. 624). "At dusk" Rice told him of Vincent's fall, and the advance on Round Top was at "9 p. m." (p. 625). Union clock; the colonel who gave the order. His hour is a length of time, not a reading; he gives no hour for the charge.

**Others of the brigade, July and August 1863.** Lieutenant Colonel Welch (16th Michigan): "About 4 p. m." the brigade moved to the left (p. 628). Lieutenant Colonel Conner (44th New York): "engaged about one hour" when Vincent fell (p. 630). Captain Woodward (83rd Pennsylvania, on Chamberlain's right): the contest "continued lively until nearly 6 p. m." when the enemy fell back (p. 632). Woodward's starting hours (2.30 and 3.15 p.m.) were called mistaken by Norton, who says Woodward admitted it in 1912; Norton does not correct "nearly 6", but the report's clock is doubtful. General Barnes, the division commander (report of 24 August), tells the charge, then "It was now nearly dark" (p. 604); he does not say he stood at the ledge.

**Major Bane, 4th Texas (Confederate), report of 9 July 1863.** "About 4.30 p. m." the regiment was ordered forward (p. 410). His Texans attacked the brigade's centre, not the 20th Maine's ledge, so this dates the first attack, not the charge. Confederate clock.

**Norton, a private of the 83rd Pennsylvania, 1913.** Computes that the attack on the brigade "must have commenced at some time between 4:30 and 5 p. M." (p. 246). A reasoned figure, 50 years later.

**Colonel Oates, 15th Alabama, the officer Chamberlain charged.** In 1905 he has the guns opening "about 3.30" (p. 207): the whole line; his regiment still had to climb Big Round Top. In 1878 (Southern Historical Society Papers, vol. 6, p. 178) he describes the retreat from the 20th Maine's front, running through the dismounted cavalry to the top of Round Top: "This was just about sunset", and the battle had begun "four hours previously". Sunset was 19:32. A memoir 15 years later, hedged, Confederate clock, but by the man at the point of contact. Gerrish, a private of the 20th Maine (1882), gives no hour.

**The Maine commission's volume, 1898.** "At the hour when the Twentieth Maine dashed upon the 15th and 47th Ala. regiments ... It was about 6 : 30 p. m. when the Confederates were driven" (p. 258). The only reading of an hour tied to the charge. No source is named; the same chapter starts the action "at about 4 o'clock p. m." (p. 253). A Pennsylvania volume (1914) prints a monument inscription with the brigade arriving "about 5 p. m.", an hour later than the reports, which shows how later hours drift.

## The weighing

- The reports of July and August 1863, by men on the hill, give lengths of time and starting hours but no hour for the charge. From them the charge cannot be earlier than the first attack (16:30 to 17:00) plus Rice's half hour or Chamberlain's hour: about 17:00 to 17:30. That is a floor, not a reading, and it ignores nothing: Rice's shorter figure gives 17:00, Chamberlain's gives 17:30.
- Two later writers state an hour. The Maine volume (1898, no source named) says about 18:30. Oates (1878) says the retreat after the charge was just about sunset (about 19:30). Oates was opposite Chamberlain, wrote 20 years nearer the battle and says how he counts; the Maine volume does not. The weight favours the later end, but a hedged memoir is not a clock reading, and the two are an hour apart.
- The reports do not contradict either: after the charge Chamberlain went back to his place, and "at dusk" Rice told him of Vincent's fall; Barnes has "nearly dark"; the advance on Round Top was at 9 p.m. (Chamberlain). Woodward's "nearly 6 p.m." is not used as a bound.
- The Union and Confederate clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes; not applied. Every figure is an "about".

## The decision

Default: about 18:30 to 19:30, drawn at 19:00; class weighted, tier inferred. Selectable: 17:30 (the plan's value; the earliest hour the reports allow), "no clock time in the sources" (true of 1863 and of the Official Records; the visible gap), about 18:30 (Maine), about 19:30 (Oates), about 18:00 (Woodward, doubtful clock, a different event) and 16:00 to dusk (the first pass, whose ends date other acts).

The plan's default 17:30 does not hold as the time of the charge: it is the floor. The first delegated default holds only in part: it read the Official Records alone.

## What would change it

A report, letter or diary of 1863 to 1864 with a clock reading for the order or the repulse (a 20th Maine officer such as Spear, Morrill or Prince; Oates or Law; the battlefield commission or Bachelder papers), the Maine commission's source for 6:30, or the plan's key SS2 for 17:30. A second independent writer for the sunset hour would make it settled.

## Searches that failed

No hour for the charge in the Official Records (reports of the Third Brigade, Barnes, Sykes, Ayres, Crawford, Fisher; Confederate reports of Oates, Perry, Sheffield, Robertson, Bane, Benning), Oates's book of 1905 (his article of 1878 does give "just about sunset"), Gerrish, Powell, Lossing, Chamberlain's book of 1915, or the Park Service texts. Not read: the Bachelder papers, the park archive, Spear's and Oates's letters, Chamberlain's magazine article of 1913 (not found among the volumes listed), Wikipedia and the key SS2.
