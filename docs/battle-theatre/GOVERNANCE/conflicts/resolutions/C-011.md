# C-011: when Hood's attack began (2 July 1863)

## The dispute
The plan lists two hours for the start of Hood's attack on the Union left: 16:00 and 16:30 (it cites a Stone Sentinels timeline and Hessler's chapter on the Peach Orchard), and recommends 16:30. The first default set under delegated authority was a window: guns about 16:00, infantry soon afterward. Hood was wounded as his division went forward and no report by him, Law or McLaws is printed in the Official Records, so no one writer states the hour for the whole division.

## What the witnesses say
- **Lee**, report written in January 1864 (not saying where he stood): "About 4 p. m. Longstreet's batteries opened, and soon afterward Hood's division, on the extreme right, moved to the attack" (OR 27/2, p. 319). Confederate clock.
- **Four regiments of Hood's division**, reports of 8 and 9 July 1863, each written by the officer commanding, in the front line: Work, 1st Texas, "ordered forward to the attack about 4 p. m." (p. 408); Manning, 3rd Arkansas, "About 4 o'clock on the evening of July 2, I was ordered to move against the enemy" (p. 407); Bryan, 5th Texas, the division "drawn up in line of battle" about 4 p.m., then the word "Forward" (p. 412); Bane, 4th Texas, "About 4.30 p. m. the 2d instant, we were ordered to advance on the enemy" (p. 410). The 4th Texas stood between the 5th and the 1st, which both say about 4.
- **Alexander**, who handled the guns with Hood and McLaws: in 1887 "I think it was about 4 o'clock when at last the word was given for Hood's division to move out" (Battles and Leaders, p. 359); in 1907, 36 guns in action about 3.45 and, after "perhaps 30 minutes", "Hood received the order to advance" (p. 395). That is about 16:15, an estimate built from two hedged figures.
- **Longstreet**, 1877-78: "At half-past 3 the order was given General Hood to advance" (Southern Historical Society Papers 5, p. 63). He gives the hour of an order, not of the step-off.
- **Law**, 1887, 24 years later: "It was near 5 o'clock p. M. when we advanced to the attack" (p. 323), with about fifteen minutes of artillery before.
- **A. P. Hill**, report of November 1863: "Hood commenced the attack about 2 o'clock", and McLaws about 5.30 (p. 608). He commanded the centre and does not say how he knew.
- **Union side**: Meade's dispatch of 8 p.m. on 2 July says the enemy "attacked me about 4 p. m." (p. 72); Chamberlain, 20th Maine, heard "a sharp cannonade" near 4 p.m. (p. 622); Cummins, 124th New York, on a bluff near the Third Corps' left, was "attacked between the hours of 3 and 4 o'clock" (p. 512). Union clock.

## Weighing
By the owner's rule (a report of the time beats a later report, which beats a recollection), the reports of 8 and 9 July from officers who were ordered forward come first. Three say about 4 and one says 4.30. "About 4" reads as 15:45 to 16:15 and "4.30" as 16:15 to 16:45, so they meet at about 16:15, which Alexander's sum also gives. Lee's "soon afterward" adds 0 to 30 minutes to his 4 p.m. for the guns. The four regimental writers are one brigade and may share orders, so they are fewer than four independent voices; Lee relays Longstreet's corps; Alexander's two accounts are one witness; Longstreet's text appears twice. Law's "near 5" and Hill's "about 2" are contradicted by the reports of 1863; Hill gives no source. Longstreet's half-past 3 is an order, not the step-off. Hood's own book (1880) dates the decision to attack "about 3 o'clock" and gives no step-off hour in the part read; Oates (1905) repeats that passage and is not a second witness. The reported 20-minute difference between the two armies' clocks is not applied. It could explain why Cummins says "between 3 and 4" where the Confederates say "about 4"; it stays uncertainty.

## Decision
**Default: guns about 16:00, Hood's infantry soon after: window 16:00-16:30, the step-off drawn about 16:15.** Class weighted; tier inferred (the window is read from hedged statements, none states it). The 16:00 and 16:30 of the plan are the two ends of the window and stay selectable; the plan's 16:30 rests on one regiment (Bane) and on two pages that could not be read here, so the recommended default holds only partly. The first delegated window holds. Also kept as selectable values: 15:30 (Longstreet's order), about 16:15 (Alexander's order), near 17:00 (Law), about 14:00 (Hill) and 15:00-16:00 (Cummins, Union).

## What would change it
A report, diary or letter of 1863-64 that gives a clock reading for the step-off of Hood's whole division or of Law's brigade; the Stone Sentinels page or Hessler's chapter with the source behind 16:30; a journal of the guns with Hood; or a report by Law or Hood.

## Searches that did not settle it
The Stone Sentinels page and Hessler's chapter were not read. Robertson's report has no clock reading for the advance. No report by Hood, Law or McLaws is printed. Local Park Service texts and McLaws's address of 1879 give no hour. Hood's 1880 narrative was not read to the end.
