## 2 Defects

**D1 (moderate): Lee's staff officer Long and the artillery chief Walker give an hour (about 10) that bears on the order and are not weighed; the text says none exists.** `.md` "Weighing": "Only one line of knowledge gives an hour: Longstreet"; "Searches that failed": "No hour for Lee's order ... in any local text outside Longstreet and those who repeat him"; `weighing.independence`: "The hour 11:00 therefore rests on one group". The local corpus has:
- SHSP-04 (Long, "Letter from General A. L. Long", 1877; offset 168184, about p. 68-69): "It was now about ten o'clock ... General Lee's impatience became so urgent that he proceeded in person to hasten the movement of Longstreet. He was, however, met on the way with the welcome tidings that Longstreet's troops were in motion." Long was on Lee's staff and rode with him; Early (SHSP-05 offset 534223) cites it. If true, the order was given before about 10, not at 11.
- SHSP-05 (Lindsay Walker, offset 538348, about p. 181): Lee at the guns "manifested more impatience ... very much disappointed and worried that the attack had not opened earlier", on the ride toward Longstreet; Freeman (FREEMAN-RELEE-V3-C005, p. 91-92, offset 221651) dates Lee's "What can detain Longstreet!" "toward 10 o'clock" and says "When Lee at last located Longstreet it was 11 o'clock or later" and then "told Longstreet in plain terms what he wanted him to do". Freeman's narrative (not his footnote the resolution cites) is the one historian's dating of the order itself, and it agrees with 11:00 as the formal order on the field, while Long's and Walker's accounts disagree with Longstreet that no order had been given before.
- NJ-TOOMBS-1888 (offset 383480): "It was ten o'clock before Lee gave the final order to form for the attack" (secondary; dossier line 204 lists it, the resolution does not).
Long and Walker are rank-2 memoirs like Longstreet's, written by men who were with Lee, and belong in `positions` (as a "before about 10" value, `different_event` or `contradicted`, not silent) and in the weighing as the counter-line. P4's reason ("Longstreet places that order later, about eleven") hides that other staff witnesses place Lee's directions earlier. Add them; the default may still hold, but "single line of knowledge" must read "the only witness who gives the hour of the order itself".

**D2 (moderate): a report of 1863 dates an attack order to about 09:00-09:30 and is explained away by relabelling the event.** Alexander's report of 3 August 1863 (OR27-2 p. 429): "arrived at 9 a. m. ... Shortly after our arrival, I was directed by General Longstreet to accompany ... the divisions of ... McLaws and Hood in the attack upon the left". Fremantle (7 a.m.): Longstreet "disposing M'Laws's division for to-day's fight"; McLaws (SHSP-07 p. 68): Lee himself told him where to place the division, "early in the morning", Longstreet present. By the owner's rule these (report of the time, diary of the day) outrank Longstreet's memoir of 1877. `holds` and the default treat "Lee's attack order" as "the formal order to move" (panel text "'order' means the formal order to move" in `weighing.summary`), a definition found nowhere in the PDF row ("Lee's attack order", p. 62) and chosen so that those reports become "different event". That choice is the owner's, not the researcher's; the resolution should say plainly that on the PDF's wording the reports of the time point to the morning (before about 9:30) and that 11:00 holds only under the narrower reading, and keep P4 in the weighing as a competing value, not a different event. At least the `overrides` text ("they date Longstreet's order to Alexander ... not Lee's order to Longstreet") needs to admit that Longstreet could give Alexander that order only after he knew the plan.

**D3 (minor): `readers` and counts.** Alexander 1907 p. 391 is counted with Longstreet's line ("its sequence is Longstreet's"), but p. 391 also says Lee "had decided" earlier and that the reports of Long and Pendleton came after he returned (about ten): that is Long's own story (D1) joined to Longstreet's hours, so the Alexander sentence is not simply a repeat. State it.
