# C-015. 20th Maine strength

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: 386 men all told, 358 of them with muskets. Class: weighted (the evidence favours one count but only one man gives it). Tier: documented (the colonel's own report of four days after states it), flagged single-source.

## The dispute

The plan gives two strengths for the 20th Maine at Little Round Top, 385 and 386, and recommends 385. The research found 386 in Colonel Chamberlain's report and no source for 385. It also found other numbers that count other things.

## What the witnesses say

**Colonel Chamberlain, report of 6 July 1863 (four days after): 386.** "We went into the fight with 386, all told — 358 guns. Every pioneer and musician who could carry a musket went into the ranks." The sick and foot-sore who had fallen behind came up and took their places, and men under sentence of court-martial were put into the fight (Official Records, p. 626). So 386 counts every man who went into the line; 358 counts those with muskets. He does not say who the other 28 were. His loss was 136, 30 of them killed. Fox's table of 1889 gives 386 under "Engaged" for the regiment without naming a source; "Barnes's" in his row is the division heading, not Barnes's report (which says 380), and the figure is counted with Chamberlain.

**General Barnes, division commander, report of 24 August 1863 (53 days after): 380.** "The Twentieth Maine, Colonel Chamberlain commanding, was posted on the left. It consisted of 380 men and officers" (p. 603). He was not with the brigade and says its record is drawn principally from its commander's report; he gives no reason for 380.

**The regiment's own later accounts: 358, and about 350.** The monument the survivors put up in 1886 (printed in the Maine volume of 1898) says the regiment lost 38 killed or mortally wounded and 92 wounded "out of 358 engaged". Lieutenant Melcher (Battles and Leaders, 1888) writes "Our regiment numbered 358 men", with Company B's 50 sent out to protect the flank, leaving 308 in line. Private Gerrish (1882) says "three hundred and fifty men", and the Maine volume "about 350". The monument and Melcher give 358 as the regiment's number; it equals Chamberlain's musket count, and a copy from him is likely but not stated.

**385.** No report, return or book read gives it. The plan took it from web pages that could not be read.

## The weighing

- Only one man counted the regiment he commanded and said what is in the count: Chamberlain, four days after. Barnes's 380 is a relay by a man who was elsewhere and wrote nearly two months later.
- 358 and 386 are not in conflict: they are the muskets and the men, in the same report. 308 and about 350 are partial and round counts.
- Nothing independent of Chamberlain confirms 386; Fox's table repeats it. That is why the default is weighted and carries the single-source flag.
- The reported 20-minute difference between the armies' clocks bears on no count.

## The decision

The default is 386 men all told, 358 of them with muskets; the range shown is 358 to 386, Chamberlain's own two counts, with Barnes's 380 between them. The soldiers drawn follow the printed unit strength: 386 for the regiment, of whom 358 carry muskets (Company B's 50, by Melcher alone, went out as skirmishers and are counted inside the regiment). The plan's 385 does not hold (no source, one man below the colonel's own count). The first delegated default (386, 358 guns) holds.

## What would change it

The regiment's muster rolls or the Maine adjutant general's roll of men present on 2 July would give a count that does not depend on Chamberlain's word. A brigade return at regimental level for 30 June 1863 would check both 386 and 380. A source for the plan's 385 would add that value.

## Searches that failed

No 385 for the regiment was found in any local text; the Official Records tables give losses, not strength; the Park Service landscape report cites the Official Records and gives no figure. Not read: the muster rolls, Busey and Martin, Pullen, Chamberlain's address of 1889, and the web pages the plan names.
