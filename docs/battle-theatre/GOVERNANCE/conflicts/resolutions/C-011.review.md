## 1 Validator

## 2 Defects

**D1 (moderate): reports of 1863 and later witnesses put Hood "engaged" about 4, which puts the step-off before 16:15; the resolution dismisses the key one.** Kershaw (OR27-2 p. 368, offset 1360126): "I was directed to commence the attack so soon as General Hood became engaged ... Accordingly, about 4 o'clock, when I received orders to advance, I moved". Kershaw's brigade is McLaws's, but his order was conditional on Hood's engagement, so the report (1 Oct 1863, rank of the best class) says Hood was engaged by about 4. `failed_searches[6]` sets it aside ("is McLaws's division, not Hood's, and is not used"). Add: Alexander 1877 (SHSP-04 offset 250925 and SHSP-06 offset 337256): "about 4 P. M. when Hood became engaged"; Wilcox 1877 (SHSP-06 p. 115, offset 342204): "Hood's attack began about 4 P. M."; Bachelder 1873 (BACH-WHATTOSEE-1873 offset 132604): "At four o'clock ... Hood's division advanced to attack". The Texas line had half a mile to three-quarters of a mile to cross before contact (Work p. 408; Bryan p. 412: "about three-fourths of a mile from our starting point"), so an engagement about 4 means a step-off at about 15:45-16:00, earlier than the drawn 16:15. None of these is cited or weighed.

**D2 (moderate): the intersection "they meet at 16:15" is an artefact of an arbitrary tolerance, and the animation instant conflates order and step-off.** `weighing.clock`: "'About 4' is read as 15:45 to 16:15 and '4.30' as 16:15 to 16:45, which overlap at 16:15." The plus or minus 15 minutes is invented; four adjacent regiments of one brigade differ by 30 minutes (Work, Manning, Bryan about 4; Bane 4.30), which shows how wide "about" is, not that the truth is 16:15. And P6 (Alexander, 16:15) is "Hood receives the order", while `default.animation.note` says "the first infantry of Hood's division step off about 16:15"; Work, Manning and Bane also date orders, Robertson (p. 404) says the division arrived "a few minutes before we were ordered to advance". Order at 16:15 would put the step-off later (Alexander) and an order at about 16:00 earlier (regiments); the three cannot all give "step-off 16:15". The window may stand; the drawn point should be justified as a plain midpoint of the window (the rule is `midpoint`) and not as a reading of any report, and the panel text "step-off about 16:15" should say "drawn at the middle".

**D3 (minor): the four regimental reports and P1's list.** They are separate reports of one brigade and `weighing.independence` says so, which is fair; but P1's label and `step` say "Work, Manning, Bryan ... ordered forward about 4" while Bryan (p. 412) says only that the division "was drawn up in line of battle" about 4 and "At the word Forward" followed (the citation note says so, the label does not), Lee dates the guns not the infantry, and Alexander 1887 is a recollection with "I think". The count "three regiments say about 4" for the step-off is really two orders (Work, Manning) and one line-up (Bryan).

**D4 (minor): stale not_read entry and an unused report.** `failed_searches[5]` (Hood 1880) says the narrative "was not read to the end": the local text is complete; every clock reading of Hood's book for 2 July is the "about 3 o'clock" decision (offset 133063; `find_passage.py HOOD-1880-C005 --regex "(o'clock|p\. ?m\.)"` shows none other); the entry should read `not_found` and say Hood gives no step-off hour. Longstreet's own report of 1863 (OR27-2 p. 358: "McLaws' division got into position opposite the enemy's left about 4 p. m. Hood's division was moved on farther to our right") is a report of the time with an hour for the guns' side and is not cited.

## 3 Questions of the brief
- Independence of the three "about 4" reports: they are not independent of each other in orders (one brigade, one division) and the resolution says so; the count above shows the correct reading is two orders and one line-up.
- 16:15 as the drawn point: not justified by any report; D1 pulls it earlier, Alexander 1907 and Bane pull it later; as a plain midpoint of a window it is acceptable only if so labelled (D2).
- Law "near 5", Hill "about 2" correctly rejected; Cummins and Meade (Union clock) correctly treated as checks.
