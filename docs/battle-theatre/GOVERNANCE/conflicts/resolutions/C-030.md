# C-030: when the infantry stepped off for Pickett's Charge (July 3)

**The dispute.** The plan lists 14:00 and 15:00 and recommends 15:00. The reports of the time give a wider spread than that, and the quarrel is really about how long the Confederate guns fired before the infantry went forward: about an hour, or about two.

**Who says what.**

- *Alexander* (Confederate colonel, at the guns that gave the signal; report of August 1863, printed in 1877, and memoir of 1907). By his own watch the signal guns fired at 1.00. He wrote Pickett at 1.25 and 1.40, and says Longstreet let the division go on "at ten minutes to 2", just as it reached his guns; Garnett's brigade came out of the wood at "1.50 or later". Note: the year in the printed dateline of his report reads 1868 in the text; the page image could not be fetched, so 1863 is assumed from "Camp near Orange C. H." and flagged.
- *Eshleman* (Confederate major, Washington Artillery, Peach Orchard; report of 11 August 1863). He gave the signal on an order between 1 and 2 p.m., and the infantry moved "about thirty minutes after the signal guns". That reads about 13:30, up to 14:30.
- *Peyton* (Confederate major commanding Garnett's brigade, in the line; report of 9 July 1863). At 2.30 p.m. the fire having abated, the order to advance was given, first by Pickett himself. His report dates the guns at 1.30 and their length at one hour, which no one else does: his clock runs about half an hour later than the others'.
- *Davis* (Confederate brigadier in Heth's division, which moved in line with Pickett; report of 26 August 1863). "The artillery ceased firing at 3 o'clock, and the order to move forward was given." Hill (November 1863): guns opened at 1 o'clock and "for two hours" fired, then the assault.
- *Hall, Webb, Harrow* (Union brigade commanders of Gibbon's division at the wall; reports of 13 to 17 July 1863) say the first line left the woods at 3 o'clock (Hall "exactly", Webb) or "nearly 3" (Harrow). *Hunt* (Union chief of artillery, September 1863): about 3 the column of attack formed; he had stopped the Union fire about 2.30. *Haskell* (Gibbon's aide, private letter of July 1863): the guns stopped at three almost precisely, then the infantry were reported advancing.
- Later: *Hays* (Union division commander) has the guns from nearly 2 until 4.30, and *Smyth* (his brigade) from 2 until 5; *Lang* (Confederate, rear brigade) from nearly 2 until nearly 4; *Meade's* dispatch of 8.35 that evening says the guns went on "about three hours". *Taylor* (Lee's adjutant, 1906) says the infantry advanced at 2 p.m.; this is the only text found that gives the plan's 14:00.

**Weighing.** All of these wrote within weeks except Taylor and the memoir of Alexander. The best classes of writing are on both sides of the hour: Alexander and Eshleman are early; Hall, Webb, Davis, Harrow, Hunt and Haskell are at three. The reported difference of about 20 minutes between the two armies' clocks is not applied and is too small anyway: from Alexander's 13:50 to the 3 o'clock reports is 70 minutes. Hays, Smyth and Lang all put the start of the guns an hour late (nearly 2 instead of 1), so their later hours carry the same shift. Meade's dispatch is the highest rank but is a round estimate of how long the guns fired, from headquarters, not an hour for the infantry. Fremantle's journal says it was about 2.30 when he set out for Longstreet and that the attack was repulsed when he arrived, which favours an earlier hour, but it names no step-off. Longstreet's report (p. 359) says Pickett reported ready about 2 p.m. and gives no step-off hour; Lee gives two hours of guns and no hour. Pickett wrote no report in the Official Records. Bachelder prints Webb's 3 o'clock and is not a separate witness.

**Decision.** Class irreducible, tier inferred. The record is divided by an hour; the viewer shows the window about 13:30 to 15:00. The march is drawn from 15:00, the hour given by the largest number of independent reports of the best class on both sides; that is a drawing choice, not a settlement. 13:50, 14:00 and 14:30 stay selectable, and so do 16:00, 16:30 and 17:00, which come from writers whose guns start late. The plan's 15:00 holds as the upper end and the drawn instant, not as the single value; the first delegated default (about 15:00, range 14:30 to 16:30) holds only as the drawn instant, and its range omits the early readings.

**What would change it.** A page image of Alexander's report that shows the year; a report by Pickett or a brigade commander of his division with an hour and clock; a Union signal or telegraph message timed during the advance.

**Searches that failed.** Longstreet's and Lee's reports name no hour for the step-off. No report by Pickett, Kemper, Garnett or Armistead exists in the Official Records; the reports of Pettigrew's and Trimble's brigades and of Wilcox and Lane were searched by clock expressions but not read to the end. The page image of Alexander's report was refused by the proxy. A search of Fremantle, Longstreet 1896, Battles and Leaders, the Southern Historical Society Papers, Bachelder and the state volumes found nothing further that names an hour.
