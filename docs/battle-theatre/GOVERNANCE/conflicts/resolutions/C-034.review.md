# Review of C-034 (Monterey Pass wagon counts): DEFECTS

Reviewer: independent, 2026-11-24. Validator passes. Quotes read in context.

## What is sound
- Huey ("150 wagons captured", 3 a.m. Jul 5) and Capehart ("the whole train was taken — 300 wagons, 15 ambulances") are real, exact, from officers present, written in 1863. Kilpatrick's "thirty wagons" kept, Stuart's citizen account of "400 or 500 wagons" and "not more than 40 wagons accompanied them", and the floors from the magazine (1864) and the guide (1892) are real. The 400-500 hearsay claim and Duncan's 37 are correctly weak and single-source. Irreducible class between Huey and Capehart, and tier documented for the range, are acceptable. The animation figure 150 has a source (Huey) and is labelled a drawing choice.

## Defects and exact fixes
1. **Lee's "about 40" is relabelled as "carried off" against his own words.** Lee writes "a number of wagons, said to be about 40, were captured" (OR27-2 p. 299). The resolution (P1 label, `reason_not_chosen`, `.md`, panel, default label "about 30 to 40 carried off") treats it as wagons that "went off with the Union column". Only Stuart ("not more than 40 wagons accompanied them") and Kilpatrick (thirty kept) say that. Lee's figure is a captured count, relayed ("said to be"), from a writer not at the pass, and it conflicts with Huey and Capehart as a count of captured wagons. Fix: (a) P1 `reason_not_chosen.kind` to `elsewhere` (relayed, not present) with text "Lee reports a hearsay figure of about 40 wagons captured; Stuart's citizen and Kilpatrick count 40 and 30 that stayed with the Union column"; (b) change P1 tier to inferred (the label "carried off" is the resolution's reading of Stuart and Kilpatrick, not Lee's); (c) in `default.label`, `source_panel_text` and `.md` say "Lee, a figure he had heard, gives about 40 captured; Stuart and Kilpatrick give 40 and 30 that went with the Union column"; (d) put Lee in `weighing.independence` as a Confederate figure of the same type (captured) that disagrees with 150 and 300, and add to `what_would_change_it` a Confederate return of wagons lost.
2. **Animation rule `best_supported` contradicts the weighing.** The weighing says nothing separates Huey from Capehart; drawing 150 because it is "the lower of the two" and the plan's default is a choice, not a best-supported figure. Fix: `animation.rule` to `range_end` (low end), note "Drawn at the low end of the sourced range, 150 (Huey). No source ranks Huey above Capehart; this is a drawing choice and is not a count".
3. **Huey and Capehart may count different parts of the train.** Capehart's regiment charged the train in the dark after Custer's brigade had been skirmishing with the guard, and says the train covered 8 miles; Huey, in Gregg's division sent to Kilpatrick, says the train "was reached at and near Monterey" at 3 a.m. and 150 wagons captured. The weighing says both counted "over a road of eight miles", which Huey does not say. Fix: replace that sentence with "Huey's count is for the train at and near Monterey at 3 a.m.; Capehart's is for the whole 8 miles; they may be counting different parts of one train, which is why the range stays", and mention the possibility in `P8` `basis_text`.
4. **Floors listed as support of positions whose value they do not state.** USSM-1864 ("more than one hundred wagons") under P2 (150) and Minnigh 1892 ("over two hundred") under P3 (300) state neither value. Fix: remove both from P2/P3 `support` and give them their own research positions ("more than 100 wagons" and "over 200 wagons", tier inferred, flags `weak`, `single_source`), or keep them out of the counts. Their notes already say "a floor".
5. P4 `reason_not_chosen` says Kilpatrick's report gives "no wagon count"; it gives "thirty wagons" kept. Fix: "no count of wagons captured or destroyed".
6. Huey's prisoner figure (1,600 in this scan, 1,500 in the other) exceeds the 1,360 Kilpatrick reports for the whole column; the `.md` mentions it in parentheses but then says nothing separates the two. State in `weighing` that this is a doubt about Huey's counting, not a settlement.
