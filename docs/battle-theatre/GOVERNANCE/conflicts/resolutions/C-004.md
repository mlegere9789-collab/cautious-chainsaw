# C-004. When the Second Corps reached Gettysburg, 2 July 1863

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window from 04:00 to 06:15 on 2 July, the main body drawn at 06:00. Class: weighted. Tier: documented (both ends are in documents of the time).

## The dispute

Hancock's Second Corps marched from Taneytown on the afternoon of 1 July and camped about three miles short of Gettysburg. The plan lists four values: 22:30 on 1 July, "at dawn", 06:00 and 07:00 on 2 July, and recommends "06:00 (head), tails later". It prints no sources. The first delegated pass chose a window of 06:30 to 07:00 from Webb's brigade and Hancock's report.

## What the witnesses say

**The night of 1 July.** Hancock: the corps "bivouacked for the night about 3 miles in rear of the town". Colonel Brooke (First Division, report of 15 August): his brigade reached a point about 3 miles out about 9.30 p.m., posted pickets and slept. So the plan's 22:30 on Cemetery Ridge (an encyclopaedia page that could not be read here) is contradicted.

**The first troops.** Colonel Smyth, Second Brigade of the Third Division (17 July): the brigade "was placed in position on the hills overlooking the town" at 4 a.m. Lieutenant Dent of the First Delaware, same brigade: the regiment "arrived on the field about 4 a. m.". Brooke: his brigade left at about 3 a.m. and arrived "soon after sunrise" (04:35). Colonel Devereux, Nineteenth Massachusetts: the corps marched "at daybreak of the 2d".

**The main body.** Lieutenant Haskell (Gibbon's staff, a letter of July 1863): the corps was roused at 3 a.m., and "before six the Corps was up to the field", halted beside the Taneytown road while other troops moved. Seth Williams, assistant adjutant general at army headquarters, a dispatch timed 6.15 a.m.: "The Second Corps is up and the Fifth is momentarily expected." Webb (13 July): his brigade was put in position at 6.30 a.m.

**The commander and the tails.** Hancock: he rejoined the corps before its arrival, "which took place about 7 a. m." Meade: by 7 a.m. the Second and Fifth Corps had reached the ground. Two regiments of Brooke's brigade give about 8 (McMichael) and about 10 (Scherrer). Gibbon, Caldwell and Harrow say only "on the morning of the 2d". The First Minnesota was ordered into position about 3 a.m.

## The weighing

A dispatch of the time is the best class of writing, and with Haskell's letter it puts the main body up by about six. Hancock's 7 comes from a later report by the one man who had ridden ahead and rejoined the column on the road; his hour may be his own arrival. Webb's 6.30 is a placement. The first troops (Smyth, Dent) were two hours earlier than the plan's "head at 06:00". The tails are later still. These are different units, so one hour cannot be the whole corps' arrival; the window shows the spread. The upper end is stated in the dispatch, and "before six" gives the drawn hour.

## The decision

Default: **04:00 to 06:15, the main body drawn at 06:00**; the earliest part of the corps is drawn from 04:00. The plan's 06:00 holds as the main body's upper bound but not as the head; its 22:30 is contradicted. The first delegated window does not hold: it dates a placement and Hancock's own arrival. Every plan value and the first pass's 06:30 stay listed.

## What would change it

An hour for the head of the corps in the Third Division's brigade or division records or a provost or signal log; Hancock's report with a legible date; the sources behind the plan's 22:30 and 06:00.

## Searches that failed

The Wikipedia and Epperson pages could not be read. No hour for the corps was found in the Joint Committee testimony or the Gettysburg weeklies; Hays, Caldwell, Harrow and several regiments give no hour. Freeman and Gibbon's book were not read for this point.
