# C-041. When Rodes's attack began, 1 July 1863

Researched 24 November 2026. Default: a window of about 13:00 to 14:00, with the picture at 13:30 (the middle). Class: irreducible (the record is divided and nothing separates the best witnesses). Tier: inferred (no report gives an hour for the attack itself; the hours are read from units that took part).

## The dispute

On the afternoon of 1 July, Major General Rodes's Confederate division came down onto the ridge north-west of Gettysburg and, after an artillery duel, sent its brigades against the right flank of the Union First Corps. The plan lists three hours for the start of that infantry attack: 13:30, 14:00 and 14:30, recommends 14:00 "for coherence", and the first pass kept 14:00 inside a window of 13:30 to 14:30. The plan's own keys point to a Park Service seminar paper (13:30) and to two sources it abbreviates W-FD (14:00) and SS (14:30); none could be read or identified here. This pass read the reports of the division, its brigades and regiments, the Union reports from the right of the line, and later accounts.

## What the writers say

**Rodes, Iverson, O'Neal, Ramseur, Carter and Doles.** Rodes's report, Iverson's, O'Neal's, Ramseur's and Carter's give no clock reading for the attack (checked for any hour); Rodes says Carter's guns opened first and he then "caused Iverson's brigade to advance" and gave O'Neal the order to attack. Doles says his brigade formed about 1 p.m. and took a hill on its left about 3.30.

**Brigadier General Daniel (report of 20 August 1863), Confederate clock.** His brigade, on the right behind Iverson, "arrived within [about two and a half miles of the town] about 12 m.", moved about three-quarters of a mile, formed, and after "some hour and a half" Iverson told him he was about to advance (p. 566). That works out to about 13:45 to 14:00. In the closing summary he says the brigade entered "the fight on the first day at about 1 p. m." (p. 569). The two statements do not agree with each other, which is why the hours are a spread.

**Regiments of the same division, July 1863, Confederate clock.** The 53rd North Carolina (Owens, p. 576) "in line of battle at or about 1 o'clock, when we advanced"; the 43rd (Lewis, p. 573) "at about 1 o'clock ... drawn up", then "ordered forward" after a cannonade; the 2nd North Carolina of Ramseur's reserve brigade (Williams, p. 589) went into action "about 1 p. m."; the 14th North Carolina of the same reserve brigade (Lambeth, p. 590) was moved to the front "about 2 p. m." after Iverson was beaten. The 2nd North Carolina Battalion (Brown, p. 577) says "between the hours of 12 m. and 1 p. m." and the 26th Alabama (Goodgame, p. 602) "about 12 m."; Daniel puts his own brigade two and a half miles from the town at that hour, so these date arrival or first fire. The 32nd North Carolina (Brabble, p. 571) says "about 2.30 p. m." it was drawn up and met the enemy about 4: an outlier inside Daniel's brigade.

**Union clock.** Major General Doubleday, commanding the First Corps (report of 14 December 1863): Ewell's forces "advanced about 1.30 p. m. in two deployed lines" (p. 249). Lieutenant Colonel Dobke, 45th New York on the right of the First Corps (21 August 1863): "At about 1.30 p. m. a long line of the enemy moved on the extreme right of the First Corps" (p. 734). Neither names Rodes. In his book of 1882 Doubleday gives about 1.30 for the opening of the Oak Hill battery (p. 139) and about 2.45 for O'Neal's and Doles's advance (p. 141), none for Iverson. Hoke (1887) repeats Doubleday's order of battle and puts the battery at half past one.

**About 14:30.** General Hill's report (November 1863) says "About 2.30 o'clock, the right wing of Ewell's corps made its appearance on my left" (p. 607): an arrival seen from his line. General Wilcox (letter of 1877, not on that part of the field) writes that "Rodes came upon the field at 2:30 P. M. and attacked"; he gives no source. A Park Service report of 1968 (McClean house) says the same hour with no source.

## The weighing

All the useful witnesses are official reports written within five months, so the rubric does not rank them apart. The men who advanced round their hour to "about 1 o'clock", but their units followed or sat beside Iverson's brigade and a rounded hour cannot date Iverson's first step closely; Daniel's own interval points a half hour to an hour later. The Union writers watched the attack arrive and say about 1.30 on a clock that reportedly ran some 20 minutes different from the Confederate one (an unsourced estimate; not applied). Hill, Wilcox and the Park Service give an arrival or a bare hour. Counting independent lines: the Confederate brigade chains (Daniel's brigade; Ramseur's brigade), Doubleday, Dobke, and Hill with Wilcox. No two lines agree on one hour for the attack itself, so the honest result is the span they cover: about 13:00 to 14:00.

## The decision

Default: **a window of about 13:00 to 14:00, the picture at its middle, 13:30**. The earlier bound (13:00) is source-supported, so the picture is not earlier than the earliest source-supported time, and every hour stays selectable: 13:30 and 14:00 (the plan's values, both inside the window, 14:00 as its upper bound), the research values about 13:00 and about noon to 13:00 (the latter marked as arrival or first fire) and 14:30 (marked as another act). The plan's recommended default (14:00) holds only partly: it is sourced and inside the window but is its upper end. The first delegated default (14:00 inside 13:30 to 14:30) holds partly: the window moves earlier and 14:30 leaves it; the tier inferred stands.

## What would change it

A clock reading of the advance by Rodes, Iverson, O'Neal, Carter or a staff officer, or a dispatch of 1 July timed by its sender, would fix a point. A page image showing a different hour in Daniel's, Owens's, Lewis's or Williams's reports would move a bound. The Seminar paper or the Stone Sentinels page, if a source is given there, would add a witness.

## Searches that failed

No clock reading for the attack in the reports of Rodes, Iverson, O'Neal, Ramseur, Carter or Early, in Early's later book, or in a Union report naming Rodes or Iverson with an hour; the sources W-FD and SS and the Seminar paper behind the plan's values were not read (hosts not reachable or paper not identified); the Union reports between numbers 30 and 70 were scanned for hours, not read in full; two local books (a Pennsylvania battery's "about 1 or 1.30" for the Oak Hill guns, and a map caption) were not read in context and are not used.
