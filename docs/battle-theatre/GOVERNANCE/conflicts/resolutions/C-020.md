# C-020 East Cemetery Hill assault

**The dispute.** The plan lists three readings of when Early's two brigades (Hays's Louisianians and Hoke's North Carolinians, then under Avery) assaulted East Cemetery Hill on the evening of 2 July: 19:30, 20:00 and 20:00 to 21:00. The plan recommended 19:30, which it took from web timelines.

**Who says what.**
- *Ricketts and Wiedrich*, captains whose batteries on the hill were charged, wrote, Ricketts on 30 August 1863 (Official Records 27/1 p. 894) and Wiedrich in 1863, undated (pp. 751-752), that it was "about 8 p. m." They were at the guns; they say about, so a quarter hour either way.
- *Godwin*, colonel commanding Hoke's brigade, wrote on 30 July 1863 that the brigade "moved forward to the attack" at 8 p. m. (27/2 p. 484). *Hays*, who led, wrote on 4 August that "a little before 8 p. m." he was ordered to advance (27/2 p. 480): that is the order, which comes before the arrival at the guns. *Early* (22 August 1863) says he ordered them forward "a little before dusk" when Johnson's attack on Culp's Hill was under way; his later narrative (printed 1912) says it was after sunset, which was 19:32.
- *Osborn*, chief of artillery of the corps on the same hill, wrote on 29 July 1863 "between 7 and 8 o'clock in the evening". The window ends at the same hour.
- *Schurz* (20 August 1863) heard the firing "between 8 and 9 p. m." and led two regiments across the road; *Steinwehr* (30 August) says "about 9 p. m." for the charge on his First Brigade and the battery. Both are estimates an hour or half an hour later than the batteries'.
- *Lutz*, a regimental commander in front of the hill, wrote on 4 July that "at about 7 p. m. the enemy appeared in force" and his regiment then met the charge. *Howard* (1907) says it was "after seven o'clock" when the first cry was heard; a guide book of 1892 says about seven. These give the first appearance of the enemy or only a lower bound.
- No writer gives 19:30.

**Weighing.** Reports of 1863 outrank the three later writers. The writers closest to the event (two battery commanders and two brigade commanders of opposite armies) agree on about 8, and a 20-minute difference between the two armies' clocks, which is not applied, would not change that. The later readings are two estimates (Schurz heard the fighting; Steinwehr gives about 9 for a brigade next to the battery) that the nearer witnesses outweigh; the earlier ones by a different moment (Lutz saw the enemy first; Hays gives the order) or by later memory. Because no two best witnesses truly disagree, the conflict is weighted, not irreducible.

**Decision.** Default about 20:00 (class weighted, tier documented). The step-off is a little before that and no minute is given, so nothing is drawn before sunset (19:32) and the advance is fitted to end in the charge at about 20:00. The plan's 19:30 does not hold (no source); the first delegated default holds as about 20:00, with its 19:30 lower end kept as a selectable window. Both stay selectable with 20:00-21:00, Osborn's 19:00-20:00 and the 19:00 reading.

**Searches that failed.** The pages behind the plan's 19:30 (Stone Sentinels, Wikipedia, Epperson) and the Park Service text for 20:00-21:00 were not read (hosts not reachable or not fetched). No local report or book gives 7.30. Gordon's memoir was not read.

**What would change it.** A report or log of the day that gives the signal or the step-off to the minute; a page image reading a different digit in the four reports of 8 p. m. (the second scan agrees).
