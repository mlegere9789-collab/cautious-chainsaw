# C-007: McLaws on the ridge

Researched 2026-11-24. Result: the head of McLaws's division was on Seminary Ridge by about 07:00; the whole division was closed up about 08:00 to 08:30. The evidence is weighed, not settled; the value is inferred, not documented.

## The dispute

The plan gives 08:00 and 08:30 for McLaws's division behind Seminary Ridge on the morning of 2 July 1863 and takes 08:00 as its default. Both numbers come from Freeman's appendix on the hour of Longstreet's arrival (and from a timeline page, EPP, that could not be read). The first pass found no hour in the Official Records, kept 08:00 as a marked stand-in and called the point a gap. The questions here: when did the head of the division reach the ridge, and what do 08:00 and 08:30 measure?

## What each writer says

1. **Fremantle**, a British officer with Longstreet's staff. His diary entry for 2 July (printed 1863; page 257 of the American edition of 1864): "At 7 A.M. I rode over part of the ground with General Longstreet, and saw him disposing M'Laws's division for to-day's fight." He saw it himself and kept the diary day by day. It does not say the division stood on the ridge, only that it was being placed. The text layer of the 1864 edition prints "Y A. M."; the page image of that edition and the transcription of the 1863 edition both read 7.
2. **Kershaw**, whose South Carolina brigade led the division. Report of 1 October 1863 (Official Records 27/2, p. 366): the camp at midnight was "some 2 miles" from Gettysburg; the march was ordered for 4 a.m. but he "did not leave camp until about sunrise"; he reached the hill overlooking Gettysburg and was "halted until about noon". No hour for the arrival. His article of 1888 (Battles and Leaders, p. 331) adds that his brigade was "at the head of the column".
3. **McLaws**, commanding the division. In his address of 1879 (Southern Historical Society Papers 7, p. 68) the order to march at four was "countermanded, with directions not to leave until sunrise", and his command "reached the hill overlooking Gettysburg early in the morning"; the head of his column halted within a hundred yards of Lee, and Lee and Longstreet then disagreed over the line the division was to take (a scene Fremantle's 7 a.m. may be). In an undated statement that Taylor printed in 1906 (p. 197): "Not long after sunrise I moved forward, and before 8 a.m. the head of my division reached Seminary Ridge, where General Lee was in person." An upper bound, not an hour.
4. **Longstreet.** In a letter of 20 April 1875, quoted by Taylor (p. 198): "two divisions nor myself did not reach General Lee until 8 a.m. on the 2d". That is wrong for himself (Fremantle has him with Lee at 5 a.m.). In his book of 1896 (pp. 362, 378) McLaws's and Hood's commands filed off the road "at sunrise" and reached Lee's headquarters "about sunrise". Taylor says the two accounts "cannot be reconciled".
5. **Hood**, letter of 28 June 1875, "from memory" (his book of 1880, p. 56): his troops "together with McLaws's Division" reached the town "before or at sunrise".
6. **Alexander**, of the reserve artillery. His report of 3 August 1863 (Official Records 27/2, p. 429) gives only the artillery's own arrival: "where we arrived at 9 a. m. on July 2". His memoir of 1907 (p. 388) says Longstreet's troops, "marching again at dawn on the 2d", "arrived near the field between 6 and 8 A.M." He was behind the infantry and does not say how he knew.
7. **The Meade family narrative** (1913, p. 69): the two divisions "left at daylight" and "about eight o'clock halted in the fields in the rear of the seminary". No source is named.
8. **Freeman**, historian, 1935. The head of McLaws's column "was probably on the field by 6 a.m."; but the command "does not seem to have been well closed, and the other division was probably not in position until 8 or 8:30". He adds that Hood and McLaws "rode ahead of their troops and probably confused the time of their own arrival with that of the appearance of their troops". These are the plan's 08:00 and 08:30.
9. **Dickert**, of Kershaw's brigade, 1899: when the troops "were aroused from their slumbers" on 2 July "the sun had long since shot its rays" over Gettysburg. Freeman uses it for a late start; it dates the men waking in camp, 36 years later, and states no arrival.

Not read: the Epperson timeline and Hessler's book (the host is not reachable, the book is not available).

## Weighing

Two writings of the time outrank the rest: Fremantle's diary of the day and Kershaw's report, written 91 days after. Both were there, both say how they knew, and they do not contradict each other: Kershaw gives the start, Fremantle the hour at which the division was being placed. Everything else is reminiscence 12 to 44 years later (McLaws, Hood, Longstreet, Alexander, Dickert) or later history (the Meade narrative, Freeman, whose figures are computed). Clocks: Kershaw, McLaws, Hood, Longstreet and Alexander wrote on the Confederate clock; Fremantle's watch is not named; the difference of about 20 minutes between the armies' clocks is not applied, because it is small beside the hour or more between the default and the plan's 08:00.

What 08:00 and 08:30 measure is the closing up of the whole division: Freeman says the column was not well closed, Longstreet's 8 a.m. of 1875 and the Meade narrative fit that, McLaws says the head came before 8, and a diary of the day has the division being placed by 7. The sunrise of Hood and Longstreet is contradicted by Kershaw's report (the brigade left camp only about sunrise) and by McLaws himself; both generals rode ahead of their troops. Dickert's late start is likewise a memory against a report of the time. Left aside as other events: Wilcox's report of 17 July 1863 (McLaws's division forming line beside his brigade at about 2 p.m., Official Records 27/2, p. 617), the artillery's 9 a.m., and Colonel Johnston's joining the head of the corps about 9 a.m., which a reply to Longstreet in the Southern Historical Society Papers 5 (p. 183) reports in his words: that dates the guiding of the march to the right, not an arrival.

Nobody gives a clock for the first arrival, so the value is an inference: the march began about sunrise (04:35) from a camp 2 miles out for the leading brigade (Kershaw) and 4 for the rear of the division (Longstreet's report), the diary has the division being placed at 7, McLaws says before 8, and Freeman and Alexander give 6 as the earliest bound.

## Decision

Class **weighted**, tier **inferred**. Default: **about 07:00**, the head of McLaws's division on the ridge, the whole division up by 08:00 to 08:30. 07:00 is the one hour of the day that puts the division on the ground; it is an upper bound for the head's arrival and the instant the picture uses, never earlier. All other values stay selectable with their reasons: 08:00 and 08:30 (the plan's; the closing up), before 08:00 (McLaws), about 06:00 (Freeman's estimate; not the floor), 06:00 to 08:00 (Alexander), and sunrise about 04:35 (later memory, contradicted).

The plan's default (08:00) holds only partly: it is the end of the arrival, not its start. The first delegated default (a stand-in) does not hold: the point is not a gap.

## What would change it

A report, letter or diary of the time that gives an hour for the first or the last brigade of McLaws's division on the ridge (Semmes's, Barksdale's, Wofford's, Cabell's battalion, McLaws's own letters of July 1863), or a source that dates the scene in which Lee and Longstreet placed the division.

## Searches that failed

No report of McLaws, Barksdale, Semmes or Wofford for Gettysburg is in the Official Records index; the reports of Kershaw, Maffett, Nance, Cabell and the batteries give no hour for the arrival. A search of all local texts, Sorrel's memoir, the Park Service texts, the books of Hood and Polley and the newspapers of 1863 found no other hour for McLaws's division. The Epperson timeline and Hessler's book could not be read. A page image of Fremantle's entry was refused by the network from one host; the same scan was then read as a PDF and the page looked at.
