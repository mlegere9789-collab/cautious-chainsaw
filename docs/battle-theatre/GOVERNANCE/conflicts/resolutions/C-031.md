# C-031: when the last charge on Culp's Hill ended (July 3)

**The dispute.** The plan gives 10:00 and noon and recommends 10:00. The reports show that the plan's two hours belong to two other moments of the same morning, and that the last charge came between them.

**Who says what.**

- *The last charge.* Geary (Union, commanding the division on the crest; report of 29 July 1863): "At 10.25 o'clock two brigades of Johnson's division ... charged upon our line on the right ... This was the last charge", and in his summary "This final charge, made at 10.30 a. m.". Kane (brigade on the right, where it struck; report of 6 July): after seven hours of fire, "until about 10.30 o'clock, when the enemy made their last determined effort by charging in column of regiments", Steuart's brigade leading; afterwards "the battle was over", with a desultory fire for some time. Rickards (29th Pennsylvania, in the line; 4 July): about 10.30 the enemy advanced to charge, Steuart's leading. Greene (Third Brigade, in the works; 12 July): the attack that began at 4 a.m. "continued until 10.30 a. m., when the enemy retired". Slocum (corps commander; August 1863): the engagement continued until 10.30. All wrote in 1863 and used the Union clock.
- *10:00.* Ruger (division commander on the right; report of 5 December 1863): the state of things "continued until about 10 a. m."; he then ordered two regiments to try the enemy on the right of the breastworks, the Colgrove charge. Hunt (chief of artillery; 27 September): the batteries "continued firing at intervals until 10 a. m., when the infantry succeeded in driving out the enemy and reoccupied their positions". Meade (1 October): the contest "continued until after 10 a. m.". Ruger's 10 dates his order for the counter-attack. Hunt, relaying twelve weeks later, gives a round 10 for the driving-out of the enemy, which Greene, Slocum and Geary place at 10.30; Meade's 'after 10' is only a lower bound that 10.30 satisfies. None of the three says the last charge ended at 10.
- *Noon.* Maulsby (Colonel, 1st Potomac Home Brigade, in the rifle-pits on the right; 4 July): his regiment held the pits "until the enemy's fire wholly ceased, about 12 m." Williams (Confederate colonel, Nicholls's brigade; 28 August): his brigade engaged "at intervals until 12 m.", when it was ordered back to the ravine. This is the end of all firing and the Confederate withdrawal, about an hour and a half after the charge.
- *About 11:00.* Pierce's history of 1914 says Johnson held until eleven. A later history with no source named.

**Weighing.** The last charge is dated by five Union writers of July and August 1863 who were at or beside the point struck, two of them (Geary, Kane) naming it as the last charge and giving 10.25 or about 10.30, and three (Greene, Slocum, Rickards) giving the same hour for the end or the advance. The 10:00 and noon figures do not contradict them once the event is read: 10:00 is Ruger's counter-attack order, with Hunt's and Meade's relayed, rounded figures for the recovery; noon is the end of the firing. No Confederate officer's report gives an hour for the last charge: Johnson says only that no further assault was made; Williams's noon is his brigade's own fire and move. Union clocks were reported to run about 20 minutes from Confederate; the difference is not applied and would not join 10:30 to noon.

**Decision.** Class settled, tier documented: about 10:30 for the last charge and the enemy's retirement. 10:00 and noon stay selectable, each labelled for the event it really dates, and so does 11:00. The plan's 10:00 and the first delegated default's 10:00 do not hold as the end of the charge: they date Ruger's counter-attack order, and Hunt's and Meade's figures are rounded and relayed. The 10:30 that the first delegated default added is the default.

**What would change it.** A Confederate report of Johnson's division (Steuart, Walker, Daniel, O'Neal) that gives an hour for its last charge, or a signal message of the morning, would test the Union clock.

**Searches that failed.** No Confederate report read gives an hour for the last charge; Johnson, Steuart's, Walker's, Jones's, Daniel's and O'Neal's reports were searched for clock words but not read to the end. A search of the other local books found only Pierce's 11:00.
