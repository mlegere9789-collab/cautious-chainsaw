# C-043: When was the Union retreat ordered on 1 July?

## The dispute

Late in the afternoon the First and Eleventh Corps fell back from the ridges west and north of Gettysburg to Cemetery Hill. The plan lists 15:45, 16:00, 16:30 and 17:00 for "the Union retreat order" and recommends 16:00. The first delegated default kept 16:00 inside a window of 15:45 to 17:00 and noted that the hours belong to "related but different orders". Read in context, that is the answer: they are not rival times for one order.

## What each witness says

**Wadsworth** (First Division), report of 4 July 1863: outflanked and short of ammunition, "at 3.45 o'clock I ordered the command to retire". He gave this order to his own division. Doubleday prints the sentence in his report as Wadsworth's, so it is one line of knowledge.

**Howard** (commanding the field), report of 31 August 1863: "About 4 p. m. I sent word to General Doubleday that, if he could not hold out longer, he must fall back, fighting, to Cemetery Hill", a conditional word; then "At 4.10 p. m., finding that I could hold out no longer, and that the troops were already giving way, I sent a positive order to the commanders of the First and Eleventh Corps to fall back gradually". At 4.30 "the columns reached Cemetery Hill". His article in Battles and Leaders (1887) reprints the 4.10 sentence; it is the same line, not a second witness.

**Doubleday** (First Corps), report of 14 December 1863: "About 4 p. m. the enemy, having been strongly re-enforced, advanced"; "I now gave orders to fall back". He gives no hour for his order and says the division commanders' statements "are slightly conflicting".

**Robinson** (Second Division), report of 18 July 1863: his division held "from about noon until nearly 5 p. m., when I received orders to withdraw", and the order did not reach him "until all other troops (except Stewart's battery) had commenced moving to the rear". This dates the receipt by the last division.

**McFarland** (151st Pennsylvania), report of 15 March 1864: he ordered the remnant of his regiment to fall back and was hit "at this instant, 4.30 p. m.", about 20 paces from the seminary: a regiment's fall-back at about 16:30.

**Schurz** says Howard's order to withdraw reached him as his corps was already falling back, with no hour.

## Weighing

Two witnesses give an order with a clock reading and each gave it himself: Wadsworth at 3.45 for his division, Howard at 4.10 for both corps. The other hours are the same retreat seen at another moment: "about 4" is a conditional word and the enemy's advance; "nearly 5" is a receipt; 4.30 is a regiment's fall-back and the columns' arrival on the hill. Robinson's "nearly 5" and Howard's 4.30 cannot both be exact clock readings of the same movement; "nearly 5" is a round figure and the clocks of the two corps are not shown to agree, so the receipt hour is kept as a separate, unreconciled value and does not enter the window. No report found gives a general order at 4.30; the plan's "W-FD" source is a web page that was not read. The two armies' clocks reportedly differed by about 20 minutes; that is not applied.

## Decision

**Default: a window of 15:45 to 16:10**, class weighted, tier documented; each end is flagged single-source. The first retirement is drawn at 15:45, the earliest order with a clock reading; the corps-wide order of 16:10 follows. The PDF default (16:00) does not hold as an order: it is inferred from the two "about 4" statements. The first delegated window holds partly: it names the orders but its late end is the last division's receipt. Seven values are listed; the printed arrival on Cemetery Hill (17:00) is not touched.

## What would change it

A dated order, message or journal of 1 July with a clock reading for the general order, or a statement by Howard's or Doubleday's staff of the hour the order reached Schurz and Doubleday.

## Searches that failed

No other order with a clock reading in the Official Records (Rowley, Biddle, Gates, Stone, Coulter, Dana, Cooper, Wainwright and Schurz give none for the general order); later histories give hours near 3.30 to 4.30 for the retreat without an order; the plan's 16:30 source was not read.
