# C-029 Cannonade end

**The dispute.** The great cannonade of 3 July (Confederate signal at about 1 p. m.) ended at some time before Pickett's and Pettigrew's infantry came out of the woods. The plan lists 14:00, 14:30 and 15:00 and recommends 14:30. The first delegated pass added 16:30 to 17:00 from two Union reports.

**Who says what.**
- *Hunt* (chief of artillery, 27 September 1863, Official Records 27/1 p. 239; he also writes: "About 3 p. m., and soon after the enemy's fire had ceased, he formed a column of attack"): "About 2. 30 p. m. ... I directed that the fire should be gradually stopped, which was done, and the enemy soon slackened his fire also." That is the Union order; the enemy's end comes after it.
- *Peyton* (Garnett's brigade, 9 July 1863): "At 2.30 p. m. , the artillery fire having to some extent abated" the order to advance was given; he also has the fire open at 1.30 and last one hour (27/2 p. 385).
- *Devereux* (19th Massachusetts, 7 July): "Just about 3 o'clock the enemy's cannonade slackened."
- *Hancock* counts "an hour and forty-five minutes" from about 1 o'clock before the enemy's fire "became less furious" (about 14:45). *Gibbon* has two hours from 1 o'clock, then the fire "on both sides slackened"; *Lee* about two hours from about 1 p. m.; *Cabell* "over two hours"; *Meade* "over two hours". *Howard* has his guns on Cemetery Hill cease at 3.30 p. m. and the enemy's "soon after".
- *Hays* and *Smyth*, one division, date the opening about 2 p. m., an hour later than the others, and the end 4.30 and 5 p. m.; their fire lasted two and a half to three hours, longer than the two hours of the others, so the later start accounts for only half of the difference. They stay selectable.
- Nothing gives 14:00 for an end: the 2 p. m. in the reports is where the fire began on Hays's and Smyth's front.

**Weighing.** The gun commanders and the corps and division commanders on the line give an end of about 14:30 (the order) to about 15:00 (the fire dying away), counted by hour or by duration from an "about 1". They are of one class and none is a diary of the afternoon, but Hunt's own report has both ends (the Union fire stopped about 2.30; the enemy's fire ceased soon before about 3 p. m.), so the window is the spread of hedged readings of one fire that died away. Howard's 3.30 (his own guns on Cemetery Hill) lies outside it and stays selectable. The Union and Confederate clocks reportedly differ by about 20 minutes; that is not applied.

**Decision.** The end is shown as the window 14:30 to 15:00, drawn from 14:30 (the earliest clock reading, Hunt's order), class weighted, tier inferred (the day is not in the sentences quoted). The plan's 14:30 holds as the lower bound and the Union ceasefire, its 15:00 as the upper bound; 14:00 has no source; 16:30 to 17:00 stays as the Hays and Smyth reading.

**Searches that failed.** The sources of the plan's 14:00 (Hess and others) could not be read. No report gives an end before 2.30 p. m.; no report of the Confederate battalions gives a clock for the last gun.

**What would change it.** A battery journal or diary of the afternoon giving the last-gun hour.
