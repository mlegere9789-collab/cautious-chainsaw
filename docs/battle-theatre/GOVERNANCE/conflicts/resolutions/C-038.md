# C-038: wounded at the Spangler farm

## The dispute
The plan's row lists five values for the wounded at the Spangler farm hospital: 1,000; 1,500; 1,800; 1,900; 2,000, and recommends 1,500. The first delegated pass found no count and left a range of 1,000 to 2,000 with no headline figure.

## Which hospital
The farm is George Spangler's house and farm on the Baltimore Pike side of the field, the Eleventh Corps hospital (the National Park Service boundary study of 1988 says the 11th Corps hospital there was where General Armistead died). It is not the Twelfth Corps hospital, which was at George Bushman's farm.

## The figures
- 1,900 and 1,800: one report, one table. J. H. Douglas, Associate Secretary of the U.S. Sanitary Commission, signed it at Washington on 15 Aug 1863, 42 days after the battle, writing to the Commission's General Secretary. He says the hospitals were visited by himself and colleagues. On page 24 he writes that the Eleventh Corps hospital "occupied the house and farm of Geo. Spangler" and "contained 1,900 wounded, of whom 100 were said to be confederates". His table gives the corps as 1,800 Union, 100 Confederate, 1,900 in all. So 1,800 is the Union column of the same count, not a rival figure. He adds that "These figures are approximative only, as that was all we sought for at the time". The count is of wounded present, at a date the report does not give. It is not a count of men admitted, treated or dead.
- 1,000, 1,500 and 2,000: no source found. They appear in the plan and the first pass; the seed notes attribute 1,000 to a diary of Dr. Brinton, but that diary was not read and the working notes are only leads. A marker said by a web search to give 1,400 for the corps hospitals was not read.
- No Official Records report, Letterman's report, the Medical and Surgical History tables, or the Park Service texts read give a count for this hospital.

## Weighing
The only count of the time is Douglas's, written by a man who went to the hospitals, six weeks after the battle. Nothing of equal or better standing disagrees. It is one source, so the value carries a single-source flag, and its rough, undated nature is stated in the viewer. The other values have no source at all.

## Decision
Class weighted, tier documented. Default: about 1,900 wounded (1,800 Union, 100 said to be Confederate), with the range 1,000 to 2,000 still shown and all five values selectable. The plan's 1,500 does not hold (no source), and the first delegated "no headline" default does not hold, because a count of the time exists. 1,800 is kept as the Union-only part of the same figure.

## What would change it
A dated hospital return or surgeon's report giving the census on a named day (Brinton, Armstrong, the Eleventh Corps medical director), Brinton's diary or the Kirkwood book's appendix read at the source, or any source for 1,000, 1,500 or 2,000. Counts on several days could turn the default into a dated series.

## Searches that failed
Official Records parts 1 to 3, Letterman, the Medical and Surgical History and the whole local library (Spangler near each figure): nothing besides the Sanitary Commission report. Three guessed nps.gov pages failed; an npshistory.com seminar essay was blocked; Scott's 1927 book names the hospital and gives no count; historynet.com, hmdb.org markers, Kirkwood's book and Brinton's diary were not read.
