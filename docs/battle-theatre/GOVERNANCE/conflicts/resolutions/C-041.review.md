Defects
1. The lower bound 13:00 is not the start of Rodes's attack, yet it is presented as "source-supported" and gates the animation. Read in context:
   - Lewis (OR27-2 p.573): "At about 1 o'clock, July 1, we were drawn up in line of battle, about 2 or 2 1/2 miles from Gettysburg. After a brief cannonade ... we were ordered forward." 1 o'clock is the formation, not the advance. Position P4's label ("say they advanced or went into action") and basis_text ("advance or entry into action about 1 p.m.") misstate it.
   - Williams (p.589) is the 2nd NC of Ramseur's reserve brigade; its "went into action about 1 p. m." is a hour the resolution's own where/notes say cannot be the start, and the other Ramseur witness (Lambeth, same brigade) says about 2 p.m. Williams counts as the second group for the lower bound.
   - Daniel's "about 1 p.m." (p.569) is contradicted by his own p.566 interval (12 m. + about 3/4 mile + some hour and a half = about 13:45-14:00), which the md admits; the only witness who dates Iverson's first step (the start of the attack) is the upper bound. Doles (p.581, "formed into line of battle about 1 p. m.", engaged 3.30) is another formation hour, mentioned only in the md.
   The md weighing concedes the rounded 1 o'clock "cannot date Iverson's first step", so the panel line "four Confederate units say about 1 p.m." and the animation note ("the earlier bound (13:00) is source-supported") claim more than the sources give for the attack start. Either the window's lower bound should be labelled deployment/first contact, with the attack start window narrower, or the evidence per witness stated (formed / advanced / engaged).
2. Midpoint point. rule "midpoint" is allowed by PLAN section 5, and 13:30 is also Doubleday's and Dobke's hour, so I do not call it invented; but the 13:30 sits inside a window whose lower half is formation hours (defect 1), so it is not shown to be a time of the attack start.
