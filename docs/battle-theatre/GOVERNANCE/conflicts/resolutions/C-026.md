# C-026: When did the Sherfy barn burn?

## The dispute

The plan's register (page 62) gives two values for the burning of Joseph Sherfy's barn on the Emmitsburg Road: the evening of 2 July (its recommended default) and 3 July. The barn held wounded men from the afternoon fight of 2 July, and many of them died in the fire. The Official Records never print the name Sherfy. The first pass of this research therefore left the date as a marked gap.

## What each writer says

**The Gettysburg Compiler, 20 July 1863** (a local weekly, 18 days after the battle; the writer was not on the field). It lists the people who "suffered in the destruction of their houses and barns by fire, while the battle was raging", Sherfy among them, and says about twenty wounded Philadelphia Zouaves perished in his barn. This is the only writing of the time that names the barn. It gives no day and no hour. The text is damaged in the middle of the line (it reads "bouses", "tyas"); I quote only the words that read correctly and could not look at the page image, because the image host refused the connection.

**Captain Edward Bowen, 114th Pennsylvania, report of 13 July 1863.** The regiment fought at the Sherfy buildings on 2 July. Bowen reports men missing "whom I have no doubt were killed and their bodies burned when the barn was burned down". He names neither barn nor owner and gives no day. It is his belief, from "own observations and memory".

**Captain Patrick Hart, 15th New York Battery, report of 2 August 1863.** On 3 July, after the infantry attack, he says: "I candidly believe it was I who caused his caissons to explode and set the barn on fire". He does not name the barn, and Lieutenant McMahon puts the battery that day "on the left of the cemetery", so it may be another barn.

**Later writers.** Luther Minnigh (guide, 1892): "The Sherfy barn was burned during the 3d of July", with no source. Robert Miller (1909): "the old barn was burned during the battle of the second day", no source. Kathleen Georg (Park Service, 1981): Sherfy's barn burned on 2 July, with no source. Bachelder (1873) and the 114th Pennsylvania's regimental narrative (1914) say the barn burned with dead or wounded Zouaves in it and give no day. E. C. Strouss of the 57th Pennsylvania walked out on 6 July and found the barn gone; he says the shells set it afire. Captain Frank Moran (73d New York, 1892) tells what a Washington Artillery soldier told him in Virginia, 29 years before: the barn "had taken fire during the cannonade", wounded men who had crept in were rescued, and no day is given.

## The weighing

No writer of the time gives a day. The two that matter (the Compiler and Bowen) put the fire inside the battle and not before wounded Zouaves were in the barn; those Zouaves fought at the farm from the afternoon of 2 July (Moran and the 114th's narrative). So the fire could fall during that fight or after it. Birney's report (the brigade held the orchard "until nearly dusk") says nothing of the barn and is not used for the bound. Hart's own report of 2 July also mentions "the barn" unnamed, so his barn cannot be tied to Sherfy on either day. The writers that give a day are far later and cite nothing, and they disagree: 2 July (1909, 1981) against 3 July (1892). The 20-minute difference between the two armies' clocks plays no part, since no one gives an hour. Hart's 3 July claim is the only report of the time with a 3 July fire, but it names no barn.

## Decision

Class irreducible; tier inferred. Default: a window, from the 2 July afternoon fight (once wounded men were in the barn; the fire may fall during that fight, as Moran's gunner allows, or after it) to the end of 3 July (the end of the battle). No single instant is drawn: the barn stands until the window opens and burns, marked "time uncertain", anywhere inside it. All values stay selectable: 2 July (the plan's "evening" is not in any source), 3 July, 3 July afternoon (Hart's barn), and 3 July morning (a lead only, see below).

The plan's default (2 July evening) holds only partly: it is not disproved and is the earliest day the record allows, but no source says "evening". The first delegated default (a marked gap, plausible-procedural) is replaced, because the Compiler, which the first pass did not read for this, bounds the fire.

## What would change it

Joseph Sherfy's own damage claim (Pennsylvania State Archives), a diary or letter from the farm, a Confederate report naming the burning barn, or the 77th New York soldier's own account would give a day.

## Searches that failed or were refused

Sherfy does not occur in any part of the Official Records, and no barn-and-fire passage there names it. The Gettysburg weeklies of 1863 name the barn only in the Compiler of 20 July. Bates (1875) and the 141st Pennsylvania's history have no Sherfy. The Stone Sentinels page, from which an earlier note takes a 77th New York soldier with a morning of 3 July, could not be opened (refused); that morning value stays listed, not weighed. The Chronicling America search, the nps.gov and npshistory.com pages tried, and the archive.org page images were not reachable. A 1942 magazine story has the barn burning on the night of 2 July but gives no source and is not used.
