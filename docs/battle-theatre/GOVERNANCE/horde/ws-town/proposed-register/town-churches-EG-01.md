# town-churches-EG-01: The church whose steeple held the sharpshooters (bld-town-belfry)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| ID | town-churches-EG-01 |
| Requirement IDs | R12, OA10 |
| Category | open gap (identity) and source silence |
| Description | Six accounts say sharpshooters held "a steeple in the town" on 1 July and "the steeples and belfreys of the churches" on 2 July (town-churches rows 0150, 0151, 0152): Wiedrich's battery on East Cemetery Hill (two officers and several men hit, horses killed, a shell fired at the steeple), a dispatch and a Times special of Thursday 2 July, the Times account of that day, Willis (during the night of 1-2 July, "houses and steeples of public buildings") and, not as a sniper post, Col. Morrow in "a church steeple" until 3 July (Park Service study). None names the church; the owner plan's record says "Not identified". Towers on record: the Catholic church had a Roman cupola of about 60 ft (documented, row 0125); the German Reformed church has an open lantern with a domed cap on its ridge in LOC 2012647712 (rows 0188, 0190), a photograph taken "from Cemetery Hill at about position of right gun of Wiedrich's Battery", the position of the battery whose men the sharpshooters in the church steeple hit; Christ Lutheran and St. James' show a yellow circle (a cupola, inferred) on the plan of 1886; the Presbyterian, Methodist and United Presbyterian churches show none. The photograph is an observation, not an identification. |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | No text names the church; no source gives a dimension of the steeple (rows 0141, 0145); the captions of the photographs 2014646001 and 2014646002 name no church (search rows 0028); no photograph of July 1863 is captioned as showing the steeple that the sharpshooters held. |
