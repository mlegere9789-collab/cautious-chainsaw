# town-public-buildings-EG-03: the clock of the court house tower in July 1863

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| ID | town-public-buildings-EG-03 |
| Requirement IDs | R12, OA10 |
| Category | source conflict (a statement about a later state) and open gap |
| Description | The History of 1886 says the steeple of the building of 1859 contained "bell and town clock" (p. 43; row 0022) and, in the borough chapter, that on 27 January 1868 H. D. Wattles presented to the borough "the elegant town clock, now in the cupola of the court house" (p. 193; row 0023); the council of 1829 had a man wind "the town clock" (the building it stood in is not stated). Tillie Pierce, writing in 1889, heard "the Court House clock" strike in 1863 (row 0024). A round dial-like element on the tower of the photographs of July 1863 (rows 0025 and 0027) fits a clock, but the captions do not name the building. Whether the clock of July 1863 was the one given in 1868 or an earlier one is not stated. |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | No text of 1859-1863 describes the clock or its dial; the photographs are small and their captions do not name the building. |
| Evidence searched | History of 1886 pp. 43, 191, 193; Tillie Pierce 1889; the weeklies of 1857-1859 and 1863 (clock, bell, tower: only the tower frame of November 1858 and the belfry of the old court house); the photographs 2014646001 and 2018671576 looked at. |
| Autonomous substitute applied | None applied. |
| Strongest evidence-based reconstruction | A tower with a cupola (documented); a dial and a town clock drawn as inferred, marked; the 1868 clock is an addition, not drawn in July 1863. |
| Confidence tier | cupola documented; clock and dial inferred |
| Competing values | A clock from 1859 (History p. 43, Pierce) against a clock first given in 1868 (History p. 193). |
| Product marking | Clock face drawn with the inferred marker; the 1868 clock is listed as a later addition. |
| Visual and audio impact | The tower seen from the south and the sound of the hour in the town scene (the strike of the clock is a memoir of 1889, inferred). |
