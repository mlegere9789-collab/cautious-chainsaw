| Description | The Alms House stood in July 1863 (a bull and a heifer "came to the Alms House in July last", notice of Jacob Culp, Steward, 26 October 1863; the steward's list of paupers of 3 March 1863) and was built as the county hospital in 1817-18, with other buildings "at different times" and a new part in 1878 (History of 1886). The Warren plate (surveyed 1868-69, revised 1873) draws nine black building symbols at the label COUNTY ALMSHOUSE with seven letters beside them: B three times, W three times, S once (legend: W wood, B brick, S stone); Bachelder (1873) calls the group "a group of brick buildings". The Hopkins county sheet of 1858 marks the "Alms Houses" with about five small marks. No local text lists the buildings, their uses (main house, asylum, workhouse, barn) or their years. Lieut. Wilkeson was carried to the Almshouse on 1 July and died there (row 0293). Bancroft's report puts a battery beside the poor-house on "the York road"; the Park Service puts the Poor Farm Grounds on Old Harrisburg Road (rows 0277 and 0279). |
