| Category | open gap, source conflict and unsourced dates |
| Description | Presbyterian (H-01): the Sanitary Commission says the Cavalry Corps Hospital (Dr. Rulison) occupied the church and two school-houses and held 300 wounded (row 0072, no day); the congregation's own history says the church "was used as a Cavalry Hospital" (row 0304); the Christian Commission says the First Corps hospitals in the town, where many had been wounded on the first day, occupied the Presbyterian church, the Catholic church and the Court-house (row 0303): **the attribution to a corps differs** and is carried as a conflict. **Start (second pass):** two neighbours brought food to the wounded in the church from the afternoon of 1 July (row 0302, documented); the owner plan's "set up June 30" and "fell to Confederates evening Jul 1" are stated by no non-lead source (their source, HMDB 17939, is a lead). **End:** the church was occupied "during the greater part, if not the whole, of the month of July, until the tent hospital, Camp Letterman, was established" (row 0301); it was a church again on 19 November 1863 (row 0074); the day is open (row 0077). Christ Lutheran: first opened on the first day and occupied for nearly six weeks by the Second Division of the First Corps (row 0329: about 10 to 12 August); the surviving patients were taken to the seminary some weeks after the battle (row 0331); the order to move came on a Monday, no date (row 0102). St. Francis Xavier (H-02): first occupied before noon of 1 July and used for several weeks (row 0132); the Sisters left Emmitsburg on the Sunday after the third day (row 0133); the end is not dated. St. James': five weeks of hospital use (row 0322), after 3 July (row 0234), damages paid in December 1863 (row 0235) and a service on 6 August (row 0236): consistent. Methodist: a hospital, no date (row 0169). Reformed and United Presbyterian: hospitals, no date (rows 0194, 0313); every church was filled on 4 to 7 July by the Christian Commission's general statement (inferred rows). |
