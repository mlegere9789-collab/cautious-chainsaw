# town-public-buildings-EG-09: the legend of the Warren plate: "every object is represented as it was at the time of the battle"

| ID | town-public-buildings-EG-09 |
| Requirement IDs | R12, OA10 |
| Category | reading of a source (affects every group that uses the plate) |
| Description | The printed legend of the Warren plate reads: "Every object is represented here as near as possible as it was at the time of the battle. Near each house is a letter, indicating its structure: W stands for wood, B for brick and S for stone." The sheet is a survey of 1868-69 revised on the ground in 1873 (LOC title). The legend is the map-maker's statement of intent; it is not a check of each building, and a building put up between July 1863 and 1873 would carry a letter too. The almshouse symbols are the case at hand (EG-04). |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | No independent date of each building drawn on the plate exists in the local library. |
| Evidence searched | the legend read from the image; the plate at the almshouse read at 2x and 7x; the History and the Park Service texts for dates of the buildings. |
| Autonomous substitute applied | Delegated decision DD-TWNPUB-02: the legend is read as intent, not as proof; the two-map rule of PLAN section 4 stands. |
| Strongest evidence-based reconstruction | Material letters used as observations (inferred); existence taken from the two-map rule or from texts. |
| Confidence tier | inferred (letters); open gap (existence of a building known only from the plate) |
