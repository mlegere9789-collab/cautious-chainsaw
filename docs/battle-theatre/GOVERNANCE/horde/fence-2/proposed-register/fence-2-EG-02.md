| Description | S-044, S-045. The PDF prints "Fences knocked down, iron fences and monuments destroyed, obelisks toppled, tombstones laid flat" on Jul 2, detail times not given. Re-matched evidence: Howard (OR27-1-R243 p.706): on Jul 3 "At about 1 p. m. a terrific cannonade opened" and the shells burst "overturning tombstones, and smashing fences" (documented, Jul 3; the date is inside the widened quote). Cemetery Hill was also under heavy bombardment from about 16:00 on Jul 2 for two hours (Steinwehr R250 p.722; Dobke R256 p.735), a possible source of Jul 2 damage that no source ties to fences. Gettysburg Compiler 20 Jul 1863 and Adams Sentinel 21 Jul 1863 (local OCR): tombstones thrown from their position or broken, graves turned up by ploughing shot, "tasteful railings and other ornamental work around the lots were somewhat shattered" (undated within the battle; three-column OCR, join editorial). Hospital Scenes (1864): the iron railings were "bent and broken into the smallest pieces" and "our soldiers had carefully laid down the highest monuments" (deliberately lowered, not toppled by shells; the NPS CLR adds that General Howard ordered the headstones laid flat). Haskell (July 1863): fences prostrate on the evening of Jul 2 in the fields in view. NPS CLR (Gettysburg National Cemetery): damage to grave-plot fences and removal of planks from the fences along Baltimore Pike; the brick gatehouse had smashed windows and holes in the brick walls (undated; the gatehouse search over every local text was repeated, nothing else found). The quotation "the fences are all down" attributed to the Adams Sentinel on Wikipedia was searched for in the local Sentinel and Compiler OCR and not found: not used. |
