Wheatfield: stone walls and fences are documented (Sweitzer R193 p.611, Crawford R218 p.653, de Trobriand R152 p.520/521, Burbank R211 p.645, Brooke R092 p.401, Kershaw R432 pp.366-368) and the NPS Record of Treatment vol. II adds a fence round the field: on the afternoon of Jul 2 the 61st NY found the wheatfield "bounded by fence" and "scaled the fence", the 118th PA marching on Wheatfield Road was "removing the fences", and the north and west perimeter is a wooden fence on the 1863 Bachelder Map (pdf p.102-103); nothing places each wall or says which is the 17th Maine wall. Kershaw formed at about 15:00 in front of and behind a stone wall and advanced "about 4 o'clock" through "numerous fences"; the NPS ties his stone house and barn to the Rose farm buildings and records the Rose yard picket fence as an obstacle (photographs); the 3rd Arkansas turned over the bottom rails of a fence (soldier's recollection via the NPS). The Trostle walls of Bigelow's stand are documented by Milton (R320 p.886, "two stone walls met at an obtuse angle", held at all hazards; park at 21:00), McGilvery (R318 p.882: batteries ordered back 350 yd, new line 400 yd to the rear) and the NPS inventory (stone wall adjacent to the gate; four guns lost). Little Round Top: Weed's brigade built a wall half way down the slope "At night" on Jul 2 (Garrard R217 p.652); Fisher's brigade a wall across the ravine "In the morning" of Jul 3 (R220 p.658); a stone fence enclosing a wood in front (D. P. Hancock R213 p.647, about 17:30); the boulders of the slopes were the material (NPS-CLI-LRT) and the present walls were restacked in 1882-1887; no rail use on the hill. The shard's Hall citation (5th Alabama, R526) is a Jul 1 passage. |

## Round 3 addendum (third reading, 2026-11-15/16)

S-032: the NPS Historic Structures Report on the Slyder farm (1959) puts Union sharpshooters (2nd US) at the Slyder stone wall and house early in the afternoon of 2 Jul and the charge of a major part of Hood's division across the Slyder farm at mid-afternoon (S-032.7); the Sanitary Commission found the Twelfth Corps hospital (Dr. Goodman, 1,131 wounded including 125 rebels) around the house of Geo. Bushman in July 1863 (S-032.8): a Union hospital, not the printed Confederate use; the Confederate cover or hospital use of the Rose house, Rose barn, Bushman barn and Slyder house and the 8 Bushman burials stay unsupported (lead: the Stone Sentinels Farms and Buildings page, https://gettysburg.stonesentinels.com/battlefield-farms/; the per-farm URLs of Rose, Bushman and Slyder are not recorded). S-033: the wheat "clipped by fire" was searched again over the 99-text library (the memoirs and every ws9 text: 0 matches; over every text under tmp/library 4 files, none a fire in the Wheatfield); still unsupported. S-038: the Record of Treatment (printed p.57) says in general that "both armies constructed defense works from stones, fence rails, trees, and earth" and that the stone wall works lay "predominantly in the vicinity of Little Round Top, Big Round Top, and Devil's Den"; no passage places rails on Little Round Top, which stays "no rail use documented". S-043: the NPS Then & Now page dates the Union fortifications on the southern slope to 2 Jul and the O'Sullivan photograph to "just days after the battle"; the hour and any reuse of field-wall stone stay unsupported. Status unchanged.
