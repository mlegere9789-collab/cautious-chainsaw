| Description | Row T1-188 (Jul 1 evening). Verdict partially_supported. Corrected claim: The National Park Service writes that the Mary Thompson House, built in the 1840s, served as Lee's headquarters and that on the morning of July 1, 1863 Lee rode just north of Gettysburg and established his headquarters in the farmstead residence of Mary Thompson. That differs from the seed's 'evening'; no Official Records passage names the Thompson house or gives Lee's place on the evening of Jul 1. The only place the sources name for Lee on the evening of Jul 1 is Early's account of a conference with Ewell and Rodes on the back porch of a small house north of the town near the Carlisle road. Longstreet (1896) reports to 'General Lee's head-quarters' at daylight on Jul 2 without naming a place. An illustration by Benson Lossing titled 'Confederate Headquarters' (a scene of September 1866, published in 1877) shows the repaired house (caption in the NPS landscape report); the same report says the house became famous for its association with Confederate headquarters following the Union retreat from Seminary Ridge on July 1, with the headquarters operations probably extending into the surrounding area, notably the Dustman orchard across the turnpike. The year of building (1833 Stone Sentinels; 1840s NPS), the 'Thaddeus Stevens building' reading of Wikipedia and the burning of Mary Thompson's fences for firewood have no passage in the local texts and stay unsupported. The seed calls Lee-in-the-cupola a rumour. Jacobs (1864, p.41) writes, in his account of Jul 3 before the 2.30 p.m. cannonade, that 'during the day General Lee had reconnoitred our position from the College cupola'; that is the statement of one resident, with no source given, for Jul 3 and not for the evening of Jul 1, and no cupola scene is shown for Jul 1. Added by the second reading: a relief worker's letter of the consecration day (Nov 1863) says the party 'saw Lee's headquarters' on Seminary Hill; Hoke (1887) places Lee's headquarters in a stone house a short distance out on the Chambersburg Pike and says Lee took observations from the cupola of Pennsylvania College (a later book; no day, no hour, no house named). Still unsupported or open: Mary Thompson's fences were burned for firewood (date not stated) / The house was built in 1833 (Stone Sentinels page named in the seed) / The date and hour of establishment at the Thompson house; the building's year beyond the NPS 1840s (the seed's 1833 is a lead); the fence burning; any report naming the Thompson house on Jul 1. |
