Corrected claim: Lee wrote that Ewell was instructed to carry the hill occupied by the enemy if he found it practicable, but to avoid a general engagement until the other divisions arrived; Ewell decided to await Johnson's division, which reached Gettysburg at a late hour, and the enemy meanwhile occupied the point Ewell meant to seize. Ewell wrote that on entering the town he received a message from the commanding general to attack the hill if he could do so to advantage; he could not bring artillery to bear, all the troops with him were jaded by twelve hours' marching and fighting, Cemetery Hill was not assailable from the town, and he determined to take a wooded hill to his left with Johnson's division. Early's report: he rode to urge an immediate advance, sent word to Hill that if he would send a division the hill could be taken, learned that Johnson was coming and that it was determined to occupy a wooded hill to the left of Cemetery Hill, but the division arrived late and 'no effort ... was made that night'. In 1877 Early added the reasons he knew: Rodes' division had lost about 2,500, only two of his own brigades were available, and constant reports of an enemy advance on the York road.
