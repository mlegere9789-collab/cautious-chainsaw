| Description | The evidence file kit/evidence/us-1c.jsonl (gap lines us-1c:0362 to 0364; second pass 2026-11-15) found no statement of the model or calibre of the shoulder arms carried on 1 to 3 July 1863 by any of the 32 regiments, and none of the side arms of any regiment. The second pass added statements of other dates for five regiments (below), so 14 regiments had a weapon line of some date and 18 had none. The third pass (2026-11-16) added statements for four of those 18 from Bates, History of Pennsylvania Volunteers: the 11th Pennsylvania's rifled muskets of calibre .69 from 2 January 1863, all arms of calibre .57 and .58 having been exchanged for them (0690); the 88th's Enfield rifle in the autumn of 1861, against Vautier's old muskets of 15 October (a conflict set, 0691 and 0692); the 90th's altered smooth-bore muskets, not exchanged until July 1864 (0693: documented for 1 July 1863); the 143rd's Enfield rifles of November 1862 (0694); and an inferred line for 1 July for the 151st's Springfield rifles (0722). So 17 regiments now have a weapon line of some date and 15 have none: the 19th Indiana, 24th Michigan, 2nd and 7th Wisconsin, 7th Indiana, 84th, 95th, 147th, 104th and 97th New York, 56th, 107th, 121st and 149th Pennsylvania and the 16th Maine (gap line 0718). What the record gives is of other dates (the second pass's lines are marked 2nd): the 6th Wisconsin's Belgian muskets had been exchanged for Springfield rifles by the winter of 1861-62 (Dawes, line 0239); the 12th and 13th Massachusetts the Enfield rifle in June and July 1861 (0242, 0244); the 94th New York "a complete equipment of Enfield rifles" in the winter of 1862 and the 105th New York, consolidated into it in March 1863, Austrian rifles in April 1862 (0252, 0407); the 76th New York the Enfield rifle in 1862, inferred from two anecdotes (0253); the 142nd Pennsylvania Belgian rifles at its first formation in August or September 1862 (0406); the 151st Pennsylvania new Springfield rifles at Belle Plain in the spring of 1863 (0255); the 13th Vermont Austrian rifles in place of old muskets (undated), the 14th and 16th Austrian and Enfield rifles in place of old French and Belgian muskets in the autumn of 1862 (0263 to 0265); 2nd: the 150th Pennsylvania's Enfield rifles drawn at Harrisburg on 4 September 1862 (0474) and still its arms when it was re-armed with Sharps rifles on 4 November 1864 (0475, Chamberlin), hence inferred for 1 July 1863 (0476); the 80th New York's Enfield rifles of April 1861 (0511) and Austrian rifled muskets from 16 January 1862 (0512, Gates); the 83rd New York's Harper's Ferry smooth-bore buck and ball muskets of caliber .69 on 30 May 1861 (0513); the 88th Pennsylvania's muskets "a species of ancient weapon difficult to classify" of October 1861 (0503); the 14th Vermont's "Austrian musket" of 7 November 1862 (0519, the regiment's diary). Bayonets: charges with the bayonet are stated for the 6th Wisconsin, 84th and 95th New York, 149th and 150th Pennsylvania, 13th Vermont and 16th Maine on 1 or 2 July (0353 to 0359). The only record of the calibre mix of the corps is the ammunition expended (Rosengarten, OR27-1-R031 p. 265, line 0292): .57, .58, .54 and .69 calibre rounds, by division, never by regiment. The kit holds `lorenz_m1854` for the 2nd Wisconsin, `springfield_model_unspecified` for the 19th Indiana, 6th and 7th Wisconsin, `m1861_springfield` for the 24th Michigan (all inferred), `m1861_springfield` or `p1853_enfield` for the 84th New York, and a default draw for 26 regiments (plausible-procedural). The record confirms none of them for July 1863. |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | No non-lead source read states the missing quantities. Ordnance returns of the regiments are not in the local library; the Official Records parts held give calibre only for the ammunition of the divisions. |
