| Description | After two passes the evidence file states the shoulder arms for July 1863 itself for six units: the 20th Massachusetts (Enfield rifles 'carried for the remainder of the war', Bruce); the 4th Ohio (eight companies had Springfield muskets and the two flank companies Enfield rifles in 1861; Carpenter's report: smooth-bore muskets, exchanged on 3 and 4 July for Springfield rifles gathered on the field; Kepler adds that eight companies were concerned); the 12th New Jersey (smooth-bore Springfields with buck and ball: Seville, Smyth's report of the fire of buck and ball, Potter); the 14th Connecticut (Sharp's rifles, carried as two alternatives: Rodenbough quoted by Page for the whole regiment against Hincks's two borrowed rifles, lines 0463-0464); the 126th New York ('rifled muskets', model not named); the 8th Ohio ('musket'). For eleven other units only earlier dates are stated and nothing for July 1863: the 19th Maine, 15th Massachusetts (Springfield rifles of .58 calibre for nine companies, October 1862), 1st Minnesota (1861), 42d New York (Harper's Ferry muskets, 1861), 7th Michigan (1861), 69th, 71st and 72d Pennsylvania (1861), 125th New York (Enfields September 1862, new Springfield rifles December 1862), 7th West Virginia (Henry rifles in 1864), 10th New York Battalion (altered Springfield muskets of 1842 and, undated in the months after Big Bethel, the Springfield rifled musket), 1st Company Massachusetts Sharpshooters (telescopic rifles 1861, Sharp's rifles 1862). No line for the shoulder arm of ten units (gap line 0497: 82d New York, 106th Pennsylvania, 19th Massachusetts, 59th New York, 14th Indiana, 1st Delaware, 108th, 39th and 111th New York, Company L); no bayonet line for twenty (0498) and no side-arm line for 27 (0499). |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | No non-lead source read states these quantities. The Official Records reports say 'musket', 'rifle' or 'bayonet' in passing (Smyth for the 12th New Jersey's buck and ball is the exception); the state volumes and the regimental histories on disk say nothing of the arms of July 1863 for the units listed. No regimental history was found for the 82d, 42d, 59th, 39th, 108th or 111th New York, the 7th West Virginia (Fox says only that it was given Henry rifles in 1864) or the 14th Indiana (the book of 1864 stops in 1862). |
| Evidence searched | OR27-1 reports R098 to R131 (whole text) and the return of casualties and the abstracts of strength read from the scans (R013 pp. 175-177, R008 p. 153); FOX-1889, NY-GB-1900, NY-GB-1900-v2, NY-GB-1902-v3, PA-GB-1904, PA-GB-1914a, PA-GB-1914-v2, ME-GB-1898, MI-GB-1889, MN-1ST-1916, NJ-TOOMBS-1888, NJ-FINAL-1891, OH-8TH-1889, OH-DED-1887, IN-1913, BL-3, HASKELL-1908; the regimental histories fetched from archive.org for this group (Bruce, Seville, Murphy, Page, Sawyer, Potter, Banes, Ward, McDermott, Cowtan, Smith, Ford, Simons, Willson, Kepler, Adams) and Walker's Second Corps. The sweeps (windows of 220 characters round each designation of each unit, over about 60 local texts) and their regexes are written in the gap lines of the evidence file. |
| Autonomous substitute applied | The kit keeps its draw (the default of the army and arm, or its own record) with the tier it carries now for every cell the evidence does not settle; the compile applies the lines it can (the dry run is in the report). |
| Strongest evidence-based reconstruction | The lines as written, each with its date, tier and quote: documented where a source states the arm for the unit on a date in the field window, background (dated outside the window) otherwise. |
| Confidence tier | plausible-procedural (cells with no line or only an earlier one); documented (cells with a line of 1863) |
| Product marking | Unit cards keep the tier mark of each arms cell; the 1st Minnesota card should not present 'M1861 Springfield, inferred' as a fact: no source read names that model for the regiment (the book names Springfield rifled muskets, Mississippi rifles of .54 and .69 muskets for 1861). |
