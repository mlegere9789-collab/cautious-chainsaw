| Description | After two passes the evidence file states a shoulder arm within 1 to 16 July 1863 for 7 units (20th Massachusetts, 71st Pennsylvania, 4th Ohio, 8th Ohio, 12th New Jersey, 14th Connecticut, 126th New York): the 20th Massachusetts (Enfield rifles "carried for the remainder of the war", Bruce); the 4th Ohio (smooth-bore muskets, exchanged after the fight for Springfield rifles captured from the enemy: Carpenter; Kepler dates the exchange 4 July and says that eight companies had Springfield muskets and the two flank companies Enfield rifles in 1861); the 12th New Jersey (smooth-bore Springfields with buck and ball: Seville, Potter, Smyth's report of the fire of buck and ball; no model year or calibre); the 14th Connecticut (Sharp's rifles, as two alternatives: Hincks's two borrowed rifles against Rodenbough's whole regiment, lines 0463-0464); the 126th New York ("rifled muskets", model not named); the 8th Ohio ("musket", model not named); for the 71st Pennsylvania the line is the rifles gathered from the field and fired on 3 July (line 0389), not its issue arm. For 11 other units only a date outside 1 to 16 July is stated (1st Company Massachusetts Sharpshooters, 42nd New York, 7th Michigan, 15th Massachusetts, 19th Maine, 1st Minnesota, 69th Pennsylvania, 72nd Pennsylvania, 7th West Virginia, 10th New York Battalion, 125th New York): the 19th Maine's one Springfield rifle is a deserter's muster-roll entry of 25 July 1863 (line 0432); the 15th Massachusetts has Springfield rifles of .58 calibre for nine companies in October 1862; the 1st Minnesota, 42d New York, 7th Michigan, 69th and 72d Pennsylvania 1861; the 125th New York Enfields in September 1862 and new Springfield rifles in December 1862; the 7th West Virginia Henry rifles in 1864; the 10th New York Battalion altered Springfield muskets of 1842 in May-June 1861 and, in the months after Big Bethel, the Springfield rifled musket; the 1st Company Massachusetts Sharpshooters telescopic rifles in 1861 and Sharp's rifles in 1862. No line gives the shoulder arm of 10 units (gap line 0497: Company L, 1st Minnesota, 19th Massachusetts, 59th New York, 82nd New York, 106th Pennsylvania, 14th Indiana, 108th New York, 1st Delaware, 111th New York, 39th New York); a bayonet line exists for 8 units (four of them inferred from the brigade's "charge bayonets"), so none for 20 (0498), and a side-arm line for 1 unit (the 1st Delaware, 0358), none for 27 (0499). |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | No non-lead source read states these quantities. The Official Records reports say "musket", "rifle" or "bayonet" in passing (Smyth for the 12th New Jersey's buck and ball is the exception); the state volumes and the regimental histories on disk say nothing of the arms of July 1863 for the units listed. No regimental history was found for the 82d, 42d, 59th, 39th, 108th or 111th New York, the 7th West Virginia (Fox says only that it was given Henry rifles in 1864) or the 14th Indiana (the book of 1864 stops in 1862). |
| Evidence searched | OR27-1 reports R098 to R131 (whole text) and the return of casualties and the abstracts of strength read from the scans (R013 pp. 175-177, R008 p. 153); FOX-1889, NY-GB-1900, NY-GB-1900-v2, NY-GB-1902-v3, PA-GB-1904, PA-GB-1914a, PA-GB-1914-v2, ME-GB-1898, MI-GB-1889, MN-1ST-1916, NJ-TOOMBS-1888, NJ-FINAL-1891, OH-8TH-1889, OH-DED-1887, IN-1913, BL-3, HASKELL-1908; the regimental histories fetched from archive.org for this group (Bruce, Seville, Murphy, Page, Sawyer, Potter, Banes, Ward, McDermott, Cowtan, Smith, Ford, Simons, Willson, Kepler, Adams) and Walker's Second Corps. The sweeps (windows round each designation of each unit over 33 to 77 local texts, and their regexes) are described in the gap lines of the evidence file and in the report of the group. |
