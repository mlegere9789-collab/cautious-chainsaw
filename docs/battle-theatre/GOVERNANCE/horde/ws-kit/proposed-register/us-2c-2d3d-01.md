| Description | After two passes the evidence file kit/evidence/us-2c-2d3d.jsonl states a strength from the record for 27 of the 36 units (the two divisions, Harrow's, Webb's and Willard's brigades, 20 regiments, the 10th New York Battalion and Company L), each with its kind and date; where the record gives several figures they are carried as alternatives (the 19th Maine 400 to 440, the 1st Minnesota 262 to 330, the 82d New York 300 to 365, the 111th New York 350 to 400). No source read states the strength of nine units: the Third Brigade of the Second Division (Hall), the First Brigade of the Third Division (Carroll) and the Second Brigade of the Third Division (Smyth) (gap line us-2c-2d3d:0495), and the 42d New York, the 1st Company Massachusetts Sharpshooters, the 14th Indiana, the 7th West Virginia, the 1st Delaware and the 125th New York (0496). Harrow's brigade has Fox's statement that "the four regiments took 1,246 officers and men into action" (line 0536) beside the inferred sum of its regiments' figures (1,201 to 1,374, line 0538); the Philadelphia Brigade has "about eight hundred men in line" (an estimate in Pennsylvania at Gettysburg, line 0150) and Willard's brigade "about two thousand" (Simons, line 0436). The four Pennsylvania regiments of the group have the War Department's return of 30 June 1863 "present for duty" (329, 331, 458 and 335: lines 0532-0535, Pennsylvania at Gettysburg vol. 2; the same figures as the "Present" column of the Pennsylvania commission's table); the same letters say that the number carried into action is not of record, while Fox prints 24 officers and 397 men carried into action for the 71st Pennsylvania (421, line 0515) and Banes and McDermott 258 for the 69th. Figures of different kinds are carried as they are, each with its date: the 7th Michigan 238 present for duty on 30 June and 165 into action, the 111th New York 475 and 350 to 400, the Second Division 4,331 on 30 June and 3,773 to 3,800 in the action (New York at Gettysburg vol. 1, pp. 103-104). Colonel Devereux writes that the 19th Massachusetts and the 42d New York together "numbered but 290 men" on 2 July (OR27-1-R110 p. 443): a figure for two regiments that is not divided. |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | No report, return or history on disk gives the figures of the nine units: the reports of the brigade commanders (R104, R109, R116, R123, R128) state losses, not strengths; Walker (p. 301) gives the corps ("fewer than ten thousand") and the losses of the divisions and brigades only; Fox's entries of the 42d New York, 14th Indiana, 7th West Virginia, 1st Delaware and 125th New York give losses without a number engaged; the strength tables of New York at Gettysburg vol. 1 (pp. 103-104) and the letters of the Adjutant General's Office in Pennsylvania at Gettysburg vol. 2 do not list these units; the 42d New York's monument gives losses; Seville's and Murphy's histories of the 1st Delaware and Simons's of the 125th New York give none; the Indiana volume of 1913 gives the 14th Indiana's war totals. Twelve title searches on archive.org on 2026-11-16 found no regimental history of the 82d, 42d, 59th, 39th, 108th or 111th New York or of the 7th West Virginia; the item found for the 19th Massachusetts' official history (historyofninetee00mass) is a scan of handwritten company rolls with no usable text, and the item found for the 7th Michigan is the cavalry regiment. |
