| Description | The kit's badge cell is empty for thirteen of the eighteen regiments. The second pass fills it: Walker's history of the Second Corps says the corps badge introduced in Hooker's administration was the trefoil, "the first division having in it red" (p. 204); the red trefoil is stated for the 5th New Hampshire (monument), the 57th and 61st New York, the 116th and 140th Pennsylvania, and (undated, inferred) the 53d Pennsylvania; for the other twelve regiments it is inferred from their place in the First Division (lines 0392-0403), and no passage says what those men wore on 2 July. The Irish Brigade's green flag is stated (Mulholland in Corby, Stewart, New York at Gettysburg vol. 1 p. 51), its insignia "its red trefoil, its green feather" (an address in vol. 2, undated), a small green shamrock added to the red clover leaf in the 116th Pennsylvania (Mulholland); the sprigs of boxwood in the hats are stated for Fredericksburg in December 1862 only. Load and equipment stated: the 140th Pennsylvania's marching load of December 1862 (overcoat, blanket, knapsack, haversack with three days' rations, shelter tent section, cartridge box with forty rounds, 65 to 70 pounds with the musket), the 116th Pennsylvania's on the march of June 1863, the 148th Pennsylvania's overcoats and dress coats stored and A tents exchanged for shelter tents in April 1863, an undated address on the 64th New York (regulation cap, army shoes, "slouch or straw hats" for half the men). Nothing is stated of the coat, shirt, trousers, shoes, sash or headgear worn by these regiments on or near 2 July 1863 (gap line us-2c-1d:0455). |
