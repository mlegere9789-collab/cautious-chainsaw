| Description | S-082, S-083, S-084 (GOVERNANCE/evidence_corrections/fence-3.jsonl). The PDF prints an iron fence separating the new cemetery from Evergreen, the platform on the Evergreen side of the dividing fence (Wills 1992, Frassanito 1995), and cut and scarred trees, broken fences, rifle pits and discarded gear around the cemetery with the Blue Ridge visible (W-CONS). Re-matched 2026-11-13: the cemetery was laid out in semicircular plots to Saunders's design (his plan shown to Lincoln on 17 Nov; NPS CLR 2017; CLI 2019) and was "unfinished" on 19 Nov; the Evergreen Cemetery Association made "an open iron railing enclosure of ordinary height ... upon the division lines" a condition of the sale of 14 Aug 1863 (NPS Adams County Historic Resource Study, quoting the agreement), but the boundary fence as built is described three ways: a low pipe-rail fence with a hedge (NPS CLI 2019, Saunders's design), a three-foot iron fence behind an arborvitae hedge (NPS), "simply a low iron fence" (Bartlett 1874), the seven-foot ornate iron fence being of 1935; nothing local shows any of them standing on 19 Nov 1863. Platform: built by 18-19 Nov near the flagstaff on the border of the two cemeteries (Becker sketch; NPS CLR 2017; NPS article); placed on the Evergreen side (NPS CLR, CLI 2019, the web page), at the later monument site or "in the centre" of the grounds (an older NPS typescript; Adams Sentinel 24 Nov 1863), or straddling the boundary (a 2023 Library of Congress blog, host refused, not read); footprint, height and rail not found. Surroundings: "the surrounding hills ... dimly seen through the November mists" (Adams Sentinel 24 Nov 1863); Tillie Pierce saw on 7 Jul that "Fences had disappeared, some buildings were gone, others ruined"; Evergreen's own road frontage had "a temporary white-painted wood plank fence and entrance gate" before the battle (CLR). Nothing local describes broken fences, scarred trees, rifle pits or discarded gear around the cemetery on 19 Nov, or the Blue Ridge; the large derivative of the crowd photograph LOC ppmsca-32850 (viewed) shows freshly dug plots, wooden headboards and bare saplings and no readable fence line. |
