| Description | S-059, S-060 (GOVERNANCE/evidence_corrections/fence-3.jsonl). The PDF prints the Leister house after the bombardment: many shot holes, porch supports and steps down, bedstead broken, 17 dead horses, apple trees destroyed, peach tree dies, fence rails burned, hay lost, spring spoiled (about 13:00-15:00 on 3 Jul; SS-Leister, NPS-Bldgs), and the headquarters moving to Powers Hill during the bombardment (HN-Thunder). Re-matched 2026-11-13: shells burst in the yard among the staff horses tied to the fence, tore up the steps, carried away the porch supports and passed through the door and the garret, and the house was "shot through several times" (Captain George Meade Jr., Life and Letters II; Haskell, July 1863; NPS Lydia Leister Farm page; Adams Sentinel 18 Aug 1863 puts the first shells over the yard at about 13:00; Hunt, OR27-1-R028 p.239: the cannonade opened about 1 p. m. and the Union fire was gradually stopped about 2.30 p. m.); 17 dead horses lay in the yard when Lydia Leister returned (NPS 3D tour page; the count rests on that statement alone); her fence rails "had been burned" and "the house and surrounding fences are all damaged" (NPS 3D tour page and Buildings and Farms Then and Now: no "all" rails, no agent, no date, no hour); all the hay was gone from the barn (NPS; "2 tons" not found). Nothing local mentions the bedstead, apple trees, a peach tree dying or the spoiled spring. Headquarters: the staff withdrew to the fenced yard behind the house (Meade Jr.), the shells forced the general and staff to seek quarters with Slocum on Powers' Hill (NY at Gettysburg vol.1 p.75; Norton, OR27-1-R017 p.206) and Meade was on Powers Hill when the assault began (Bachelder 1904); the 2nd Pennsylvania Cavalry narrative (PA vol.2 p.806) names instead "more secure quarters in the woods, south of the Taneytown road". No text gives the hour of the move. |
