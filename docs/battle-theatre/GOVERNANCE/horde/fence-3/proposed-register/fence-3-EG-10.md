| Description | S-065, S-066, S-071, S-072, S-073 (GOVERNANCE/evidence_corrections/fence-3.jsonl). S-065: the Granite Schoolhouse as a Second Corps, First Division hospital evacuated toward McAllister's Mill after the overshots of 3 Jul (HN-Spangler): no local text names this schoolhouse as a hospital; the only local statement is that it no longer stands (NPS General Management Plan 1999); "built 1862" and "razed 1921" have no local source. S-071: the Hummelbaugh farm as a Second Corps hospital where Barksdale died and as Pleasonton's headquarters on 4-6 Jul (SS-Hummelbaugh). Round 3 (2026-11-16): HABS PA-1961 (Library of Congress record pa1357) says the house "was used by General Alfred Pleasonton of the Union as a headquarters during the Battle of Gettysburg and also as a hospital by U.S. troops" and that "Confederate Gen. William Barksdale died at the house and was buried on the grounds", and the NPS Record of Treatment vol. I (pdf p.219) puts Pleasonton's headquarters at the farm "during the early stages of the battle" and names "hospital use"; the house stood in 1863 (NPS 1999); no local text names the corps, the days of the hospital use or the hour of Barksdale's death, or dates Pleasonton's stay to 4-6 Jul; OR27 has no Hummelbaugh text and Pleasonton's report (OR27-1-R336) names no headquarters site. S-066: Armistead was brought to the Eleventh Corps (2nd Division) hospital at the George Spangler farm on the afternoon of 3 Jul, "about 4 o'clock" by the attending surgeon Brinton (letter of 1869, via NPS Common Pride and Fame part II), not about 20:00 as printed; Holland (Confederate Veteran 1921, via NPS) has him moved "during that afternoon" to "a temporary hospital beneath the shade of some trees"; no local text names a summer kitchen or any building. S-072: he died on 5 Jul at about 9 a. m. (Holland via NPS; Round 3 (2026-11-16): the NPS web page "Confederate Commanders at Gettysburg", as served 2026-11-13, says he "died in a Federal field hospital the day after his wounding", i.e. 4 Jul, a second selectable value; the NPS Boundary Study of 1988, pdf p.39, puts his death at the 11th Corps hospital on the George Spangler farm), Brinton writing that the death "resulted [not] from his wounds directly, but from secondary fever and prostration". S-073: the Trostle farmhouse was used briefly as a hospital and the barn area as Sickles's headquarters (NPS CLI 2024 citing CLR 2002); the barn has a cannonball hole in the brick gable and dead horses of Bigelow's battery lay on the property (NPS Then and Now); no local text dates the end of the hospital use or says "abandoned by Jul 6". Round 3 (2026-11-16): the NPS inventory of the Emmitsburg Road Ridge (pdf p.82) says the house "was used for temporary hospital purposes after the evacuation of the Confederate army on July 5" and that the barn likely served so (hedged); Letterman (Medical Recollections, 1866, pp.158-159) has the army moving toward the Potomac on 6-7 Jul with more than a hundred medical officers ordered to remain. |
