S-086: the PDF prints sun and shadow computed for 39.82 N, 77.23 W and the cloud default cloudy clearing to sun at the prayer (E-007: cloudy per Wikipedia, "perfect" morning per a blog). Four contemporary newspaper statements describe the morning: the Adams Sentinel of 24 Nov 1863 ("Day dawned on Thursday with an unclouded sky, giving assurance of lovely weather"), the New-York Daily Tribune of 21 Nov (a correspondent on the morning of 19 Nov: "The sky is bright, and all nature has donned her best attire"), the Evening Star of 19 Nov ("The weather is fine to-day") and of 20 Nov ("Thursday morning opened bright and beautiful"); the Sentinel adds the distant hills "dimly seen through the November mists". None says cloudy; "sun at the prayer" and the afternoon sky are in no text; the sun position is a computation, not a source claim. S-087: the PDF prints about 15,000 (W-CONS) to 15,000-20,000 (EWH) with soldiers, civilians, widows, boys at the edge, six governors, reporters; the local estimates are over 10,000 (NPS article), some 15,000 (NPS monument page), "about 15,000" civilians between the stand and the military and "at least 15,000" in town with "four or five thousands" at the grounds ahead of the procession (New-York Tribune of 20-21 Nov), "at least twenty thousand" round the stand and "from twenty to forty thousand" in the town (Adams Sentinel); the Evening Star calls it "probably as large if not a larger number of people than ever assembled before at any one celebration in this country". The Tribune documents a military escort of one squadron of cavalry, two batteries of artillery and a regiment of infantry, civilians "including men, women, and children", "the attendance of ladies was quite large", and on the platform Governors Curtin, Seymour, Coburn, Morton, Bradford and Parker, Governor Brough, ex-governors Tod, Wright and Dennison and Pierpont (OCR garbled, read from the surrounding words; no count is printed); widows, boys at the edge and reporters are not found. S-088: the PDF prints celebrating crowds, bands and alcohol in the streets on the night of 18 Nov (EWH). The President arrived about 5 o'clock (Tribune, letter of 18 Nov), "yesterday afternoon" (Tribune and Star dispatches of 19 Nov), "last evening" (Tribune) or at 6½ o'clock on Wednesday evening (Evening Star, 20 Nov, read from the page image); a large gathering greeted him at the depot, "a large number of persons continued to linger round the residence", the band of the 5th New York Artillery serenaded him after supper and then Secretary Seward ("Serenades seem to be the order of the evening"), and the train with the Governors came in nearly at midnight (Tribune); torches and alcohol are in no text, including the two newspapers read in full for 19-21 Nov. |
