| Description | S-062 (GOVERNANCE/evidence_corrections/fence-3.jsonl). The PDF prints the Emmitsburg Road fences as Intact -> Splintered / breached; attackers climbing, about 15:30-16:15 on 3 Jul (ABT-Charge), and a rail with 836 holes (Savas-Hessler). Re-matched 2026-11-13: fences stood on the line of the advance on 3 Jul and had to be climbed (Peyton, OR27-2-R441 p.386: "climb three high post and rail fences"; Shepard R552 p.647: "a lane inclosed by two stout post and plank fences" [OCR "jjlank"], "a very great obstruction"; Davis R553 p.651: "a strong post and rail fence" and "other fences of a similar character"); double post-and-rail fences enclosed both sides of the road (NPS Record of Treatment vol.I; NPS Cultural Landscapes Inventory, Emmitsburg Road Ridge) and the attackers crowded at the openings where the fences had been thrown down (Martin, 53rd Virginia, via the NPS Pickett's Charge inventory). They were not intact along the whole road: on 2 Jul Union troops took down or levelled fences to the road (Higgins, OR27-1-R146 p.511, 35 men, 10-11 a. m.; Bodine R167 p.556; Humphreys R158 p.532; Gibbon R098 p.416; Harrow R099 p.419; Hancock R076 p.370; Joslin R101 p.423; Wright, OR27-2-R539 p.623, for Anderson's sector) and built rail breastworks along it (NPS). On 17 Nov 1863 a New-York Tribune correspondent saw along the Emmitsburg road "wooden fences ... torn down and the stone ones strengthened and used as breastworks" (the November state, EG-17). Not found: 15:30-16:15 for the fences (Webb has the line crossing the road about 15:00), the 836-hole rail and "perforated slabs" (the Savas-Hessler excerpt is a publisher PDF on a host that refused the connection), the breach points. |
