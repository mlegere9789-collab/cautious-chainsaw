| Description | S-080 (open), S-081 (closed) (GOVERNANCE/evidence_corrections/fence-3.jsonl). The PDF prints 17 acres bought by Wills for about $2,475.87 in late July and August, and the first of 3,512 Union bodies moved on 27 Oct, 1,258 reburied by 19 Nov, work due to finish March 1864 (Oct 17 / Oct 27 start conflict; W-CONS, GC-COMP). Re-matched 2026-11-13: about seventeen acres were bought through David Wills, the Governor's agent, in five lots at $135 to $225 an acre (Wills, report of 21 Mar 1864); NPS (Unrau, Administrative History, 1991) dates the first twelve acres to 14 Aug 1863 (sold by the Evergreen Cemetery Association) and five more acres to a day or two later; $2,475.87 is the cost of "a site" in the Governor's statement and, in NPS's account, the price of the twelve acres; no official text gives the total for the seventeen acres, and a New-York Daily Tribune correspondent writing on 17 Nov 1863 (Library of Congress, Chronicling America, issue of 21 Nov) says "some seventeen acres of land were purchased for the sum of $3,150" (the OCR prints a garbled currency sign). Reburial: the contractor began exhuming on Tuesday 27 Oct 1863 (Weaver, Cemetery Report 1867; NPS CLR), the first reinterment in the national cemetery (from Gettysburg's Presbyterian graveyard) was about 17 Oct, 3,512 bodies in all, completed on 18 Mar 1864 after a winter stop; on 19 Nov the superintendent's list printed in the Tribune of 21 Nov 1863 ("a list of the remains already interred in the Cemetery, with the States they represent", figures from Mr. Townsend of Rahway, N. J., "the Superintendent of the grounds") totals 1,188 (606 by State, 582 unknown; read from the page image, the OCR keeps "188"), the NPS typescript says "1,188 bodies had been reinterred" and the CLR "approximately 1,200 graves"; 1,258 is in no text. |
