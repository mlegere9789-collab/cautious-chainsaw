| Strongest evidence-based reconstruction | The command of the Provost-Marshal-General (Brig. Gen. Marsena R. Patrick) belongs to general headquarters in the organization table (93d New York, eight companies of the 8th United States, a Pennsylvania cavalry regiment, two companies of the 6th Pennsylvania Cavalry, regular cavalry detachments); the abstract of returns for 30 June gives 84 officers and 1,445 men (strength records of this file). On 3 July Ewing turned 400 to 500 stragglers over to General Patrick on the road behind the line at about noon, and Hopper took 1,800 prisoners to General Patrick's headquarters, which he reached at about 6 p. m.; both are entered as positions of the command. The 93d New York was at Taneytown on 30 June and 1 July, at Westminster guarding the supply trains from 2 July to the afternoon of 4 July and at Gettysburg from the afternoon of 5 July (Robertson, 1895: records of this file); Companies E and I of the 6th Pennsylvania Cavalry were the escort of army headquarters throughout the campaign (Gracey, 1868: records of this file). Not printed: the arrival, road and places on 1 and 2 July of the eight companies of the 8th United States, of the Pennsylvania cavalry regiment of Colonel Price and of the regular cavalry detachments, and where General Patrick himself stood.
