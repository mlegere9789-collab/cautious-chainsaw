- **G-02 is reversed.** The first close-out called the PDF's 'behind the E-01 rail barricade' contradicted. Cooper's monument address (Pennsylvania at Gettysburg, vol. II, p.909; the address at the dedication of Cooper's monument, spoken in the first person plural) calls it 'the rail barricade in front of us' and 'our works', says Cooper 'caused our immediate front at the barricade to be cleared of our infantry' and fired 'double charges of canister' at Perrin's troops, and says the enemy 'turned our position after 4p.m.' and the battery left 'on the north side of the seminary'. Robinson (OR27-1-R036 p.280) had a battery 'directly in my rear' and the loose-rail breastwork 'some 40 yards in front of this battery'; the NPS Cultural Landscape Report (printed p.40, PDF p.62) has the Union artillery 'behind breastworks built of fence rails'; Bates (1875, p.79) has Wainwright post his batteries 'at a barricade of rails'. So the relative position is supported; what the OR does not support is the rails as the guns' own work (they were the infantry's) and Breck's guns, which Wainwright puts behind a stone wall on the crest. Verdict corrected -> partially_supported. The request note of 2026-11-13 asked the world-content workstream to show the guns NOT behind E-01: that request is withdrawn in the updated note.
- **G-04: the 'about 20' is documented and reconciled.** The same dedication address (pp.910-911) gives the guns of the five batteries on 2 Jul (Brown four 10-pdr Parrotts; Carpenter two 3-inch and two light 12-pdr; Dement four light 12-pdr; Raine two on the hill and two 20-pdr Parrotts beyond the Hanover road; Graham four 20-pdr Parrotts: 14 on Benner's Hill and six beyond the Hanover road = 20) and the Union batteries that answered (Stevens 6, Reynolds 5, Cooper 4, Wiedrich 4 = thirteen 3-inch and six 12-pdr, 19 guns, as in Hunt's OR figures). With Ewell's 'Andrews' battalion and Graham's battery, the whole under Major Latimer', Andrews' 16 plus Graham's four make 20: the 16-versus-20 conflict is a difference of command, and the first close-out's 'no per-battery count' is superseded (the figures are one retrospective address; the OR give none). The PDF's 37 Union guns stay unsupported.
- **G-12: the canister states.** The NPS study 'A Common Pride and Fame' I (printed pp.170-171) quotes the battery's sergeant Fuger (JMSI XLI, 1907, p.408): single charges of canister from about 400 yd, 'double and treble charges' from 200 yd, Cushing 'shot through the mouth and instantly killed' when the enemy was within about 100 yd. The PDF's 'double canister at about 100 yd' joins two of these; the first close-out had it unsupported. Fuger's article itself is not local.
- **G-13 / G-14: counts.** The 'about one hundred and twenty guns' at OR27-1-R030 p.262 is John Newton's report (First Corps) and is a Confederate count, so the PDF's 'as many as 120' as a Union count stays unsupported. Meade's own dispatch of 3 Jul (OR27-1-R006 p.74, digits agree in the other two scans) has 'about 150'; New York at Gettysburg (1900) gives 138 for the whole line (the low end of the PDF's range); a participant narrative in Pennsylvania at Gettysburg II (p.633) 137; an 1863 Richmond account (Longstreet 55 and Hill some 60) about 115. 179 is still found nowhere.
- **G-16: a possible origin of the Whitworth signal.** The Richmond Enquirer of 24 Jul 1863 and the Burlington Weekly Hawk-Eye of 1 Aug 1863 (a reprint under 'Further Rebel Accounts of the Battle of Gettysburg') have, at nearly noon, 'presently the shrill sound of a Whitworth awakes the silence, then batteries slowly open'; the reprint has 'At twelve o'clock, the signal gun was fired'. No place and no word signal for the Whitworth; four witnesses still have Miller's two guns. New York at Gettysburg (1900) is a second secondary witness for two Whitworths at Oak Hill. A 3-inch pair of Hurt's battery is plausible (the battery as Hardaway's had two 3-inch rifles and a Whitworth in Feb 1863; McIntosh has a remaining section of Hurt's with Johnson's battery) but it stood with Johnson's battery, not on Oak Hill.
- **G-09: the brick house narrowed, not found.** Bachelder (1870) calls the Codori house 'a small brick house, known as Cordora's house'; New York at Gettysburg (1900) has 'the brick farm house known as the Codori House' and 'the brick house (Sherfy's)'; the NPS study has 'the red brick Codori farmhouse'. No source ties either house to Cabell's guns.
