## New evidence lines (all documented unless stated; `supports_in_part` unless stated)
- R13.P-01 Angle (`WLK2-angle-nps`): the National Park Service says "The stone wall at the Angle is the remnant of a wall that was built by the local farmers before the battle and was originally two to three feet high" (a wall that pre-existed the battle: no construction animation, PDF B.7.3). The Copse of Trees (`WLK2-copse-nps`): "a clump of bushes and small trees grew here" in 1863, the iron fence was built in 1887, and none of the trees inside it today date to 1863.
- R13.P-04 (`WLK2-wheatfield-wall-nps`, supports the sub-claim): fighting "along a stone wall between Rose's Woods and the southern edge of the Wheatfield", Third Corps units on the wall; pre-existence, size and damage stay inferred or open.
- R13.G-06 and R14.E-15 (`WLK2-hazlett-nps`): NPS page on Hazlett's Battery D: the gunners dragged the guns, caissons and limbers by hand up the rocky slopes of Little Round Top, the battery "helped save Little Round Top" over two hours, Hazlett was killed on 2 July.
- R14.S-032 (`WLK2-bushman-house-shelter`, tier inferred): "On the night of July 2, the house was probably used as a temporary shelter by wounded Confederates" (NPS Record of Treatment, vol. 2); the report hedges, so it is inferred and not documented; it concerns the house only.
- R14.S-036 and S-037 (`WLK2-sherfy-minnigh-house`, `-barn`): Minnigh's guide of 1892, two scans: the Sherfy property "was struck by over 150 minie balls" and "four shells passed entirely through the building" (S-036); "The Sherfy barn was burned during the 3d of July." - R14.S-040 and S-073 (`WLK2-trostle-petition`): the petition of Catherine Trostle for Abram Trostle (1868) as printed by the NPS: 6,400 rails ($512), house and barn used for hospital ($200); the claimant's own statement, the file in the Pennsylvania State Archives not read.
