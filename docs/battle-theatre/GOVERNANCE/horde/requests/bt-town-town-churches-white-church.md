# Request from the group pass `town-churches` to the group passes of the Baltimore Pike and of the hospitals, and to the integration step: the White Church is St. Mark's, not a church of the borough

Written 2026-11-16. Nothing was edited outside the files of the group. The group `town-churches` did **not** declare this building.

## What the local texts say

- The Sanitary Commission report of 1863 (USSC-GETTYSBURG-1863, p. 23): the First Corps Hospital "was divided. A portion was in the town, occupying several churches and the Court-House ... The second portion was about 2^ miles from the town on the Baltimore pike. The First Division was in and about the White Church and Lightner's house" (the text layer reads 2^ for 2 and a half).
- `hospitals.json` H-04 names the site "Near White Church (Marks German Reformed), Baltimore Pike" and gives no structure id.
- The Senate list of church claims of 1912 (WARCLAIMS-CHURCHES-1912, p. 27) has "St. Mark's German Reformed Church, of Gettysburg" (a large building 40 by 50 feet with gallery; age, condition, character and quality not shown; taken for hospital purposes and damaged after the battle); the Compiler of 28 December 1863 announces a sermon "at Mark's Church" at three o'clock, besides the German Reformed church in the borough.

## What is asked

1. The pass that holds the Baltimore Pike farms (`farms-culps-hill-baltimore-pike`) or the hospital pass: declare St. Mark's (the White Church) with its own id; it is **not** one of the eight churches of the borough that `town-churches` carries, and it must not be merged with the German Reformed church of the borough (`TWN-CHU-0002`, High and Stratton Streets).
2. The hospital pass: H-04 can name the building once it has an id; the First Division of the First Corps hospital of 2 July was there (USSC p. 23).
3. The integration step: do not draw the White Church in the town scene.
