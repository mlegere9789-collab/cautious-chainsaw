| Description | The study says the trees of the Copse were a thick growth of thin chestnut oaks little above a horse's head, on rough rocky ground covered with brambles (Shields), scrub oaks (Cowan), small trees (Russell), and reasons that they were no more than 8 to 10 feet high; the owner was cutting them after the war until Bachelder stopped him. The layer draws the Warren cluster that holds the Angle (5.85 acres, scattered-tree density) with oaks 2.4 to 3.1 m high (inferred); the thick growth, the brambles and the true extent of the Copse are not drawn. Ziegler's Grove was a large wooded area in 1863 with mature white and chestnut oaks and perhaps some black cherry (the study's hedge), probably much reduced when it was surveyed (1868-69) and replanted with 125 trees in 1888: the layer draws the Warren wood (2.95 acres), so the extent and density of the grove of 1863 are not known. The HABS report on the Cyclorama building says the grove area had been denuded of trees since the battle and does not date the felling (VC-NPSC-HABS-pa3988-p006-01): the layer draws the Warren wood standing through 19 November, which that sentence neither confirms nor excludes. |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | The Warren sheet is five years after the battle and the study says the trees of both places were cut meanwhile; no map of 1863 holds them. |
