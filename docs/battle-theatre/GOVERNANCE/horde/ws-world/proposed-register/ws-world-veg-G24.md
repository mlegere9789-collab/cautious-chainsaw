| Description | The booklet on the ecology of Big Round Top says its virgin timber (white pine, white oak, black oak, in places chestnut) was cut before 1850, so the forest of 1863 is second growth of an age nobody gives; the Cultural Landscape Report calls it a mature forest (VC-NPS-022). The layer draws mature trees at the ridge forest's heights (plausible-procedural). The Slaughter Pen study says Houck's 47 acres were almost entirely clear-cut at the time of the battle against the Warren woods of 1868-69 (the choice CF-VEG-HOUCK): the extent of the clearing and what grew back by 1868 are not known. |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | No count of trees, no ring count and no photograph of 1863 of either hill was read; the study of Houck's land gives no source for its statement and hedges the clearing ("apparently"). |
