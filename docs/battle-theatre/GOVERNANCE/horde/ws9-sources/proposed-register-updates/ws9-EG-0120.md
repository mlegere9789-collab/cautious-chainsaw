## What each supports
- E-001 (Nov 18): arrival by special train in the afternoon of the 18th and a serenade of the President and Secretary of State that evening with remarks by Seward: Everett pp.20-21 (and Sentinel: President "serenaded twice during the evening"; Seward replied "by making a brief speech"). Supported. NOT found: an 18:00 hour, William McDougall.
- E-008 (U.S. Marine Band): Everett p.12 states the Presidential party was accompanied by the Marine Band from the Navy Yard; programme p.25 lists "Music, by the Marine Band" after the prayer; the Sentinel procession list begins with the Marine Band. Supported by two independent contemporaneous prints.
- E-005 (troops saluting at the cemetery): only the programme (p.24) says the military will form in line "for the purpose of saluting the President". That is a plan, not an account of the act. The Sentinel list does not report a salute.
- E-014 (church service): the Sentinel reports an address by Col. Anderson at 5 p.m. on the 19th in the Presbyterian church with the President and suite in the pulpit. That supports "an address in a church with the President present", not a "church service" as the row words it.
- E-002 (Harper house, Nov 18 evening): NOT found in either text (the Sentinel's editor was Robert G. Harper; no "Harper house" statement). Remains a gap.
- EG-0120 scenario values to add: procession start planned "precisely ten o'clock" (Everett p.24) and "About ten o'clock" formed (Sentinel; consistent with the PDF/NPS 10:00 and in conflict with an actual start of about 11:00 carried from other sources); dirge position in the PROGRAMME is after the President's remarks and before the benediction (Everett p.25, and the pamphlet prints the Dirge after Lincoln's address; contents page puts the Dirge at p.85) which supports the "after Lincoln" scenario as the planned order, not as what happened; crowd "twenty to forty thousand" (Sentinel estimate, new upper value); morning sky "unclouded" (Sentinel), supporting the "perfect morning" scenario against "cloudy clearing" without resolving it; address text printed in the Everett pamphlet with "under God" (one version for the selectable text set). Lincoln departed "late in the evening in a special car" (Sentinel).
