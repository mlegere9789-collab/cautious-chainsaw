## What each supports
1. Rain start (ENV-NOTES D1; PDF row T1-003 "8 am Jun 25"). Jacobs 1864 p.14 places a heavy rain on the night of Thursday 25 June ("Late on Thursday evening ... the heavy rain of that night") and p.16 has rain "during the most of the day" on Friday 26 June. This is first-hand 1864 text and is consistent with the evening reading, not with a morning start on 25 June. It gives no clock time: the "8 p. m." figure remains attributable only to the 1885 transcription, which was not read in this pass.
2. Jul 4 rain timing (ENV-NOTES D2). Fremantle records torrential rain beginning at 1 p.m. and again shortly after 9 p.m. on 4 July, with thunder and lightning that night. Those two moments match the Strategypage-type schedule ("heavier 1 to 2 pm, very heavy from 9 pm") and are not the TeachersFirst schedule (6 am; 2:15-4 pm; 4 am Jul 5). Limits: one witness, his own clock, on the Confederate side with the army's rear and the retreat route (exact place not stated), not on the Union ground; the TeachersFirst schedule is NOT refuted for the morning hours, and no source read here gives a Union-side clock. Letterman p.156 only says a "drenching rain ... fell after the battle" (no hour).

## Suggested tier change
- Sub-claim "heavy rain the night of 25 Jun and most of 26 Jun": from inferred to documented (single eyewitness, no clock). PDF "8 am" to be marked "contradicted by Jacobs 1864 p.14", kept selectable.
- Sub-claim "torrential rain from about 13:00 and again after 21:00 on 4 Jul (Confederate-side witness)": documented as a witness statement; keep the whole-timeline tier at inferred because location and clock are single-witness. Do not delete either schedule; add Fremantle as the basis for the 13:00/21:00 scenario.
- Humidity, pressure, 87 F "in shade", Jul 2 14:00 cloud, Jul 3 rainfall: NOT touched (nothing in these texts); EG-0019 and EG-0026 stay open. Jacobs's thermometer table is not in the 1864 Notes (searched for thermometer, cloud, barometer, weather: no hit).
