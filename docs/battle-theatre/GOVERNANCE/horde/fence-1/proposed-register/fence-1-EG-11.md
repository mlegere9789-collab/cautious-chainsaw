| Description | Close-out 2026-11-13. At the first close-out S-004 (Presbyterian church) and S-008 (St. Francis Xavier) had no source here (see WAVE 4 below); S-012 and S-013 (seminary): hospital documented (McFarland R060, Dance R532, NPS: hospital for both sides for weeks), damage documented only as holes in walls and a rebuilt northeast corner (NPS), glass loss not documented; S-016 (George Spangler farm): a field hospital per the NPS, corps and hour not documented at that time (corps now documented, see WAVE 4 below). WAVE 4 (2026-11-14): S-004: the documented church hospital is Jul 1 after noon, not Jun 30: the 8th Illinois Cavalry's surgeons moved their wounded from the depot hospital to 'the Presbyterian church near the center of the town' (Hard 1868); the Cavalry Corps tablet stands before the church (NPS 1932 list; NPS treatment guidelines); the marker page (Jun 30, 208 Baltimore St) is unreadable here. S-008: the Catholic church was a hospital for both armies from Jul 2-3 (Bates 1875) through the Sunday after the battle (Stoever); 'by noon Jul 1, pews removed, boards laid' (Aleteia) has no source. S-016: the Eleventh Corps hospital at the George Spangler farm is documented (NPS Common Pride vol. 2: Dr. Brinton's 2nd Division hospital, Armistead there on the afternoon of Jul 3; NPS 1988; Bates 1875); the opening day and hour are not. S-012: the Adams Sentinel of 21 Jul 1863 says the seminary 'was struck by a number of shells and balls'. WAVE 5 (2026-11-15): S-004: the Sanitary Commission's report of 1863 (printed pp.15 and 24, page images looked at) says that most of the wounded of Jul 1 were carried into the churches and public buildings under the organization of the First and Cavalry Corps, and that the Cavalry Corps hospital 'occupied the Presbyterian Church on Baltimore street, and the two school-houses in the immediate vicinity' (Dr. Rulison, 300 wounded). S-008: Reily's Conewago (1885, p.164): St. Francis Xavier's 'was first occupied as such before noon of July 1, 1863, and was used for this purpose for several weeks'; only the pews and boards remain web-only. S-016: the same report (p.24): 'the Eleventh Corps Hospital occupied the house and farm of Geo. Spangler' (1,900 wounded). S-012/S-013: the Adams Sentinel of 21 Jul 1863 (page image read): the Seminary 'struck by a number of shells and balls, and several serious fractures caused ... part of one gable end to be taken down'; the glass loss ('nearly all the glass destroyed') is Dr. Schmucker's house; the Seminary and the College were hospitals 'of both armies' for a month to come. |
