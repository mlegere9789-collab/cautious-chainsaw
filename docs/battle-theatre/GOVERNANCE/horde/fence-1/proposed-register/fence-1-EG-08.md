| Description | Close-out 2026-11-13. CONTRADICTION confirmed and widened: rail and stone breastworks are documented by unit, material and window on Jul 1 evening (Robinson's division on the line left of Cemetery Hill, parallel with the Emmitsburg road), Jul 2 from about 16:00 (Harrow's regiments, the 7th Michigan, the 80th NY and 151st Pa.), and Jul 3 (28th Mass., 69th Pa., Stone's brigade, Vermont brigade, 142nd, 121st and 69th/71st Pa., 88th Pa. at 18:00). Still open: continuity of a line, dimensions, per-regiment clocks, and the 69th Pa. date (July 2 in two scans, July 3 in the Cornell scan). WAVE 4 (2026-11-14): the 69th Pa. date is settled by the page image (EG-16 closed): 'on the morning of July 2'. The 126th NY (Hays's division) made breastworks of 'a low stone wall, and an old rail fence' on the crest from about 08:00 on Jul 2 (Willson 1870). NPS Common Pride: earth thrown over the existing stone wall by Gibbon's division, the 20th Mass. behind a rail-topped work, one printed height 'a little over knee-high', the 13th Vermont's forenoon rail work under Sergeant Scott. WAVE 5 (2026-11-15): an Official Records sweep for construction statements in reports no fence shard had cited adds the whole First Division of the Second Corps on the morning of Jul 3 (Caldwell R077; McKeen R078, Kelly R082, Brooke R092; the 5th NH, 57th NY, 61st NY, 63rd NY, 64th NY, 69th NY, 88th NY, 53rd Pa., 116th Pa., 140th Pa. and 145th Pa.: breastworks, earthworks or rifle-pits begun early on the morning of the 3d and serviceable before noon; the 116th Pa. 'by 11 a. m. had quite a formidable breastwork thrown up'); one printed profile (the 20th Mass.: 'a slight rifle-pit, a foot deep and a foot high', dug with a single shovel on the night of Jul 2, 'too thin to stop anything more than a spent ball', Abbott R111); a statement of near-continuity ('the infantry had constructed a slight breastwork of such materials as the ground afforded along the entire crest of the hill', Tyler R315, morning of Jul 3); and artillery earthworks (McGilvery R318: 'a slight earthwork thrown up' in front of thirty-nine guns; Dow R331). The remaining gaps: any length, the fabric of the Caldwell works (never 'rails'), the continuity beyond one officer's summary. WAVE 6 (2026-11-16): Ashe (Clark's North Carolina Troops vol. 5, 1901, p. 142, a retrospective compilation, source not named) describes the Hall and Harrow line on Jul 3 as 'breastworks of rails covered with earth and with rifle pits and shallow trenches in their front' and the first line of Hays's division holding 'a strong fence along the pike': the only text read that gives the fabric 'rails covered with earth'. An NPS study of the Brien farm reads defense works built quickly at the edge of the tree line north and west of the house from Brady's photograph (Tilberg's report of 1943, not found online). An NPS seminar essay has the Angle wall standing on Jul 2 evening with one small opening for the artillery. The PDF's NPS-CWS10 is identified as the NPS Civil War Series booklet; section 10 has no statement on rail breastworks. |
