| Description | Norton (OR27-1-R017 p.201): Buford's signal officer on Jun 30 'in the steeple of the college'; the NPS First Day inventory: Buford used the cupola of the Seminary building on Jun 30; Jacobs (1864): a signal officer on the College cupola at 08:30 Jul 1 and Lee on it on Jul 3. No source names Pennsylvania Hall. WAVE 4 (2026-11-14): the Jul 1 morning post is documented as the Seminary cupola (Bates 1875 quoting the signal officer: 'early in the morning taken his station in the cupola of the Theological Seminary'; Toombs; the New York monuments volume; Bachelder's guide), while Norton's 'steeple of the college' is dated Jun 30 and Jacobs's 'College cupola' is one of Howard's signal officers at 08:30 on Jul 1: two buildings, two events; whether Norton's college steeple is Pennsylvania Hall is not stated in any source read. WAVE 5 (2026-11-15): the History of Cumberland and Adams Counties (1886) describes the College's main building ('the college proper', built 1836-38) as 'surmounted by an octagonal cupola 17 feet in diameter and 24 feet in height, with observatory', so a 'college cupola' (Jacobs, Hoke, Battles and Leaders) is a feature of that building; the name 'Pennsylvania Hall' is not printed in the texts read (S-002 sub-claim d is documented/partial). WAVE 6 (2026-11-16): HABS PA-359 (1966, National Park Service) says the seminary cupola was used as a post of observation by Buford and Reynolds on Jul 1, 1863; the 1988 boundary study has the seminary's Old Dorm serving during the battle as an observation post and signal station; HABS PA-360 (1958) names the College's old dormitory 'Old Dorm, Gettysburg College (formerly called Pennsylvania College)' and the Library of Congress catalogue title is 'Pennsylvania Hall' (a modern catalogue title). No text read says that the cupola of the College belonged to a building named Pennsylvania Hall in 1863. |
