| Description | Close-out 2026-11-13. Wainwright (OR27-1-R071 p.357) says a paling fence parallel to and within 50 yd of the road; Dawes (R035 p.276) a fence on the turnpike without a type; the NPS First Day CLR says the north side through the McPherson Farm was lined by Virginia worm fence in 1863. 'Post-and-rail along the whole pike' has no source here (web only). WAVE 4 (2026-11-14): Dawes (1890): the regiment halted 'at the fence along the Cashtown Turnpike', fired 'resting on the fence rails' and climbed 'the turnpike fences' (two) where it charged on Jul 1 (the 2026-11-14 text quoted 'the two post and rail fences that lined the turnpike' here; that sentence is the Antietam turnpike, see WAVE 5); Fairfield (National Tribune, via the NPS) found an old fence on the south side and 'a new post-and-rail fence' on the north side, Sullivan (memoir, via the NPS) 'a stake and rider fence on each side'; fences on both sides are therefore documented for the McPherson-farm stretch (type: a conflict), paling for the stretch near the seminary (Wainwright), Virginia worm for the NPS's 1863 north-side fence; no whole-pike type. WAVE 5 (2026-11-15) CORRECTION: Dawes's sentence 'In climbing the two post and rail fences that lined the turnpike, great numbers of men were killed' is on printed p. 95 of his book, in the Antietam chapter (the Sharpsburg and Hagerstown turnpike, 19 Sept 1862); it is not evidence for the Chambersburg pike and was withdrawn. Dawes's Gettysburg chapter names two turnpike fences and a rail fence and no type other than 'fence rails'; the type at the McPherson farm stays a conflict between Fairfield (new post-and-rail on the north, an old fence on the south), Sullivan (stake and rider) and the NPS (Virginia worm, north side). |
