| Description | Close-out 2026-11-13. Builders are now documented: the battery crews on the artillery chief's orders (Breck: 'by order of Colonel Wainwright, I caused [the position] to be intrenched'; Cooper's Battery B finished four lunettes on the morning of Jul 2; the 1st NY battery men built redans or lunettes for each gun). Window: begun at dusk Jul 1, finished the morning of Jul 2, in use by 16:00. The word lunette is not in the OR; the 1900-1914 books and the NPS use it. Still open: the 1863 dimensions (the preserved lunettes were reconstructed in 1878 and measure about 30 in high: not an 1863 profile). WAVE 4 (2026-11-14): the builder, fabric and hour are a three-way conflict of testimony: the OR (battery crews, light earthworks at dusk, lunettes finished the morning of Jul 2); Bates 1875 and Curtis 1891 (Steinwehr's division, solid lunettes with 'smooth and perfectly level platforms' during Jul 1; the New York and Pennsylvania volumes and Toombs and Pierce also call Steinwehr's position fortified); Hancock (letter of 17 Jan 1878, who reached the hill about 15:00-15:30): 'a great error ... all that I saw ... were some holes (not deep) dug to sink the wheels and trails'. Biddle 1880: Bates's authority 'does not appear'. No 1863 report credits Steinwehr. WAVE 5 (2026-11-15): the 61st Ohio's report (Bown, OR27-1-R258 p.738) puts the regiment 'to the cemetery, upon the south of Gettysburg' on Jul 1 and 'still in position behind the breastworks' on Jul 2 and Jul 5: breastworks existed at one regiment's place on Cemetery Hill by the morning of Jul 2, builder, hour and form not stated. Wiedrich (Battery I, 1st NY, R265 p.752): about 8 p. m. on Jul 2 the Louisiana brigade 'got into the intrenchments of my battery', a second hour at which the gun works existed. WAVE 6 (2026-11-16): two NPS Gettysburg Seminar essays by Barnett agree with the Official Records on the builders and the window: 'vigilant cannoneers put the finishing touches on lunettes dug around their guns' on the evening of Jul 1; Wainwright's cannoneers 'had constructed works around their pieces' and Wainwright 'strengthened his batteries by having them entrench their positions on the hill' in the morning of Jul 2; a gunner of Ricketts's battery buried a severed hand 'in one of the lunettes' as the battery went into position at the relief of Cooper's battery (Ricketts put it at 4 p.m.; the essay notes the dispute). Minnigh's guide of 1892 is one more book that names Steinwehr's command as the builder ('which still remain on Cemetery Hill'; no source named). Breck's letter dated Jul 2 (quoted by Barnett) has the cemetery fences 'all down' by Jul 2: with Howard's Jul 3 sentence the fences were smashed on both days. |
