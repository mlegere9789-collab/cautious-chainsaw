# EG-MED-WW-01: No table of the wounded of Gettysburg by the character of the missile is in the local texts

| Category | evidence missing (searched, not found) |
| Description | The Surgical History tabulates shot wounds by the nature of the projectile for the whole war (Table CXXI, printed page 696: 245,790 shot wounds, the missile ascertained in 141,961) and prints named Gettysburg cases, but no table for Gettysburg. Letterman prints a classified table (cannon-ball, shell, bullet, unclassified) for the Army of the Potomac at Fredericksburg (page 86) and the form for such a return in his Appendix (page 193); his pages on Gettysburg (153 to 160) give counts, not missiles. A weight over weapon for the patients of a Gettysburg hospital is therefore borrowed. The second pass (2026-11-16) added what the record does say of Gettysburg and of the weapons of the war, none of it a share: the Official Records name the missile that struck a named man or a few men of a regiment (rows .326 to .331: a shell, a fragment of shell, a musket-ball, artillery, fragments of shells that burst prematurely), Letterman writes that many shells of the enemy passed over the ground enclosed by the line (.325), a Park Service essay quotes a chaplain on the wounds of the hospitals and a nurse on what shells did on the field (.384, .385). |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | The record does not state it, or the text is not available to this project (Law cl.3); nothing is invented to fill it. |
| Evidence searched | Searched on 2026-11-15 and again on 2026-11-16: the three surgical volumes of the Surgical History (all Gettysburg passages; named cases only), Official Records vol. 27 parts 1 and 2 (the reports of Letterman, Cuyler, Vollum, Guild, Patterson; no classification by missile; part 1 searched a second time for counts of men hit by shell, artillery, canister, grape or musket-ball, with isolated statements only), Letterman 1866 (pages 79 to 87 and 153 to 160 and the Appendix form), the Sanitary Commission report of 1863, Souder, Hospital Scenes, Jacobs, the Park Service essay on Letterman (NPS-SEM13-ESSAY5, acquired 2026-11-16: no classification). The corps medical directors' reports and Surgeon Janes's report are not local (EG-MED-PLAN-01). |
