# EG-MED-WW-06: The missile of the soldiers killed on a field, and so the share of artillery wounds fatal on the field, is not printed

| Description | The Surgical History states that its records show the seat of injury in only 1,173 soldiers killed on the battle-field and gives no missile for them; the share fatal among the wounded by large projectiles is printed only for evulsions of the lower limbs (62 of 164, 40 per cent.). The dead of a field are therefore not weighted by weapon in this work, and no share of artillery wounds fatal on the field is stated. Tripler wrote in 1862 that "the statistics of the positions of the wounds of those killed upon the field would be very interesting" (row med.wounds-weapons.383): he knew of none. The History says that the impact of the large projectiles "is followed by instant death or by wounds of great severity" (row .271) and gives the number of wounds of important blood-vessels as small, with a surgeon's observation near the line of battle of one case of external primary haemorrhage as the instant result of a shot wound (row .377). A nurse's description of the field after the battle (shells severing heads and limbs, bodies flattened against the rocks, as quoted by a Park Service essay, row .385) gives no share. |
