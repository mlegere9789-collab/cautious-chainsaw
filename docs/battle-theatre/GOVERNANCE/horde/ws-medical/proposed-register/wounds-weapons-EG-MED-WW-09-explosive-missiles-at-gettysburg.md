# EG-MED-WW-09: Explosive missiles at Gettysburg: one alleged case, attributions the History itself doubts, no record of issue or use by regiment

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| ID | EG-MED-WW-09 |
| Requirement IDs | OA12 (accurate field hospitals, battle trauma and period-accurate treatment); PDF B.8.5; gate Q26; Law clauses 3, 4 and 7 |
| Category | evidence present, limited in what it supports (an alleged case, a doubted attribution) |
| Description | The Surgical History (Part III, pages 701 to 704) says that 130 cases of wounds were attributed to explosive missiles on the returns (the number of the explosive-musket-ball cell of Table CXXI), that the Ordnance Office records show 33,350 of Gardner's explosive bullets issued early in the war and over 10,000 of them abandoned on the field for want of transportation, that it is probable these fell into Confederate hands, and that many of the reports are doubtful because an ordinary ball can be greatly distorted. Of the sixteen cases it appends as alleged, one is at Gettysburg: Sergeant J. L. Fenton, 9th Massachusetts Battery, admitted to Jarvis Hospital, Baltimore, on 10 July 1863 and dead on 28 July with supposed pyaemia. The Army Medical Museum catalogue (1866, page 378) says "probably Gettysburg" for the same specimen (1613). None of the texts searched states that either army had explosive bullets at Gettysburg. |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | The record does not state it, or the text is not available to this project (Law cl.3): the Ordnance returns of the issue by regiment and the case file of the one alleged case are not among the local texts. Nothing is invented to fill it. |
| Evidence searched | The three volumes of the Surgical History (both scans or issues of each), the Army Medical Museum catalogue, Gross, Tripler, Chisolm 1864, Hammond, the Sanitary Commission report on Gettysburg and Souder, for the words explosive followed by ball, bullet, shot, missile, musket, conical or conoidal within 700 characters of Gettysburg; pages 701 to 704 of Part III read in the OCR text, pages 702 and 703 also in the page images. A search of the whole local library (2.2 GB) for explosive followed by ball, bullet, shot, musket or missile finds nine files: the seven medical texts above (the three volumes of the History in their scans and issues, and the catalogue) and two others, a regimental history and Battles and Leaders volume III, which do not name Gettysburg within 700 characters of the phrase. |
| Autonomous substitute applied | None. The evidence file carries the 130 attributions as a count (rows ex.130-cases and ex.share), the doubt as a row (ex.doubtful), the one Gettysburg case in two rows with a conflict set (cs.explosive-case-1613), and the museum entry for the Figure 386 specimen (ex.spec4561-amm). |
