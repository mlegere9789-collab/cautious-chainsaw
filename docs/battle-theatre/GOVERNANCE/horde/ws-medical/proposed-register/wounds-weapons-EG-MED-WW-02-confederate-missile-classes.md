# EG-MED-WW-02: No classification of the Confederate wounded of Gettysburg by missile is in the local texts

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| ID | EG-MED-WW-02 |
| Requirement IDs | OA12 (accurate field hospitals, battle trauma and period-accurate treatment); PDF B.8.5; gate Q26; Law clauses 3, 4 and 7 |
| Category | evidence missing (searched, not found) |
| Description | Table CXXI mixes the Union and Confederate returns the Surgeon General's Office held and does not separate them; the Confederate medical reports local to this work (Guild, Patterson, Official Records vol. 27 part 2) print counts of wounded and no classes of missile. The Confederate side is carried by the texts its surgeons used: Chisolm's manual (third edition 1864: rows .154 to .174, .252 to .259 and, from the second pass, .324 and .359 to .367, on windage, the spent ball, the face, the head and lodged shot) and the Richmond reprint of Gross (rows .368 to .370), which give the descriptions and no count. |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | The record does not state it, or the text is not available to this project (Law cl.3); nothing is invented to fill it. |
| Evidence searched | Searched on 2026-11-15: Official Records vol. 27 part 2 (reports 427 and 431) and part 3 for missile words in medical context; the Surgical History for "Rebel" cases (named cases only); Chisolm 1862 and 1864. Second pass, 2026-11-16: the report of Surgeon Lafayette Guild (OR27-2-R427; the hits fall on printed pages 327 to 341) searched again for shell, artillery, musket, minie, solid shot, round shot, canister, grape, bullet, gunshot, projectile and ball: the words found are the names of artillery battalions in the casualty return and one use of musket in the narrative; no missile class. The Richmond (1862) and Augusta (1861) reprints of Gross were searched for the passages of rows .001 to .007: the Richmond printing carries them (pages 82 and 83), the Augusta text does not in its OCR. |
| Autonomous substitute applied | None applied; for a Confederate patient the same borrowed bases as for a Union patient may be used and the card says so. |
| Strongest evidence-based reconstruction | The missile word of a Confederate patient is drawn from the same marked weights; the named Confederate Gettysburg cases of the evidence file (rows gc.*: 10th Virginia Cavalry, 9th Virginia Cavalry, 10th Alabama, 5th Texas, 1st North Carolina Cavalry, a Texan regiment) are shown as documented cases; the descriptions of the wounds come from the Confederate printings of the manuals. |
