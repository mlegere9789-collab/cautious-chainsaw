# EG-MED-WW-05: The two scalp tables of the Surgical History, and the 72.6 per cent. of the 1870 text

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| ID | EG-MED-WW-05 |
| Requirement IDs | OA12 (accurate field hospitals, battle trauma and period-accurate treatment); PDF B.8.5; gate Q26; Law clauses 3, 4 and 7 |
| Category | source conflict and internal discrepancy |
| Description | Table V of the first surgical volume (1870, page 91) names the missile in 4,002 gunshot wounds of the scalp (round musket ball 384) and leaves 3,737 unnamed; Table CXXI of the third volume (1883, page 696) gives for the scalp round or musket ball 521 and 3,600 not ascertained (the totals agree: 7,739). The 1870 text under its table says that 72.6 per cent. of the named wounds were by small-arm missiles; the table's own small-arm items (2,612 + 384 + 2 + 94 + 25 = 3,117) are 77.9 per cent. of 4,002. The second pass looked at the page image of page 91 (PDF page 255 of the PDF of the second scan, 2026-11-16): the table, the total 4,002 and the sentence of 72.6 per cent. are printed as the OCR reads them, so the disagreement of the text with its table is the History's own and not an OCR slip; no subset of the eleven items of the table gives 72.6 per cent. of 4,002 (all 2,047 subsets tried). Comparing the two tables cell by cell (row med.wounds-weapons.298) they agree once the columns of 1870 are merged into those of 1883 (buck shot 94 and pistol ball 25 make the 119 of pistol or buckshot; grape shot 9 and case and canister shot and shrapnel 6 make the 15 of grape, canister, etc.; shell fragments 861, with torpedo fragments 4 and a piece of iron 2 if the later table counts them with the fragments of shell, make the 867 of fragments of shell, which the 1883 text does not say) except that 137 cases moved from the not-named column to the round or musket ball (3,737 to 3,600; 384 to 521). The History does not say why. |
