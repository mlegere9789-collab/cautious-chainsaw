# EG-MED-WR-03: The rule and the place for the mortally wounded at Gettysburg are not stated in the local texts

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| ID | EG-MED-WR-03 |
| Requirement IDs | OA12 (accurate field hospitals, battle trauma and period-accurate treatment); PDF B.8.5; gate Q26; Law clauses 3, 4 and 7 |
| Category | evidence missing (to be read) |
| Description | No local text found says where the men judged mortally wounded were laid at Gettysburg, who sat with them, and what they were given. The History says only that men with penetrating wounds of the abdomen should be treated as near as practicable to the spot where they fall and that every rod they are carried adds to the peril; Gross (1862) says dying patients should be screened from their neighbors, placed in the easiest posture, given air and not disturbed; the History says of the head that so many with fractures and penetrating wounds were left dead on the field that the scalp wounds outnumbered them among the treated, and of the spine that many must have rested on the field. |
| Why literal 100% is not possible or blocked | The record does not state it in the texts held (Law cl.3); the hospital narratives read for this group (Jacobs, Hospital Scenes, Souder, Letterman) give single remarks, not a rule. |
| Evidence searched | Letterman (1866), Jacobs (1864), Hospital Scenes (1864), Souder (1864), USSC Gettysburg (1863), Tripler and Blackman, Gross, Chisolm and the three surgical volumes, by the words mortally, moribund, hopeless, dying, aside, apart. |
