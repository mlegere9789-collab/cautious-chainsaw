| Description | The third surgical volume states that plates XXXIX and XL show wounds of entrance and exit made by spherical and conoidal balls and small fragments of shell, drawn from recent wounds at field hospitals during the war by Hospital Steward E. Stauch, U. S. A., as faithful delineations of their early appearances (row med.wounds-weapons.084). The footnote of printed page 712 lists every figure: what the wound is, by which missile, which drawing is the entrance and which the exit. After the second pass the legends are rows, one per drawing or pair of drawings (rows .299 to .319 for the figures the first pass left in a group, with rows .085 and .086 for the shell and ball figures of Plate XXXIX): Plate XXXIX Figs. 1 and 3, 3 and 1, 1 (clavicle), 2, 4, 6, 8, 13 and 14; Plate XL Figs. 1 to 12. The plates were looked at in the page images of the second issue on 2026-11-16 (PDF pages 801 and 805, 70 dpi): each is a page turned sideways with numbered round vignettes of skin, a pale pink to cream ring about a dark red-brown opening of round, star-shaped, slit-like or ragged form, some with a bluish-grey rim, one on Plate XL with pale tissue within the opening. That observation is the engineer's and no row carries it: no text describes the colour, size or edges of a figure. The legend of each figure and the rows on the entrance and exit forms (.081 to .083) are what a card may state. |
