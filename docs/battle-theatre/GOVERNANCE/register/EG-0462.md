| Description | Row T1-114 (Jul 1 noon). Verdict partially_supported. Corrected claim: A published history of the Sisters (Barton, 1898) and a Christian Commission delegate (Prof. Stoever) describe the Catholic church in Gettysburg filled with wounded, with a large oil painting of St. Francis Xavier; the soldiers lay on the pew seats, under them and in every aisle. Barton and Stoever place it after the battle (Stoever: the Sabbath after the battle); twelve Sisters, not fourteen, left Emmitsburg. The row's "already a hospital by noon on Jul 1, mostly amputations, alternate pews removed and boards on the rest" is not supported. Third reading of 2026-11-16: a local history of 1885 (Reily, the Gettysburg church history credited to Mr. Wm. McSherry, Jr.) says the church "was first occupied as such before noon of July 1, 1863" and used for several weeks, with Mass said meanwhile at the house of Nicholas Codori; as a later book alone it carries tier inferred. The Sanitary Commission's report of Aug. 15, 1863 says that most of the wounded of Jul 1 were carried into the churches and public buildings of the town. Neither says "mostly amputations"; Barton has the soldiers lying on the pew seats and under them, so the row's removal of alternate pews is contradicted. Still unsupported or open: The church was used mostly for amputations on Jul 1 / Alternate pews were removed and boards laid on the rest / An account of 1863 or by a participant that dates the first occupation of this church to Jul 1 or says what was done there (Reily 1885 is a later book and gives no hour); the operations (mostly amputations); the pew arrangement (Barton says the pews stood); fourteen Sisters (twelve per Barton). |
