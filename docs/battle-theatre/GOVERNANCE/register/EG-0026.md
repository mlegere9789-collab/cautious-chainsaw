## History

### 2026-11-11: ws9 library pass (decision: applied-narrowed)

The table above is unchanged and remains the entry text of record before this note. Source texts are archive.org OCR (unverified against page images). Review record: horde/ws9-sources/APPLIED.md.

- Jacobs 1864 (published eyewitness, Gettysburg resident), under the printed heading "Thursday, June 25.": "Late on Thursday evening, however, 100 picked ... But the heavy rain of that night caused them to be detained" (p.14). Under the heading "Friday, June 26.": "Being wet from the rain which had fallen during the most of the day" (p.16). Supports: heavy rain the night of 25 Jun and rain most of 26 Jun. Sub-claim tier: documented (single published eyewitness, no clock time). It gives no start hour for the 25 Jun rain, so it neither confirms the PDF "8 am" nor the 1885 "8 p.m."; the proposal word "contradicted" is NOT adopted (rain earlier on the 25th is not excluded by "that night").
- Fremantle (British observer with the Confederate army), diary of 4 Jul: "At 1 p. M. the rain began to descend in toiTents" (p.274, OCR) and "Shortly after 9 p. m. the rain began to descend in torrents." (p.276); the following entry has "thunder and lightning, torrents of rain". Sub-claim tier: documented as one witness statement (his clock, Confederate side, place not stated). Added as a selectable Jul 4 rain scenario (13:00 and after 21:00). Existing schedules are not deleted and not refuted. Comparison with the named preset schedules was not re-checked in this review.
- Letterman 1866 p.156: "drenching rain which fell after the battle" (no hour).
- NOT touched: humidity, pressure, 87 F in shade, Jul 2 14:00 cloud, Jul 3 rainfall, Union-side Jul 4 clock. Entry remains open.
