| Description | Row T1-197 (Jul 1 NIGHT). Verdict partially_supported. Corrected claim: The National Park Service landscape report says: troops began to arrive on Cemetery Hill on July 1 when Howard posted a reserve of his Eleventh Corps; late in the day, as Union troops retreated, they reinforced the defensive lines around Cemetery Hill; lines of artillery were positioned near the summit along the northern and western borders of Evergreen Cemetery with caissons and horses among the grave plots; it was reported that General Howard ordered troops to lay the headstones flat on the ground to prevent their destruction; troops tore down wood and stone fences and rebuilt stone fences as breastworks. Battle damage included bullet holes and cracks in some grave markers, damage to grave-plot fences, removal of planks from the fences along the Baltimore pike, and a damaged gatehouse. The report does not date the laying flat of the headstones or the fence damage within the three days, says nothing of 'iron fences', and names no monument destroyed. The seed's attribution of the night of Jul 1 is therefore not documented; the damage is shown as occurring during the battle, with the evening of Jul 1 as the earliest time. Added by the second reading: newspapers of July 1863 say that in the cannonade of Jul 3 the tombstones were knocked over and iron railings torn (Boston Journal's correspondent, printed Jul 23), that tombstones and beautiful monuments were demolished (Bellows Falls Times, Jul 10), and that monuments and headstones lay overturned and graves were trampled by horses (Chicago Daily Tribune, Jul 12); none dates this damage to the evening of Jul 1. Still unsupported or open: The share of this damage that occurred on the night of Jul 1 (against Jul 2 and Jul 3) / The Jul 1 evening share of the cemetery damage (the newspapers date the iron railings, tombstones and monuments to the cannonade of Jul 3); a contemporary (1863) description of the damage of Jul 1 itself. |
