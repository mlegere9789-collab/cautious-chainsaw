| Description | Row T1-092 (Jul 1 ≈09:30-10:20). Verdict corrected. Corrected claim: In a letter of 18 October 1865 to Maj. Gen. Hancock (the original is in the Bachelder Papers; it is printed in a 1989 U.S. Army War College study, which was read, the original was not seen) the signal officer Aaron B. Jerome says that he was sent back to find a prominent position and watch the enemy; that as early as seven A.M. he reported their advance and took his station in the steeple of the "Theological Seminary"; that Buford came up and looked at them through his glass and then formed his small cavalry force; that, looking toward Emmitsburg, he called Buford's attention to an army corps advancing about two miles off and distinguished it as the "first" by its "corps flag"; and that Buford held on, unaided, for half an hour against a whole corps of the rebels until Reynolds and a few of his staff rode up at a gallop and hailed Buford, who was with him in the steeple. The New York commission history (1900) and Bachelder (1873) tell the same scene without his name. No report of 1863 states it, and Weld's diary of 1863 has Reynolds talk with Buford in a field on the right of the road (T1-096): both are carried as scenarios. Jerome gives "seven A.M." and "half an hour" and no clock time for the meeting. Third reading of 2026-11-16: the National Park Service's HABS record of 1966 says that the Seminary cupola was used as a post of observation by Buford and Reynolds on Jul 1 (naming Wentz and Tilberg as its basis). Two later narratives give other orders of the scene and are carried as scenarios beside Jerome's: Wentz (1926: Buford alone in the cupola at the start, Reynolds there about an hour after the engagement began, then both ride out) and Doubleday (1882, printed by Hoke 1887: Buford in the steeple after an hour's fighting, Reynolds climbing up to him). No new source gives a clock time or the ladder. Still unsupported or open: A ladder led from the attic to the cupola in 1863 / Time of the meeting 09:30-10:20 / The ladder from the attic to the cupola; a clock time for the sighting and for Reynolds' arrival at the steeple (Jerome gives "seven A.M." and "half an hour"); Jerome's letter was read only as printed in a 1989 study, the original (Bachelder Papers, U.S. Army Military History Institute) was not seen. |
