Owner addition OA01: "read-only reuse of the 16 tour stops and 512 monuments as camera bookmarks". This note says where the site's data is, what the theatre did with it before, what the two added modules do, what rests on what evidence, what was run and what was not.

## 1. Where the site's data is, and why the theatre "showed" 77 of 512 and 2 of 16

The main site keeps its data as literals inside its entry bundle and in one small file; nothing is in `data-src/` as a separate monument file (`data-src/legacy-manifest.tsv` and `data-src/build/data-manifest.json` only pin the hash of the one file that is).

| data | where | count | hash |
|---|---|---|---|
| tour stops | literal `d2` in `assets/index-*.js` of every site tree (`baseline/dist`, `legacy/dist`, every `release/*/dist`, R1g included) | 16 | canonical JSON sha256 `31149ceaa519…` (pinned by `tests/t0/walkthrough-guard.spec.mjs`) |
| monuments, memorials, markers, tablets | literal `_A` in the same bundle | 512 | `0204397d5ccc…` (same guard) |
| second list of state memorials | `data/monuments.json` of every tree | 9 | `c23ef400f626…` (pinned in `data-src/legacy-manifest.tsv` and `data-src/build/data-manifest.json`) |

All ten trees of this checkout hold the same three datasets (checked by `node app/tests/ext-site-source.mjs`). The shell already read them before this work: `data/site-bookmarks.json` (written by `app/tools/extract-site-bookmarks.mjs`) feeds the Views panel.

**The premise "the theatre shows 77 of 512 monuments and 2 of 16 tour stops" is the wrong way round.** The Views panel lists all 512 and all 16, and flies to 435 monuments and 14 stops. The 77 and 2 are the ones it disables as "outside the loaded ground": the loaded ground is 6,160 by 6,123 m, and the shell's rule `geo.inside(x, z, 0)` gives exactly 77 monuments and 2 stops (Tour Stop 2, Eternal Light Peace Memorial, 1,120 m beyond the north edge, and Tour Stop 3, Oak Ridge, 557 m beyond it). By the site's own place lines: 45 lie north of the town on the First Day field (Doubleday, Howard, Buford and Reynolds Avenues, Barlow's Knoll), 28 east and north-east (24 place lines name East Cavalry Field or East Cavalry Battlefield, 3 the Hanover Road or Brinkerhoff's Ridge, 1 Highland Avenue; the farthest 3,201 m beyond the edge), 2 on the Chambersburg Pike west of the town, 2 at South Cavalry Field and on the Taneytown Road. Nothing in the shell drops them for another reason. The new modules offer all of them; the 77 and 2 from the edge of the ground (section 4, DD-WORLD-02, gap ws-world-EG-MON-02). A request note to the Views owner asks that its own list use them too (`GOVERNANCE/horde/requests/world-content-views-beyond-ground.md`).

## 2. Read-only reuse and the drift check

`tests/ext-site-source.mjs` finds the two literals with the JavaScript parser (never by pattern over the data), projects each record to the fields a mark, a row or a bookmark needs, and writes `data/site-memorials.json` and `data/site-tour-stops.json`. Each file records the hash of the site's data it was read from (`source`), the pin it must equal, and the hash of its own contents (`copy`). What is left in the site: for a tour stop the walkthrough narration, the video link and the street-level image id and key; for the state memorials the note at the head of the file. A field of a monument the copy does not know stops the copy (a new field is a decision, not a silent loss).

* `node docs/battle-theatre/app/tests/ext-site-source.mjs` reports every tree, its hashes and whether the copies are current.
* `... --check` exits 1 when a copy differs from the site's data in any tree, when a tree differs from the guard's pin, or when the copy's own hash no longer matches its contents; each difference is a sentence.
* `... --write` rewrites the two copies from `baseline/dist` (refused when the trees disagree among themselves or with the pins). Nothing under `baseline/`, `legacy/`, `release/`, `data-src/` or `tests/t0/` is ever written: the only write calls of the file go to the two copies (a test reads the source to prove it).
* The pages also check, at start, that each file still holds the hash recorded with it (`crypto.subtle`; where an address gives no crypto the check is left out and the panel says so). A file that fails is not shown and the shell logs why.

## 3. The later memorials (`monuments.mjs`)

* **Off at every entry.** No canvas exists until the viewer switches the layer on or picks a memorial from the list; nothing is remembered between visits (no storage). The switch is titled "Later memorials (post-battle; not part of the 1863 scene)" (More drawer and under the structure switches of Views); the panel is "Later memorials" (More, then Later memorials).
* **Never part of the 1863 scene.** A memorial is a flat mark on the module's own 2D canvas over the picture (right under the shell's own overlay), never a scene object: no scene hook is registered (checked in the page: `host._hooks.length` stays 0), nothing changes at any minute of the scrubber (`sceneObjectsAt(t)` is `[]`; gate Q03). The camera never moves the clock.
* **Labelled.** While the layer is on, a notice stays on the picture, in two rows and about 80 px high (the left column is shared with the clock face and the notices of the other added layers): the tier pill, "Later memorials shown" and a Hide button; then "512 marks, 77 of them beyond the loaded ground. Put up after the battle: not part of the 1863 scene." with a link to the panel. On a phone the shell hides its own legend and lays the left column out loose, so the notice is placed under the clock face (its height is read) and is not hidden by the phone layout. While a memorial's card is open the notice gives way to it (a card says the same, and carries a "Hide the marks" button), so that the card has room: the shell makes room for a card by shrinking the clock face when its root says `data-card="on"`, and the memorial card sets the same mark (it never takes it from a unit or structure card that is open); the evidence of the card (tier, position, what "after the battle" rests on, the gaps) is a closed section under the facts. Every named mark reads "<name> · later memorial", the picked one adds "· N m beyond the loaded ground" when it is. The card says "Later memorial", carries the layer's tier pill (inferred), a "Post-battle" pill, and the year as an open-gap pill (Q23). The tier of a mark is the dashed outline and the shape beside every name, so it never depends on colour alone; the fill is the side the site's list gives; a memorial beyond the loaded ground is hollow.
* **All 512 can be found.** The panel searches name, honouree, place line, group and park number, filters by side, group and ground (all, on the loaded ground, beyond it), and lists in the site's order; a row flies there and opens the card. A tap on a mark picks it (the shell's own pick comes first and wins; a drag or a long press is not a tap); Escape puts the card away.
* **Positions.** The site's Monuments list for all 512; for the nine state memorials the site also lists in `data/monuments.json` (1 to 24 m apart), a choice in the panel (DD-WORLD-01).
* **The year each was put up.** It is not in the site's data. For 33 memorials a National Park Service Cultural Landscapes Inventory prints it for a memorial of the same name (`data/site-memorial-years.json`, built by `tests/ext-site-years.mjs`, DD-WORLD-05); the card shows the year with its tier pill, the report, the PDF and printed page and the quotation, and the list row says "put up 1929". For the other 479 the card says so as an open gap (ws-world-EG-MON-01). The rule is strict: the report names the memorial with the site's own name letter for letter and says it was erected or placed (the words "and Fence", "and Flank Markers", "and Benches" after the name do not change which memorial it is); a name two entries of the site share, a name the report continues with "Position Marker", two different years, and a place the site puts far from the area the report describes are left out and printed with their reasons. 32 are documented; one (26th Pennsylvania Infantry Monument, "ca.1888-1900") is inferred because the report hedges.
* **What the layout leaves out.** A mark is left out while the ground lies between the camera and the place: the straight line from the camera to the mark is sampled against the terrain height (`groundHides` in `site-places.mjs`; a step of a fortieth of the distance, 12 to 60 m; the last 6 m before the mark and 1.5 m over the line are not counted), so at soldier's eye level about 40 % of the marks in view of Cemetery Ridge are behind the ground (67 of 154 in a node count at 9 m behind the Pennsylvania State Memorial, 1.8 m up) and from the shell's overview none are (0 of 442). The picked memorial is always drawn. The panel says in one sentence that marks are left out behind the ground and names kept from under panels. Trees and buildings are not in the terrain, so they never hide a mark. A name is not put under a panel: the layout is given the rectangles of the top bar, the dock, the open drawer and the panels of the left column (read at most every 400 ms while marks are shown, redrawn only when one moved), and a name that would lie under one is skipped for the next nearest; the marks themselves may lie under a panel, and the picked memorial is always named. Neither rule changes which memorials are marked (`marksFor`, gate Q03 and Q23 do not see them).
* **Cost.** Off: nothing runs. On: one loop over the 512 positions when the camera moves (same camera, same marks: nothing is redrawn), a layout without allocation, a few canvas fills however many marks are in view, at most 12 names. The ground test adds one line of terrain samples per mark in view: in node, on the shared 4-vCPU machine under load (an upper bound, not a timing claim), one layout took a median 0.59 ms from the shell's overview (442 marks in view, p95 4.6 ms), 0.25 ms from 95 m up over the ridge and 0.13 ms at eye level; without the test 0.03, 0.08 and 0.01 ms.
* **Limits (stated, not hidden).** A mark is a flat mark on a 2D canvas over the picture: it is hidden by the ground (see above) but not by trees, buildings, figures or the fences and walls of the scene, and it does not move with the scene's depth. That is the cost of "never an object of the 1863 scene" (gate Q03) and was chosen over a 3D object that would need a date. Nothing about the marks is tied to the minute on the scrubber. The marks are drawn on a canvas of their own, so no renderer draws them; they were run on WebGL2 with software GL only, and no run was made with WebGPU. The positions are the site's list as it stands (503 of 512 state no source: gap ws-world-EG-MON-03).

## 4. The tour stops (`tour.mjs`)

Each of the 16 stops is a camera bookmark in the panel "Tour stops". For the 14 stops on the loaded ground the camera stands at the viewpoint the site's walkthrough uses for the stop, 30 m up, and looks at the stop (the arithmetic of the shell's own `flyToLngLat`, tested equal for all 14, so the Views list and this panel never disagree); where the viewpoint and the stop are one place it looks along the walkthrough's heading. For the 2 beyond the ground the camera stands 60 m inside the edge, 220 m back from the nearest point of it and 140 m up, looks toward the stop, and the pin, the row and the announcement say how far beyond which edge it is. The only mark is one pin ("Tour Stop 8 · Little Round Top · modern tour stop"), shown while the stop is in view, with a close button; its label goes to the other side of the place when it would run under the open drawer or off the picture. A stop is a stop of a modern tour: it is not an 1863 feature, nothing is drawn for it, and choosing it moves the camera only, never the clock (DD-WORLD-04); the day the site gives it is shown beside it.
