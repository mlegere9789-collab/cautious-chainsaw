Part of the figures round of 2026-10-31 (brief `GOVERNANCE/briefs/bt-visuals-common.md`, ANIMATION part). Code: `app/src/anim3d/`. Data: `app/data/anim3d/`. Tests: `app/tests/anim3d.test.mjs` (62 tests, about 35 s, node only; 37 from the first round, 10 added by the follow-up of section 10, 15 by round 3 of section 11). Decisions: `DEC-ANIM-01..03`. Event interface: `app/docs/BT-EVENTS.md`. Register entries: `GOVERNANCE/register/EG-ANIM-01..13.md`. Decision: `DEC-ANIM-01` in `decision_log.md`.

**Round 3 of 2026-10-31 (section 11): prop drag in 40 re-authored clips (16 clips above 30 cm to 2), fall_spin grip, sabre contacts 0.1-0.3 cm, the cover_wall_fire flip, ground-aware kneeling (lying load open), six variants per crowd state with a unison test, a load-independent allocation gate (23 B average, 160 B ceiling restored and lowered to 64 B), and steep ground to 80 degrees (4.05 cm worst). Still open: arm-solver hysteresis (D-ANIM-05), the lying load (G-ANIM-25), the drag of the stretcher, limber, ambulance and bayonet clips.**

**Follow-up of 2026-10-31 (section 10): the items the first report listed as NOT MET are closed in code with node measurements, except where section 10 says otherwise: joint limits (0 frames with excess in 207 clips), hand to grip (at most 0.2 cm in every drill, musketry and artillery clip), G9 clips (85 added, 8 new families), gear springs (built), real DEM slopes (measured on the application's heightfield), rifle socket proportions (agreed and applied).**

**Status in one paragraph.** Everything here was built and measured in node only (no browser, no GPU: a Release 1 acceptance run was timing the machine). Nothing has been looked at on screen. The clips are procedural reconstructions authored from the drill manuals and general knowledge, every clip is tiered, and no clip is a "real motion". The gates that node can measure are reported below with their numbers, including the ones that are **not met** (joint limits outside walking and standing, several attachment errors, gate G9 coverage). The renderer is not yet wired to this module (no file under `render/`, `src/stage.mjs` or `src/theatre.mjs` was touched, as the brief requires); the façade `AnimSystem` is the integration surface.

| `clipdef.mjs`, `library.mjs` | keyed and procedural clip definitions, baking at 60 Hz, nlerp sampling and blending, the library (122 clips), content hashes |

| `events.mjs`, `index.mjs` | the bus and the `AnimSystem` façade (`advance(t0, t1)`, `sampleFigure`, near set, placement hook) |

Data files (all regenerated by `node docs/battle-theatre/app/tools/build-anim3d.mjs`, checked equal by the tests): `clips.json` (catalogue with tier, sources, gap id, content hash), `drill-timing.json` (every table of section 2), `drill-march-extras.json` (items ANIM-*), `activity-map.json`, `layout.json`, and `measured.json` (written by `tools/measure-anim3d.mjs`; the numbers of section 6).

Conventions: right-handed, +Y up, +Z forward, +X the figure's left (as `skeleton.json`); pose = local quaternion relative to the rest A-pose; clip frame = `[28 core quaternions | 6 translations | gripL, gripR, contactL, contactR, rootX, rootZ, rootYaw]` = 137 floats (28 core joints; the root, the pelvis and the four prop bones are translatable; fingers follow the grip scalars; gear/spring bones and eyes are not stored). Time `t` of events is simulation seconds since 1863-06-01 00:00 LMT (`src/clock.mjs` minutes times 60).

### 2.2 The manual of arms

* Every command is one time, divided into motions; each motion lasts **1/90 minute = 0.6667 s**, except the cartridge, rammer and (un)fix-bayonet motions, which have no fixed cadence (H.S.S. paras 128-131; `INF-GEN-02`, `INF-GEN-03`, `HAR-S01`). Documented. The clip durations of the regulated changes of position equal motions x 0.6667 s (test): order arms 2 motions, shoulder arms 2, right shoulder shift 2, trail arms 2, Ready 3, Aim 1, charge bayonet 2.
* **Load in nine times** (H.S.S. 156-170, Casey 163-177; `INF-L9-01..09`, `HAR-S02..S10`): Load (1 motion), Handle cartridge (1), Tear cartridge (1), Charge cartridge (1), Draw rammer (3), Ram cartridge (1), Return rammer (3), Prime (2), Shoulder arms (2): 15 motions, **5 regulated, 10 exempt**. Documented. Which motions are exempt is read from the printed rule (cartridge and rammer motions are exempt; Load, Prime and Shoulder arms are not named, so they are held to the cadence: inferred reading).
* **Ready / Aim / Fire** (H.S.S. 171-186; `INF-FIRE-01..04`, `HAR-S12..S16`): Ready 3 motions, Aim 1, Fire 1; "after Load the men load and return at once to Ready" (`HAR-S29`): in the firing cycle the ninth time (Shoulder arms) is replaced by Ready. A "short pause between Aim and Fire" is recommended (`INF-COY-04`, `HAR-C09`): length not printed; 0.3-1.2 s range, 0.6 s used (plausible-procedural).
* The **length of the exempt motions** is not printed (gap G-ANIM-05). The loading cycle is fitted to the fire plan's seconds per round: regulated motions keep 0.6667 s, the ten exempt motions share the remainder by relative weights (handle 0.9, tear 1.1, charge 2.0, draw 3 x 0.9, ram 1.6, return 3 x 0.9; the weights are plausible-procedural, only their sum matters). The fastest cycle the clip allows is 12.5 s (every exempt weight unit at 0.5 s): `cycleDurations(...).floor`.
* Kneeling and lying fire (`INF-KNEEL-01`, `INF-LYING-01..03`, `HAR-S33`, `HAR-S34`): the order of actions is documented; the poses are inferred.
* The bayonet exercise (`HAR-S20..S22`, `HAR-S35`): fix (3 motions, exempt), charge (2), guard against infantry and cavalry (2) are documented as motions; thrust, parry and the clubbed musket are **plausible-procedural** (no text; G-ANIM-08).

### 2.4 Artillery detachment (Instruction for Field Artillery 1863, School of the Piece)

Gunner + Nos. 1-8 + the chief of the piece (`ART-FA-01..09`); posts unlimbered (para 95) are in `drill-timing.json crew_posts_default` (No. 1 and 2 two feet outside the wheels; Nos. 3 and 4 abreast the cascable; No. 5 five yards behind the left wheel; Nos. 6 and 7 at the limber chest; No. 8 four yards behind the limber; the limber's pole end six yards from the trail handspike end). Duties and order: Load (sponge, load, ram, point), Ready (prick, insert primer), Fire, run up (`ART-FA-12..46`, `ART-FA-50..55`). Documented. Relative step lengths: sponge 4 : ram 3 follow the printed motion counts (`ART-FA-15`); the others are plausible-procedural (G-ANIM-01). Recoil: no distance printed (`ART-FA-14`); Gibbon's theoretical free recoil of 17.4 ft is a derived upper bound; the carriage run-out used is 0.9-1.8 m, plausible-procedural (G-ANIM-04); FIGURES' rig note models 0.5-0.6 m.

## 4. How the pieces run

* **State machine** (`unit-anim.mjs`). A unit has a timeline of segments `{t0, activity, speed, route, posture, plan, night}` (the activity events of the history layer, the speed from the path). Contract activities: march, double_quick, halt, stand, rest, fire, serve_guns, gallop, build_works, camp. Extra names accepted: advance, retreat, charge, skirmish, night_camp, picket, wounded_walk, carry_wounded, walk, trot, canter, cheer, bury (`data/anim3d/activity-map.json`). `clipAt(k, t, out)` is pure. March: the gait is chosen from the ground speed; the clip is played at rate `v / v_clip` clamped to 0.85-1.15 and cross-faded between the two nearest gaits in the gaps, with the same cycle phase (both start at the left heel strike), 0.35 s blends; a unit moving exactly at a drill speed has **zero slip**. Fire: before the first cycle `ready_from_shoulder` ends exactly on the cycle boundary; the cycle clip is time-warped per segment so that its Fire step lands on the plan's shot; after the plan ends the man finishes the cycle, then `shoulder_from_ready`. Falls: from the stage's `state` events (time, fate), style by the variation scalar; a 0.12 s blend in, 0.2 s settle into the lying loop.
* **Fire plan** (`fire-plan.mjs`). Pure `shotTime(i, k)`; `shotsBetween(t0, t1, cb)` enumerates a window (a few arithmetic operations per figure plus one hash per candidate shot: 0.004 ms per 1/60 s window for 280 men). Modes and their documented structure in section 2.3 and BT-EVENTS.
* **Events** (`BT-EVENTS.md`): fire (with the six fixed arguments and the optional `info`), misfire (never unless a rate is set), reload (near set), drill_step, footfall (near set), unit_march, command, fall, lie, gun, hoofbeat, activity, impact. The FX requests R-FX-1..3 are implemented (see the REQUESTS block of BT-EVENTS).
* **Terrain** (`terrain.mjs`): `slopeAt`, `slopeAlong`, `FootPlanter.conform(frame, ground, x, z, yaw)`: the pelvis follows the lowest planted foot, each leg is re-solved to its ankle target, the planted foot is tilted onto the slope and its heel/ball put on the ground (the pelvis is lowered further until both planted legs reach). 3.6 microseconds per figure.
* **Bake layouts** (`bake.mjs`, section 5 of the notes below and the header of the file).

## 5. Cost model (on paper, from measured node numbers; GPU numbers are in the browser list)

* Per figure on the CPU (near list): sample + blend 0.4 us, expand 0.2 us, FK 0.5 us, skin matrices 0.4 us (61 bones), terrain pass 3.6 us: **about 1.5 us without and 5 us with the terrain pass** (measured, node on this machine; the plan's budget for the CPU is 8 ms at 4x throttle: that is about 400 near figures with terrain pass, or about 1,300 without, at 4x). The prototype's near list is at most 1,536 figures (largest seen 334).
* Mid LOD (up to 60,000 in the list, 4,886 largest seen): **no CPU work per figure**; the instance carries clip index and time (16 B), the vertex shader fetches the baked bone matrices. Bone-matrix texture for all 115 non-horse, non-gun clips at 60 Hz, the 36 mid bones: 41,708 frames x 108 texels x 8 B (RGBA16F) = **36 MB**; 61 bones: 61 MB. Slow clips (stand, lie, camp, sleep) can be stored at 30 Hz (the plan's G15 first-interactive budget is 60 MB). The pose buffer for the compute path is 22.9 MB (float32). VAT would cost 333 MB per 1,000 vertices for the same frames, which is why it is for props and the far-near LOD only (render/PLAN.md section 2 measured bone matrices 17x smaller).
* G1 fit on paper: the animation adds per frame (a) the activity-to-clip evaluation for the near and mid lists (about 0.5 us per figure on the CPU, or the same logic in the pose compute pass: 0.05 ms for 600 x 60 bones measured in render/results), (b) event enumeration (0.004 ms per window for one firing battalion), (c) nothing for the far LOD (impostor views are baked from the same clips). The measurement itself is in the browser list.

### 11.1 Hand-anchored props (D-ANIM-01): the drag is gone from the repaired clips
**Root cause.** The first rounds keyed a held prop by the position of its ORIGIN and an orientation, and put the grips at distances measured from that origin. A sponge-and-rammer staff keyed with its head 0.6 m ahead of the chest and its axis pointing forward had its grips 1.0 to 1.8 m from the shoulders (No. 1: up to 112 cm out of reach); the shovel, axe, ladle, colour staff, rail, sabre and the gunner's handspike were keyed the same way. The least-squares pull did not solve that: it moved the prop to the hands and the measurement was only a number in `measured.json`. A second cause: the weight of a hand that is letting go of the piece (0.05 to 0.3) multiplies its error by 1/weight in the pull, so a hand that barely touched the rifle dragged it (the falls).

**Fix.** A new spec channel `hold` (1 = prop B, 2 = the long arm) makes the solver derive the prop from the HANDS: `rhp`/`lhp` are the palm targets, the right grip is at `rhgb`/`rhga` from the prop's origin, the left grip is at the right grip plus or minus the hand spacing (rifle: further along the stock; staff, tool: nearer the head), the axis runs between the two hands (one hand: the authored direction), the weight of the left hand blends the two. A hand target is first brought inside the arm's annulus (0.30 to 0.52 m from the shoulder joint, measured on the rig); the distance is reported (`holdClampL/R`) and the prop shift reported by `attachment()` is now (hand target moved from where it was authored) plus (any ordinary pull that followed). Authoring helpers: `authoring.mjs holdProp / holdCh / holdify / reachOK`. The arms are solved to the hand targets; where a hand still misses by more than 1.5 mm it takes the palm position it reached and the prop is derived again (up to six rounds, only for hands that grip fully), then the old pull finishes the job.

Re-authored: the whole gun detachment (gunner, Nos. 1-8; staff, rounds, wire, spike; `clips-artillery.mjs`), the eight falls (the rifle is held until the man lets go, then a snap key releases it from where the hand had it; `clips-fall.mjs`), officers' sword (carry, raise, point), colours (carry, plant, pick up), dig, chop, cook, the rail, drink, the orderly's bucket (`clips-misc.mjs`), the rider's sabre draw, carry, cut, point and command (`clips-cavalry.mjs`), the paired duel (`clips-g9.mjs`). The hand positions were taken from the solver's own reach (`tools` scratch scripts printed, per key time, where the hand could be): e.g. the shovel's left hand at (0.09, 0.83, 0.51) instead of (0.06, 0.72, 0.58).

### 11.6 Allocation gate replaced (DEC-ANIM-03)
The old gate micro-benchmarked single calls and had been loosened from 160 B to 200 B because the same unchanged `clipAt` measured 73 B or 178 B. Root causes found: the figure evaluator stored doubles in object fields (11 per `clipAt`, 16 B each in optimised code, tier dependent), passed freshly computed doubles to calls V8 did not inline (`sampleClip`, `qnlerp`, `cycleDurations`, `hash4` results above 2^31), built strings and tables per call (`'gun_crew_' + role`, an object literal for the mounted gait periods), and the benchmark itself ran its "march" workload into the fire segment after 12,500 iterations. Fixes: times, weights and rates live in Float64Arrays (`ClipSel.d`, `Slot.d`, `wv`, `loc`), the clip index tables are precomputed, `sampleClipAt` / `blendFramesAt` read the time from an array and inline the quaternion nlerp, `FirePlan.locate` and `cycleDurationsArr` return through arrays, the jitter hash is one function with its four rounds inlined (`jitHash`, equal to `hash4` for 200,000 argument sets).
New measure (`tools/measure-alloc.mjs`, fresh process, `--expose-gc --min-semi-space-size=16`): total heap growth over **200,000 figure updates per workload** (drill march, march in the speed gap, change of activity with a blend, fire at will, the gun cycle, camp, a falling and a fallen man), warm-up first, a forced GC before every chunk of 10,000 and at the end, a GC observer to prove no scavenge ran inside a chunk, in three V8 configurations. Result (bytes per update; one boxed double is 16 and the harness itself passes one): default average **23.1**, worst 34.7; `--no-maglev` 20.3 / 31.8; `--no-turbo-inlining` 27.8 / 55.6; retained 38 KB over 1.4 million updates (0.03 B per update). The old micro-benchmarks had reported, for the same unchanged code, 73 or 178 to 190 B for `clipAt` on the march path (184 B without inlining) and 204 B for `sampleFigure` on the fire path without inlining. Test ceilings: 64 B per workload, 40 B average, 0.1 B retained per update, in all three configurations (the original gate was 160 B, then 200 B).

### 11.7 Steep ground (D-ANIM-04 / G-ANIM-24)
**Which real cells exceed 60 degrees.** On the application's DEM (1536 x 1536, about 4 m, modern bare earth; `render/prototype/data/height.bin`) the steepest bilinear patch is **80.4 degrees** (81.9 by a one-posting difference); **1,105 places** (native step, slope over 2 m) exceed 60 degrees, 384 exceed 70, 74 exceed 75, 1 exceeds 80; 2,173 exceed 45 and 11,192 exceed 28. All the places above 60 degrees lie in ONE area, x 1693 to 2199, z 704 to 1222 (about 500 m by 500 m, 24 clusters of at least one patch), 700 to 900 m from Powers' Hill, Wolf Hill and Rock Creek and 1.4 km from Spangler's Spring: man-made-looking cuts of the modern DEM (the first round called them "quarry and railway cuts", an inference that was not checked against a source; they are not 1863 ground). The faces the brief names are not steeper than 60: the steepest slope within 150 m of Devil's Den is 54.3 degrees, Big Round Top 50.6, Little Round Top 40.5, Wolf Hill 35.6, Powers' Hill 37.3, Culp's Hill 28.0, Cemetery Hill 28.8, Seminary Ridge 19.6, Cemetery Ridge 12.0.
**Failure.** The planter lowered the pelvis to the lowest foot, which cannot work when the other foot's ground is far above it: at 70 degrees two feet 0.2 m apart along the slope stand 0.55 m apart in height; the hinge cannot fold that far and the foot floated (45 cm at 50-60 degrees, 16 m worst at 80).
**Behaviour (`terrain.mjs`, plausible-procedural, G-ANIM-24; no acquired text gives a marching or scrambling limit).** Classes by slope under the figure: walk to 28 degrees (the planter is exactly the old one), steep_walk to 50, scramble to 60, climb above; `slopeSpeedFactor` (1 to 28 degrees, 0.5 at 50, 0.15 at 80) for the formation layer. Above 28 degrees the planter climbs by steps: each foot's offset along the slope from the hip is soft-limited to half a stair (STEP_H 0.30 m, so both feet differ by at most 0.30 m in height), the body leans into the hill about the hip (0.5 degree per degree of slope, up to 30), the foot is tilted onto the face and its lowest sole point put on the ground, and the pelvis is placed analytically so that both legs are inside the knee's reach (not past full extension and not past 110 degrees of flexion where it can be helped), instead of only ever lowering it. Everything else of the clip (arms, head, timing) is kept.
**Result** (3 clips, 4 headings, 4 frames, every 2nd place above 28 degrees plus planes, contact error of the sole points to the same bilinear ground, cm):

| slope under the figure | p95 before | max after |
|---|---|---|
| up to 30 | 22 | 0.001 |
| 30-40 | 28 | 0.11 |
| 40-50 | 40 | 0.58 |
| 50-55 / 55-60 | 56 / 69 | 1.12 / 1.43 |
| 60-65 / 65-70 | 86 / 109 | 1.96 / 3.70 |
| 70-75 / 75-80 | 142 / 190 | 4.05 / 3.28 |
| 80-90 | 219 | 0.00 |

p95 after is 0.00 in every bin; the maximum over about 291,000 samples is **4.05 cm**, under the 5 cm asked. Cost: 5.9 microseconds per figure on 60 degrees (3.6 on flat ground before); the 441-place scan of section 10.5 and the 30 degree gate are unchanged. Limits that remain, stated: (1) a foot on a face steeper than the ankle can take (about 40 degrees) is tilted onto the face beyond its joint limit (the planter does not clamp the ankle, as before); real climbers use ledges the 4 m DEM does not resolve; (2) hands do not touch the slope in scramble mode (no hand contacts), and the carried piece keeps its pose; (3) no new climbing clip was authored: the behaviour is a pose pass on the existing clips, so the stage should apply `slopeSpeedFactor` to the unit's speed.

## Cavalry round (ws10)
`UnitAnimator` (arm `cavalry`): segment kind `dismount` (field `dm` = printed share, `roles` from `history/cavalry.mjs mountRoles`: 0 mounted, 1 fights afoot, 2 holds horses); `clipAt` is `_clip` plus `_mountHorse`; the horse of a fallen mounted trooper plays `horse_fall` from his fall time (option `horseWithRider`, default the model's 1); a man afoot has `ClipSel.m` 1 or 2 and the horse offset `d[SEL_HZ]` (-12 m after 7 s, smoothstep, a walk clip while it moves, idle after); the holder clip is `horse_holder`.
