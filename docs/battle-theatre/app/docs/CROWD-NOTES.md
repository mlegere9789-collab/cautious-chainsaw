## What is drawn
Every drawn figure (127,572 in the real layer), one-to-one, from its own kit manifest: `instanceFromHistory` per figure (no `Math.random`; the variation scalar `sold1.w` still comes from the manifest). The prototype's near / mid / far soldier passes are switched off (`R.o.flags`), guns, terrain, structures and the effects stay.

| LOD | distance (1080p, 60 deg; scaled by pixels and fov; the two Settings sliders move them) | what | skeleton / pose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | < 10 m (budget 96 men, nearest first, the rest drawn at LOD1) | the parts of the man's kit (head, hair, hands, coat, trousers, shoes, hat, accoutrements, arm ...), about 43,500 triangles | 61 bones, CPU pose from anim3d `sampleFigure` with the foot-planting pass on the real ground, palette texture / storage buffer |
| 1 | 10 - 13 m | merged class mesh + accessory mesh (about 1,900 + 150) | 36 bones, bone-matrix texture, two clips x two frames blended in the vertex shader |
| 2 | 13 - 75 m | low class mesh + accessory mesh (about 620) | same |
| far | beyond 75 m: every figure, one instanced draw over the whole world | baked 8-view impostors (material ids; colours from the outfit variant), small dab below 7 px | rest pose |
Every swap in this table (0 to 1, 1 to 2, 2 to the impostor, the baked view to the dab, one baked view to the next) is a dithered cross-fade over 15 percent of the distance, not a step: see "Cross-fade between levels of detail" below. The distances above are the centres of the fades. Mounted men: the rider with the rider clips and the horse (LOD0 / 1 / 2 meshes, horse bone texture). Gun crews: the stage's crew units with the artillery detachment clips and a by-piece fire plan. Structures draw lists (workstream 6): train guns, limbers, caissons, wagons, forge, draught horses and drivers, and the works (draped mesh plus crews), drawn rigid from an identity frame of the horse bank.
Animation: `UnitAnimator.clipAt` (activity timeline from the stage's admitted events + fire plan + falls + per-figure phase / rate / fall style) evaluated on the CPU for every figure inside 75 m, a pure function of simulation time; DD-10: Wheeler 1863 gaits for cavalry, the 1863 field artillery manual for horse artillery (`modern_range` stays selectable in anim3d).
Colour: variant slots, skin / lip / hair / iris from the man's identity, wear and dye lot on cloth (the shader port of `resolveColours`), flag cloth in the mean colour of its pattern.

## Choices that are ours (plausible-procedural, to be decided or confirmed by the owner)
* Fire: `at_will`, 20 to 30 s per round per man (ANIM3D-NOTES 2.3); guns by piece, 30 to 60 s. The record has neither (G-ANIM-01, 02).
* The 140th New York, whose dress is disputed: drawn in the army's generic infantry dress with the gap flag, no reading chosen (the card still says both are carried).
* Night for the camp clips: 19:30 to 04:30 local time.
* A turn of a block takes max(25 s, angle x extent / 4.4 m/s): a rigid train no longer swings its far end at 30 m/s.
* Animation time is simulation time. Above 2x playback the legs run at the playback rate (the prototype's slowed animation clock is not used).

## Cross-fade between levels of detail (`src/crowd/lod.mjs`, 2026-11-15)

Every swap (level 0 to 1, 1 to 2, the low mesh to the impostor, one baked view to the next, the baked view to the flat dab) is a dithered cross-fade, the same rule on WebGL2 and on WebGPU. The picture is a pure function of (simulation time, camera, settings): nothing is kept from one frame to the next, so a seek to a time lands on the pixels that playback shows there (gate Q12; the bar "same simulation time and camera, same pixels").

* **The rule** is the figures3d radius rule (FIGURES3D-NOTES 9.2): a fade of 15 percent of the distance, centred on each radius (plus and minus 7.5 percent, a smoothstep of the distance), and a 10 percent hysteresis. The radii are the table above (10, 13, 75 m at 1080p and 60 degrees, scaled by pixels and field of view, moved by the two sliders).
* **Hysteresis, made memoryless** (delegated decision DD-REN-LOD-01). A true Schmitt trigger needs the level every figure had in the last frame; the picture would then depend on the path of the camera and a seek could not reproduce a played frame. Here every figure has its own hand-over distances: its effective distance is `nu = d / f` with `f = 1 + 0.10 (2 h - 1)`, `h = hsh(figure * 13 + 5)`, so the thresholds of a regiment are spread over plus and minus 10 percent of the nominal ones by the figure number (fx3d decides near and far per shot the same way, FX3D-NOTES 3). A rank of men at one distance does not change level in one stroke; a figure's thresholds never depend on where it came from.
* **Dither.** Every fragment of every level of a figure draws one number `n = fract(ign(pixel) + hsh(figure * 7 + 3))` (interleaved gradient noise, shifted by the figure's own phase) and keeps itself when `lo <= n < hi`. The ranges `[lo, hi)` of the levels of one figure are consecutive and partition `[0, 1)` (they come from the cumulative shares of the three boundaries), so each pixel is drawn by exactly one level: no hole, no doubled pixel. `lo` and `hi` are 16-bit numbers packed in the fourth word of the 48-byte instance record, which no shader read before; the word 0 means "keep everything" (rigid props, the works). The adjacent levels decode the same integer into the same float. The pixel the noise is taken from is `gl_FragCoord.xy` on WebGL2 and the same pixel with the y axis turned over on WebGPU (its origin is at the top), so a figure is dithered with the same pattern on both APIs and a pixel comparison of the two pictures does not show the dither as a difference (the hair cards' stochastic coverage on WebGPU now uses the same flipped pixel).
* **LOD0 budget as a radius** (96 men at once: the pose palette has 96 slots). When the weighted count of men within the LOD0 reach exceeds 96, the LOD0 radius is cut to the effective distance of the 96th and its fade band ends there, so at most 95 listed men have a LOD0 share. The K-th smallest of continuous distances is continuous: no man swaps with his neighbour as the camera moves (the earlier rank cut did). Men just outside the view use up the budget with a weight that falls from 1 at the edge of the view to 0 over 0.2 radian (otherwise a rank of men entering the view in one step of a turn moves the radius by a whole gap); in a dense column about a quarter of the budget is therefore held for men beside the view. On the real layer more than 96 men stand within 10 m of 18 to 23 percent of its men (up to 800 in the densest columns, 1,699 on the evening of 2 July), so the cap binds there.
* **The impostor** computes its far share in the vertex shader from its own distance, the band, the spread and the same hash (it is drawn from 0.83 of the low mesh's outer radius; the CPU lists the low mesh to 1.18 of it). The eight baked views stand 45 degrees apart: between two, a fragment takes the next view with the share `fract(yaw / 45 degrees)` (it took the nearest one, so a formation of one heading changed view all at once when the camera crossed an angle). Below 7 pixels high the baked view gives way to a flat two-tone dab, cross-faded over the same band of its height. The dab used to fill the whole impostor quad, which is 5.5 times the area of the figure it carries (data/figures3d/impostor-atlas.png: 131 upright foot classes, the silhouette fills 0.181 of the quad, 22.1 of 48 texels wide, 56.1 of 64 high, empty 0.105 above and 0.018 below); a dither cannot hide that, so the dab narrows to the silhouette between 3 and 7 pixels (the whole quad below 3, as before; DD-REN-LOD-03).
* **Shadows.** Both levels of a fading figure are drawn into the shadow maps in full (the union of two near-identical silhouettes; no dither in a depth-only pass). Two edges of the shadows are NOT covered by the cross-fade and are open (listed, not dropped; read from the code, not yet seen in a picture): (1) the impostor casts no shadow (`shadow()` of `gl.mjs` and `gpu.mjs` draws the level lists only), so a man's shadow ends, in one step, when his low mesh is no longer listed: at 75 m x 1.075 x his own scale of 0.9 to 1.1, that is between 72.6 and 88.7 m, one man after another, so a regiment's shadows thin out over 16 m and do not end at a ring; (2) only men whose sphere meets the view are listed (`selectFigures`), so a man just outside the picture casts no shadow into it and his shadow appears when he enters the view (long evening shadows make this visible). A far shadow (a sun-facing quad for each impostor in the shadow passes) would remove (1); it is a change of the look of the whole field and of the shadow cost, not a level swap, and is not made here.
* **The train and the works** (guns, limbers, caissons, draught horses, drivers, works crews) vanish beyond 350 m with no far level; they fade out over the same band (a dither range `[0, q)`).
* **Comparing with the hard swap.** `crowd.lod.band = 0; crowd.lod.hyst = 0` (a live object; `Crowd.create` also takes `lod`) restores the earlier behaviour: steps at the radii, the 96 nearest men at LOD0, the nearest baked view, the whole-quad dab. It is there to put pictures side by side and to test that the fade removes the step; it is not a setting of the product.

Alternatives not taken, and why: a fade timed by the wall clock or a per-figure memory of the last level (a Schmitt trigger) make a picture depend on how the camera got there, so a seek could not reproduce a played frame (Q12); alpha blending of the two levels needs a sorted draw and breaks the depth writes of hair cards and cloth; alpha-to-coverage needs a multisampled target (the host's HDR target has one sample); a temporal accumulation to smooth the dither would carry the last frame into this one (the motion blur of the prototype already reads the previous camera; nothing here adds to it).

Limits, measured and stated: turning the camera on the spot inside a block of 2,500 men (a stress test: the camera stands at the edge of the block, looking along it) moves the LOD0 share of one man by up to 0.45 in one 0.23 degree step, where few men stand near the budget radius and a small change of their soft weights moves the radius by a quarter of a metre; on the real layer's usual cameras the budget does not bind. The dither is fixed to the screen, so a fading figure that moves across the picture shows a fine crawl of the two levels (the two renderings differ by less than 0.5 px and 2 L* at the hand-over distances, FIGURES3D-NOTES 9.2; at the impostor swap the mean L* step is 1.5 to 3.5 beyond 40 m, EG-FIG-0011, still open).

## Cost model on paper (mobile G1, 125,000 figures; modelled from `figures3d/cost-model.json`, NOT measured)
* Triangles: LOD0 median 43,512 per man (x 96 budget = 4.2 M; a phone budget of 16 men = 0.7 M), LOD1 about 2,050, LOD2 about 770 with accessories, far 2 (a quad), horse 768 / 330, gun carriage + tube + limber + caisson a few thousand per piece. Dense view from 3 m (cost model): 10 k men at LOD2 = 7.7 M triangles; on the Arc iGPU model 4.5 ms per M triangles on WebGL2 that is over the 7 ms allowance, so the LOD2 radius (75 m) is the lever (the plan's bias b: radii x b, never the count).
* Draw calls: LOD0 one per distinct part in view (30 to 100), LOD1 / LOD2 one per outfit class in view plus one per accessory class (at most 312 + 15 each), horses 3, train 8, works 2, far 1. Measured in the browser (SwiftShader, 3 Jul 13:30, 30 m from a battery): 37 draws, 507 k triangles, 1,314 instance records.
* Bytes: instance table 2.0 MB (16 B x 127,572), variant info + palette 2.6 MB (about 40 k variants), impostors 13 MB (133 classes x 384 x 64 x 4), mesh store 20 MB vertices + 10 MB indices (measured 726 k vertices, 2.6 M indices with a few LOD0 parts; LOD0 parts stream in on first use, up to about 45 MB), bone banks 14 MB human (16,384 frames at 30 Hz or less) + 2 MB horse, LOD0 pose palettes 0.3 MB, per frame upload 48 B per listed figure.
* CPU per frame: selection (units in range x figures) 0.3 to 10 ms measured under SwiftShader, `clipAt` about 1 to 6 microseconds per figure (units built lazily), LOD0 pose 5 microseconds per man with foot planting.
