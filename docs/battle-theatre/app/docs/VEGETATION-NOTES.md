# Vegetation of July 1863: notes (requirement R20, gate Q21)

What grew where on the field, and how it looked on each day of 26 June to 14 July 1863 and on 19 November, as far as the record allows; drawn in the theatre as an added layer. Everything the picture shows that no source names is marked with its tier and listed as a gap. Code: `app/src/ext/vegetation/`. Data: `app/data/vegetation.json`. Build and sources: `tools/bt/vegetation/`. Tests: `app/tests/vegetation.test.mjs`, `vegetation-shell.test.mjs`, `vegetation-scene.test.mjs` (node), `app/tests/vegetation-browser.mjs` (browser harness). Register proposals: `GOVERNANCE/horde/ws-world/proposed-register/ws-world-veg-G01..G16.md`. Decisions taken under the delegation of 2026-11-11: `GOVERNANCE/decisions/DELEGATED-DECISIONS-2026-11-11.md`, DD-VEG-01 to DD-VEG-10.

## 1. What is in the data

`data/vegetation.json` (schema `bt-vegetation/1`, 1.7 MB):

| Part | What |
|---|---|
| `units` (927) | 549 field cells, the Rose Wheatfield, 185 woodlots (one of them the east slope of Little Round Top, cut out of the Big Round Top polygon, see section 4), 103 tree clusters, 85 orchards, 2 marsh patches, the boulder ground of Devil's Den, the cleared west slope of Little Round Top. Each has an outline (lng/lat), a land cover, its crop or its canopy and species, seeded parameters, the claims that bear on it (role `exact`, `nearest` or `area`) and the damage events that reach it. Trees are not stored: they are placed at run time by a seeded scatter inside the outline (rows for orchards). |
| `calendar` | crop calendars as keyframes of RANGES (colour min/max, stalk height min/max, tier, the reason), harvest ranges with their bounds, the leaf cover of the window and of the epilogue, orchard fruit by species, the epilogue stages of 19 November, the rain-lodging window. |
| `claims` (432) | every passage the data rests on: the words (verbatim from the saved text, an OCR misreading of the saved text kept as it is), the source and the place in it (printed page of the Official Records, PDF page of a National Park Service report, offset in the sha256-pinned text of a book or paper), the tier (`documented` 393, `inferred` 39) and what it says. 130 are placed on a polygon; 302 are listed with `placed: false`: they bear on the farms and the field in general (and on what is NOT 1863: replanting, regrowth, later clearing) and name no outline the repository has. |
| `events` (10) | trampling and tree-stripping with their windows and a ranged final share, each with its basis, its tier and the units it reaches (area-weighted). |
| `scenarios` (3) | `CF-VEG-LRT` (Little Round Top cover), `CF-VEG-RAIN` (rain flattening), `CF-VEG-HARVEST` (pin the cut dates to the early or late end). Every option stays selectable; the default is marked "default under delegated authority". |
| `witness_trees` (2), `farms` (22), `gaps` (16), `meta` | the large oak at the Trostle lane and the tree at the Codori lane (positions inferred to 50 to 60 m); the farmsteads used as anchors; the sixteen gaps with their register ids; counts, source titles and hashes, the crop mix and the farms it is computed from. |

### Sources read (all local; nothing is fetched at run time)

Official Records Series I vol. 27 (29 claims: the wheat-fields, oat-fields, clover and corn-fields of 1 to 3 July, wheat stacked in Maryland on 10 July, new hay there by 10 to 25 July, and the accounts of the stony hill, the forest, the scrub and the heavily timbered ground); National Park Service reports saved in the library (374 claims from 21 of the 73: the Cultural Landscapes Inventories of the First Day, Emmitsburg Road Ridge, Pickett's Charge, the Gettysburg Landscape, Little Round Top and the National Cemetery, the Cultural Landscape Reports of the First Day, Little Round Top and the Record of Treatment, the Development Concept Plan for Little Round Top, the General Management Plan, the administrative history, the studies of the Harmon farm, the Brien farm, the vista cutting of about 1940 and the Spangler's Spring area) and three pages of the park's own site (6 claims, all about what is NOT 1863); the public-domain library (Haskell 1908 on the fields in front of the Second and Third Corps, Bachelder 1889 and 1904, the New Jersey reports, Toombs 1888, Fremantle 1864 on cherries, Everett's address on the foliage, the Gettysburg Compiler of 22 June and 27 July 1863: 20 claims); one National Park Service page on the Trostle petition (3 claims). The Gettysburg Compiler and Adams Sentinel issues of June to August 1863 were searched by keyword (wheat, harvest, crops, grass, corn, oats, clover, cherries, trampled): the two Compiler passages used speak of the Gettysburg district; the other crop notes found speak of Maryland, Virginia, western Pennsylvania or the State at large, and are not used. A keyword search is not a reading of every page: the issues are listed in the register as searched by keyword and not read page by page (G03). The Official Records and the library were searched for wheat, oats, rye, corn, clover, hay, grass, orchards, fruit, woods, thickets and tree damage; what a search found and the curation kept is in `tools/bt/vegetation/claims_*.json`. Every quote is looked up again in the saved text by `tools/bt/vegetation/verify_claims.py`; a claim whose quote is not found is dropped, never cited.

The vegetation rows of the evidence corrections of the park landscape reading (`GOVERNANCE/evidence_corrections/web-nps-landscape.jsonl`, 277 rows with id R20) are all accounted for: most were already claims of the first reading (or on its list of rows read and left out); 55 are claims made from the rows the first reading had not used (`gen_nps_corrections.py`: each row read by hand, its quote copied from the row and found again in the saved report; some were found only as the saved text reads, "turing" for "during", "f a m i n g" for "farming"); 5 more are left out with their reasons in that script. A node test (`vegetation.test.mjs`, "the park landscape evidence") fails when a row of the corrections file is in no claim and no left-out list.

Not read, and said so in the register: the 1860 census schedules, the 1868 damage-claim files other than the nine farms the National Park Service quotes (the Biesecker, Bollinger and McClean claims are quoted in saved reports whose text layer is too garbled to take figures from; no page image is kept), the NPS Historic Vegetation Study (not located), the vegetation inventory layer (blocked by the allowed route), and any 1863 photograph (the photo-match catalogue exists; no vegetation state is read from a photograph here; a park report says the Brady photographs of the weeks after the battle show the west slope of Little Round Top nearly bare, which is carried as a statement of the report, not as a reading of the photographs).

## 2. What is documented, inferred and procedural (counts)

* Field cells (549 plus the Wheatfield): 1 documented crop (the Wheatfield: standing wheat when the battle began, chin-high; VC-LIB-007, -008, -001), 11 inferred (a passage names the crop and a rule places it at the nearest field cell of the farm or landmark: the Russell, Barrett and Culp corn fields, the McMillan corn, the oats by the Peach Orchard and on Seminary Ridge, the Rogers meadow, the Wentz field, the wheat in front of the Brien house and others), 538 plausible-procedural (a seeded draw from the district mix).
* The district mix (wheat 36%, hay 21%, oats 15%, corn 15%, pasture 9%, barley 2%, rye 1%) is computed from the crop acreages of eight farms' damage claims (McPherson, Wills, Harman, Forney, Slyder, Josiah Benner, Abraham Spangler, Trostle), pasture raised to a floor of 9%. A draw from it is never presented as the crop of that field.
* Outlines: 253 documented (dense Warren tree symbols), 170 inferred (the east slope of Little Round Top is one: the Warren wood cut out of the Big Round Top polygon by a rule), 504 plausible-procedural (field cells cut inside a block of open ground by straight cuts across the long axis of each piece, placed by a seed: the cut lines are not mapped; the blocks are bounded by the roads, lanes, railway and the 30 confirmed fence lines).
* Trees: about 125,000 drawn at the real projection (110,000 canopy and orchard trees, 15,000 understory shrubs where an account says the undergrowth was moderate or thick); every position is seeded and plausible-procedural; the species family (oak-hickory) is inferred from two woodlots, chestnut and black locust are shown present by the fence timber, every share and every height is a range; no blight and no modern species list is used (G16).
* What is NOT 1863 is claimed too (62 claims of kind `ground`): the replanted orchards (Sherfy, Herbst, Henry Spangler, Rogers, Codori), the woods that grew back or were cut after the battle (McMillan, Shultz, Forney, McClean, the Codori-Trostle thicket, Devil's Den in 1999), the modern species list the park publishes. They set no crop and no species; they are the reason a modern tree is not drawn as a tree of 1863.

## 3. The state function and the gate hooks (Q21)

`src/ext/vegetation/model.mjs` is pure (no DOM, no GPU, no wall clock, no unseeded random numbers).

* `stateAt(model, unit, tMin, choices)`: crop and stage; colour and height as the calendar's range with the unit's own seeded place inside it; cut share and the cut date; trampled share (events, area-weighted, each ramped over its window); flattened share (the rain scenario); leaf cover; stripped share; the share of trees kept (a reading of Little Round Top); the undergrowth of a wood; fruit by species; the tier of every part; the claims and events that bear on it. The overall tier is the weakest of the parts.
* `checkVegetation(model, opts)`: the check of Q21 (schedule, events, anachronism, backward scrub). Every unit at every sampled date (every day of the window at 06:00, 12:00, 18:00, every event boundary, 19 November noon) under six scenario sets lies inside the documented-or-ranged schedule (colour and height inside the keyframe ranges that bracket the unit's own time; cut date inside its harvest range; leaf cover inside its range; undergrowth and fruit state present with a tier); damage never precedes its event; trampling never falls with time inside the window; two asks give the same answer and the states walked backward equal those walked forward; no species is one introduced after 1863; a documented crop needs an exact documented claim; every claim has a quote of at least 12 characters, one of the two allowed tiers and a place in its source. Returns `{gate, ok, checked, failures, notRun, summary}`.
* `checkScene(model, opts)` (`scene-check.mjs`): "scene equals schedule" (Q21.2). The 20 floats per unit that the layer writes to the GPU (`packUnitState` at `sceneMinute(t)`) equal the schedule at every sampled minute and set of choices; the scene draws more than 60,000 trees and 50,000 ground-cover triangles and every unit has ground cover; the trees placed are about the density of the data; a reading of Little Round Top draws the share of trees it keeps. It says it does not see what the shaders make of the numbers.
* Both hooks are listed in `docs/GATE-HOOKS.md`. Whether `passes` may be set to true is for the gate runner and the owner: nothing here sets it, and Q21 as the plan words it also asks for photo-match of the vegetation against 1863 photographs (Q21.5), which is not done (G13), and for a scene that does not draw the modern aerial vegetation (G15: the 2022 ground photograph shows through where no field cell is drawn).

## 4. How it is drawn

WebGL2 only (the default renderer). On WebGPU the module registers its panel and says "not drawn" (the hook has no WebGPU path; this is the same position as the weather layer, EG-W3D-G08).

* Ground cover: every outline is laid over a lattice of 40 m squares fixed to the ground (a square inside is two triangles, a square the outline cuts is cut along it; a cut piece the ear clipping cannot do exactly is made again from the triangles of the whole outline); about 147,000 triangles; the triangles of every unit add up to its projected outline within 1 per cent, so nothing is painted outside an outline. The colours of the data are sRGB; the shaders light them as linear albedo (the same 2.2 the structures layer uses). The vertex shader lays each vertex on the terrain with a lift that grows with distance. One state texture (5 texels per unit) is rewritten from `stateAt` at a quantised clock (0.5 min) and when a choice changes, so the same minute always gives the same picture. The fragment shader draws: crop rows (corn), drilled grain, shocks of cut grain, stubble, windrows, plough furrows, trampling as matted, darker and browner straw with bare soil showing through in patches (patches that grow with the share, and the table of first visits), lodging, forest floor, boulder ground, marsh; the tier overlay (tint and hatching: none for documented, diagonal lines for inferred, cross-hatch for plausible-procedural, dashes for an open gap). The soil colour and the patches are plausible-procedural stand-ins: the record gives the share trampled and when, not what the ground looked like.
* Trees: instanced, one parametrised crown-and-trunk mesh at two levels (full tree under 190 m, a blob under 2,300 m, thinned with distance and enlarged to keep the cover), chunks of 250 m culled by the camera. Colour from the species range; leaf cover from the state (leaf-off on 19 November, stripped trees where the events strip them); the share of trees kept from the state (a reading of Little Round Top); shadows from trees within 420 m.
* Little Round Top: the Warren land cover draws the hill's west slope as cleared (the Hanaway tract) and puts the wood of the east slope into the Big Round Top polygon of 217 acres. To let the three readings of the hill's cover (`CF-VEG-LRT`) act on the hill and not on Big Round Top, the half of a disc of 230 m round the summit that lies east of the crest is cut out of that polygon into a unit of its own (15 acres; documented wood by the Park Service report, extent by that rule, tier inferred). Under the default reading it is a wood; lightly wooded keeps half of its trees; unwooded keeps none and its ground is open rock, like the west slope. No other wood is touched (a test checks 60 woods). Before this, the reading changed the card but not the trees.
* Standing crops near the camera (`shell.mjs`, `?vegshell=on`; the switch "Vegetation: standing crops near the camera"): the ground cover of a crop or meadow is drawn again as 14 layers lifted by shares of the crop's height within 70 m of the camera, each layer kept only where a stalk, a head or a leaf of that height would be (a cell pattern that thins with height); a soldier who wades in is covered up to the height the state gives, and where the ground was trampled or laid flat the layers are lower. A stand-in for single plants; its default is stated in section 6.
* Trampling that follows the troops: `plan.mjs` asks the stage (the event cache, the presence windows, the formation extents: the same figures `Stage.apply` uses) where every drawn unit stands at every 8 minutes from 30 June to 8 July and records for each 8 m cell of every field, meadow and orchard the first minute any formation stood on it. The shader treats a cell as trampled from that minute (a 25 minute ramp); backward scrubbing un-tramples exactly. A scenario change restarts the plan. The documented events (the Wheatfield 16:30 to 19:30 on 2 July, the Trostle, Peach Orchard, Codori and McPherson fields, the advance of 3 July) trample their fields by their own windows whether or not a drawn unit walked there.
* The panel (More, then Vegetation of July 1863): the switches, the three conflicts as choices with the tier of each option, a card for the ground under the centre of the picture (state, tiers, the quotes with their sources, the events), places to look at, how sure each part is, the sixteen gaps. A notice on the picture says the layer is shown and what it rests on.
* Wetness of the ground is the weather timeline's channel (Q22): the weather pass darkens and puddles ground pixels, this layer's included; the layer stores no wetness of its own.

## 5. What is not done, or not right (nothing hidden)

* No real GPU: the pictures were seen on a software renderer only (see section 6). No frame time is claimed. Owed: the cases of `perf.spec.mjs` with the layer on and off, and with the standing crop on, on a real GPU.
* The 2022 ground photograph under the picture still shows modern trees on road verges, fence lines and in the town; field cells cover it where they are drawn (G15). The gate spec counts a scene that draws the modern aerial vegetation as a failure of Q21.2: the layer does not meet that.
* Single plants are not drawn: crops are colour, rows, shading and (within 70 m of the camera, when switched on) stacked layers. Grass and crop height outside that range are not geometry.
* No fruit on the trees (the state says which stage the fruit is in; the card shows it).
* The straight edges between neighbouring field cells are cuts made by the build, not fences: no fence is drawn for them, and the tier overlay marks every such cell plausible-procedural.
* A field cell whose outline has a wood or an orchard inside it is painted whole; the wood or orchard is drawn above it, so the hole shows nothing wrong, but the cell's own acreage counts the hole.
* The ground of a wood is its floor close to and its canopy (leaf-green in summer, bare crowns in November) from far off, blended between 70 and 520 m; the trees themselves are drawn out to 2,300 m, thinned with distance, and beyond that only the canopy colour of the ground shows.
* Hedgerows, gardens, potato patches, flax and buckwheat are named in claims and have no place: not drawn (G14).
* The First Day farms (Forney, Wills, Herbst, Gilbert, Harman) have no farmstead outline in the repository: their field-by-field crops (wheat, timothy, corn) are claims with no placement, listed as "documented but not placed".
* 302 of 432 claims are not placed on a polygon; they are listed in the data (`placed: false`) and counted in the panel.
* WebGPU draws nothing. The visit table uses the stage as it is when the visit starts: units that other layers place later will be walked from then on without a rebuild of this file.
* The east slope of Little Round Top: the extent (a half disc of 230 m) is a rule; the Bushman and Weikert tracts are named by the report, not outlined. No photograph was read for it.
