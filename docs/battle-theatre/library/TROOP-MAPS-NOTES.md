# Troop-position maps: method, accuracy, problems, automation (2026-10-21)

Read with `TROOP-MAPS-ASSESSMENT.md` (sources) and `docs/battle-theatre/terrain/troopmaps/ACCURACY.md` (numbers). Outputs: `docs/battle-theatre/history/positions/troopmap_jul3_b1876.jsonl` (164 claims), `docs/battle-theatre/terrain/troopmaps/` (GCPs, transforms, labels, crops, previews), tools in `tools/bt/troopmaps/`. The position ledgers `positions_*.jsonl` were not edited.

## 1. What was done

1. **Source choice.** The Bachelder 1876 Third-day sheet (LOC 99447491, `cw0325000c.jp2`) was already downloaded and registered to the Warren sheet on 2026-10-19; no new download was needed for it. Its hash is now recorded. Nothing was fetched from blocked hosts.
2. **Georeference reuse, one map at a time.** Sheet 3: the committed chain (TPS scan to Warren px, Warren affine) was kept as the transform of record. A fresh check used 12 of the 23 Warren GCPs (farm buildings, road junctions, bridges), re-found by eye in the Bachelder scan (`gcp_montage.py`), giving chain RMSE 15.05 m and a direct-affine leave-one-out of 14.4 m (the Warren error itself is 16.5 m). Absolute 1-sigma **16.7 m**, same as Warren; no better and no worse. LOC 99447494 (July 1, 2 to 3.30 p.m.) was also registered, coarsely (LOO 48.8 m, preliminary, unused).
3. **Slice choice: July 3 (the sheet is one whole day).** A time-labelled slice at an hour was not available in an acquired source: sheet 3 prints no hour for most lines. The slice is therefore "Third day's battle", recorded with `t_iso: null`, `approx: true`, and the two clocks the sheet does print ("moved to its right at 1 P.M." beside Pender's division, "5 P.M." on a Bartlett movement arrow) carried verbatim and flagged as not proven attached.
4. **Reading.** The scan was cut into 900 x 720 px windows (1.33x) in which the two ink colours of the positions (red Confederate, blue Union) are isolated and the base-map linework is greyed (`layer.py`). Windows were chosen by ink density; 35 were viewed, 32 produced labels. For each legible label: text as printed (square brackets and ellipses mark cut or illegible letters; arrow labels are lower-case script on dashed movement arrows), side, kind (brigade, division, corps, artillery ...), label centre in scan px (to about 10-15 px), and, where the line symbol the label belongs to could be identified, the centre of that symbol and its half-extent. Pixel to ground: the chain of record (`build_claims_c.py`). Units were matched to `units.json` by the commander recorded there (fuzzy name match); where the printed name is not a recorded commander (Walker for the Stonewall Brigade, Sherrill and MacDougall for the Willard brigade, Madill for Graham's brigade, Dana for the Stone brigade) the claim is **inferred**, not documented, and says so.
5. **Claim schema.** Same fields as `positions_*.jsonl` plus a `geo` block (lng, lat, sigma_m, basis, scan px, label px, scan and transform names) and `source.crop` (the crop file the label was read from; recipe in `tools/bt/troopmaps/crops_c.json`). `claim_support_verified: true` only because a crop reference is logged for every claim. Tier `documented` means: the map prints this unit label at this place on the sheet titled with this day. It does not mean the position is independent of the OR: Bachelder compiled the positions in 1876 from the Official Reports, field consultations, letters and oral accounts, so each claim is `flag_single_source: true`. `place.gazetteer_ref` is null for all (the gazetteer has 78 places, none at brigade-line scale).
6. **Verification.** (a) 12 random claims were re-viewed with the stored label and anchor points drawn on the scan: all 12 label points lie on or beside the printed text; worst label-centre miss about 60 px (TM3-153); 4 of 5 anchored samples lie on or within about 30 px of the line symbol, the fifth (McGilvery) on the adjacent battery-name column. This set the 15 px reading base. (b) Plausibility against independent gazetteer centres (monument-derived, not Warren): Meade's headquarters label 86 m from the Leister house (the label stands beside it); Webb's label 135 m from The Angle; Benning's line 112 m from Houck's Ridge; Hays's line in Gettysburg 98 m from the borough centre. These are order-of-magnitude checks only (several gazetteer entries are aggregates with 200-500 m spread). No check contradicted a placement.

## 3. Problems met

- **Time.** The 1876 sheets carry day, not hour. The legend's line shading (first position, intermediate, evening, all day) was not classified per line; it is the main unresolved time signal and could be read by pixel pattern (section 5).
- **Dense zones.** East Cemetery Hill, Culp's Hill and the Ridge carry overlapping text at 1:12,000. Ink-layer separation helps for colour, not for overlap.
- **Names are commanders, not units.** Labels are surnames and ordinals ("DANA 2d", "SHERRILL 3d", "MADILL 1'"). Mapping to `units.json` needs the OR chain of command for the day and is the weakest step (6 inferred claims).
- **Arrow labels.** Lower-case script names on dashed arrows (Garnett, Armistead, Kemper, Perry, Wilcox) mark an advance route, not a standing position; they are recorded as `movement`.
- **Transform limit.** Warren's 16.5 m absolute error and its smooth distortion pattern carry straight through; the Bachelder sheets add about 2.4 m. Nothing in this work improves absolute accuracy.
- **Access.** LOC and tile.loc.gov returned HTTP 520 repeatedly on 2026-10-21; Digital Commonwealth, the BTAA geoportal and Rumsey sit behind browser checks; Yale returned nothing to the fetch tool. All recorded, none worked around.
- **Hour-labelled series not found** (section 1 of the assessment).
- **Tier question for the owner.** The brief ties "documented" to the map's own time label. Here the time label is the sheet title (the day). I used `documented` for (unit, place, day) with `t_iso: null`. If the owner wants a clock on every documented position claim, the whole slice would drop to `inferred` for time; this is the owner's decision (Law 6), not mine.

## 5. What can be automated later (owner may run a paid vision-model batch; no keys in the repo)

**Feasible protocol (per sheet or per time-labelled map):**

1. **Tile.** 900 x 720 px windows with 150 px overlap over the printed area, in the same ink-layer rendering used here (red, blue, both) at 1.33x. About 70-90 windows per Bachelder sheet. Each window keeps its origin and scale in a manifest, so every output coordinate can be mapped back to scan px by one rule (`scan = origin + px / scale`).
2. **Read pass A (model).** The prompt gives the window image plus the commander-name list from `units.json` for the side and day, and asks for strict JSON: `text_as_printed`, `bbox_px`, `side_by_ink_colour`, `kind` (brigade / division / corps / artillery / note), `orientation`, `line_symbol_bbox_px` if one clearly belongs to the label, `printed_clock` verbatim, `legible` (full / partial). It must not guess: unreadable letters are returned as `?`. Output is stored verbatim and hashed with the prompt and model id.
3. **Read pass B (independent).** A second run with a different window offset (shifted 300 px) and, preferably, a different model. Only labels whose normalised text agrees in both passes and whose centres lie within 40 px are auto-accepted; disagreements go to the review queue.
4. **Deterministic checks.** (a) Fuzzy-match the printed name to a commander in `units.json` (side must agree with ink colour); unmatched or ambiguous names become `inferred` or queue items, never silently assigned. (b) Georeference through the committed chain; reject points off the sheet's inked area or outside the known battlefield polygon. (c) Per-claim sigma as in `ACCURACY.md`. (d) Compare with the OR ledger: flag any unit placed more than 3 sigma from an OR-derived gazetteer position on the same day. (e) Schema validation against `positions_*.jsonl`.
5. **Human spot check.** 10 percent of accepted labels plus every queue item, shown as the logged crop with the label and anchor drawn (as in `sheet3_verification_sample12.jpg`). Acceptance threshold: at most 2 percent wrong-text or wrong-unit errors in the sample, otherwise the batch is re-run with the prompt changed and the error log kept.
6. **Shading classification (time phase).** After symbol anchoring, classify each line by pixel pattern in the symbol (solid, striped, hollow ... per the legend) into first position / intermediate / evening / all day, using the legend swatches as templates, verified on 20 hand-classified lines first. This turns day-level claims into ordered phases, still without clock times.
7. **Order and cost.** Sheets 1, 2, 3 (days 1-3), then LOC 99447494 after a feature-based registration to the Warren raster, then any 1886 hourly sheets if they are obtained. Estimated size per sheet: about 80 windows at roughly 2,000 image tokens and 1,500 output tokens each, two passes: about 0.6 million tokens per sheet; the hand pass here used about 0.25 million for one slice with lower coverage and no second pass.
8. **Keys and files.** The key lives only in the owner's environment variable; scripts read it from there. Raw model outputs and crops stay in `tmp/` (hashed, listed in a manifest); only accepted claims and their crop references are committed.

**Not automatable without a decision:** the tier meaning of a day-level label (see section 3), and mapping non-matching commander names (Dana, Sherrill, Madill ...) to brigades, which needs an OR chain-of-command table per day.

**Recommended batch protocol (updates section 5).**
1. Tile at 900 x 720 px with 150 px overlap at 1.33x for text, and 500 x 400 px at 2.4x with rotations (0, 90, 180, 270 degrees and the dominant bar direction) for dense zones; the ink-layer rendering used here (`layer_batch.py`) is the input.
2. Pass A reads labels (JSON: text, bbox, side by ink colour, kind, printed clock verbatim). Pass B reads the same windows shifted by 300 px with a different model; accept only labels agreeing in text and within 40 px.
3. A third pass reads line state per symbol: send a 220 x 140 px native-resolution crop of the symbol with the four legend swatches of that sheet and colour as reference images, ask for one of four states or "unreadable", run it twice, accept only agreeing answers. 5. Human spot check: 10 percent of accepted labels plus all queue items, shown as the logged crop with label and anchor drawn (`previews/sheet2_verification_sample20.jpg` is the template).
6. Cost for the three 1876 sheets: about 0.6 million tokens a sheet for labels (two passes) plus about 0.3 million for line state; no keys in the repo.
