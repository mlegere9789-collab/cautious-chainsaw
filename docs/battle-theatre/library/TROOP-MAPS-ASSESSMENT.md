# Period troop-position maps as the position source: assessment (2026-10-21)

Scope: where scans of the period troop-position maps exist, their rights, resolution and labelling, what was acquired, and which source to use. Companion: `TROOP-MAPS-NOTES.md` (method, accuracy, automation). Everything below was checked on 2026-10-21 unless a date is given. A failed or blocked lookup is recorded as such; nothing blocked was worked around (no retries beyond one polite repeat, no header changes, no browser-check bypass).

## 1. Recommendation (short)

1. **Use the Bachelder 1876 three-sheet map (LOC 99447491) as the position source of record for the day-level layer.** It is already acquired (2026-10-19, `docs/basemap1863/maps/`), georeferenced to the Warren sheet (relative CV 2.2-2.4 m, absolute about 17 m), prints brigade names, ordinals and regiment bars on 1:12,000 topography, and one slice (Third day) has now been read: 167 labels, 164 claims, 75 of the 104 `brigade` units in `units.json` (see section 5).
2. **It does not give hours.** Its legend has four line states (first position, intermediate, evening, all day), a few printed clocks ("moved to its right at 1 P.M.", "5 P.M." on a movement arrow) and dotted movement arrows. Hourly slices exist only in Bachelder's later tactical maps (28 sheets, July 1 from 10:30 a.m. in half-hour steps; July 2 at 9 a.m., 4, 5, 7 and 8-9 p.m.; July 3 in four windows) which I found listed **only by a retailer**; no digitised public-domain copy was found. That is a recorded open gap, not a negative finding about the originals.
3. **LOC 99447494 ("Battle of Gettysburg, July 1, 1863. First day. General position of troops, 2 to 3.30 p.m.", plate marked II, 1:23,400)** is the only hour-labelled, brigade-level sheet found and acquired. Its author and date are not stated by LOC; its drawing is Warren-derived (streams, roads and contours match the Warren sheet). The Commons copy is 4462 x 4943 px (about 2 m per pixel): enough for brigade names and lines, coarse for placement. Georeferencing is only preliminary (leave-one-out 48.8 m). It is the right model for what the missing hourly series would look like, and a sibling series (plate I, III ...) may exist at LOC: **not established**.
4. **OR Atlas plates are not a position source here**: the acquired archive.org scan is about 3250 x 2000 px per plate spread, so Gettysburg plate XLIII shows corps/division lines and no readable brigade labels, and plate XCV map 1 is the Warren/Humphreys survey with no troop positions.
5. Owner decisions that would raise coverage: (a) ask LOC / NARA whether the 1886 tactical sheets are held and digitised (an owner act: a request, not something done here); (b) whether to buy the commercial reprint for private reading (copyright of the reprint is the owner's call); (c) whether LOC 99447494's unsigned status is acceptable for tier-2 use.

## 5. Proof of concept result in one paragraph

Third-day sheet of Bachelder 1876: 32 ink-layer tiles yielded labels (3 more were viewed and added nothing; 35 crops are archived in `docs/battle-theatre/terrain/troopmaps/crops/`) and were read by eye; 167 printed labels were recorded with pixel position and georeferenced; 164 became claims in `history/positions/troopmap_jul3_b1876.jsonl` (158 documented, 6 inferred because the printed name does not match the commander recorded in `units.json`). They cover **75 of 104 brigade-level units (US 39 of 60, CS 36 of 44)**, 21 divisions, 8 corps, 7 Confederate artillery battalions, 6 Union artillery brigades and army headquarters. Brigade positions carry a 1-sigma of **37-205 m, median 62 m** (transform 16.7 m combined with symbol width and label-to-line offset). The 29 brigade-level units not recovered are listed in `TROOP-MAPS-NOTES.md` section 5: 13 are not on this sheet or not on the field on July 3 (12 cavalry brigades, mostly on East Cavalry Field or off the Warren sheet, and Corse at Hanover Junction), 1 is an unnamed placeholder unit, and 15 are infantry brigades whose labels I could not read (Cemetery Hill, Culp's Hill and the Ridge are crowded) or did not find; they are not asserted absent.
