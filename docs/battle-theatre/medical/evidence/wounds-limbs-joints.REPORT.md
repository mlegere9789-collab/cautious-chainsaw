## 8. Why 74 rows carry W002

The validator's page model reads the running heads, and in the Surgical History the heads are often not recognised, so its hint is two to ten pages out in chapter X (the plates bound in the second issue shift the PDF pages, not the printed ones). The printed pages of the rows were read from the running heads and page numbers in the text itself (the number printed with the head before and after the passage) and, for the three tables, from the page images. A reader who finds a page differing from the hint should trust the printed head. Where a head is unread, the row's page is a range (for example `87-88`) and the row says so; the Otis 1869 rows take the validator's hint and say so in `notes`; the Otis 1867 row `201` takes its page from the running heads (68 before the passage, 69 after it) and says so, the validator's hint is 71; the forearm and wrist rows of the second volume have the page `n/a` (the page numbers are not legible in the OCR near them).

## 10. What was done with care, and what was left

Rules followed: no number, name or date in a claim that its quotation does not carry (the validator checks the numbers; a script compared every claim's words with its quotation and the rows it flagged were rewritten or their quotation widened: Robeson's operation, Durbin, Mullen, Saunders, the ankle and foot counts, the museum and manual rows); period terms as the sources use them (conoidal ball, flesh wound, shot fracture, expectant plan, conservation, excision, exarticulation, circular and flap amputation, primary, intermediary and secondary, double-inclined plane, extension by adhesive strips and a weight over a pulley); a single witness is stated as that witness (Gross writes, Tripler writes, Chisolm writes, the History quotes Surgeon X); the validator's number reader joins spelled-out numbers that follow each other, so such sentences were split into two rows or the pieces reworded; no modern medical knowledge was used as evidence; no mortality or count is averaged across sources.

## 12. How the file was made, so it can be rebuilt

The rows were written as specification files (`tmp/medical-wounds-limbs-joints/specs/s01` to `s13`, `_prelude.py`, `claims_override.json`) and turned into the evidence file by a builder (`tmp/medical-wounds-limbs-joints/build.py`) that finds every quotation in the local text from start and end anchors with the validator's own matcher (offsets come from there), keeps ids stable through `ids.json` and refuses to drop an id. The tools (`tools/`: `show.py`, `grep.py`, `paras.py`, `cases.py`, `xcheck.py`, `xcheck2.py`, `audit.py`, `pdfpage.py`, `crops.py`, `crops2.py`) read the texts; the scratch is not part of the deliverable, which is complete without it.

To regenerate a page image: `pdftoppm -f <PDF page> -l <PDF page> -r 130 -png -singlefile tmp/library/medical/MSHWR-P3V2-1883-2NDISSUE-PDF/medicalsurgical32barnrich.pdf out` (PDF page 197 is printed page 175, 439 is 367, 512 is 432, 525 is 443; `tools/pdfpage.py <printed page>` finds the others).
