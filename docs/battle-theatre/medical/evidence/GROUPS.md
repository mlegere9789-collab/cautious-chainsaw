## 20. `camp-letterman`: Camp Letterman, the general hospital, and what became of the wounded afterwards

*Area after. Rows: at least 60, aim 180. Depends on: `org-evacuation`, `burial-practice`.*

The general hospital on the York Pike east of the town (about 80 acres of the Wolf farm; the 11 acres of Hospital Woods remain): the decision (Cuyler, 27 July: "a large field hospital established at a suitable place near the town"), opening (20 or 22 July; construction from 10 July; tents moved in 22 to 27 July), the layout (rows of tents, the dead house, the embalmers' tents, the cook house, the warehouse tent, the hospital cemetery of about 1,200, amputated limbs buried in barrels), patients over time (11,425 sent away by 22 July, 16,125 by 25 July, 1,772 on 10 August against "over 4,000"), staff, nurses, the Commissions' posts, deaths, the rail depot link, the closing (20 November or by January), the cemetery dedication of 19 November, and the pictures of August 1863.

**Questions the rows answer**

* Location and extent: the farm, the acreage, the road, the rail siding if any, the water supply, as the sources state.
* Opening and closing dates and their conflicts (20 or 22 July; 20 November or January 1864): every statement as a row with its source and its quotation.
* The layout: tents (number, size, rows, spacing, patients per tent), the streets, the kitchens, the dead house, the embalming tents, the warehouses, the chapel or Commission tents, the hospital cemetery.
* Population over time: arrivals, departures by rail, the number on named dates, deaths per day, the range of conflicting counts.

* The routine as the accounts give it (Bucklin, Souder, Brinton, the newspapers of August to November 1863) and what the LoC pictures of August 1863 show.
* What happened after: the last patients, the removal of the dead to the National Cemetery, the limbs to the Army Medical Museum, the ground restored, the land today (the NPS Hospital Woods).

## 21. `burial-practice`: Burial: in the field, at the hospitals, embalming, the horses, and the beginning of the National Cemetery

*Area after. Rows: at least 50, aim 140. Depends on: `camp-letterman`.*

How the dead were buried in July to November 1863: by their comrades in shallow graves beside where they fell, in trenches with headboards where the names were known, the Confederate dead in trenches, the burial details and their dates, the hospital burial grounds (the Spangler orchard, the Seminary campus, the camp hospital's cemetery), embalming and the shipping of the dead home (embalmers at the camp hospital and in the town), coffins, the Massachusetts committee's work, the Soldiers' National Cemetery (the first burial on 27 October 1863, the contractor, the dedication on 19 November), and the horses (3,000 to 5,000 carcasses burned with kerosene per a magazine; the Park Service and period sources are checked). The burial of the Confederate dead in 1870 to 1873 is later and never drawn in 1863.

**Questions the rows answer**

* The burial practice of July: who buried, in what, with what marking, how deep, how soon (Jacobs, Hospital Scenes, Pierce, the Commissions, regimental narratives, the newspapers).
* The hospital burial grounds and their place (Spangler orchard, Seminary, the camp hospital), numbers, the registers.
* Embalming: who, where, how advertised, prices as printed, the shipping of bodies (the newspapers and the Massachusetts report).
* The horses: counts and disposal as stated by period sources (Gettysburg Compiler, Adams Sentinel, soldiers, the Park Service), and the smell and the burning.
* The National Cemetery: the decision, the dates (the first interment, the committee), what existed on which date (the Cemetery Ridge ground, the contractor, the removal of Union dead), so that nothing is drawn before its day.
* Which of the dead were Union and which Confederate in each ground, and how the Confederate dead were treated.
