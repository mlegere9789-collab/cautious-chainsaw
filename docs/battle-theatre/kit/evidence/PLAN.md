# Kit evidence pass: plan

Workstream `bt-oob-kit`, written 2026-11-13. Binding text: `CLAUDE.md` (the Law), the horde rules, `GOVERNANCE/FINAL-ACCEPTANCE.md`.
Regions between `<!-- BEGIN GENERATED x -->` and `<!-- END GENERATED x -->` are rewritten by `python -I tools/bt/kit_evidence_plan.py` from the roster, the kit tables and the validator, so they cannot drift from the code. Everything else is hand-written.

## 1. What the pass does

Requirements R04, R10, R17 and OA04 and gates Q01, Q03, Q14 and Q20 ask for: unit counts and strengths that match a verified order-of-battle table (ranges with a declared basis); uniform, equipment and arms of every unit correct for July 1863 with a tier; commanders from lieutenant to general named where the record names them; gun types and counts per battery that match a verified battery table.

Where the data stands (`KIT-NOTES.md`, `INVENTORY.md`): the kit tables were transcribed from the owner's PDF, which cites Wikipedia, forums and web pages. Of the 357 tiered unit fields of the kit tables (210 dress and arms fields of infantry and support units, 22 weapon and sidearm fields of cavalry, 125 `flag_or_instrument` fields of batteries), 170 carry `weak_source` (recounted 2026-11-13 from the tables); 29 of 125 battery cards state their guns; the roster (`history/units.json`, 726 units) holds a printed strength for 21 units (13 corps-level formations, from Stone Sentinels as the PDF prints it, and eight brigades with "basis not stated"), no commander below the brigade and no casualty figure below the brigade. Nothing in the kit was ever checked against the Official Records.

The record can settle much of it, and the pass finds out how much: the organization tables (`OR27-1-R009`, `OR27-2-R424`) print the commander of every regiment and battery with the order of succession and the footnote "Not engaged"; the returns of casualties (`OR27-1-R013`, `OR27-2-R427`) give losses by regiment, and `OR27-1-R013` pp. 188-192 list every Union officer killed or mortally wounded by state and regiment; the abstracts of returns (`OR27-1-R008`, `OR27-2-R425`) give strengths by corps and, for 4 July, by division and artillery brigade (Union), and by corps, division and artillery for 20 and 31 July (Confederate); the kind of figure (present for duty, effective, for duty equipped) differs by corps and is stated in the footnotes; regimental and brigade reports say how many men went into action; Hunt's, Pendleton's and the battalion reports give guns by battery; reports and the state volumes (`NY-GB-1900`, `PA-GB-1904`, `ME-GB-1898` and others in `sources.json`) sometimes name arms, dress and officers. What the record does not give stays at its tier or becomes a registered, visibly marked gap (Law cl.3, cl.7). Nothing is invented (cl.4).

**Not in this pass:** drawing, arrival times (ledger B.2.4), terrain, audio. The pass changes what the kit tables and the verified tables say, nothing else.

## 2. The rules this plan applies

1. **Every unit, every field, nothing skipped (cl.1).** Each unit of `groups.json` lists its `required` fields. A required (unit, field) pair is either evidenced by a line or recorded by a gap line (a failed lookup, with what was searched). `kit_evidence_check.py <group> --require-complete` fails while a pair is open. A pair may be left open only with the reason in the group's `REPORT.md`; never silently.
2. **A datum is `documented` only with a non-lead source, a verbatim quote, and a mechanical match (cl.4).** The checker re-finds every quote in the local text. Wikipedia, forums, blogs, auction pages and memory are lead sources: they cannot support a `documented` or `inferred` line. All sources of `sources.json` are printed works (Official Records, state commission volumes, Fox, Battles and Leaders, 1863 newspapers, period manuals); their class is recorded.
3. **A failed lookup is recorded as failed (cl.4, cl.7).** A gap line names the source ids and the terms searched and the result. A gap that matters (a unit's strength, a battery's guns) also gets a proposed register entry.
4. **Conflicts are carried, never resolved silently (cl.6).** Two non-lead sources that state different values of one quantity are two lines of one conflict set (`conflict: {key, alt}`); the pass chooses no default. Different dates are not a conflict (Hunt gives battery L, 1st NY, six guns, five on the evening of 1 July and four on 3 July: three lines with three `as_of`). Nor are casualty figures of different basis or period: Baxter's report says 645 (the action it describes, 1 to 3 July) and the revised return says 648 (1 to 4 July, with the skirmishes of 4 July): two lines, each with its own `basis` and `period`, and the note of the report's line says which return figure revises it (`us-1c:0281` beside `us-1c:0193`; the checker's identity of a casualty line is unit, basis and period). Two reports that give different figures for the same period are a conflict set. A lead value that the record contradicts is not a conflict: the record becomes the value and the lead stays visible as the value it replaced (section 8).
5. **Ranges, not points, where sources differ; the basis is always declared.** A strength is a figure of a named kind (`taken_into_action`, `present_for_duty`, ...) at a date; figures of one kind combine into a range; kinds are never mixed.
6. **Tier of what the pass cannot document stays marked.** A reconstruction is `plausible-procedural`, needs a reason and a register entry, and never feeds a verified table.
7. **Copy audit.** Nothing the pass writes into data the app loads may say how the data was made: no "agent", "generated by", "built by <script>", "reviewed by automated ..." (`pack_theatre.mjs AUDIT_RE`). The tools write provenance as sources, pages and quotes only.
8. **Nothing is deployed, no account is made, no host is circumvented.** Reachable hosts: see the horde rules; a refused host is recorded as blocked, never worked around.

How the groups were formed. By formation: a corps or a division for infantry (one corps where it is small), the seven corps artillery brigades together, the Artillery Reserve with the two horse artillery brigades, each Confederate corps' artillery battalions together, the Union cavalry corps and Stuart's cavalry division; `army-hq` holds both army headquarters with their guards, escorts, engineers, signal and support troops, the nine army and arm defaults, and the kit records no formation claims. A unit belongs to exactly one group (the generator asserts it), so no fact has two writers. Kit batteries and cavalry regiments the roster does not break out are listed under their battalion or brigade; a few kit records the roster lacks or mislabels (`US.MN_CO_L`, `US.5NY_INF`, `CS.5ALBN`, `CS.SCSSBN`) are placed where the record says they belong, with the question stated in the unit's `kit[].note`. The workflow caps the pass at 26 groups; none of the 825 units is left out. Reading volume ranges from 35k characters of reports (Pickett's division, for which the record holds two reports) to about 400k. Every Union group also reads, for its own formations only, the abstracts and field returns of `OR27-1-R008` pp. 151-155 and the lists of officers killed and mortally wounded of `OR27-1-R013` pp. 188-192; every Confederate group the abstract `OR27-2-R425` pp. 291-293. Those tables are listed in each group's `reading_list.tables`, with the rule that a row belongs to the group that owns the unit.

What `groups.json` gives each unit (hints only; none of it is evidence): `required` (the fields to address), `search_terms` (how the OR spells the unit: "Seventy-sixth New York", "Battery C", the commander's name), `reports` (`own`: reports whose heading is this regiment or battery by number, state and battery letter; `commander_match`: reports by the commander of this formation; `parent`: the same for the formation above), `state_sections` (sections of the state volumes with this regiment number), `kit` (the kit records that describe it), `fields` (what each field holds now and at what tier), `kit_fields` (the same for the thirteen kit fields), and the group's `reading_list` (tables with printed pages, reports with mode `read` or `search`, volumes). Hints can miss a report (OCR garbles headings such as "One himdred and fourth"): search before concluding a unit has no report.

## 5. Sources and how to cite them

`groups.json` is 1.5 MB: do not read it whole; `group <id>` prints your working sheet and `unit <id>` one unit. `sources.json` lists every text a line may cite: id, class, local path, sha256, page model. The three Official Records parts are `OR27-1` (Union reports and returns), `OR27-2` (Confederate), `OR27-3` (correspondence); state volumes and the rest follow. Commands (run with `python -I`; they need no network):

```
python -I tools/bt/kit_evidence_lib.py find  OR27-1 "went into action with 17 officers" -C 200     search; each hit prints source_id, part, report_id, printed_page, offset
python -I tools/bt/kit_evidence_lib.py cite  OR27-1 "<exact quote>" [--offset N]                  the citation fields of a quote (and whether it is unique)
python -I tools/bt/kit_evidence_lib.py image OR27-1 173 --dpi 160                                  render the scanned page; view it with the Read tool (crop with `convert -crop WxH+X+Y +repage`)
python -I tools/bt/kit_evidence_lib.py group us-1c                                               your working sheet: tables with pages, reports with modes, one line per unit with its hints
python -I tools/bt/kit_evidence_lib.py unit  us-1c-1d-meredith-19in                               one unit's entry: required fields, what the kit holds, reading list
python -I tools/bt/kit_evidence_lib.py template guns                                              a valid line for a field
python -I tools/bt/kit_evidence_check.py <group> --coverage --diff                                 validate your file; --diff prints what the kit holds now beside each line
```

**Take pages, report ids and offsets from these commands, never by hand and never from `or27_find.py` or `or27_index.json`.** Measured while planning: `or27_find` placed the passage "The loss of the Fifty-sixth Pennsylvania was also severe" on p. 280 (Cutler's report); the scan shows it on p. 282, which is what the leaf map says. The index gives the organization table as pp. 162-169 and the casualty record as pp. 171-192; the leaf map and the scans give pp. 155-168 for the organization table and pp. 173-192 for the casualty record (the return itself 173-187 with its recapitulation on p. 187, then the lists of officers killed and mortally wounded 188-192). The leaf map (`or27_leaf_pages.json`, built from the per-leaf OCR and the archive's own page numbers; 100 %, 99.8 % and 99.9 % agreement with the running heads the OCR kept in parts 1 to 3) does not depend on running heads, so it covers the tables; it was checked against the scanned page at pp. 153, 173, 238, 282, 329 and 339, and the checker's `image` field (the scan page of a quote) lets every later reader do the same.

**The OCR is wrong in known ways.** (a) Digits: Hunt's list of the Cemetery Hill batteries on the morning of 3 July prints "Stevens' (Fifth Maine, six 13-pounders)"; the scan reads six 12-pounders. (b) Table columns: the return of casualties comes out as groups of numbers detached from their rows ("1 nth Indiana", "21th Michigan", "lOil", "1.^)8"); no figure can be matched to a row from the text. (c) Names ("Maksena", "Gten.", "Sudsburg"). (d) Ordinals and dates, the misread that changes the unit or the day and that no checker can see: the two groups finished first found twelve (a report headed "July 3" that the scan dates July 2; "3d Delaware" for the 2d; "34th" for the 24th; "53d Infantry" in the list of officers killed for the 52d; "E. C. Bentley" for R. C.; "action of the 3d" for the 2d). Where a quote carries an ordinal, a date or a figure that decides the claim, look at the scan, and list every misread you find in the report (section Conflicts, "OCR digit errors found against the scan"). For (a) use `quote_mode: ocr_corrected` with the page image; for (b) use `quote_mode: image_row`: the quote is the label as the OCR has it, the figures are read from the scanned page into `value.row` with their column names in `value.row_columns`, and the checker verifies the arithmetic. Three casualty layouts occur: the Union return `killed, wounded, captured or missing` each split into officers and enlisted men, then the aggregate (seven figures); Guild's Confederate return (`OR27-2` p. 329 and the pages after it): killed, wounded, total (three); the Confederate Addenda return (pp. 338-346): killed, wounded, captured or missing, aggregate (four, no split by rank). In each the figures must add up to the aggregate and each must equal the same field of `value`; the "Total" rows of the table give a second check you can do by hand (the five regiments and the staff row of the Iron Brigade add to its printed total 13, 158, 54, 666, 13, 249, 1,153); where the print itself does not tally (a footnoted row) `waive_numbers` carries the reason. The abstracts of strength (`OR27-1-R008` pp. 151-155, `OR27-2-R425`) are read the same way: `row`, `row_columns` and an `identity` (the cells that add up to the printed total). **Read the footnote of each return for the kind of figure**: the Union field return of 4 July says the First, Second, Fifth and Eleventh Corps reported "present for duty", the Third "effective strength", the Twelfth "for duty equipped" (the Sixth is not named), so one table gives figures of three kinds, each its own `basis`. A scan PDF missing from `tmp/kit-plan/or27-pdf/` is fetched once: `curl -sS -L -o <path> https://archive.org/cors/<identifier>/<identifier>.pdf` (identifiers are in `sources.json`), at most one request per second, nothing else from the network without a reason in your report.

**New sources.** A text that `sources.json` lacks (a regimental history found on archive.org, a Library of Congress item) is declared by a `type: source` line at the top of your file (local path under `tmp/`, class, title, URL); the checker pins its sha256. Only public-domain or openly readable texts; reachable hosts only; one request per second; no bot-block circumvention. A text that cannot be fetched is a gap line with result `source_unavailable` or `host_blocked`. Tell the library ledger about each new text in a request note (`GOVERNANCE/horde/requests/bt-oob-kit-<group>-library.md`); the pass does not write `GOVERNANCE/library/`. Keep a declared text where its line says (`tmp/kit-<group>/lib/`) until the integration has validated the file: a missing text fails its declaration (`DECLARE`) and rejects every line that cites it (`SOURCE`) and every gap line that lists it as searched (`GAP`): for `us-1c:0215` alone that is 19 lines and 25 required pairs (tested on a copy of the file), and only the url and the sha256 of the line let the text be fetched again. Before fetching anything, see what the finished groups already declared (`grep -h '"type":"source"' docs/battle-theatre/kit/evidence/*.jsonl`: id, title, local path, sha256, url); a text already on disk is declared again in your own file with the same `local_path` and `sha256`, with no second request. `us-1c` declared seven: Dawes (6th Wisconsin), Curtis (24th Michigan), Cook (12th Massachusetts), Davis (13th Massachusetts), Small (16th Maine), A. P. Smith (76th New York) and Benedict (Vermont in the Civil War, vol. 2).

**Tier of a source.** `official_records`, `state_or_commission_report`, `statistical_compilation`, `participant_account`, `period_or_later_history`, `period_newspaper`, `regimental_or_state_history`, `primary_manual` are non-lead. Fox's *Regimental Losses* is a compilation from the rolls: a good source for strengths on the rolls and for losses, a weak one for who commanded in July 1863. A participant account written years later (Haskell, Alexander) supports what its author saw; it does not outrank a report of July 1863 on the same point, and a disagreement between them is a conflict set.

## 6. The evidence file contract

One file per group: `docs/battle-theatre/kit/evidence/<group>.jsonl`, UTF-8, LF, one JSON object per line, no comments, no blank structure. The group's engineer writes only this file, `<group>.REPORT.md`, proposed register entries and `tmp/kit-<group>/`. A line is never edited after another line cites it: a correction is a new line with `supersedes`. The checker is the contract in executable form; this section is printed from the same tables the checker uses.

**Rules that go with the tables.**

- *Unit.* `unit_id` is a unit of your group in `groups.json`. For a unit the record shows and the plan lacks, write the line with `unit_id: null`, `unit_name` as printed and `parent_unit_id`: it is reported to the roster owner and never applied. A roster unit the record does not show (no table row, no report) gets a `roster` line saying so, or a gap line.
- *Quote.* The shortest passage that supports exactly this claim, copied from the text with whitespace collapsed; it names the unit or the passage just before it does; a figure and its unit are not quoted from different paragraphs. Never paraphrase inside `quote`; never put a number in `value` that the passage does not state (the checker looks for every figure of `value` in the quote, spelled-out numbers included; a sum of two stated figures is derived and exempt).
- *Commanders.* One line per commander per unit: the first name the organization table prints is `order: 1`; each further name is the next order. The table lists the regiment's commanders in order of succession; deaths and wounds come from the reports and the casualty lists (a second line of the same commander with `change`, or the `change` on the successor), each with its own quote. Rank as printed (`rank_printed`) and in full (`rank`). The table's footnote marks (`*` not engaged, `+` killed) are quoted with the row.
- *Strength.* Figures of different kinds are different lines. "Went into action with 17 officers and 235 men" is `taken_into_action`, `men: officers_and_men`, `officers: 17`, `enlisted: 235`, `low = high = 252`. "About 400" is `estimate_in_report` with `low`/`high` as the words allow ("less than 3,500" is `high: 3499` only if you say so in `note`; otherwise `high: 3500` and `note: "less than"`). A return's "present for duty" is `present_for_duty` with the date of the return. Nothing is added across kinds.
- *Casualties.* From a return, one `image_row` line per regiment, battery or formation row (the layouts of section 5), plus the "Total" rows you used to check; from a report, `report_statement` with the report's own words. A blank cell stays `null` in `row` and `{}` or null in `value`; nothing is entered that is not printed. `total` is the aggregate as printed. Where a statement prints none (a monument text: 11 killed, 62 wounded, 159 missing) leave `total` null and give each kind as `{"total": n}`: the verified table then shows the sum of the three kinds, and null, never 0, when a kind is missing. A statement the value cannot hold (killed and wounded together with no figure for the missing; men lost by rank only) is not forced into `total`: its quote and page go under "Not done" in the report, as the first two groups did. The checker verifies row arithmetic; compare each brigade total by hand. The period is an ISO range (the Union return's title says 1-3 July and a footnote says it also includes losses in skirmishes of 4 July: say so in `note`).
- *Guns.* One line per battery per date and state. "Six 3-inch" is `count: 6`, `type_id: "3inch_ordnance_rifle"` only if the passage says rifles of that kind or the kit's catalog resolves it unambiguously; "12-pounders" alone is `type_id: null` with `type_candidates` (Napoleon, 12-pounder howitzer). Catalog ids are those of `groups.json` `catalog.artillery_pieces`; an id outside it is a request note, not an invented id.
- *Arms and dress.* One line per unit, kit field and date; each weapon or item with its `share` word only when the passage gives one. `weapon_id` and `item_id` come from the catalogs of `groups.json`; where the catalog lacks the item, give `null` and describe it in `printed`, and write a request note listing the missing items (`bt-oob-kit-<group>-catalog.md`): catalog additions are code changes of the integrator.
- *Dates.* `as_of` is the date the statement is true for, not the date of the report. Outside 26 June to 16 July 1863 the checker warns; background facts are fine.
- *Gap lines.* `unit_ids` and `fields` may be lists, so one search that came up empty for twelve regiments is one line. Say exactly what was searched (source id, the report ids or pages, the regex or the words) so a reader can repeat it.
- *Superseding.* If you find your own line was wrong, append the corrected line with `supersedes`; do not delete the old one. The checker ignores superseded lines and lists them.

## 7. How a group works

The same order for every group; write results to the file as you go (files are the memory), validate often (about two seconds), do not re-read what you already extracted (note passages and offsets in `tmp/kit-<group>/notes.md`). A group that was interrupted is continued, never restarted: read `evidence/<group>.jsonl` (run the checker with `--coverage` to see what is open), then `tmp/kit-<group>/` (its builder, drafts such as `draft.jsonl`, notes, downloaded texts under `lib/`) and the `progress.md` lines of the group, and carry on from there; a draft that the checker passes is moved into `evidence/<group>.jsonl` before more is added.

1. **Orient.** Read section 5 and 6 once. `python -I tools/bt/kit_evidence_lib.py unit <id>` for a unit; the group's entry in `groups.json` for the reading list. Then `grep -l "<your group id>" docs/battle-theatre/kit/evidence/*.REPORT.md` and read the "For other groups" lines that name your group: they point to pages and figures other groups met on the way (return rows of your artillery, strength rows, a regiment's arms). A pointer is a lead: re-match the quote and read the scan yourself before you write a line.
2. **The organization table first** (pages in `reading_list.tables`; read the scan, the OCR garbles names): for every unit of the group a `roster` line (designation, place in the organization, engaged yes/no/partly, footnotes) and one `commander` line per name listed, in order. Brigade and division commanders from the same pages. A "Not engaged" footnote is a `roster` fact with its quote and a `detachment` line where companies are named absent.
3. **The return of casualties** (pages in `reading_list.tables`): one `casualties` line per row of your units and formations, read from the scan, checked against the totals.
4. **The reports** (`reading_list.reports`, `reports.own` of each unit): for each unit its strength statements, casualties statements, officers killed or wounded and who took command, guns by battery and date, horses, ammunition, arms, dress. Corps and division reports carry brigade and regiment figures: read them for all units below them. Reports marked `search` are not read through: search them for your units' names.
5. **The state volumes** (`reading_list.volumes`, `state_sections`): regimental histories for arms, dress, officers, strength on the rolls, strength at Gettysburg. These are the main source for arms and dress; most regiments will end in gap lines for dress, and that is a finding, not a failure. Work in two passes: first the reports and the sections the index gives each regiment; then a scan of the whole text of every local volume and history for each designation of `search_terms` within a few hundred characters of an arms, dress or strength word, reading each hit. The first two groups found by the second pass what the first missed (the Adjutant General's letters and the monument inscriptions in the Pennsylvania volumes, a regiment's Belgian rifles, a strength in the Maine volume, five officers of the lists of the killed).
6. **Compare with the kit** (`check --diff`): for every line the checker prints what the kit holds now. A line that contradicts the kit stands as written; do not adjust a line to match the kit. List the differences in the report.
7. **Close the pairs.** `check <group> --coverage` lists every required (unit, field) pair still open. Search once more with the unit's `search_terms`, then write the gap line. Register proposals for the gaps that matter (Law cl.3).
8. **Report.** `<group>.REPORT.md` and register entries (templates below). Append one dated line to `GOVERNANCE/progress.md` with a single `printf ... >>`.

**Search patterns that work on this OCR** (case-insensitive; `find <source> "<regex>"`; add the unit's `search_terms` or a report id range in your own filtering): strength `(went|carried|took|taken|marched|moved)\s+into\s+(the\s+)?(action|fight|engagement|battle)`, `(effective|present for duty|aggregate|total)\b[^.]{0,40}\d{2,}`, `\d{2,4}\s+(men|muskets|rifles|officers)`; losses `(killed|wounded|missing|captured)[^.]{0,50}\d`, `(lost|loss)\b[^.]{0,60}\d`; guns `(Napoleon|3-inch|10-pounder|20-pounder|Parrott|rifled|12-pounder|howitzer|James|Whitworth|Blakely|ordnance|smooth)`, `(six|four|five|three|two|\d)\s+(guns|pieces)`; commanders `(assumed|took|succeeded)\s+(the\s+)?command`, `(fell|killed|wounded|mortally)`; horses `(horses|mules)[^.]{0,40}\d`; ammunition `(rounds|shot|shell|canister|case)[^.]{0,40}\d`; arms `(Enfield|Springfield|rifle.?musket|smooth.?bore|Sharps|Spencer|Burnside|Lorenz|Mississippi|buck and ball|calibre|caliber)`; dress `(uniform|zouave|jacket|blouse|shoeless|barefoot|hat|cap|overcoat|blanket|knapsack)`. The OCR spells numbers wrongly now and then (1 for l, 5 for S, 13 for 12): when a number matters, look at the scan.

**Done means**: `kit_evidence_check.py <group> --require-complete` exits 0, or every open pair is listed under "Not done" in the report with its reason (blocked host, unreadable page, record silent after the search of section 7); `<group>.REPORT.md` and the proposed register entries are written; the progress line is appended. Say in the report what you did not do; an unfinished pair that the report does not mention is a defect.

**For the independent reviewer of a group**: (1) run `kit_evidence_check.py <group> --coverage --diff`; any error is a defect. (2) Draw at least ten lines per field kind and re-read each passage in the source (`cite` prints it; the scan for table rows and OCR-corrected quotes) and confirm the passage supports exactly the claim: the unit named, the place, the date, the number. (3) Add up two brigade totals of the casualty return by hand. (4) Look for what the checker cannot see: a quote that names another regiment of the same number, a claim a quote supports only partly, a tier higher than the passage allows, a lead source used for a documented line, a conflict declared as a correction (or a correction hidden as a conflict), a gap line whose search was too narrow to support "not found", a required pair neither evidenced nor listed. (4a) Repeat three gap searches yourself with different terms. (5) Check that nothing was written outside the group's own files.

**Do not**: edit `groups.json`, `sources.json`, `PLAN.md` or another group's files; run `kit_evidence_plan.py` (it rewrites those shared files), `kit_overrides.py compile`, `kit_verified.py build` or `build_kit_data.py` (the integrator's commands); write under `kit/overrides/` or into the kit tables; promote a tier; pick a default between conflicting sources; use a source that is not in `sources.json` without declaring it; copy a number from the kit or the roster into a line without a quote that states it.

## 8. The override mechanism: how evidence corrects the kit tables

The kit tables (`kit_specs.json`, `weapons.json`, `artillery_batteries.json`, `cavalry_units.json`) are generated by `tools/bt/build_kit_data.py` from the `kit_*.py` data modules and are never edited by hand. Evidence reaches them through an override layer inside the build: `build_tables()` makes the base tables, `kit_overrides.apply_overrides()` applies `kit/overrides/*.json` to copies of them, then they are written.

```
evidence/<group>.jsonl --kit_evidence_check.py--> accepted lines --kit_overrides.py compile--> kit/overrides/<group>.json  and  kit/evidence_index.json
kit_*.py modules --build_kit_data.py: build_tables(), apply_overrides(), write--> kit_specs.json, weapons.json, artillery_batteries.json, cavalry_units.json
```

- **Compiled overrides** (`kit/overrides/<group>.json`, schema `kit-overrides-1`) are committed and deterministic. They hold primitive operations with guards, so the build needs only these small files and not the source texts under `tmp/`. They are never hand-edited. `kit/overrides/manual.json` is the one hand-written file (the integrator's decisions); the loader refuses an operation in it that has no `why`, or neither `evidence_ids` nor a `decision` id from `GOVERNANCE/decisions/DELEGATED-DECISIONS-2026-11-11.md`.
- **Operations** (each carries `table`, `evidence_ids`, `why`): `set` and `remove` (path in the table's JSON; records are addressed by id; guard `before: <value>` or `before_absent: true`), `append` (to a list, optionally unique), `create` (a new record under a dict; the key must be absent) and `register_source`. A guard that no longer holds means the base changed after the compile: **the build fails and names every stale operation** (never skipped, never silently applied); `kit_overrides.py compile` is re-run. A `set` with no guard is refused.
- **Compile rules by field.** Only lines that pass the checker, tier `documented` or `inferred`, `as_of` inside 26 June to 16 July 1863 or absent. What cannot be applied by rule is not lost: it is listed in the override file under `unapplied` with the reason, stays in `evidence_index.json` and in the verified tables.

| evidence field | target | what the compile does |
|---|---|---|
| `guns` (battery) | `artillery_batteries.json` `batteries[card]` | Lines with state `present` or `engaged` at battery scope. The card takes the earliest date: `guns`, `guns_printed`, `guns_status: stated`, `tier`, `weak_source: false`, `source_keys`; what it held is kept in `guns_replaced`; every date in `guns_by_date`; `evidence_ids`. Not compiled (reason under `unapplied`): a piece with `type_id` null (the check requires a known piece type on a card), a card that joins two roster batteries (`3rd US F and K`: its count is not split), lines of state lost or disabled |
| `commander` (battery, cavalry) | `batteries[card]`, `cavalry_units.json` `cards[card]` | `commanders_evidence`: ordered list (order, name, rank, change, tier, `evidence_ids`); the PDF's `commanders_printed` stays beside it |
| `arms` | `weapons.json` `assignments[UA-EV-<kit id>]` and the unit's `fields.weapon`, `bayonet` or `sidearm_sword` | Field `allowed` from the lines (the kit's share words with its tuning weights; no share word means equal shares, noted), `tier` and `source_keys` from the evidence. A weapon that is barred, restricted (Spencer, Henry, M1863, Whitworth), a placeholder, of the wrong category for the kit field, or with no catalog id is not compiled. A unit with a record is changed in place. A regiment with none gets one (`US.<n><ST>` or `CS.<n><ST>`, cavalry `US.CAV.<id suffix>`) that inherits the default of its army and arm, which `kit_presence.py` and `kit_crosswalk.py` resolve by their existing suffix rule. A regiment that takes its brigade's record (crosswalk `how: brigade`) gets a record of its own that starts as a copy of the brigade record, so no brigade field is hidden and the other regiments of the brigade are untouched |
| `dress` | the unit's `fields[kit_field]` | Same, for items of the catalog whose kind matches the kit field; an `item_id` of null stays in the evidence index and the report (catalog items are code) |
| `roster`, `officer`, `strength`, `casualties`, `horses`, `ammunition`, `detachment`, battalion and brigade `guns` | the verified tables (section 9) | no change to a kit table |

- **What evidence does to an existing value** (`kit_overrides.decide`): the same value on a documented non-weak field adds the source and the ids (confirm). A `documented` line replaces a field that is absent, `plausible-procedural`, `inferred`, or `documented` on sources that are not primary reports; the displaced value stays inside the table (`guns_replaced`, or the field's `lead_value`). A `documented` line that differs from a field resting on a primary report (source class `primary_report`, `primary_manual`, ...) is a conflict: nothing is replaced, the evidence goes into `guns_alternatives` (for fields: left to a decision), a delegated decision names the default. An `inferred` line replaces only absent or `plausible-procedural` values. A conflict set among the lines (two non-lead sources disagree) also stops at `guns_alternatives` and `unapplied`. A tier is never lowered.
- **Sources.** The evidence sources each table's operations cite are registered in that table's `sources` block (class, `weak: false`, `claim_support_verified: true` because the quote was matched in the local text, `text_sha256`), so `check_kit.py` accepts `documented` on them. Provenance travels as ids: records carry `evidence_ids`; `kit/evidence_index.json` maps each id to source id, part, report id, printed page, offset, quote, mode, tier, field, unit, value and conflict key, for the unit card's evidence panel.
- **Meta.** Tables an override touched get `meta.evidence` (files, operations, lines cited) and re-counted `meta.counts`.
- **Reversible and inert.** Remove an override file and rebuild: the base tables return (tested). With no override file the applier returns the base tables themselves, so the build output is byte-identical to the build without the layer (tested; also checked against a rebuild made before the layer existed).
- **Checks after every rebuild**: `python -I tools/bt/kit_overrides.py check` (kit data checks on base plus overrides), `python tools/bt/check_kit.py` (data checks, self-tests, population test of every present unit on three dates; run it through the machine lock), the kit, manifest and figures node tests, and `python -I tools/bt/kit_overrides.py status`, which reports a committed table that differs from base plus overrides (a hand edit, or a changed generator) with the position of the difference.

## 9. Verified tables for the gates

`python -I tools/bt/kit_verified.py build` writes, from the accepted evidence lines and `groups.json` (never from the kit tables), the tables the gates compare against. It runs with no evidence at all: every unit is then `unverified` and nothing is filled in. A unit or battery without a non-lead source is listed as `unverified`.

- **`kit/verified_oob.json`** (Q01, Q20). `units`: an entry for each of the 825 units of `groups.json` (roster id; kit-only records under their kit id) with `kit_ids` (the id map the gate needs between `units.json` ids and kit ids), `group`, `required`, `fields` (status of each required field: `verified` = at least one documented line, `inferred`, `conflict`, `gap`, `unverified`) and `status` (`verified` when roster, commander and strength of its required set are verified, `partly`, `unverified`). Present only where evidence exists: `presence` (`engaged` yes/no/partly, designations, tier, ids), `commanders` (ordered, with `change` and conflict `alt`), `strength.figures` (basis, men, low, high, `as_of`, tier, ids, citation) and `strength.ranges` (per kind and `men`: low = lowest low and high = highest high among the documented lines, tier, number of lines, and the basis sentence: the meaning of the kind, the date, the number of lines, the report ids), `casualties.figures` and `ranges` (per basis and period), `officers`, `horses`, `ammunition`, `detachment`, `arms` and `dress` by kit field, `conflicts` (each alternative with its ids), `gaps` (what was searched, result). Top level: `rules`, `inputs` (sha256 of `groups.json` and of each evidence file), `totals`, `roster_additions` (units the record shows and the plan lacks, never applied), `reconstructions_not_verified` (plausible-procedural lines). Q01's comparison rule, proposed for the gate engine to adopt or amend: a layer figure passes when it lies inside the range of one kind of its unit; a unit with no verified range is reported `unverified`, neither a pass nor a failure. Q20 reads `presence` to decide which cavalry units are documented.
- **`kit/verified_batteries.json`** (Q14). `batteries` (battery roster ids and kit battery cards) and `formations` (battalions, brigades, groups): `guns` figures (date, state, scope, pieces with type id or candidates, total, tier, ids, citation), `range_by_date` (per date and state: low and high of the totals), `status`, `unverified`, and `kit_card` (what the kit tables hold now, for comparison only). Q14's rule, proposed: a drawn battery passes when its gun count equals a total of its figures for the date asked (state present or engaged); a battery with no verified figure is reported `unverified`.
- Both files are deterministic and carry `inputs`, so a gate can tell when they are stale. Evidence ids resolve in `kit/evidence_index.json`.

## 13. Findings made while planning

1. **Generated tables were hand-patched** (section 10, step 0): a rebuild would have undone the copy-audit rewording. Corrected in the generators; `kit_overrides.py status` now guards against it, and against any later hand edit of a generated table.
2. **Page numbers**: `or27_find.py` and `or27_index.json` are unreliable on tables and can be off by two pages even in running text (measured above). The leaf map is the page authority; the checker refuses any other page.
3. **The OCR of the casualty return cannot be matched to rows** and the OCR misreads digits in running text; a quote-only contract would have produced no casualty evidence at all. Hence the `image_row` and `ocr_corrected` modes with arithmetic checks, and the scans kept at `tmp/kit-plan/or27-pdf/`.
4. **The roster merges batteries** ("1st RI A and B", "1st NY E and L", "1st OH I and K"), and the kit has one card per battery for some and one card for two for others (`US.ART.1st_NY_E_L`, `US.ART.3rd_US_F_K`, `US.ART.Pa_Independent_C_F`); `kit_evidence_plan.py` carries the aliases it needed (`PLAN_ALIAS`) with the reason for each, and one open question (`1st Conn Heavy B and H` in the kit against `B` and `M` in the roster).
5. **Gun cards and their tiers**: 84 of 125 cards say `documented` on `SS-ArtReserve` (Stone Sentinels), which the kit classes as a secondary site, not a lead. The pass does not downgrade it; the Official Records either confirm it (the source and ids are added) or contradict it (the record replaces it and the card keeps the old value as `lead_value`).
6. **Some Official Records reports cannot be read as text**: one page of the Third Maryland report (`OR27-1-R277`) is upside down in the scan and is not findable by author name; a group meeting it reads the page image.
7. **The Third Division of the III Corps** (reports `OR27-1-R179`, `R180`: operations of 23 to 25 July and of August) is a division formed after the battle; it is in the reading list of `us-3c-2d` only to rule units in or out, as are other reports of units that joined later.
8. **Shared tables.** The lists of Union officers killed and mortally wounded (`OR27-1-R013` pp. 188-192) and the abstracts of returns (`OR27-1-R008`, `OR27-2-R425`) serve every group; the plan hands each row to the group that owns the unit, so nothing is written twice.
9. **What the local library does and does not hold** (`sources.json`). Regimental and state volumes: Pennsylvania (three volumes), New York (three), Maine and Michigan, with Fox's *Regimental Losses* and *Battles and Leaders* vol. 3, are indexed by section and page (a page range where the OCR lost the running heads); Vermont (two volumes), New Jersey (two), Ohio (two), Indiana (1913) and the 1st Minnesota are plain text with no page model: `report_id` and `printed_page` are null and the page is not checked. **Not held:** regimental or state volumes for Massachusetts, Connecticut, New Hampshire, Rhode Island, Wisconsin, Maryland, Delaware, West Virginia, Illinois and Kentucky, and every Confederate state; the Confederate side has the Official Records, five volumes of the Southern Historical Society Papers, Alexander, Longstreet, Fremantle and Battles and Leaders. So for the Iron Brigade's Wisconsin regiments, the Massachusetts, Connecticut, New Hampshire and Rhode Island regiments and all Confederate regiments, arms and dress will end mostly as gap lines unless the group declares public-domain texts it finds (section 5; a Confederate state regimental history such as a North Carolina one, a Wisconsin or Massachusetts regimental history, are the kind of text meant) or records `source_unavailable`. The integration turns those gap lines into a list of library needs; the owner-only steps (accounts, purchases) are never taken.
10. **A casualties line with no printed aggregate came out of the verified table as 0.** Found 2026-11-13 on `us-1c:0410` (the 16th Maine's monument text: 11 killed, 62 wounded, 159 missing, `total` null): `kit_verified.py` summed only officers and enlisted men and so gave a total of 0 and a range of 0 to 0. `kit_verified.cas_total` now gives the printed aggregate, else the sum of the three stated kinds (232 here), else null; a basis whose lines give no aggregate has figures and no range. Five tests in `kit_overrides_selftest.py`.
11. **The roster is not the record.** The two groups finished first found units the record shows and the roster lacks (12th and 15th Vermont, 1st Maine Cavalry Company L, Companies D and K of the 6th New York Cavalry, headquarters staff rows of the return), a regiment under the wrong brigade (the 11th Pennsylvania, transferred to Paul's brigade on the afternoon of 1 July), the order of a division's commanders reversed against the table, and a person-unit that overstates the man's authority (`us-hancock-acting`). Each group will find some. They are reported as `unit_id: null` lines and in the report's Roster section, copied into a request note for the roster owner (`GOVERNANCE/horde/requests/bt-oob-kit-<group>-roster.md`); the pass never edits the roster, and `verified_oob.json` lists them under `roster_additions`.
