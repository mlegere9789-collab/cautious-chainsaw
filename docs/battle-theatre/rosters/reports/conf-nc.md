## 0. Summary

* **41 unit files** under `rosters/data2/confederate/nc/` (33 infantry regiments and battalions, 4 cavalry regiments, 4 batteries), **54,339 lines in all** (4,361,278 raw bytes, 987,141 gzipped), format `roster2/2` (`pipeline2/r2lib.py`), each with a flag file `<unit>.flags.json.gz` (624,516 bytes in all). One line per name entry of the printed roll, exactly as the text layer prints it (name, rank, remarks), with the printed page and the character offset of the name in the pinned text. **No unit is a gap**: all 41 units of the work item have a file.
* **One printed source is parsed**: John W. Moore, *Roster of North Carolina Troops in the War between the States* (Raleigh, 1882), four volumes, each read in **two Internet Archive scans** (8 texts, `NC-MOORE-1882.<vol>` and `.<vol>~b`; section 3). For each unit the scan with the better measured score is the reading of the file (1 from NC-MOORE-1882.1; 14 from NC-MOORE-1882.1~b; 2 from NC-MOORE-1882.2; 11 from NC-MOORE-1882.2~b; 8 from NC-MOORE-1882.3; 2 from NC-MOORE-1882.3~b; 1 from NC-MOORE-1882.4; 2 from NC-MOORE-1882.4~b); the other scan is the second reading of the reconciliation only, and **no line of a unit file is taken from it**. The roll of each unit as Moore prints it: the field and staff and every company (officers, non-commissioned officers and privates), under the company the heading gives.
* **Not parsed**: the 12 printed notes inside the rolls (Moore: "Note.", prose, listed in section 5), the 1,350 tokens of text between a section heading and the first roll heading (the list of abbreviations and the like, counted in `diagnostics/<unit>.json`), Moore's Recapitulation (read only for the printed totals of section 1), and the **Appomattox parole list** and the other lists of Clark volume 5 (not Gettysburg lists). **Clark's histories of 1901 are read for notes, not for rows** (section 1.3, gap EG-ROS-NC-05): 89 notes (statements of strength, number present for duty, number taken into action, number left and losses, each with a verbatim quotation and its offset) are in the ledger `data2/sources/NC-CLARK-1901.json`; 32 of the 41 units have a note, 9 have none. No name, date or figure is invented, completed or repaired.

## 1. Lines per unit against the strength

"Strength" is the value of the baseline order of battle (`baseline/dist/data/oob`, second hand, from Busey and Martin as the earlier families recorded it; GAPS G15). **No registered strength range with a declared basis exists for these units**: `history/units.json` holds a strength for 0 of the 35 registry ids of the 41 units, and the kit tables carry none for the cavalry and the batteries. The baseline has no row for 1st North Carolina Cavalry, 2nd North Carolina Cavalry, 4th North Carolina Cavalry, 5th North Carolina Cavalry, Branch Artillery, Charlotte Artillery. Clark (1901) gives statements of a regiment's strength at Gettysburg for 3 units (section 1.3, as notes of the ledger, with the author of each; they conflict where there are several). "Printed total" is Moore's own count of the men of the regiment or battalion "all told", read from the Recapitulation at the end of volume 4 (`recap.json`: the quoted line and its offset in both scans, section 4); for a company of a regiment (the four batteries) the printed total is that of the **whole regiment or battalion** and is not comparable with the lines. A unit file holds the roll of the whole war, as Moore prints it; the strength is the men present on the field: the two are not the same kind of number.

### 1.3 Statements of strength and losses in Clark (1901), as notes of the ledger

Clark, *Histories of the Several Regiments and Battalions from North Carolina in the Great War, 1861-'65* (published by the State, Raleigh and Goldsboro, 1901, five volumes), is read here for **statements, not for names**: work item first task 2 asks for its present-for-duty figures as notes in the ledger, not as rows. `data2/sources/NC-CLARK-1901.json` pins the ten texts (each volume twice, sha256) and holds, under `notes`, **89 notes** (casualties 37, identification 4, present_for_duty 14, remaining_after 9, strength_other 4, taken_into_action 21). A note is the quotation exactly as the text layer prints it (white space joined), its offset and end in the pinned text, the printed page, the chapter and its author, the figures as printed with a reading of each as a number, the comparison with the second scan and, where the scans read a figure differently, a page-image check (36 notes; 23 looked at by eye, the rest read with tesseract only). **A note is a statement of the author of the chapter or of the person he quotes, not a muster**; where authors disagree every statement stays and none is made the default (DD-ROS-NC-08, DD-ROS-NC-09). The list is a **floor**: the chapters of the 41 units, the Gettysburg chapters of volume 5 and the brigade histories of volume 4 were scanned sentence by sentence for a figure that names men, guns or losses near a mention of Gettysburg (the scan is `nc_clark.py`; the counts per unit are in the table), every hit was read in its context and kept only where the sentence states a strength, a number present, a number taken into action, a number left or a loss of that unit (or of the brigade, battalion or company named); a figure the scan does not know is not in the list (gap EG-ROS-NC-05). Kinds: P present for duty, T taken into action or in line, R left or present after the fight, L losses, S other strength (the crossing of the Potomac, guns), I identification of a battery (no figure).

## 2. The unit files

One gzip JSON-lines file per unit, `[offset delta, name, rank, remarks, remark delta]` lines with carried state lines `co` (company), `pg` (printed page) and `rk` (the rank heading above the lines, as printed), one **segment** per source text (`NC-MOORE-1882.<vol>` or `.<vol>~b`, layout `flow`; every unit has exactly one: no line is read from a second scan). Lines are in the order of the printed page. The writer is `r2lib.write_unit` (deterministic bytes).

* **name** (`name_printed`): the tokens of the head of the entry as the text layer prints them, joined by single blanks, with the printed commas and periods. Moore prints privates "Surname, Given, e. date; County co.; remarks" and officers "Given Surname, Rank, [cm. date;] County co.; remarks"; the head is cut where the entry begins (a surname-first head with an enlistment or commission marker, or a given-name-first head with a rank phrase); a damaged name stays damaged. Running heads ("North Carolina State Troops." and "Company X. Nth Regiment.") and page numbers are left out.
* **rank**: where the entry prints a rank (7,681 lines) it is the printed rank; every other line carries the rank of the **heading** above it (`^PRIVATES`, `^NON-COMMISSIONED OFFICERS`, `^OFFICERS`, `^FIELD AND STAFF`), carried as printed.
* **company**: the letter of the company heading above the line (the printed letter; where the scan damaged it, the letter the sequence of headings gives, flag C). **`FS` is the code of the field and staff** of the regiment or battalion (810 lines): it is not a company. A battery that is one company of a Moore section holds that company only.
* **remarks**: every other token of the entry in the order of the text layer (county, event, date, remarks); 54,303 of 54,339 lines have remarks. Where the text layer inserts running heads or page furniture in the middle of an entry, the remark tokens skip them (the verifier allows a step-over of at most 200 characters inside the leaf): 36 lines have such a step-over.
* **Rights per name**: the segment names the source text; `data2/sources/NC-MOORE-1882.json` holds the eight items, their dates and their sha256; `rosters/sources/conf-nc.json` is the tracked pointer file (section 3).

**Flag codes** (flag file, by row number from 0 over the whole file; `ocr_uncertain` is the union of N H C R Y G M W; the rules are those of `nc_flags.py`, heuristics on the printed cell, never a change to a line). Totals over the 41 units:

| code | lines | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| N | 1,841 | name text damaged: a digit or a mark inside a name token, a lower-case first letter, a capital inside a word, or a name that is only a surname |
| H | 2,332 | the head of the entry was cut by a rule that is a reading: a head recovered backwards from a marker inside the remarks of the line above, a weak marker (c. o. a. for e.), a head with no enlistment marker (county or event word right after the name), an officer head without rank, a stray mark taken out of the name |
| C | 1,560 | the company heading above the row is damaged: the company letter is the one the sequence of headings gives (company_inferred lists the stretches) |
| R | 128 | rank text damaged: the printed rank is not made of the rank words the roll prints |

| G | 1,675 | garbage marks of the scan (^ | ~ and the like) stand in or beside the line, or a lone mark or letter stands between two words |

**Kinds of head** (`head_kinds` of the flag file lists the rows whose head was cut by a rule that is a reading; the regular head, a surname-first head with a strict marker `e.` or `cm.` or a given-name-first head with a rank phrase, is not listed): `G-mark` 353; `S-county` 179; `S-event` 92; `S-mark-junk` 143; `S-mark-weak` 345; `S-month` 33; `S-rank` 20; `S-recovered` 540. `S-recovered`: a head recovered backwards from a strict marker inside the remarks of the line above (the text layer ran two entries together); `S-mark-weak`: a weak marker (`c.` `o.` `a.` for `e.`); `S-mark-junk`: a damaged marker; `S-county`, `S-month`, `S-event`, `S-rank`: no marker, the name is followed by a county, a date, an event word or a rank phrase; `G-mark`: a given-name-first head with a marker.

**Company headings** (the rule that gave the letter, rows of all units): inferred-after-privates 1; late 1; letter 362; missing-roll 1; sequence 9; skip 2 (headings). `letter`: the printed letter is the next letter of the sequence; `sequence`: the printed letter is one the scan confuses with the expected letter or is no letter, the sequence gives it (flag C); `skip`: a clean letter further on in the alphabet than expected, taken as printed (listed in the flag member `company_skips`, no flag); `late`: a clean letter that no heading above had, taken as printed; `missing-roll`: Moore prints "Missing" for the company and no lines follow; `inferred-after-privates`: an officers heading appears after privates without a company heading, the next letter is given (flag C). Rows under a letter that the sequence gave: 1,560 (flag C; the flag member `company_inferred` lists the stretches); rows under a clean letter that skips a letter of the sequence: 263 (`company_skips`).

**`on_rolls_july_1863`** (inferred, in the flag file only): yes: a printed enlistment or commission date on or before 30 June 1863 (or inside 1-3 July 1863), no printed separation (died, killed, discharged, resigned, deserted, transferred out, promoted out of the regiment) dated before 1 July 1863, no undated separation, no return after a separation, no damaged date in a start or separation clause; no: the printed enlistment date is after 3 July 1863, or a separation is printed with a date before 1 July 1863 and nothing returns the man afterwards; every other row is unknown (no date, a damaged date, an undated separation, doubtful cases). A month-and-year date counts only where it lies certainly before or after the day compared with. It is **no statement of presence**: a man on the rolls may have been sick, absent or detached; no muster roll of 30 June 1863 is in the source.

**`names_gettysburg`** lists the rows whose printed remarks name Gettysburg (an OCR-tolerant match, a lower bound), **`gettysburg_dated`** those whose clause also prints 1, 2 or 3 July 1863; **`normalised_names`** only re-orders the printed tokens as "Given Surname" and strips stray marks at their ends (no letter is changed); **`page_image_readings`** (one entry, section 5 item 12) holds a reading of a printed name from a page image, with its evidence.

Files: unit files `rosters/data2/confederate/nc/<unit>.jsonl.gz` (41), flag files `<unit>.flags.json.gz` (41), ledger `rosters/data2/sources/NC-MOORE-1882.json`, pointer file `rosters/sources/conf-nc.json`, manifest `tmp/library/rosters/MANIFEST-conf-nc.json`, this report; work files (diagnostics per unit, `build-summary.json`, `reconciliation.json`, `differences-<unit>.csv` (35), `recap.json`, `stats.json`, `copy-audit.json`, `verify-unit-run.out`) under `GOVERNANCE/horde/ws-rosters/conf-nc/`; the tools are in `pipeline2/conf_nc/` (`nc_parse.py`, `nc_sections.py`, `nc_build.py`, `nc_flags.py`, `nc_recap.py`, `nc_reconcile.py`, `nc_stats.py`, `nc_gaps.py`, `nc_report.py`, `nc_audit.mjs`).
