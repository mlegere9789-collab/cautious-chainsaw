## 1. Lines per unit against the strength

"Strength" is the value of the baseline order of battle (`baseline/dist/data/oob/`, which cites Busey and Martin and the Official Records; second-hand). **No strength range with a declared basis exists for these four units in the repository**: `history/units.json` has `strength: null` for `us-3c-2d-burling-2nh`, `us-2c-1d-cross-5nh`, `us-3c-2d-carr-12nh` and `us-art-reserve-huntington-1nh`. The range here is therefore a single value, with the Official Records figure where one was found.

## 4. How the register is cut and what was checked

* **Section of a unit**: the leaves from the title leaf of the unit's Register to the leaf that carries the heading SUMMARY (asserted in the parser against the text: the title is found on the first leaf, the heading on the last). The register of an organization is one alphabetical list of every man who served in it, with the company printed inside the entry, so there are no company headings.
* **An entry** begins at its head, "Surname, Given." followed by the first data token ("Co. A", "F. and S.", "Band", "Unas'd", "Non-Com. Staff"; for the battery "b.", "Substitute", "Drafted", "Age", "See"), or is a bare cross-reference line ("Surname. See Surname."). The head is found backwards from the data token: the leftmost capitalised word with a comma such that everything up to the data token is a given name (capitalised words, initials, Jr./2d, "alias" and the name that follows it), and the head follows the end of the previous entry (a token that ends with a period, closing quotes allowed, a few marks of scan dirt stepped over) or page furniture. Fallbacks, each marked in the flag file: a head without the final period; a period printed for the comma ("Connor.  John."); a surname alone ("Kiniston.  Co. K.  See ..."); the comma lost ("Doe  Joseph  A."); and, only where the data begins after three or more blanks (the OCR keeps two between words), an irregular head back to the end of the previous entry. A head near "with", "identical" or a post-office address ("P. O. ad.,") is never taken. Place names are kept out by a list of state and place abbreviations.
* **Checks of the cut** (Measured, `parse-diagnostics.json`): (1) every company token that the OCR set off by three or more blanks lies in a head, except three that lie inside an entry (`orphan_anchors`, section 6, item 11); (2) the first letter of each surname against the one before it: the breaks are 23, 29, 12 and 2 (section 6, item 7); (3) the number of rows holding two "age" items (an entry with two holds another man): 3 (two in the 5th, one in the 12th); (4) the number of rows holding a head-shaped run followed at once by entry data: 3, one of them an address and not an entry (section 6, item 3); (5) printed lines per page (section 2); (6) the earlier roll files as an independent second reading (section 5); (7) the Register of Commissioned Officers as a third (below).
* **Register of Commissioned Officers** (volume 2, printed pp. 1225-1248; `pipeline2/nh_officers_check.py`): it re-lists the officers by rank. Each register line was compared with the roll rows of its unit by surname (difflib ratio at least 0.8) and first given name: 2nd Infantry 202 lines, 21 matched no roll row; 5th Infantry 175 lines, 15; 12th Infantry 137 lines, 11; battery 11 lines, 0. A second pass (ratio of the whole printed name at least 0.75) names a candidate roll row for 18, 11, 8 and 0 of them (for example the register line "Farr, Evarts W." and the roll row "Parr, Evarts W."); 3, 4 and 3 lines have no candidate (fragments such as "ON, Sylvands." and "AM, John AV." among them). A candidate is where to look, not a match: the register is **not proven** to name no man outside the roll, and what it adds, the rank heading and the "Not mustered" mark, is not carried (EG-ROS-NH-01).
* **Parser assertion** (Measured): for every line, before writing, the first name token and the first remark token stand at the offsets the row records; `r2lib.verify_rows` then runs V1 (all name tokens, in order, at the offset), V2 (all remark tokens in order from the offset; a step over page furniture of at most 200 characters is allowed) and V3 (the offset lies in a leaf whose printed page is the row's page). A failure aborts the run: no file is written. The Internet Archive page numbers give a printed page for every leaf of the four registers (0 lines without a page).
* **After writing**: `python3 -I pipeline2/verify_unit.py --all --root docs/battle-theatre/rosters/data2/union/nh`: 4 files, **0 V1, 0 V3, 0 problems**, ledger sha256 matched; the files read back to the rows written; the bytes of a rerun are identical (the file sha256 are in `rosters/sources/union-nh.json`).
* **Alignment to the source**: no word boxes are used (layout `flow`); the offsets are character offsets in the decoded search text of the item, so a row can be found with one slice.
* **Not checked by machine**: that a remark belongs to the name before it on the page, and that an entry begins where the cut says in the 154 lines marked H. Both need the page images (EG-ROS-NH-02).
