## 1. Lines per unit against the strength

The register lists **every man who served in the unit up to 1865** (a line for each man, each recruit, each cross-reference), so its line count is a multiple of the strength at Gettysburg; the lines say nothing about who stood on the field on 1-3 July 1863 unless a printed remark does.

"Strength" is the value of the baseline order of battle (`legacy/dist/data/oob`, second-hand, Busey and Martin as the earlier families recorded it). **No strength range with a declared basis exists for these four units in the repository** (`history/units.json`, field `strength`, is null for all four), so the range here is a single value, or two values where a report of the Official Records gives one.

* **Strength**: the baseline values are 354, 179, 224 and 107. The 5th New Hampshire figure is the only one with a second source: Maj. Richard E. Cross reports that the regiment "went into action, July 3, with 14 commissioned officers and 165 men" (OR Ser. I vol. 27 pt. 1, report R079, p. 383), 179 in all, equal to the baseline value. Capt. Edgell's report of the battery (R326) says "The casualties in my battery were 3 men wounded (only 1 seriously)" against 11 casualties in the baseline: a source conflict the roster does not resolve.
* **Printed Summary** (Documented; offsets in `GOVERNANCE/horde/ws-rosters/union-nh/summary-figures.json`): the Summary tables are printed as columns of labels and columns of numbers and the OCR lists the two apart, so a number is matched to its label by arithmetic. 2nd Infantry: 1,022 original members (printed "officers, .37; enlisted men, 98,5; total, 1,022"), 1,144 recruits ("1,133 ... 1,144"), 22 band, 1 band gained by transfer, 366 recruits gained by transfer ("366 ... 3G0"): 1,022 + 1,144 + 22 + 1 + 366 = 2,555, printed twice as "2,555". 5th Infantry: 978 original members, 24 band, 1,507 recruits, 53 gained by transfer from the 2nd U.S. Sharpshooters: 2,562, printed twice. 12th Infantry: "1,403" printed twice; the figure of the original members is damaged in the OCR ("l,ni9"), so 959 original members and 444 recruits is arithmetic, not a reading. Battery: "Original members officers, 5; enlisted men, 151; total, 156", "Recruits 101", "Recruits gained by transfer 1", "Total strength 258".
* **Lines minus spelling and alias lines** is the count of lines that stand for a man: the lines whose remarks are only "See Surname." (spelling) or "Co. X. See Given Surname." (alias) do not. It is the nearest comparison with the printed total. The battery matches to the man; the three infantry regiments are 18 and 43 below and 40 above. The 5th Infantry's 51 lines of the form "See 2 U.S.S.S." and the 2nd's 357 lines "See 10 N. H. V." (and 12, 13, 17) are the men gained by transfer: they stand for men and are counted. **The residuals are not explained** (EG-ROS-NH-04): a man with two enlistments, an entry the text layer destroyed (section 6, item 3), a leaf lost, and the treatment of unassigned men in the Summary are candidates; the page images that could settle it are not reachable.
* **"Names Gettysburg"** counts lines whose printed remarks match an OCR-tolerant pattern for the word (`GETTYSBURG_RE` of `r2lib.py`), and "has a July 1-3 '63 date" those in which the same clause prints 1, 2 or 3 July 1863. They are counts of printed text, not a judgement of presence, and the count of a damaged word is a lower bound (the battery's casualties are mostly "wd." clauses whose place the register does not print).
* **"On the rolls" yes** is not a presence count: it lists men whose printed dates allow them on the regiment's rolls in July 1863 (it includes men absent sick or detached). The 2nd Infantry's 353 is within one of the baseline strength; that is a coincidence of totals, not a reconciliation.

## 2. The unit files

`rosters/data2/union/nh/<unit>.jsonl.gz`, gzip, fixed header, UTF-8, `roster2/2`: line 1 the header, line 2 the one segment `{"s":"NH-AYLING-1895","v":"1" or "2","t":"NH-AYLING-1895.1" or ".2","lay":"flow"}`, then rows `[offset delta, name, rank, remarks, remark delta]` with carried state lines `{"co":...}` and `{"pg":...}`. `rank` is empty on every line: the register prints no rank in the head of an entry (the rank stands in the remarks, "as Priv.", "app. Capt."). `co` is the printed company letter (A to K), `FS` (printed "F. and S."), `BAND`, `NCS` ("Non-Com. Staff"), `UNAS` ("Unas'd") or empty where the company token is damaged or absent (cross-reference lines to a name, damaged tokens). A letter the OCR printed that is no company of these regiments (J, L, M) is not recorded as a company.

The "about 90 bytes a line" aim of the plan is **not met** (200 to 240 bytes): a New Hampshire entry prints about 215 characters of service history, and remarks are never cut (Law, clause 1). Per full printed page the files hold 35-46 (2nd), 32-41 (5th), 32-39 (12th) and 39-42 (battery) lines (mean 39.4, 38.1, 36.8, 40.6), so no full page is short.

**What a line leaves out.** The running head and the page number of each leaf are page furniture, not part of an entry: their tokens are not in the remarks (the verifier steps over them; only 1, 2, 0 and 0 lines needed a step over, because the compositor rarely lets an entry run over a page). Everything else between one head and the next is in the remarks, including trailing marks of the scan, which stay as the OCR printed them (`' , „ _ ^`).

**Flag files** (`<unit>.flags.json.gz`, form `roster2-flags/1` as the other roster items write it; row numbers count the lines of the unit file from 0): `ocr_uncertain` and `rows` by code (N name text damaged, H head cut by a fallback rule or by review, C company token damaged, Y a year damaged, A age damaged, G garbage marks); `on_rolls_july_1863` (`yes`, `no`, each with an index into a list of the printed dates it rests on; the rule is in the file); `normalised_names` (the printed tokens re-ordered as Given Surname with stray marks stripped at their ends; **no letter is changed**, nothing is completed or repaired; empty where the head is not regular); `head_kinds`; `errata`. An empty `ocr_uncertain` does not say a line is clean; it says no pattern matched.

## 4. How the register is cut and what was checked

* **Section of a unit**: the leaves from the title leaf of the unit's Register to the leaf that carries the heading SUMMARY (asserted in the parser against the text: the title is found on the first leaf, the heading on the last). The register of an organization is one alphabetical list of every man who served in it, with the company printed inside the entry, so there are no company headings.
* **An entry** begins at its head, "Surname, Given." followed by the first data token ("Co. A", "F. and S.", "Band", "Unas'd", "Non-Com. Staff"; for the battery "b.", "Substitute", "Drafted", "Age", "See"), or is a bare cross-reference line ("Surname. See Surname."). The head is found backwards from the data token: the leftmost capitalised word with a comma such that everything to the data token is a given name (capitalised words, initials, Jr./2d, "alias" and the name that follows it) and the head follows the end of the previous entry (a period; up to four marks of scan dirt are stepped over). Fallbacks, in this order, each marked in the flag file: a head without the final period; a period printed for the comma ("Connor.  John."); a surname alone ("Kiniston.  Co. K.  See Kenaston"); the comma lost ("Bond  Henry  B."); and, only where the data begins after four or more blanks (the typographic gap of this register), an irregular head back to the end of the previous entry. A head near "with", "identical" or a post-office address ("P. O. ad.,") is never taken. Place names are kept out by a list of state and place abbreviations.
* **Checks of the cut** (Measured): (1) every company token that the OCR set off by four or more blanks lies in a head: 0 left over in each unit (the typographic gap catches heads that the rules above missed); (2) the surname sequence of each register is alphabetical: the lines out of order are 23, 29, 12 and 2, almost all damaged first letters ("Ooyle" for Coyle, "Pife" for Fife); (3) the count of "age" items against entries (an entry with two "age" items holds another man): 5 such lines remain (section 6, item 3); (4) a scan of the remarks for a head-shaped run followed at once by entry data found 2 more of the same kind; (5) per-page line counts (section 2); (6) the earlier roll files as an independent second reading (section 5); (7) the Register of Commissioned Officers as a third (below).
* **Register of Commissioned Officers** (volume 2, printed pp. 1225-1249): it re-lists the officers by rank. Every register line was compared with the roll rows of its unit by surname (difflib ratio at least 0.8) and first given name: 2nd Infantry 202 lines, 21 not matched; 5th Infantry 175 lines, 15; 12th Infantry 137 lines, 11; battery 11 lines, 0. The unmatched lines are OCR fragments ("ON, Sylvands.", "AM, John AV.") or two printings damaged differently; Le Gro, Parr (for Farr), Wiggin, Marshall and Bean were found in the roll by hand. So no officer is missing from the unit files; what the register of officers adds, the rank heading and the "Not mustered" mark, is not carried (EG-ROS-NH-01).
* **Parser assertion** (Measured): for every line, before writing, the first name token and the first remark token stand at the offsets the row records; `r2lib.verify_rows` then runs V1 (all name tokens, in order, at the offset), V2 (all remark tokens in order from the offset; a step over page furniture of at most 200 characters is allowed) and V3 (the offset lies in a leaf whose printed page is the row's page). A failure aborts the run: no file is written. The Internet Archive page numbers give a printed page for every leaf of the four registers (0 lines without a page).
* **After writing**: `python3 -I pipeline2/verify_unit.py --all --root docs/battle-theatre/rosters/data2/union/nh`: 4 files, **0 V1, 0 V3, 0 problems**, ledger sha256 matched; the files read back to the rows written; the bytes of a rerun are identical (the file sha256 are in `rosters/sources/union-nh.json`).
* **Alignment to the source**: no word boxes are used (layout `flow`); the offsets are character offsets in the decoded search text of the item, so a row can be found with one slice.
* **Not checked by machine**: that a remark belongs to the name before it on the page, and that an entry begins where the cut says in the 154 lines marked H. Both need the page images (EG-ROS-NH-02).
