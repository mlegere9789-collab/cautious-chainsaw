# Ask the Historian: the Official Records corpus (Series I, volume 27)

What this is: the text of the *War of the Rebellion: Official Records*, Series I, volume 27 (the Gettysburg Campaign), parts 1, 2 and 3, cut into 8,414 citable passages, linked to the site's units and people, and wired into the engine so that questions about what an officer **reported**, **wrote** or **said** (and at what **time** he said it) are answered with his own words and a page citation.
Status: built and tested, **not shipped**. The candidate page still reads `historian-src/dist/hx`, which has no corpus; the corpus KB is built next to it (`historian-src/dist/hx-or27`, git-ignored). Whether to ship it is a decision for the owner (section 9 lists what the decision costs).

Everything below was measured on the committed tables and the build of 2026-10-04 (the second build: section 3b says what it changed and why, with the figures of the first); the numbers are reproduced by `tests/historian/or27.test.mjs`, `tests/historian/or27-rebuild.test.mjs` and `tools/historian/measure-corpus.mjs`. Where something was **not** checked, it is said.

- Confederate coverage is uneven, which is the volume's, not the corpus's: no report by Pickett, Hood or McLaws is in it. The engine says so when asked (sections 6 and 8).

## 3. How it is built

```
node tools/historian/ingest/corpus.mjs build                                  # fetch (or read the cache), normalise, segment, chunk, page-map, link, claims; writes historian-src/raw/or27 and historian-src/corpus/or27
node tools/historian/ingest/time-evidence.mjs                                 # historian-src/corpus/time_evidence.json (proposals; section 5)
node tools/historian/build.mjs --corpus or27 --out historian-src/dist/hx-or27 # the KB with the corpus tiers; V01-V09 validators
node tools/historian/gen-sources.mjs                                          # docs/historian/sources.yaml from the manifest
node tools/historian/golden-or27.mjs --set A|H                                # the OR golden sets
node tools/historian/hand30.mjs                                               # the 30 hand questions (section 7)
node tools/historian/measure-corpus.mjs --out historian-src/corpus/or27/measurements.json
node --test tests/historian/*.test.mjs                                        # the tests that need the corpus build it into a temp directory, or SKIP LOUDLY with these commands
```

Stages (details of the normaliser and segmenter are in `PIPELINE.md` section 2):

1. **Normalise**: running heads, signature marks and page folios read by what they say; footnotes, the compilers' notes and the fragments of a head the OCR broke across lines taken out (and listed); de-hyphenation (a compound the volume itself prints with a hyphen keeps it, by the volume's own vote); a conservative dictionary repair; every edit logged. **The page of every passage is not read from the heads at all**: a private-use character per scan leaf rides through normalisation, and the page is the Archive's folio of the leaf the text stands on (`ingest/leaves.mjs`).
2. **Segment**: Parts 1 and 2 by report number and contents list; Part 3 by a grammar of 1863 headers (date, place, clock, received time, addressee, signature, indorsement), section 3b.
3. **Chunk** into 150 to 250 word passages with sentence offsets (`sb`), never across documents; the id is `<doc>#<sha1 of the text, 8 hex>`, so any change of text changes the id.
4. **Page map**: every passage carries `pg = [[offset, printed page, scan leaf, reader index], ...]`.
5. **Link** to the site's registry (the T1 the KB is built from), **extract clock claims** (with the day each is said about, a register read in order through the document), **build the tiers** (postings, entity postings, text shards, a file list).

**The dispatch grammar** (`ingest/or27-dispatch.mjs`): a dateline is a line that holds a date in the spellings of 1863 and of the OCR (month names within an edit or two, `1363`, `I860`, `18-63`, a bracketed part, a day the scan could not read), with its place, its header lines ("Headquarters ...", "War Department,"), its tags ("[Circular.", "Confidential."), its order number, its clock (even when the "m." was lost), its "(Received ...)" time and sometimes the addressee on the same line; a date inside a sentence is no dateline. The lines above it belong to the dispatch (the place line, the addressee the OCR set above the date), down to the signature of the dispatch before. A signature is a name and a title (the name on one line and the title under it, or the title after a comma, or an initial pair the OCR spelled with a zero); a name alone counts only where nothing but a heading follows; a signature the OCR damaged past that is read weakly, from the capitals, and says so. An indorsement marker keeps the indorsement with its dispatch, a reply printed with no heading (a signature, a text, another signature) is cut as a dispatch of its own with a null date and addressee, a forwarded message with no signature of its own stays in its cover, and the closing of a cover that the OCR set after the inclosure goes back to it. The place lines are told from text by a gazetteer of the volume's own datelines.

**How it was measured, and what the measure cannot say.** One reader (the builder) read each sample in full against its text and wrote down, for every dispatch it holds, its first words, date, clock, place, addressee and signer (`tests/historian/fixtures/or27-dispatch-*.json`, with the policy: an indorsement under its dispatch stays with it, one with a dateline of its own is a dispatch, a forwarded message with no signature of its own stays in its cover). The 100-document sample was drawn from the first build and the segmenter was tuned on it: **its 112 of 112 is a measure of the fixes, not of generalisation**. Three further samples were drawn from the corpus the build then produced, **each read and labelled before the segmenter was scored on it**, and the honest figure is the first score of each: **83.0% (50 documents), 87.8% (40), 87.8% (40)**. After each draw the classes it exposed were fixed in the grammar (the first: a clock whose "m." the OCR lost, a zero for the O of "0. 0. HOWARD", an address block whose unit line is spelled out and ends in debris after the colon, "TJpperville"; the second: an address printed above its place line, a title line carried to the next dispatch, a stray letter after the day; the third: a stray "#" in a signature's title and a given name in brackets, "[DAVID M.] HARKEY"), and the figures after the fixes (52 of 52, 40 of 41, 39 of 41) are no longer unseen. The grammar as it stands has therefore not been scored on a draw it had not influenced; the expectation, from three draws, is 85 to 90% of documents cut and read right in every field at first meeting, with each further round finding a few more classes. Three label slips of mine (an OCR garble copied into a label, a clock label, one forwarded message the policy keeps in its cover) were corrected after a score and are marked in the fixtures. What the samples leave open: **an unsigned message with an address and no heading printed under another dispatch** ("Major-General Brooks: Beverly reports all quiet, raid is played out.") is not cut from it (the final sample's two misses); a clock whose "p. m." the OCR dropped ("3.20.") is read as no clock. **Because every sample is drawn from the documents a build produced, it measures how well those documents are cut and read, not how many true dispatches the build lost or swallowed** (a dispatch cut to nothing is not in the draw); the volume has no independent count to check that against, and none was made. A second reader would be better.

## 4. Citations: quote spans, pages, scan leaves

- **Verbatim quote spans.** An engine quote is `{ passage, s, e, text, h, page, scan, leaf }`: `text` is `passage.text.slice(s, e)` (whitespace-normalised), `h` is the FNV-1a hash of that text. Invariants checked on every quote of every golden answer (`tests/historian/lib/or27-golden.mjs`): **Q1** the words are the passage's words at `[s, e)` and the hash is theirs; **Q2** the printed page is the one the passage's page list gives for the span, inside the document's own page range; **Q3** the scan link is the Archive reader of the leaf that list gives, on the right volume. No quote of the 106 golden answers broke Q1 to Q3.
- **Verbatim at build** (validator V03): every one of the 8,414 passages is a slice of the normalised OCR of its own report (paragraph by paragraph, in order); **8,414 checked, 0 failed**. The text is the OCR as read, with the 1863 spelling and the OCR's damage; it is not corrected beyond the logged normalisation, and a quote shows it as it is.
- **Printed page.** All 8,414 passages have one (10,275 page entries). **The page is the Archive's folio of the scan leaf the text stands on** (`ingest/leaves.mjs`): the Archive's own per-leaf text says which words are on which leaf, its `page_numbers.json` says which folio the leaf is, and where it is silent the folio is read from the leaf's own head and chained from its neighbours; a leaf whose folio cannot be had is left without one and counted (`run-report.leaves` in the manifest: 3,582 leaves, 3,541 with text, 3,516 folios read, 3 inferred). The first build read the page from the running heads of the raw OCR, which the OCR damaged in about one in fifty places.
- **Independent check** (`tests/historian/lib/leaf-check.mjs`, tests `RB-PG-1` and `RB-PG-2`). It reads **no page list, no folio and no leaf number from the corpus**, only the text of each passage and the offset of each sentence start; it finds the sentence in the Archive's per-leaf text by looking its letters up (the first 40 letters and digits, once in the whole volume), accepts a leaf's folio only where the leaf's own running head and the Archive's reading agree (or a chain of such leaves runs through it), and compares. Result over every sentence start of every passage: **66,353 sentence starts, 61,851 located and checked, 7 wrong (0.011%)**, in 5 of 8,357 passages (Part 1 5 of 21,365, Part 2 2 of 16,792, Part 3 0 of 23,694); the wrong ones are 1 to 4 pages off. The first build: 1,236 of 62,628 (1.97%). The test fails above 0.1% and was shown to be able to fail (`RB-PG-2` seeds one page too high everywhere, and a one-in-ten fault, and finds both). **Residual, not resolved:** 4,131 sentence starts (6.2%) are not found in the Archive's text once (a word the normaliser changed, a phrase the volume prints twice, a table), 43 are set aside as printed twice, 328 stand on a leaf whose folio could not be read twice: **those 4,502 are not checked by it**, and the 0.011% says nothing of them. The verifier's own 24 hand-checked sentence starts are all cited on the page the scan shows (`VI-PG-1`).
- **Scan leaf and reader link.** The leaf is the leaf the text stands on; the reader index subtracts the leaves the Archive excluded from the access formats (`scandata.xml`). The opening page of Part 3 (the first two dispatches, June 3 and June 1) prints no running head and the Archive could not read its folio; the OCR shows it printed as "(3)" at the page's foot, and the build takes it as page 3 (leaf 9) on that evidence. *Not verified:* **no scan link was opened in a browser** (the build was not allowed to launch one), so the leaf-to-reader arithmetic is derived from the files, not checked against the live reader; the printed page is the citation, the link is a convenience.

## 5. Entity links, clock claims, time proposals

### Entity links

Every passage is linked to the site's units and people with the engine's own linker (`ingest/link.mjs` over `core/linker.js`): tier **A** (the unit grammar names the regiment), **B** (a formation, battery or person alias resolved by side, rank class and given names) and **C** (a bare surname or an uncertain match). **Only A and B are emitted** as passage entities; C is kept in the raw mention log for measurement and never reaches the KB. Counts: A 6,992, B 6,884, C 6,183 (not emitted); 5,675 passages carry an entity. Links are kind-aware: a unit named in a sentence about a death is not the unit that was in the field, a person is dated (a man who died in the spring is not the signer of a July dispatch), and an arm of the service ("the First United States Sharpshooters" against "the first United States quartermaster's post") is told by the word that follows it.
Authors: of 573 reports by a person, 270 are linked to a person of the registry and 300 to a unit (157 to neither: the registry holds the 706 officers of the battle, not every captain who reported). Senders: of 3,177 dispatches with text, 3,034 carry exactly one signature and **579 are linked** to a person (the signature, its title line, the side, the rank class and the date must agree; a text with two signatures has no single sender). The first build linked 463 of 2,938. **The links of a sender outside the registry are not links**: the engine reaches such a sender by the surname the corpus itself marks (section 6), without inventing an entity.

**Hand-checked precision.** One reader (the builder) read each link in its sentence, against the contents-list title for authors, and against the signature and its title line for senders, and labelled it correct, wrong or uncertain (uncertain counts as wrong). The sample was drawn with a seeded generator from the links the build emitted: `tests/historian/fixtures/or27-links-checked.json` (items, sentences, labels and reasons; the text is public domain). Draw S1 was made on the build before the first pass's fixes, S2 after the first fixes. **The 105 sender items point at documents by id; the re-segmentation changed the ids of 18 of them, and each was moved to the document of the rebuilt table with the same date, signature surname and an addressee that shares a word (the fixture records the old id); none of the 305 labels was changed.**

### Clock claims

`ingest/times.mjs` (a grammar of 1863 clock expressions and of the day a sentence names; the engine's `core/clock.js` is the question-side reader) and `ingest/anchors.mjs` (the claim in its passage and sentence) read every clock expression in the corpus: **5,572 claims** (5,307 with a minute; 265 with an ambiguous meridiem that are kept apart; clock times 4,116, "o'clock" 596, sunrise and sunset 459, noon 275, midnight 79, ranges 44, spelled-out 3). Evidence kinds: report text 3,522, dispatch header 1,387 (the sender's own clock as he sent it, the best clock evidence in the volume), dispatch body 663. Each claim carries its sentence offsets, its day and the basis of that day ("same" sentence 1,680, "dateline" 1,387, "carried" from an earlier sentence of the document 1,931, "rel" for "the next morning" 170, "scope-other" for the June actions 351, "later" 3, "none" 50), the author's rank class, unit, the date written and the lag, and the printed page and scan leaf **of the claim's own page** (not of the report's first page). A claim in a report is testimony written days to months after the fight and is labelled so ("somewhere near 4 p. m.").
**The day of a claim is a register read in order through the document**, and it was wrong in ways that the day-aware answers made visible: "On the night of June 30 ... on the morning of the 1st" read June 1; "On the 28th" in a narrative already at July 1 read July 28; "the 1st instant" in the second of two reports printed under one number took the month of the first; "opened the battle at 4 a. m. on the following morning" kept the day before. Each is fixed (`RB-DY`); the passages carry the day their narrative stands on when they open (`ds`), so that a question about a day can tell a passage of that day from a passage of another. **Not measured:** the share of the 1,931 carried days that are right; the 36 hand-checked time proposals below are the only reading of them.

### Time proposals (`historian-src/corpus/time_evidence.json`)

For each of the 137 chronicle events of the site's time model, claims from the corpus that may bear on its minute are attached in tiers: **A** (the unit itself, same kind of event), **B** (a sentence naming the unit), **F** (a part of the unit, the same kind of thing, within 60 minutes of the site's own minute), **C** (weak). Counts: A 1, B 5, F 72, C 453 over 99 events; 38 events have no claim; 5 events have a report found. (The hand check was drawn from a table of A 1, B 5, F 68, C 434; the corrected day register moved 4 claims into F and 19 into C, and every one of the 36 checked claims is still proposed, in its tier.) **These are proposals**: the file's `status` says nothing in it is shown to a reader or applied to the time model; the time workstream decides.
*Hand check (36 attached claims, seeded draw: 6 of tiers A and B, 30 of tier F; `fixtures/or27-time-evidence-checked.json`):* a claim is "about the event" when it states the time of that event (same day, same phase of the fighting). **Tier F: 17 of 30 (57%; 95% interval 39 to 73)**; tiers A and B: 3 of 6. ## 6. Retrieval in the engine

The corpus is a set of **lazy tiers** the manifest only describes: `manifest.corpora.or27` is a small descriptor (base number 1530, count 8,414, a content-hashed file list `f/or27/files.<hash>.json` of 85,600 bytes). Opening the KB reads no corpus file. `core/kb.js` holds a stub; `core/corpus.js` and `core/reports.js` (outside the page's static module graph) are imported by the first request that reaches into the corpus, which fetches the file list once. Corpus retrieval is **opt-in** per call (`scorePassages(terms, true)`, `entityPassages(ids, true)`), and only the report-seeking paths opt in, so a site question never touches a corpus file.

What it answers (`core/reports.js`; nothing is generated, the lead is a template over the document's metadata and every other sentence is a verbatim quote):

- "what did Chamberlain report about Little Round Top" - the report he wrote (author link, role 2), the passages closest to the topic, with author, rank, unit, date written and lag ("official report, written July 6, 1863, 3 days after the fighting ended"), volume and page. **A topic of three words or more needs two of them in the window quoted** ("the cannonade and Cemetery Hill"), chosen among the windows that have them; **the word asked for is preferred to its synonym** (an "assault" window over an "attack" window); **a word the print spells with a hyphen is asked for as one** ("Wheatfield" for "wheat-field").
- "what time did Longstreet say his assault began" - the sentences of his report that give a clock time and bear on the topic, as he wrote them. **With a day in the question ("on July 3") a quoted window holds clocks of that day and of no other; when the author gives none for it, the answer says so and names the days he does give** ("gives no clock time for July 3 in the volume that I could find. The times he does give are for June 22, June 24, ..."), and a window whose clock is of another day is never quoted in its place.
- "what did Meade telegraph on July 4", "what did Lee write to Imboden", "what did Pleasonton write to Williams on June 3", "what did Pleasonton write on June 20" - dispatches only, restricted before ranking by pseudo-entity postings (`K:dispatch`, `K:report`, `D:<date>`, `A:<addressee stem>`, `F:<sender stem>`; the stems are the question side's `stem()` of the name), labelled "written at the time". **The engine never says no dispatch exists.** It says what it did not find and what it searched: "I did not find a dispatch or letter from Maj. Gen. Alfred Pleasonton to Williams dated June 4, 1863 ... What I searched: the 106 passages of dispatches, letters and orders in Part 3 whose printed signature reads “Pleasonton” (97 of them tied to Maj. Gen. Alfred Pleasonton himself). ... The volume prints 13 passages of dispatches dated June 4, 1 of them with no signature I could read. A signature the scan garbled is not among those, so this does not show that none was written." **When a date and an addressee are asked and the sender wrote that day, but not to him, the others are shown and the answer says so** ("None of ... Pleasonton's dispatches of June 9 is addressed to “Meade”; the 6 he wrote that day went to Major-General Stahel and Brig. Gen. S. Williams and Major-General Hooker."). A "write" with a date is a question of the dispatches of that day.
- "what did the reports say about the Railroad Cut", "what did Confederate reports say about the Peach Orchard" - one passage per report, side filtered.
- When the corpus holds no report by the person ("what did Pickett report"): the answer says so, and says what he did write, instead of quoting someone else as if it were him.
- **An author the site's registry does not hold** ("what time did Ruger say fire was opened", "what did Dobke report", "what did Halleck write to Hooker", "what did Kelley write to Averell"): the first build took such a question for a site question and answered with an unrelated site timeline entry. The corpus marks every sender itself (`F:<surname stem>` on the passages of a report's author, as on a dispatch's signer, built from the contents list's author and the signature), so the capitalised words of the question that name no site entity are asked of the corpus, the first that is a sender is the author, the label is the corpus's own author line (the surname alone when several men of it wrote), and the same restrictions apply (a kind, a day, a clock of a day, an addressee). A name the engine's typo repair would turn into a registry name ("Kelley" to "Kelly") is asked of the corpus as written first and read as the registry's name only when the corpus knows no sender of it. A name no sender of the volume bears is not answered from the corpus at all.
- Questions naming another source ("what did Meade tell the Joint Committee", "newspapers report the outcome") are not about a report of the volume and fall through to the site's own path.

**Additive.** Over the 274 questions of the three site batteries and the 150 questions of the golden core (411 distinct), the answer is identical with and without the corpus except for **4**, each a question that asks for what someone wrote or said in his own report ("What did Colonel Oates say about Chamberlain's charge in his own words?", "What did Longstreet say in his own report about when the July 2 attack began?", "What did Alexander write to Pickett before the charge?", "What does Chamberlain's official report say about the bayonet charge?"), where the site had said "the site's records do not quote anyone saying that" and now the report is quoted. Site questions fetch the same files with and without the corpus (test OR-11, and 7 site questions in `measurements.json`).

## 9. Limits, what was not checked, what needs a person

- **Authors outside the registry are now found by their surname, not by an entity.** The registry holds the 706 officers of the battle; 303 of the 573 reports by a person have no person link, and 2,599 of the 3,178 dispatches have no linked sender. A question naming one of them is answered from the corpus (section 6) with the corpus's own author line and no chip or card for him; two men of one surname are not told apart (the answer then names the surname only). A dispatch whose signature the scan garbled (73 have none readable) is not found by its sender, and the answer to a question about a day says so.
- **Page citations are checked for 93% of the sentence starts, not all** (section 4: 4,502 sentence starts the independent check cannot place or cannot confirm); the 0.011% disagreement is over those it can. Seven disagreements are known and listed by the check (`node tests/historian/lib/leaf-check.mjs`).
- **Dispatch segmentation is measured on four samples of 100, 50, 40 and 40 documents, the last three read before they were scored** (section 3b; first scores 83.0%, 87.8%, 87.8%), drawn from the documents a build produced; it cannot count the dispatches a build lost. Known residual classes: a clock whose "p. m." the OCR dropped is read as no clock; 57 dispatches have no date the scan lets me read (a dateless reply has none by design); a signature the OCR garbled past recognition is read weakly from its capitals (the signer is then what the capitals say, not a repaired name); the signer link is made for 579 of 3,177.
- **Footnotes are taken out** (537, listed in `notes-removed.jsonl`): a footnote at the foot of a page is not the author's sentence and was being spliced into one, but **a few of the 537 are an author's own footnote** (for example a note of Gen. Sam. Jones's at Part 2, p. 957), and their words are not in the corpus. Two head fragments remain in passages the stripper cannot see (a head run into the first line of a leaf), and three "ETC." in capitals are headings of the text (a newspaper title, "PRISONERS, ETC.").
- The OCR is quoted as read: "Beport of", "i ms was replied to", "undei". A quote can carry OCR damage (3 passages carry a word the site's copy rules would flag, V06; they are verbatim text).
- Tables are not read, so numbers in returns (strength, casualties by regiment) are not in the corpus.
- **The day of a clock claim** is a register, not a reading: the carried days (1,931 of 5,572 claims) were never hand-checked as a class; day-aware answers decline rather than quote a claim whose day is another, and a claim whose day is wrong in the table is wrong in the answer.
- **The 30 hand questions are a reconstruction** (section 7), and the golden set H, once the second build's fixes were made for it, is no longer an unseen measure.
- **Rights sign-off** (section 1) and the **decision to ship** are the owner's. Shipping means building `--corpus or27` into the served `hx` (about 15.7 MB raw, 4.8 MB brotli across 1,212 files, 3.5 MB of it text), 1,068 bytes more at first paint, and the lazy modules.
- **Not verified**: scan links against the live reader (no browser was launched; printed pages are the citation); the leaf of Part 3 page 3 (by folio count, with the printed "(3)" as the only direct evidence); HathiTrust rights records (section 1); the per-question latency on a phone (modelled); links outside the 200 + 105 read (precision of the other 13,000 is inferred from the sample, with the interval above); that the corpus helps the time model (proposals are not applied); the verifier's own list of 30 questions (not recovered).
- One reader labelled every sample, the builder; labels are judgements. A second reader would be better.
