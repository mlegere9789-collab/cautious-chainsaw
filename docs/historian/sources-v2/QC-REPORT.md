## 1. The short answer

**Does the list help Ask the Historian? Yes, but a little, and not in the way "1,714 writings" suggests.**

- **1,714 entries are not 1,714 writings.** The list itself says 1,057 of them are page-level pointers into one Official Records volume. Another 543 are Official Records reports. Only 114 entries point at anything else, and 12 of those are indexes, guides or duplicates, leaving **102 distinct other works** (76 individual writings, 13 modern scholarship pieces, 13 whole volumes counted once).
- **93.3% of the list is the Official Records, which we already ingest.** The historian's OCR index holds 588 OR reports and 2,945 Part 3 dispatches from the Internet Archive scans. The list names 543 of those reports (92.3%) and 1,057 of Part 3's pages. These entries add **no new text**. What they add is an independent index and scan links, and they gave us a real check of our own ingestion (section 5).
- **Only 12 of the 114 other entries have been reviewed by two independent verifiers.** Result: 7 approved for the quote corpus (5 at tier A1, 2 at tier A2), 2 approved for link-out only, 3 sent to a human because the verifiers disagreed. **98 have not been reviewed by anyone and are not approved.** Nothing may be ingested from them yet.
- **Ready to ingest today: 4 works** (entry 597, entry 596, entry 594, entry 592). One more waits for a human transcription (entry 590) and 2 wait for a rights review (entries 586, 591). That is a handful of works: on the order of a hundred passages by a rough estimate from the page ranges (the reviewers counted paragraphs only for the 597 letter, 15 to 23). It is not a thousand.
- **The honest summary: the list is a good starting index and a useful audit, not a finished, verified source bank.** It was built by another AI session whose own preamble admits it could not reach the 4,000 entries requested; our checks found the structure sound where we could test it and the descriptions unreliable where they say anything specific.

## 5. Findings that apply across the list

1. **Most of the list is blocked to automated checking.** 1,940 of 2,046 links (94.8%) go to ehistory.osu.edu (1,390) or HathiTrust (550), which return HTTP 403 to automated clients. We did not circumvent that. For those entries the check is structural: report number, author, side and first page against the Internet Archive OCR index. The links themselves are unconfirmed; a human should open a sample (section 7).
2. **The HathiTrust page numbers look computed, not looked up.** In all 332 Part I entries the HathiTrust `seq` equals the printed page plus 20; in all 211 Part II and III entries it equals the printed page plus 2, with no exception. A real scan with plates or tipped-in maps normally breaks a constant offset, and the document says every link was taken from a retrieved page. The links may still be right, but nobody has confirmed even one.
3. **The Official Records entries are sound where we could test them.** 504 of 543 page ranges equal the OCR index exactly, 32 differ by one page at a boundary, 4 differ only at the end page, and **2 (entries 10 and 134) name the wrong first page** (entry 10's range starts at p. 155 where the return begins on p. 168; the registry carries the OCR page). All 542 report numbers and author surnames agree. Entry 543's end page (776) also looks wrong: the OCR index ends the Roll of Honor at p. 784 and its text is far longer than three pages.
4. **The OR descriptions are one template.** 527 of 543 say the report was "written by the officer in command, describing the unit's marches, positions, fighting and losses in the Gettysburg Campaign". In the OCR index 75 of the 542 reports are not about the campaign proper (for example Winchester and Martinsburg in June), 10 are composites of several papers, and 16 are returns, itineraries, orders of battle or rosters. They were written a median of 22 days after the battle (10th percentile 1, 90th 49, longest 408 days), so "contemporaneous" in the document means weeks to months after the fighting, not same-day.
5. **Part XIII is every page, not a selection.** Pages 3 to 1059, no gaps. 119 of them have no dispatch beginning on the page (continuations). The 60 pages labelled "Union / Confederate (section transition)" are not transitions: pages 814 to 857 (44) hold only Union dispatches and pages 858 to 873 (16) only Confederate ones (the first Confederate dispatch is on p. 858); the registry carries the corrected side. The list stops at p. 1059 but 61 dispatches begin on 24 later pages (the end of the volume, which the list does not cover).
6. **Dead links cluster by host.** All 13 of 13 Dickinson Archives links are dead (404): the Civil War Resources section disappeared in a site migration. Of the 102 unreviewed other-works links, 11 are dead and 4 redirect to a generic landing page: a dead-link rate of 14.7%. Of the 12 reviewed entries, 3 had a dead listed link. The Wayback Machine holds a capture whose page title matches the entry for 11 of them, and 3 more captures whose titles are missing or unclear (candidate replacements in the registry; a reviewer must read them before they count).
7. **The Perseus links cannot be used by a program.** www.perseus.tufts.edu/robots.txt says `Disallow: /`; 12 entries (all the SHSP articles at Perseus) point there. We did not fetch them. The Historian should take SHSP from the Internet Archive scans its plan already lists (SRC-08), not from Perseus.
8. **"Free full text" is often not true.** Among the 12 reviewed: 3 are image-only handwriting (589, 590 and the 587 manuscript), so the only text would be our own transcription; 593 and 586 sit on hosts that keep rights in their transcription; 587 and 588 carry an "all rights reserved" notice; 589's repository forbids quotation without permission. Among the unreviewed: 3 links open a modern edition or reprint rather than the period text.
9. **Most of the non-OR list is about the invasion, not the battle.** By the entries' own descriptions only 25 of 114 are about the fighting; 69 are about the invasion and occupation (Carlisle, Chambersburg, York), 7 come after the battle and 13 are volumes or indexes. Of the 12 reviewed, 9 describe no fighting at Gettysburg at all.
10. **"Peer reviewed" is a small part.** The list's scholarship items number 13 (11 in Part VII, 2 in Part X). Its own description says one of them, Gettysburg Magazine, is not formally peer reviewed. Free to read is not free to reuse: none was checked for a licence, so they are link-out or facts only. The six journals.psu.edu links could not be reached (robots.txt unreadable; the earlier probe timed out).
11. **Every reviewed entry needed a correction.** Each reviewer listed 5 to 7 issues per entry: ambiguous titles (586 is not Lt. Gen. Leonidas Polk), missing or wrong dates (590's letter is 10 July 1863, 588's order is 21 June not "June 27-30"), Google Books page anchors two pages off (592, 594), and the document-sharing and blog hosts standing in for the originals. The registry stores a corrected title for each; none was approved as listed.

3. *A to-do list (medium value).* 98 unreviewed entries are ordered in batches of 12 (`qc/review-queue-next.json`), the most useful first.
4. *What to avoid (useful).* Dead links, robots-disallowed hosts, modern editions and rights traps are now recorded per entry instead of being discovered during ingestion.
5. *Not a help:* the bulk of the numbers. Page pointers and OR crosswalk entries cannot raise answer quality; the Historian needs the text, and that text is already in.

## 8. What this run did not do

- **Two expected inputs do not exist.** `qc/auto.json` and `qc/review-queues.json` were named as inputs for this stage but were never written to the repository (confirmed absent on disk). The structural check and the link probe in this directory replace the automated part; the builder will read them if they appear.
- **98 other-works entries have no reviewer.** Reviewer outputs exist for 12 entries (586-597) only. Nothing was approved on automated checks alone, by design.
- **No reviewer output was re-run.** Their evidence is stored verbatim (`qc/review-pass1.json`, `qc/review-pass2.json`). Where cheap, this builder re-fetched the metadata a reviewer cited (Internet Archive item records, the HathiTrust catalog API) and attached it; those fetches agreed with the reviewers.
- **No circumvention.** eHistory and HathiTrust were not fetched; robots.txt was obeyed (Perseus and one blog host forbid all automated access); at most one request per second per host with an honest user agent; everything cached under `tmp/sources-v2/cache/`.

## 9. How to finish the QC

1. Run two independent reviewers over `qc/review-queue-next.json` batch by batch (9 batches of 12). Save their outputs as new arrays in `qc/review-pass1.json` and `qc/review-pass2.json` and add a normalisation block per entry to `qc/review-norm.json` (the test checks that every basis phrase occurs in the reviewer's text).
2. `node tools/sources-qc/build-registry.mjs` re-applies the rules, regenerates the registry, the ingest queue and this report.
3. `node --test "tests/sources-qc/*.test.mjs"` fails if any entry lacks a status or evidence, if anything is approved without two agreeing verifiers or a structural check, or if an A1 lacks a rights basis.
4. Ingest only what is in `ingest-queue.json` under `ready`, through the adapter named there, after the verbatim check against the scan.
