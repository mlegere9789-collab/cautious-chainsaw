## 1. The short answer

**How much does this list help Ask the Historian? A real but modest amount, and almost none of it comes from the size of the list.**

- **1,714 entries are not 1,714 writings.** 1,057 are page-level pointers into one Official Records volume and 543 are Official Records reports: **93.3% of the list is the Official Records, which the Historian already ingests** (8,405 passages from the Internet Archive scans). Those entries add **no new text**. What they add is an independent index and a check of our own ingestion (section 8).
- **114 entries point at anything else; 4 are duplicates, leaving 110 distinct entries and 102 counted works.** Every one of them has now been read by **two to four independent reviewers** (86 entries by 2, 24 entries by 4). The merge rule is unanimity: an entry is approved only if every reviewer agrees.
- **Result: 56 approved for the quote corpus** (40 at tier A1, 16 at tier A2 that wait for a rights review), **33 approved for link-out only**, **16 sent to a human** because the reviewers disagreed or asked for one, **5 rejected** by every reviewer.
- **Genuinely new text.** Of the 56 approved works, 4 (entries 544, 546, 547, 652) are printings of Official Records text the Historian already holds and 3 (551, 584, 649) overlap in part, leaving **52 works that carry text the Official Records corpus does not hold** (19 confederate, 17 union, 12 civilian, 2 mixed or neutral, 2 reviewers disagree). 34 of those 52 are in none of the Historian's two source catalogues; the other 18 are sources those catalogues already name (planned or skipped) and are now verified.
- **Ready to ingest today: 35 works (37 entries)**: tier A1, text a program can read, every reviewer in agreement. 15 more works (16 entries) are approved but wait for a rights review (tier A2) and 6 are image-only and wait for a human transcription (3 of them also need the rights review). The ordered queue is in section 6 and in `ingest-queue.json`.
- **What that is worth.** The Historian's corpus is officers' reports and dispatches. It is thin on private letters, diaries, newspapers and civilians, and on Confederate voices the Official Records do not print. The approved list is mostly that kind of material (23 Confederate, 17 Union, 12 civilian, 4 neutral or contested works), 32 of the 56 written in 1863 or the following months. But it is 56 entries, 48 of them single pieces (a letter, a diary, a newspaper item, an article or a chapter) and 8 whole volumes (551, 622, 628, 635, 653, 654, 655, 656), against a corpus of 588 reports and 3,178 dispatches. Only 30 of the 56 touch the fighting of 1 to 3 July (the reviewers' labels "battle" and "campaign and battle"); 23 are about the invasion and occupation. 24 are written years later (memoirs, regimental histories), with the partisanship that carries.
- **The honest summary:** the list is a good starting index, and now a reviewed one. It yields a source bank of 35 works (37 entries) that can be ingested today and 53 works (56 entries) once the rights and transcription holds are cleared. That will make a visible difference to questions about civilians, Carlisle, York, Chambersburg and the Confederate cavalry, and a small one to questions about the fighting itself. It does not change the bulk of the Historian's corpus, and its descriptions of the Official Records entries cannot be trusted (section 8).

## 4. Rights tiers

Tiers are decided by evidence, never by "it is free to read": A1 quotable public domain, A2 public domain needing review, B facts only, C link-out only. A1 needs the imprint year read from a title page or the archive's own metadata; a free link is not a licence. Where reviewers differ the more restrictive tier is recorded.

## 6. The ordered ingestion queue

Every SHSP item is taken from the University of Florida Internet Archive scans (archive-fulltext), which is what the reviewers read; no queue item has a perseus, ehistory or HathiTrust text source.

## 8. What the audits found about the Official Records entries and the page pointers

**Official Records entries (Parts I-III, 543).** Report number, author, side and first page agree with the independent OCR index for every report found; the structural check was re-run against the current historian corpus (below). They are approved as a **citation crosswalk**: identity and page range, never text.

1. **The listed description is untrusted, and this is quantified.** An auditor checked a systematic sample (every ninth entry: 61 entries) against the scan and the report text; 16 of them were checked by a second, independent auditor. Worst rating across auditors: 37 accurate, 23 partly inaccurate, 1 inaccurate: **39.3% not accurate (95% interval 28.1% to 51.9%)**. Scaled to the 543 entries that is about 214 with a materially wrong or misleading description (between 152 and 282). The rule: the template is systemically inaccurate when the lower bound of that interval exceeds 10%; it is 28.1%, so **481 unaudited descriptions are marked untrusted** and the 62 audited ones carry their own rating (partly inaccurate: 24, accurate by one reviewer: 28, accurate by two reviewers: 9, inaccurate: 1). Identity and page range are kept.
2. **One auditor under-counts.** On the 16 entries two auditors rated, they gave the same rating on 12 and the second was stricter on 4 (entries 244, 352, 496, 532); the first auditor alone would have found 18.8% of these not accurate, the two together 43.8%. The first auditor alone rated 41 accurate, 20 partly inaccurate and 1 inaccurate of the 62 entries he rated (the sample plus entry 134); the second auditors moved 4 entries from accurate to partly inaccurate. The registry uses the worst rating given, so the quoted share is, if anything, low.
3. **What is wrong with the template.** The audit found that 60 of its 62 entries carry the identical sentence ("written by the officer in command ... marches, positions, fighting and losses in the Gettysburg Campaign"), and that it fails for returns, summaries, supplements, papers by officers who were not at the battle, and several papers printed under one number. Across the whole OCR index (not only the sample), 83 of the 542 reports are not about the campaign proper, 101 are composites of several papers under one number, and 16 of the listed entries are returns, itineraries, orders of battle or rosters; the reports were written a median of 24 days after the battle (10th percentile 3, 90th 49, longest 408 days), so "contemporaneous" means weeks to months. Do not use the description for anything; use the report number, the author and the page range.
4. **First pages.** 2 entries have a first page the audit viewed on the scan and found wrong: entry 10 (the list says p. 155, the report begins on p. 168), entry 298 (the list says p. 842, the report begins on p. 843); the structural check had passed 298 inside its one-page tolerance. Both are sent to a human with the corrected page recorded. Two more are doubts from the auditor's OCR heading scan, not confirmed on the image: entry 251, entry 509 (a first page one too low and an end page two too low); they stay approved with a flag. Three OCR defects the audit noted (the printed year misread in entries 208 and 325, one lost number line in 397) are recorded on those entries.
5. **The structural check was re-run against the current historian corpus**, which the second historian build (2026-10-04) changed. It moved 33 of the 543 report statuses (flags fell from 2 to 1: entry 134's blank-plate start page is fixed in the historian index), 33 OCR page ranges and the per-page dispatch counts on 424 of the 1,057 pointers (the historian's second build re-cut the dispatches, so the per-page counts moved). `qc/structural-refresh.json` has every change. The auditors had read the stale file, which is why some of their registry findings are now resolved.
6. **Page links.** The HathiTrust `seq` values the list uses were copied from the Civil War Cycling index (an auditor compared ten of them), not computed, and a constant offset is normal for a scan with fixed front matter, so the earlier suspicion that they were computed is not supported; whether they open the right page is still unconfirmed. The Internet Archive's own folio file for Part 1 runs two leaves too high from about p. 452 (the registry's `scan.reader` is right, `scan.leaf` is not). The eHistory transcription is not verbatim (a footnote date, an omitted line, a typo), so eHistory is a link-out, never a quote source.

**Part XIII page pointers (1,057).** The audit read 47 pages (every 35th page, 31 of them, plus 16 the auditor chose for known problems) against the Archive OCR, and a second auditor re-read 12 of them.

1. **Template accuracy.** In the systematic sample 29 of 31 descriptions are accurate (6.5% not accurate; 95% interval 1.8% to 20.7%), so the pointer description is **not** systemically wrong. Among all 47 pages read (the targeted ones included) 37 are accurate, 9 partly inaccurate and 1 inaccurate (p. 673 is a plate of two pen sketches with no text). The sentence fails on the Addenda (pp. 841-857: documents of 1863 to 1864 and a letter of 1878, not "June 3 to August 3, 1863"), on pages that hold a table and no dispatch, and for one page-specific note placed a page too early (p. 445 should be 446). By the auditor's estimate from the header dates, about half the pages predate 1 July and about 7% fall on 1 to 3 July.
2. **Side labels.** The 60 pages the list labels "Union / Confederate (section transition)" are not transitions: the odd-page running head agrees with the structural check on all 536 odd pages the auditor read, Union through p. 857 and Confederate from p. 858. Pages 814 to 857 (44) are Union only and pages 858 to 873 (16) are Confederate only; the registry carries the corrected side on each.
3. **The list is not complete.** It stops at p. 1059; the volume's correspondence runs to **p. 1092**. The audit read **33 unlisted pages (pp. 1060-1092)**: the rest of the Army of Northern Virginia organization of 31 July, Confederate returns and dispatches of 1 to 5 August, a map plate (p. 1071), and an **Appendix** of documents "received too late for insertion" that begins on p. 1076 and is Union until p. 1090. **61 dispatches begin on 22 of those pages** in the current historian table (the earlier structural run counted 24 pages, because it read an older table; the auditor's hand count is about 60 on 22). The task brief names pages 1060-1091 and "24 pages"; the review items run to p. 1092, so both figures are kept. **These pages are notes about the list's incompleteness, not entries**: a first-pass auditor approved them as pointers and a second-pass auditor refused to approve entries that do not exist; the registry holds neither as an entry (`meta.list_incompleteness`).
4. **A finding against our own ingestion.** The audit found that the historian's dispatch table labels the 42 Union Appendix dispatches on pp. 1076-1090 "confederate", cites three pages one too low where a plate or a footer folio intervenes, and holds two table headings as dispatches. ## 10. Findings that apply across the list

1. **Most of the list is blocked to automated checking.** 1,940 of 2,046 links (94.8%) go to ehistory.osu.edu (1,390) or HathiTrust (550), which return HTTP 403 (a Cloudflare challenge, even for robots.txt) to automated clients. Nobody circumvented that. For those entries the check is structural and the audits used the Internet Archive scans instead. A human should open a sample (section 11).
2. **The Perseus links cannot be used by a program.** www.perseus.tufts.edu/robots.txt says `Disallow: /` (and names the Claude agents); 12 entries point there (all of Part IV). No reviewer fetched it. Every SHSP item was verified on the University of Florida Internet Archive scans of the original volumes ("Out of copyright" in the Archive's own metadata) and is queued with the archive-fulltext adapter. The listed Perseus anchors cannot be trusted either: an auditor found one whose page parameter does not match the article's first page.
3. **"Free full text" is often not the period text.** Several listed Google Books links open a 2001 or 2008 reprint or a modern book (entries 579, 606, 635); the reviewers found the original-imprint scans (Fremantle 1864, Hoke 1887, Wingate 1896, Early 1912) and took the text and the tier from those. Entry 606 is a modern book about the man, not his words, and every reviewer rejected it.
4. **Some entries are image-only or carry a host's own restriction.** 6 approved works are scans of manuscripts or clippings with no text layer and wait for a person to transcribe them and a second person to check the transcription; hosts add "all rights reserved" notices (Dickinson Archives), a repository rule against quoting unpublished manuscripts (entry 589) and non-commercial licences (the Valley of the Shadow pages), which is why 15 approved works are tier A2 and held.
5. **The list's descriptions understate or mislabel.** 65 of the 110 other works have an accurate description; 33 are partly inaccurate and 6 inaccurate, on the worst rating any reviewer gave (the rest could not be checked). Titles that name the wrong volume (549), "Vols. I & II" for a single volume (653), dates the letter does not have, and Google Books page anchors that land on the wrong page are the common faults. The registry stores a corrected title only for the 12 entries of the first run (586-597); for the others the reviewers' issues are in `verification.reviews`.
6. **Duplicates inside the list.** Four entries are merged (572, 573 and 657 are the HathiTrust volume links the Part I to III and XIII entries already use, proved by the catalogue API; 599 is the second page of 595's report). The reviewers add soft duplicates that stay as entries but are ingested once: 553 and 554 (one 1908 printing by two routes), 653 and 654 (one volume, two scans; entry 655 is Vol. III, not a copy) and 636 and 648 (one newspaper issue as a PDF and as a transcription). 7 approved works print Official Records text in whole or in part (section 1).
7. **The classification of the 114 other works** (writing, volume, index, scholarship; battle, campaign, post-battle) comes from the entries' titles and descriptions (`lib/classes.mjs`) and is labelled as the builder's judgement; the reviewers' own kind and relevance labels, which are computed here from their outputs, are the ones used for the coverage tables. Of the reviewed entries 18 are about the battle itself and 33 about the campaign and the battle.

## 12. What this run did not do, and could not verify

- **No page image was viewed by this builder.** What was seen on page images is what the reviewers say they saw (every OR audit entry's first page, p. 673, 858, 1071 and 1077 among them). This builder re-fetched only the Internet Archive metadata of the 54 items the reviewers named (all answered HTTP 200; the one dated after 1930 is twohundredyearsi00unse (1951, named for entry 619)) and re-ran the structural check offline. It did not re-run a reviewer.
- **Identity and extraction path are parsed from the reviewers' prose** (`lib/derive.mjs`), with every parsed token anchored in their words. For 4 approved entries (592, 594, 598, 604) the reviewers named different scans of one work and "same edition" rests on both stating the same imprint year; for 4 approved entries at least one reviewer named no path and it was read from the kind of source they named; 53 of 268 reviews yielded no adapter at all (11 on NEEDS-HUMAN-REVIEW entries, 40 on APPROVED-LINKOUT-ONLY entries, 2 on REJECTED entries; an approved entry needs one from every reviewer). The derivation reproduces all 12 of the first run's hand-normalised decisions (a test).
- **Two expected inputs do not exist.** `qc/auto.json` and `qc/review-queues.json` were named for an earlier stage and were never written (confirmed absent); nothing depends on them now.
- **Authorship and dates are the reviewers'.** No reviewer could see the Stanford copyright-renewal database (bot-challenged, not circumvented), so the renewal status of the one 1920s work (Storrick, entry 558) is unchecked; it went to a human.
- **No circumvention.** eHistory, HathiTrust and Perseus were not fetched; robots.txt was obeyed (Perseus and one blog host forbid all automated access; the reviewers read the Internet Archive capture of the blog page instead of the host, and said the owner may veto that); at most one request per second per host with an honest user agent.
- **Disclosed by a reviewer:** the first six Internet Archive metadata calls of one reviewer went out about 0.5 s apart (a short burst over the 1 req/s rule, from separate processes sharing no throttle); every later request was spaced 1.25 s or more.

## 13. How to finish the QC

1. A third independent reviewer, or the owner, settles the 16 other works in section 9 (`qc/review-queue-next.json`). Save the outputs unchanged under `qc/` and add the file to `lib/reviews.mjs` REVIEW_FILES.
2. `node tools/sources-qc/verify-ia.mjs` (polite, cached) confirms any new Internet Archive item a reviewer names; `node tools/sources-qc/build-registry.mjs` re-applies the rules and regenerates the registry, the ingest queue, the review queue and this report.
3. `node --test "tests/sources-qc/*.test.mjs"` fails if any entry lacks a status or evidence, if anything is approved without every reviewer agreeing (other works) or a passing structural check (OR and pointers), if an A1 or A2 lacks a rights basis, if a rights tier is not the most restrictive one the reviewers gave, if the queue holds anything but an approved, ready, A1 item or uses a forbidden adapter, or if the registry differs from a rebuild of its inputs.
4. Ingest only what is in `ingest-queue.json` under `ready`, through the adapter named there, after the check of every quote against the page image.
