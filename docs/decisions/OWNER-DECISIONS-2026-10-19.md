# Owner decisions, 2026-10-19

1. **Main-map background = a reconstruction of the field as it was in July 1863**, photoreal overhead look, built from public-domain USGS imagery and elevation with modern features removed and 1863 roads, structures, fences, woodlots, orchards and fields added from period maps and records; checked eight times; labelled as a reconstruction. (Google Earth imagery cannot be used: its terms forbid extraction and republication.) Workflow `gettysburg-1863-basemap`.
2. **The site uses only actual images of Gettysburg.** Every image audited; non-Gettysburg or unknown-provenance images replaced with real, properly licensed Gettysburg images or removed; og images regenerated from real ones.
3. **Part F is a separate page ("Battle Theatre"), not the main-page animation.** Rome: Total War style: the whole battle runs; visitors fly around with keyboard and/or mouse; every soldier individually animated; historically accurate uniforms, equipment, weapons and commanders (including Rufus Dawes); unit activity, orientation and position matching the record; film-grade animation including artillery fire and impact; every soldier present rendered (total equals the number present); every named soldier and commander; every event from the first shot to the Confederate withdrawal.
4. **Standards stated by the owner as non-negotiable:** 100% historical accuracy; studio-grade smooth, fully human-like movement; no exceptions.
5. Plan constraint (from me, recorded here so it is not lost): individual soldiers' positions and actions are not recorded anywhere, so at that level they are Modelled and labelled so; unit-level facts (formation, position, orientation, activity, times) come from the record; names are shown only where a source ties a named person to the action. See the staged plan in the working-session reply.

- Sound (Part F): use real, period-accurate sound effects for weapons: every individual weapon firing (each musket, rifle, carbine, revolver, cannon) shows accurate visuals and plays its accurate sound; the sheer number of overlapping shots creates realistic battle chaos.
