# Vegetation of July 1863: the build (requirement R20, gate Q21)

Makes `docs/battle-theatre/app/data/vegetation.json` and the register proposals `GOVERNANCE/horde/ws-world/proposed-register/ws-world-veg-G01..G15.md`. Notes for readers of the result: `docs/battle-theatre/app/docs/VEGETATION-NOTES.md`.

## Order

0. `python -I tools/bt/vegetation/gen_nps_corrections.py` and `python -I tools/bt/vegetation/gen_bachelder_claims.py` write their claim files (each quote is taken from a saved text or a correction row; a quote not found is reported).
1. `python -I tools/bt/vegetation/verify_claims.py` looks every quote of the claim files up again in the saved text of its source (Official Records, the National Park Service reports, the public-domain library, one saved page) and writes `claims_verified.json`. A generated claim whose quote is not found is dropped and listed; a hand-written claim whose quote is not found makes the run exit 1. Nothing is cited that this step did not find.
1b. `python -I tools/bt/vegetation/short_quotes.py` writes `claim_extracts.json`: the SHORT quote of every claim (at most 14 words, the plan's rule and gates T-Q02-1 and T-Q21-1), chosen from the claim's passage, read by hand against the claim (`short_quotes_curated.json`: id -> the words, with a reason where it is not obvious; the rule of the script only proposes), copied letter for letter from the saved text, placed in it (character offsets inside the passage; the PDF page of a National Park Service report), and found again in the saved text both by the skeleton compare of `srcs.py` and by the gate library's own text store (`tools/bt/gates/lib/extracts.py`, when it is on the machine). `--review A B` prints claims A to B with the passage and the extract; `--context ID` the passage with 400 characters either side; `--check` exits 1 when the file is not what a run would write. The file is named `claim_extracts.json` on purpose: `verify_claims.py` reads every `claims_*.json` of this folder, and an extract file with that name would be read as claims.
2. `python -I tools/bt/vegetation/build_vegetation.py` builds the units (the land-cover layer of the 1863 base map, the open ground cut into field cells), attaches the claims by rule, draws the crop of every cell from the district mix, writes the data and the register proposals. It is deterministic: `--check` compares a fresh build with the committed file and exits 1 when they differ. `--no-register` leaves the proposals alone.
3. `node --test docs/battle-theatre/app/tests/vegetation.test.mjs` (the data, the pure state function, the scene check, the meshes, the plan of the troops' walk, the wind, the module with a stand-in theatre and a recording GL context: `vegetation.test.mjs`, `vegetation-shell.test.mjs`, `vegetation-scene.test.mjs`, `vegetation-wind.test.mjs`).
4. The browser harness `docs/battle-theatre/app/tests/vegetation-browser.mjs` goes through the machine lock (`tools/horde/bench.sh`); software GL only.

## Files

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `srcs.py` | search and verify across the source families (`OR27`, `NPS:<id>`, `WS9:<id>`, `WEB:<id>`); a quote is matched on letters and digits only, and its place (printed page, PDF page, character offset with the sha256 of the text) is recorded |
| `claims_*.json` | the claims: id, source, quote, tier (`documented` or `inferred`), kind, what it says, what it is about |
| `gen_nps_corrections.py` | the vegetation rows of the evidence corrections of the park-landscape reading (`GOVERNANCE/evidence_corrections/web-nps-landscape.jsonl`) that the first reading had not used: each row read by hand in the CURATED table (its quote copied from the row, a sentence of its own, a tier, an anchor), the rows left out with their reasons in LEFT_OUT, and a report of any row in neither: `claims_nps_corrections.json` |
| `gen_bachelder_claims.py` | claims from Bachelder's guides (the earliest edition in which each quote is found is recorded) and from other eyewitness books of the library (Pierce, the Lancaster ladies' Hospital Scenes, the Eighth Ohio, the Massachusetts burial committee, the Vermont and Minnesota histories, the Southern Historical Society Papers, Meade's Life and Letters, Alexander): `claims_library_bachelder.json`, `claims_library_more.json` |
| `claims_library_jacobs.json` | five claims from Jacobs, 1864 (hand-written; verified like the others) |
| `gen_nps_claims.py` | selects claims from the extract sheets of the web task into `claims_nps_extracts.json` (the snapshot is kept, not regenerated by the build) |
| `veg_tables.py` | the calendars (crop keyframes as ranges, harvest ranges, leaf cover, orchard fruit, the epilogue), the species, the woodlot classes, the three scenarios, the fifteen gaps |
| `build_vegetation.py` | the build; the cut of a block of open ground into field cells is `split_cells` (straight cuts across the long axis of each piece, placed by a seed: not a reading of any map) |
| `short_quotes.py`, `short_quotes_curated.json`, `claim_extracts.json` | the short quote of every claim (step 1b); a quote is an extract of at most 14 words, the passage stays in the working files and is named in the data by its offsets (`loc.span`); where the saved text lost glyphs or is letter-spaced the saved words are the `quote` (the gate finds them again) and the reading as printed is `quote_printed`; the id the data carries as `src` is the one the gate's text store knows (the report id of the Official Records, the library id of the others, `#layout` or `#raw` kept where the extract exists only in that reading of a two-column report) |
| `or_scan.py`, `warren_px.py` | scratch readers kept for repeat runs (a scan of the Official Records, the pixel size of the Warren sheets) |

## Rules the build keeps

* A crop is `documented` only for a field a claim names and can place; every other cell is a seeded draw from the district mix and says so (`plausible-procedural`).
* Every colour, height, date and share in the calendars is a range with a tier and a reason; the pure state function takes a place inside the range from the unit's own seeded offsets.
* Source titles come from the index of the web task, the library index or the page's own heading; the build adds no author that those do not give.
* Nothing in the shipped data speaks of how it was made (the copy audit words are checked by the node test).

## Added on 15 November (the second and third reading of the park's landscape evidence)

* `gen_nps_corrections.py` now holds `CURATED` (claims), `LEFT_OUT` and `LEFT_OUT_B` (rows read and not used, each with its reason). An entry's options: `q` (a verbatim part of the row's quote; `None` takes the whole quote), `excerpt` (a poor scan: the row's own excerpt of the saved text, letter for letter, the cleaned reading goes into the note), `own_quote`, `anchor`, `about`, `note`. The node test of the corrections fails when a vegetation row of the corrections file is in no claim and in no left-out list: run the script, then `verify_claims.py`, then the build whenever that file grows (it was 277 rows, 370, 388 on this day).
* Anchors (`build_vegetation.attach_claims`): `poly` (a land-cover polygon: role exact), `farm`, `gz` (a gazetteer place), `lnglat` (with `bearing`, `dist_m`, `r_m`, `class`, `k`, `area`), and `box` ([west, south, east, north] in longitude and latitude: the woods and clusters whose centre lies in it, role area). `class` is `field`, `woods` (woods and clusters), `orchard`, `boulder`, `cleared`, `any`.
* `about` keys the build reads (role exact or nearest only): `crop`, `crop_class`, `understory`, `age`, `height_m` (a range, metres: the trees of that unit take it), `species` (a dict of shares: a wood's or an orchard's), `spacing_m` (a range: an orchard), `witness` (the named tree the claim is the basis of), `alt_reading` (the claim is the other reading of a conflict and is not in the district mix that is used). A value set from a claim is documented only where the claim names the polygon (role exact) and is itself documented; placed by a rule it is inferred.
* Choices (`veg_tables.SCENARIOS`): `CF-VEG-UNDER` (acts on the woods with `understory_by_scenario`), `CF-VEG-HOUCK` (the units in `HOUCK_BOX`), `CF-VEG-MCPHERSON` (the cells with a `crop_alt`). `python -I tools/bt/vegetation/build_vegetation.py --mcpherson study` makes the study's figures the default reading and the report's the other.
* `veg_tables.ROW_LINKS` lists the timeline rows that name a vegetation state with the claims that bear on them and what the claims do and do not cover; the build checks that every row is in `timeline_seed.jsonl` and every claim exists, writes `meta.rows` and `claims[].rows`. `build_vegetation.NAMED_RULES` is the table from the words of a row to a unit (`meta.named`).
* The data carries `meta.coverage` (the layer's acres beside the park's totals of 1863, a check and not a target), `meta.mix.alt` (the mix under the other reading of the McPherson acres) and `meta.units_of_measure`.

## Added on 16 November (short quotes, gate-readable source ids, the farmyards)

* **Claims in the data now carry**: `src` (the gate's source id), `fam` (`OR27`, `NPS`, `NPSWEB`, `WS9`, `WEB`), `quote` (at most 14 words), optionally `quote_printed` and `text_defect`, `loc` with `char_start`, `char_end`, `pdf_page` or `page` and `report`, and `span` (the offsets of the whole passage, when it is in the same text as the quote). `meta.source_titles` is keyed by the same ids (`OR27` for the Official Records); `meta.source_manifest` has for every source its family, title, date written and what the date rests on, rights, and reliability tier by the year written (the plan's weighting; no `independent_of`: no relation between the sources was checked).
* **The farmyards** (`make_yards`, `veg_tables.YARD`, `YARD_CLAIMS`): a unit class `yard` for the farmstead of each of the 22 farms the structures layer places, an ellipse round the farmstead point (plausible-procedural: no source outlines a yard), crop `pasture` with a seeded worn share (30 to 55 per cent), three damage events where a passage names the yard (EV-VEG-11 McMillan, 12 Leister, 13 Weikert). The yards come last in the id order (V-0928 and up: no other id moved) and overlay the field cell or wood that holds the buildings (the mesh layer of `yard` is above the fields). Gap G25.
