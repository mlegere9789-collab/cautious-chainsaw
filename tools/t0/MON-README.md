# MON - the Monuments layer (optional patch group)

Restores the **Monuments** feature of the animated map: the toolbar button (desktop) and the Tools tile (phone) draw the 512 battlefield monuments and the 20 story markers
**inside the Mapbox map**. Owner decisions of 2026-10-04 (relayed by the task): the stars that production lost were removed **unintentionally** and must come back properly;
the Virtual Walkthrough and its Monuments tab must **not** be broken by anything.

Status: built and tested on scratch trees of R1a, R1b and R1c (`tmp/mon-fix/`, 2026-10-05, after the independent verifier round below) and, on 2026-10-19, on the stack R1c + CLOCK + DATA + MON (`tmp/mon-stars/stack1`, after the story-star round at the end of this file); **not registered** in `trains.json` and not in `release/` (see "Registering it"). Nothing was deployed.

## In plain words (Barney style)

1. **What the map used to do.** There is a little stone-statue button, "Monuments". Click it and stars popped up on the map where famous monuments stand.
2. **What went wrong.** Somebody put a newer copy of the home page (`index.html`) on Netlify. The star-drawing script was in the *old* page and is *not* in the new one. Nobody
   meant to delete it: the owner says "not intentional". The button was still there but did nothing. (On the phone the "Monuments" tile never worked at all, so an earlier
   fix removed it.)
3. **Why the old stars were not good enough anyway.** They were stuck to the *screen*, not to the *map*. When you dragged or zoomed the map the stars stayed where they were,
   like stickers on a TV while the picture moves underneath. The verifier measured it: 282 px off after a drag, 491 px off after a zoom.
4. **What I did.** The stars are back, drawn the right way: they are now *part of the map picture*. Drag, zoom, rotate, tilt, resize the window, change the day: every monument stays
   glued to its statue, because the map itself carries it. Nothing runs on every frame, and while the button is off nothing exists at all.
5. **What you see.** 512 small dots (blue Union, red Confederate, grey other), 20 bigger stars for the story spots (Reynolds, Armistead, Little Round Top ...), and a gold glow
   around a star while the regiment it belongs to is on the field. Click or tap one: a small card tells you what it is and has a Google Maps link; a story star also has
   **Jump to this moment**. If a mouse cannot be used there is a **Browse monuments** list on the map (Tab to it, type, Enter).
6. **The Walkthrough.** It has its own, separate map with its own copy of the 512 monuments. I did not change one byte of it: a test compares the Walkthrough's code and data with
   the same build without this feature and they are identical. The only thing connected is an optional card button, "Open in Walkthrough list", that opens it for you.
7. **Is it switched on?** No. It lives in a side file (`tools/t0/patches-next/MON.json`) until the integrator signs it into the build (section "Registering it").
8. **The second look (2026-10-05).** An independent checker attacked the first version with real mouse, keyboard and touch and found 16 faults. The worst ones: a monument under the zoom
   buttons or the compass swallowed the click; the Distance Tool and the unit picker could not be used next to a monument; choosing a far monument from the list only slid the map; on a small
   phone the card covered the star you had just tapped. All 16 are fixed at their cause (section "The verifier round"), and the checker's own tests now pass. One fix is a judgement call
   that was later reversed by the owner decisions of 2026-10-05 (the clock is corrected): **Jump to this moment** goes to the star's own tick again, and on 2026-10-19 all 20 stars were re-derived from the sources (section "The story stars, re-derived").

## Behaviour

* **State.** Off at load (the legacy `sn` default `true` is patched to `false`, so the button and the layer agree). Not remembered across visits, like every other tool toggle except Legend and Minimap.
  `aria-pressed` is true exactly while the layer is on (desktop: group AX's `Vs`; phone: the tile). Desktop button and phone tile drive the same switch (`GTMON.on`). The desktop shell stays
  mounted while the phone layout shows, so the toolbar button's React state outlives the desktop layout; **entering the desktop layout never switches the layer on by itself**: on the first
  run after the layout mounts the button state only counts while the layer is still on, and the button is set to the layer (a layer switched off on the phone stays off, and the
  button says so). The phone layout shows the layer as the desktop left it; the desktop layout after a phone session starts with it off (T0-MONL-14, T0-VMON-17).
* **In the map.** A GeoJSON source and circle layer for the dots (radius grows with zoom: 1.9 px at z11 to 9 px at z18; colours are the app legend's unit colours `#3a6bc4` / `#c43a3a` and grey `#9aa0aa`), a symbol layer for the 20 stars (a canvas-drawn star image, 16-37 px by zoom),
  a blurred gold circle under every star driven by `feature-state active`, a gold ring driven by `feature-state hover`. No DOM overlay, no per-frame work (idle script time with the layer on is the same as off: 0-1 ms per 4 s).
* **Glow.** A story star glows while the unit it links to is *on the field* at the current tick: the unit has a placement at or before that tick (this is what the unit layer itself draws, it carries every unit's latest placement forward; the data are `placements_day*` and `density_*` files); a regiment that has no keyframe yet counts as on the field once its own brigade (`parent_brigade_id` of the order of battle) is, so the 140th Pennsylvania and the 26th North Carolina (first keyframes at ticks 290 and 120) are not dark at their own marker moments. It is recomputed only when the tick, the loaded placements or the order of battle change, and only the stars whose state flipped are touched.
* **Card** (click or tap; desktop: a Mapbox popup anchored on the marker, so it follows the map; phone: a sheet above the dock, a side sheet on the left in a landscape phone). Name, side and group, honors, location, coordinates, Google Maps link (new tab);
  a story star: the narrative, the linked unit (its order-of-battle name; the line stays out until the order of battle is loaded, never a raw id) and whether it is on the field, `Marker moment: July D, tick T of 675`, the star's note where the Park or the sources leave something open, and the distance at which the animation draws the linked unit (`On the map this unit is drawn about N m from the monument at this moment (unit positions are approximate)`; see "The story stars, re-derived"), and **Jump to this moment** (store `setPlaying(false)`, `setTick(jump tick)`, select the unit when the order of battle knows it, fly to the marker at zoom >= 15, or, when the animation draws the linked unit more than 250 m from the star, fit the view to the star and the unit (padded clear of the unit panel and the dock; the jump waits at most 3 s for the days up to the tick to load, then moves once); a cut instead of a flight under reduced motion; focus goes back to where the card was opened from). **Open in Walkthrough list** opens the Walkthrough
  with its own opener, picks its Monuments tab and types the name in its search (the card closes first). **Also here (N)** lists up to 4 other markers within reach of the pointer, so the 28 co-located pairs are reachable.
  Role `dialog`, named by its heading, `Esc` and the close button (`data-gt-close`, `data-gtbg-card="4"`) close it and give the focus back to the control it came from (the Browse button for a card chosen from the list), a click on bare map closes it, clicking another marker replaces it.
  On the desktop the card is panned clear of the toolbar, the dock and the screen edges, **after** a flight to the marker has landed (the card's own pan used to cancel the flight of a list pick).
  On the phone the sheet is sized so that it never lies on the marker the visitor just tapped or on the Browse control: a bottom sheet is at most as tall as leaves 110 px of map under the top bars and clear of the control (213 px on a 320x568 phone, whose map is 331 px high; the card scrolls inside), a landscape phone gets a side sheet and the marker is panned into the free part of the map beside it.
* **Jump to this moment and the clock.** Jump goes to the star's own tick, always. Since 2026-10-19 the corrected clock (group CLOCK) makes the tick, the clock text and the story agree (the clock may differ from a time in the text by at most the uncertainty it states), so the earlier jump to "the tick whose legacy clock reads the time the text names" (`JUMP_BY_CLOCK`, `jumpTick`, `clockIsLegacy`, `clockAt`) is gone from the runtime. Without CLOCK in the tree the clock is the legacy formula and 9 of the 20 texts disagree with it by 2 to 9 hours; MON no longer hides that. The card prints no clock time of its own: the line names day and tick, the toolbar and time bar print the clock.
* **Hit testing** is by projection (the nearest markers to the pointer, no dependence on what is drawn): 12 px for a mouse, **22 px (a 44 px target) for a tap** (phone layout or `pointer: coarse`); stars get a 6 px head start over dots. A marker only claims a click that is meant for it. The map container's capture handler looks at (1) the target: only a click that lands on the Mapbox canvas itself counts, so the zoom buttons, the compass and the attribution links keep their clicks even when a monument is drawn under them; (2) the Distance Tool (`window.__gtbgApp._gbDist` is set while it is armed): it owns every click; (3) the nearest **unit**: the app's unit picker takes every click on the map (it selects the unit nearest to the point), so a unit whose drawn centre is nearer to the point than a dot (by 2 px) or clearly nearer than a story star (by 5 px; the stars sit on their units' positions) keeps the click, the card does not open and the app selects the unit; units are taken as the unit layer draws them (latest keyframe at or before the current tick; none while Toggle Units hides them). Otherwise the click on a marker is consumed (`stopPropagation` in the capture phase), so the unit picker does not also select the unit nearest to it; a click on bare map is left to the app.
* **Hover** (mouse): gold ring, pointer cursor, small tooltip with name and side. Not on the phone, not over a control, a card or while the Distance Tool is armed.
* **Keyboard.** While the layer is on a map control "Browse monuments" (top-right stack, Tab-reachable) opens a panel: search (name matches first, then text), side select, up to 40 rows, Arrow keys, Enter opens the card with focus inside; `Esc` closes list or card and returns focus to the Browse button. Reopening the list selects the old search text (the next letters replace it). On the phone the list is a sheet of its own above the legend, minimap and dock (it is moved out of the map box, whose stacking context lies under all three) with a close button, and the on-screen keyboard is not raised. Cards and the list show a 2 px focus ring (gold on R1a; from R1b the AX group's global `:focus-visible{outline:2px solid #fff!important}` with its black halo wins, which is the app's standard) and have 44 px targets on touch.
* **Narrow desktop windows.** Group AX's rule for widths under 1000 px put the toolbar's middle group (Toggle Units, User Guide, Virtual Walkthrough) on top of the right-hand button row, so at 960 px the Walkthrough button covered the centre of the Monuments button (pre-existing in every train). MON replaces that rule (`MON:toolbar-narrow`): the group stays in the flow with tighter padding and gives way step by step, the PDF link under 990 px (clipped to 148 px beyond that: R1c's text is 135 px, the legacy "Download Website Tutorial" of R1a and R1b 232 px; it has no other link on the desktop: the guide is still `/user-guide.pdf`), Toggle Units under 850 px, the Walkthrough label is clipped under 750 px; the groups need 977 px (all three), 836 px (Toggle Units and the Walkthrough) and 739 px (the Walkthrough) beside the title and the 13 icon buttons, so nothing overlaps and nothing leaves the window from 640 px up (T0-MONL-16).
* **Style changes.** `style.load` (full reload) and `styledata` (a `setStyle` with a diff drops layers it does not know; the repair runs one tick later, never inside the event, because adding layers in the middle of Mapbox's diff breaks its symbol placement) rebuild sources, layers and images; `styleimagemissing` re-adds a star image.
* **Cost.** Before the first toggle nothing exists (no source, layer, style tag, listener, store subscription, popup; `window.__gtmon` is an object with one function). The first toggle adds two sources, four layers, two images and one `<style id="gtmon-css">`. On: 4 DOM listeners on the map container (`pointerdown` and `click` in the capture phase, `mousemove`, `mouseleave`), 3 map listeners, one store subscription, the list control. A card on the phone adds one `resize` listener on `window` and a list panel is moved into the phone root while it is open; both are removed with the card / the control. Off again: the layers are hidden (a hidden layer costs the renderer nothing; the sources, images and the style tag stay), and every listener, subscription, popup and control is gone (T0-MONL-11 counts the listeners on the real container over 6 cycles). Entry bundle: +32.8 KB raw, +9.0 to +9.5 KB brotli (R1c: 2,305,571 to 2,338,435 bytes: the runtime grew by the verifier fixes, 27.3 KB before); stylesheet +0.5 to +0.6 KB (the toolbar rule).

## Building and testing without touching anyone

```
node tools/t0/mon/build-mon.mjs [--check]                                    # gtmon.js -> patches-next/MON.json (--check: exit 1 when they differ)
python tools/t0/mon/mon_build.py --train R1c --out tmp/mon-fix/mon-R1c [--no-gate] [--without-mon]
python tools/t0/mon/selftest.py                                              # 5 cases: generator in sync, loader refusal, anchors on R1a/R1b/R1c, wrapper == stock builder, no write into release/
node tests/t0/monuments.spec.mjs --dist tmp/mon-fix/mon-R1c/dist [--filter 'MONL-0[1-3]'] [--expect-red] [--control <same train without MON>]
node tests/t0/verify-monuments.spec.mjs --dist tmp/mon-fix/mon-R1c/dist [--filter 'VMON-0[1-4]']          # the independent verifier's spec (ids T0-VMON-NN), same tile-budget rule
```
Ports: both specs take `T0_PORT_BASE` (the server and up to 8 Chrome debugging ports above it) and `T0_AGENT` (the folder under `tmp/` for Chrome profiles); the runs of 2026-10-05 used `T0_AGENT=mon-fix T0_PORT_BASE=8920`.
`mon_build.py` reads `trains.json` in memory, adds `"optional_groups": ["MON"]`, stages the patch set plus MON.json in a scratch directory and calls the unmodified `build_train()`; it refuses to write into `release/<train>`.
Why not `tools/t0/patches/MON.json` right away: `patches.load_groups` rejects any group file whose name `trains.json` does not list, so the file would stop **every** build, including the integrator's (selftest case 2 shows the exact error). The same precedent is `patches-next/CLOCK.json`.

`tests/t0/monuments.spec.mjs` (ids `T0-MONL-01..18` and `-X`; the prefix is `MONL` because `T0-MON-01` already names the idle-commits test of `journeys-desktop.spec.mjs`). Real Chrome, real CDP mouse/wheel/keyboard/touch, tiles only through the harness.
`T0-MONL-15` (static, since 2026-10-19: the 20 stars of the shipped entry equal `tools/t0/mon/stars-spec.mjs`, lie on their monument and link a unit on the field; it used to prove the clock-derived jump tick in a `vm`), `T0-MONL-17a/b` (all 20 stars in the browser on a tree that has CLOCK) and `T0-MONL-18` (the 65 source claims) and `T0-MONL-16` (toolbar, 16 window widths, tiles blocked) are new with the verifier round; `--control` is the same train built with `--without-mon` (MONL-12 compares the Walkthrough span with it).

## The story stars, re-derived (2026-10-19)

Task of the owner decisions of 2026-10-05 (items 2 to 4): the corrected clock is in (group CLOCK), the displayed data are corrected (group DATA), and the stars must tell the truth with both. All 20 stars were re-derived from the sources; the evidence per star is in `docs/data-audit/STORY-STARS.md` (generated by `tools/t0/mon/stars-doc.mjs`).

**In plain words.** A star says: here is a monument, here is what happened, press the button and the map goes to that moment. Three things have to match: the *place* (the star sits on the Park's monument), the *time* (the clock on the screen says what the story says) and the *unit* (the regiment of the story is on the map). Before, 9 of 20 clocks were wrong by hours, all 20 stars stood 100 m to 2.3 km from the monument of their story (8 of them more than 100 m from any Park marker; the 20th Maine's star stood among the Regulars' monuments on Wheatfield Road, 723 m from its own), 10 stars linked a unit that is not the one in the story (an id that is not in the order of battle, or another brigade's) and one text described a different regiment. Now every star stands within 5 m of its monument record (or of the Park's marker where the record array has none), every Jump lands on the story's own tick where the corrected clock reads the time of the text, and the card says plainly how far the animation draws the unit from the monument.

What changed (one patch per star, `MON:story:<id>`, data in `tools/t0/mon/stars-spec.mjs`):

* **Jump to this moment** always lands on the star's own tick; `JUMP_BY_CLOCK`, `jumpTick`, `clockIsLegacy` and `clockAt` are gone from `gtmon.js`. `T0-MONL-17a/b` shows, for all 20 stars, that the clock the app prints after the jump is the clock of that tick and that the times in the story text lie within the uncertainty the clock states.
* **Ticks** re-derived from the story's own time statement and the sources, through the corrected clock (examples: Iron Brigade 20 -> 28, 1st Minnesota 295 -> 302, Greene 380 -> 320, 26th North Carolina 16 -> 95, Irish Brigade 278 -> 290; 12 of 20 changed).
* **Units** corrected (Iron Brigade and Reynolds now link the Iron Brigade `U-I-1-1`, Barlow's star keeps von Gilsa's brigade `U-XI-1-1`, Avery's star the brigade he commanded `C-II-1-2`; the 140th, 83rd Pennsylvania, 1st Minnesota, 72nd Pennsylvania and 26th North Carolina keep their own ids).
* **Coordinates** moved onto the monument record (`_A` index in the spec) or the Park's marker as OpenStreetMap maps it (node id in the spec): all 20 were 100 m to 2.3 km from it in the legacy array (3 had already been moved by the first MON round) and lie within 5 m of it now. Four stars (15th Alabama, 20th North Carolina, Avery, Hood) have no regiment marker and no record in the array: their star stands at the Park's tablet named in the spec (an OpenStreetMap node) and the card says what it stands on; all four are renamed (`Law's Alabama Brigade (15th Alabama)`, `Iverson's Brigade (20th North Carolina)`, `Hoke's Brigade (Avery mortally wounded)`, `Hood's Division (Hood wounded)`).
* **Texts** (the label is set by DATA first; MON replaces it only where the sources showed it wrong): 15th Alabama (Oates's own report says one charge and five Union charges repulsed, the rest is his postwar account), Reynolds (no report says he was directing the Iron Brigade), Hancock (the hour had no source), 1st Minnesota (Fox says Willcox's brigade, Hancock's report says a regiment), 16th Vermont ("first day of combat" replaced by Stannard's words), 20th North Carolina (97th New York took 213 of its men), Hood (the hour removed). 65 source claims are checked against the cited pages by `tools/t0/mon/stars-sources.mjs` (`T0-MONL-18`).
* **Jump frames both.** When the linked unit is drawn more than 250 m from the star (15 of the 20), Jump fits the view to the star and the unit instead of centring on the star, so the user sees both (the 26th North Carolina's unit is 946 m away; before, it was off the screen).
* **Card**: the star's `note` (what the Park does not mark, where sources differ, what the animation does not draw), the distance of the linked unit from the monument (`gap`, measured on the stack by `tools/t0/mon/stars-measure.mjs --write`, re-measured in the browser by `T0-MONL-17a/b`), the action buttons stay in view when a long card scrolls, and "Open in Walkthrough list" is offered only for a star that stands on a record of the monument array (the other four would search the Walkthrough for a name it does not have).
* `window.__gtmon.info()` also reports the store's tick, the selected unit and where the unit layer draws each story's unit (read-only, for the tests).

**What is not solved (stated, not hidden).** The animation draws 14 of the 20 linked units more than 350 m from the monument at the star's tick (largest 946 m, the 26th North Carolina, whose July 1 fight is not drawn at all; then the Iron Brigade 736 m, Reynolds 723 m, Greene 661 m, Avery 434 m, Hood 572 m ...). The rows are DATA's, the cause is the animation's old geometry (DATA's audit, section 8, lists the Herbst and McPherson ground, Little Round Top, Devil's Den and the Wheatfield among the positions it left unmoved), and the owner has not decided whether the rows of those sectors are moved onto the monuments; moving a single unit would pull it away from its brigade. `docs/data-audit/STORY-STARS.md` lists, per star, which way and how far the unit lies. The hour of four events (20th Maine's charge, Hancock's and Hood's wounds, 1st Minnesota's charge) is not recorded by any participant.

How to rebuild and prove it: `node tools/t0/mon/build-mon.mjs` (writes `MON.json` from `gtmon.js`, `stars-spec.mjs`, `stars-gaps.json`), `python tools/t0/data/data_build.py --train R1c --out tmp/mon-stars/stack1 --with CLOCK,DATA,MON` (the stack; the gate row `data-untouched` fails by design, DATA changes served data files), `node tools/t0/mon/stars-measure.mjs --write` (after DATA changes), `node tools/t0/mon/stars-sources.mjs`, `node tests/t0/monuments.spec.mjs --dist tmp/mon-stars/stack1/dist --filter 'MONL-17a'` (and 17b), `node tools/t0/mon/stars-doc.mjs`.

## Known limitations and what was not verified

* **Tested only in headless Chrome 153 on Windows**, input synthesized through CDP (mouse, wheel, keyboard, touch). No real device, no iOS Safari / WebKit, no Firefox, no screen reader, no physical touch, no GPU other than the software one headless Chrome uses. The pixel checks read real screenshots of that renderer.
* **R1a phone** (the legacy phone shell, group C absent): the tile works, the markers are drawn, but the legacy overlay canvas swallows every tap (`preventDefault` on `touchend`), so no click reaches the map and the card cannot be opened by touch there. Group C (R1b and later) fixes it. Not handled on purpose: intercepting `touchend` would unbalance Mapbox's touch state.
* **Glow before group B**: without B's merge fix the app itself drops units when it merges a day's files, so on R1a/R1b a marker can stay dark although the data has its unit (the test checks a subset there).
* **Story markers vs the clock**: solved with CLOCK in the tree (2026-10-19, see "The story stars, re-derived"): Jump lands on the star's own tick and the corrected clock reads the time of the text within its stated uncertainty (`T0-MONL-17a/b`). Without CLOCK the legacy clock is still 2 to 9 hours off for 9 of the 20 texts; MON no longer works around it.
* **Story data**: the 140th Pennsylvania text (it described the 140th New York) was corrected by DATA, the units, ticks and coordinates of all 20 stars by MON (2026-10-19). What remains open: the linked unit is drawn 350 m to 946 m from the monument for 14 of the 20 stars (the animation's rows, DATA's; the card states the distance), five stars stand at a Park tablet rather than a regiment monument, and four hours are not recorded by any participant. See `docs/data-audit/STORY-STARS.md`.
* **Unit panel after a Jump**: the unit is selected only when the order of battle has it (`oobData[unit_id]`); all 20 linked ids are in it now. 5 ids have no keyframe in `placements_day*.json` (their positions come from `density_*.json`), a regiment without keyframes counts as on the field with its brigade.
* **Phone sheet** sits above the dock at z-index 300 like the unit popup; if a unit popup is open at the same time the sheet covers it. In portrait the sheet is at most 213 px tall on a 320x568 phone, so a long story card scrolls inside it.
* **Hit testing** does not know about panels drawn over the map (they receive the click first, as they should). A unit whose drawn *bar* (a line of battle is up to 120 px long) is under the pointer but whose centre is farther away than a dot does not keep the click: the unit picker itself picks by centre.
* **Narrow windows**: below 990 px the PDF user-guide link leaves the toolbar (below 850 px also Toggle Units): there is no other link to the guide on the desktop; Help does not carry it.
* **"Open in Walkthrough list"** drives the Walkthrough like a user would (clicks the tab whose sibling is "Tour Stops", types the name into the first search field). It never edits the Walkthrough, but it depends on that markup; when it is not found within about 3 s the Walkthrough just opens.
* **Persisted state**, a Help/user-guide sentence about the new card and list, the keyboard shortcut, and the legend entry for dots/stars are not part of this group (copy belongs to CP).
* **Not checked**: Lighthouse/Web Vitals with the layer on (the layer is off at load, nothing runs), long play sessions with the layer on (the store subscription costs 20 comparisons per tick change), memory over hours.
