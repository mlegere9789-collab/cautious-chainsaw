Privacy Policy

Effective [[OWNER: date]]. Replaces the interim version of September 30, 2026 (which replaced the version of September 7, 2026).

The short version

  • There are no accounts, no sign-up, no ads, no advertising cookies and no analytics cookies, and this site's own code sets no cookies. We do not sell personal information.
  • What you save (bookmarks, settings, a tour you build) stays in your browser and is not sent to us.
  • To show you the site, two services necessarily receive your IP address and basic browser details: Netlify (our host) and Mapbox (the map). Some features contact other services only when you use them: Google (the Virtual Walkthrough's Street View frame and Google Maps links), the National Park Service (video and links), and PeerJS, Google and jsDelivr (Watch Together's live sync).
  • Battlefield AR uses your camera, location and motion sensors on your device. Our code sends none of it anywhere.

What we collect

This site has no accounts, sign-up, forms or comment boxes, and its own code does not ask for your name, email address or precise location. The sections below describe what our host and the services we use receive automatically. If you email us, the message reaches our mailbox, which is a Gmail account.

Hosting and traffic statistics (Netlify)

The site is hosted by Netlify, Inc. Like any web host, Netlify's servers receive your IP address, the pages and files you request, your browser's user-agent, the referring page and the time of each request, and keep server logs. [[OWNER: confirm Netlify Web Analytics is switched on; register open question O-12]] Netlify's Web Analytics is built from those server logs (no code runs in your browser for it) and reports aggregate page views, unique visitors (counted by IP address per day), referrers, countries and pages not found; we see totals, not individual visitors. See Netlify's privacy policy. [[FACT-CHECK]] Netlify's log retention depends on its plan and policy; the owner has not confirmed either. [[COUNSEL]] Netlify is a processor for the site's traffic logs; whether a data-processing agreement is needed is a legal question.

Performance monitoring (Netlify Real User Monitoring)

Each page loads a small script from this site's own address (/.netlify/scripts/rum) that measures how fast the page loads and responds. It sends Netlify (at ingesteer.services-prod.nsvcs.net) the page's hostname and path, the time of the visit, your browser family (for example Chrome or Safari), operating-system family, device type (mobile or desktop), your available screen size, and page-load, layout-stability and responsiveness timings. The script sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser and does not run inside embedded frames. Like any request, it reaches Netlify along with your IP address. Content blockers can block it without affecting the site. [[OWNER: confirm RUM stays on]]

Maps (Mapbox)

Map imagery, styles, fonts and symbols come from Mapbox, Inc. (api.mapbox.com) on the main map and on the map pages. Your browser contacts Mapbox directly, so Mapbox receives your IP address, your browser's user-agent, this site's address as the referrer (the site sends only its origin to other sites) and the tiles you view. The Mapbox map software also sends usage and performance events to events.mapbox.com: a random anonymous identifier kept in your browser's localStorage (keys beginning mapbox.eventData) and replaced about every 24 hours, the software version, a map-session token, the map style and zoom, device pixel ratio, connection type, your browser's user-agent, screen and window sizes, your graphics card's renderer and vendor names, and load timings. Mapbox's code, not ours, does this. See Mapbox's privacy policy. In checks on 2026-09-30 Mapbox's software set no cookies on this site. [[COUNSEL]] ePrivacy Art. 5(3): is the anonymous identifier "strictly necessary"? This draft does not decide; it discloses. [[FACT-CHECK]] Whether blocking events.mapbox.com leaves the map working has not been tested.

Virtual Walkthrough (Google Street View)

The Virtual Walkthrough, opened from the button on the main map, shows a Google Street View panorama in a frame served by google.com. Today the frame opens as soon as the Walkthrough does (with the first tour stop) and changes as you pick other stops; the site does not contact Google before you open the Walkthrough. Google receives your IP address, your browser details and this site's address (as the referrer); the frame loads its own scripts, fonts and images from Google's servers (checked: maps.googleapis.com, maps.gstatic.com, streetviewpixels-pa.googleapis.com, fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com); and Google may set or read its own cookies and identifiers, under Google's privacy policy. [[WHEN SHIPPED (register D-MEDIA, click-to-load): "Only when you press Load Street View, ..." and the frame stops opening by itself]] Links such as "Open in Google Maps", "View on map" and "Open Street View at this location" open Google Maps in a new tab only when you click them.

National Park Service video and links

Some stops of the NPS Auto Tour have a Show Video button. Only when you press it, a video page from the National Park Service (www.nps.gov) opens in a frame, and the National Park Service receives your IP address, your browser details and this site's address (as the referrer), under its privacy practices. "NPS Video" links in the Walkthrough and the Find Your Ancestor link to the NPS Soldiers and Sailors database open nps.gov in a new tab only when clicked.

Watch Together (live sync)

Building a tour, Export tour and Import tour happen in your browser. Live Sync is optional: it connects two browsers directly with WebRTC, arranged by the public PeerJS service. Only when you press Host or Join: the page downloads the PeerJS code (version 1.5.4) from jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net), so jsDelivr sees your IP address and browser details [[WHEN SHIPPED (WP-INFRA-08): "or from this site, once self-hosted"]]; your browser registers a peer ID (for the host it contains the 5-letter session code) with the PeerJS public broker at 0.peerjs.com over HTTPS and WebSocket, so the broker sees the code and your IP address; PeerJS's default settings list Google's public STUN server (stun.l.google.com) and PeerJS's relay servers (eu-0.turn.peerjs.com and us-0.turn.peerjs.com), which your browser may contact while the connection is set up; and the other person in the session can learn your IP address, as with any direct WebRTC connection. Our code sends only two things between browsers: the tick number the host is showing and the host's optional note. A session code is not a password: anyone who has it can join. Do not put personal information in notes. Policies: jsDelivr, Google. [[FACT-CHECK]] PeerJS publishes no privacy policy for its free cloud server that we could find (peerjs.com/privacy is a 404 as of 2026-09-30), so its retention and server location are unknown; Appendix H's statement that the broker "reports an EU location" was not verified and is not repeated here. [[OWNER]] The register's default for this page is to retire it (D-SCOPE-TIERS); if it is retired, delete this section.

Battlefield AR

After you press "Start Battlefield AR", the AR page asks your browser for permission to use your rear camera, your location and your device's motion/orientation (compass) sensor. Camera video is shown on your screen only; it is not recorded or uploaded. Your location is used inside your browser to work out distances and bearings to landmarks, and the compass aims the overlay. The page's only network request is for this site's own landmark data, so none of it is sent to us or to anyone else. You can withdraw permission at any time in your browser or phone settings; the rest of the site does not need it.

Tools that work only on your device

Cinematic Export records the map view into a video file on your device. Immersive Mode makes its sound in your browser. Ask the Historian searches this site's own data in your browser; what you type is not sent to us.
