Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 30, 2026 · What changed

What this site is

gettysburginteractivemap.com is a free, ad-free, no-login animated map of the Battle of Gettysburg. There is no account system, no payment of any kind, and no data sold to anyone.

Information we collect

This site has no accounts, sign-up, forms or comment boxes, and its own code does not ask for your name, email address or precise location. Your browser does contact a few other services directly to show the site, though, and they receive technical information such as your IP address. This page lists every service the site uses, what it receives and when.

  • Hosting and traffic statistics (Netlify). The site is hosted by Netlify, Inc. Like any web host, Netlify's servers receive your IP address, the pages and files you request, your browser's user-agent, the page you came from and the time of each request, and Netlify keeps server logs. Netlify's server-side analytics turns those logs into aggregate reports for us (page views, unique visitors counted by IP address, referrers, countries, pages not found); we see totals, not individual visitors. See Netlify's privacy policy.
  • Real User Monitoring (Netlify). Each page loads a small script from this site's own address (/.netlify/scripts/rum) that measures how fast the page loads and responds. It sends Netlify (at ingesteer.services-prod.nsvcs.net) the page's hostname and path, the time of the visit, your browser family (such as Chrome or Safari), operating-system family, device type (mobile or desktop), your available screen size, and page-load, layout-stability and responsiveness timings. The script sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser and does not run inside embedded frames. Like any request, it reaches Netlify along with your IP address.
  • Mapbox (maps). The main map and the map pages (Compare Moments, Camera Modes, What They Could See, What-If Sandbox, Immersive Mode, The Ground Remembers, Watch Together and Cinematic Export) get their map imagery, styles, fonts and symbols from Mapbox, Inc. (api.mapbox.com). Your browser contacts Mapbox directly, so Mapbox receives your IP address, your browser's user-agent, this site's address (as the referrer) and the map tiles you view. Mapbox's map software also reports usage to events.mapbox.com: a random anonymous identifier that it keeps in your browser's localStorage (keys beginning mapbox.eventData) and replaces about every 24 hours, the software version, a map-session token, the map style and zoom, device pixel ratio, connection type, your browser's user-agent, screen and window sizes, your graphics card's renderer and vendor names, and load timings. That code is Mapbox's, not ours; what Mapbox does with the data is governed by Mapbox's privacy policy.

Pages that don't show a map (for example the Casualty Dashboard, Battle Reports, Search Everything and Ask the Historian) load nothing from other companies' servers apart from the Netlify monitoring script described above.

Features that contact another service only when you use them

  • Virtual Walkthrough (Google Street View). The Virtual Walkthrough, opened from the button on the main map, shows a Google Street View panorama in a frame served by google.com. The frame opens as soon as the Walkthrough does, with the first tour stop, and changes as you pick other stops. Google receives your IP address, your browser details and this site's address (as the referrer); the frame loads its own scripts, fonts and images from Google's servers (for example maps.googleapis.com, maps.gstatic.com and streetviewpixels-pa.googleapis.com); and Google may set or read its own cookies and identifiers, under Google's privacy policy. We don't see anything from that frame, and the site does not contact Google until you open the Walkthrough.
  • Links to Google Maps and the National Park Service. Some panels link out: "Open in Google Maps", "View on map", "Open Street View at this location" and "NPS Video" links, and Find Your Ancestor links to the NPS Soldiers and Sailors database. They open the other site in a new tab only when you click, and that site's own policy applies there.
  • NPS Auto Tour video (National Park Service). Some stops of the NPS Auto Tour have a Show Video button. Only when you press it, a video page from the National Park Service (www.nps.gov) opens in a frame. The National Park Service receives your IP address, your browser details and this site's address (as the referrer), under the NPS's privacy practices.
  • Watch Together, Live Sync (PeerJS, Google, jsDelivr). Building a tour, Export tour and Import tour happen in your browser. Live Sync is optional: it connects two browsers directly with WebRTC, arranged by the free public PeerJS service. Only when you press Host or Join:
    • the page downloads the PeerJS code from jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net), so jsDelivr receives your IP address and browser details (jsDelivr's privacy policy);
    • your browser registers with the public PeerJS broker at 0.peerjs.com (over HTTPS and WebSocket) using a peer ID, which for the host contains the 5-letter session code, so the broker sees the code and your IP address;
    • PeerJS's default settings also list Google's public STUN server (stun.l.google.com) and PeerJS's relay (TURN) servers (eu-0.turn.peerjs.com and us-0.turn.peerjs.com), which your browser may contact while the connection is set up (Google's privacy policy);
    • the other person in the session can learn your IP address, as with any direct WebRTC connection.
    Our code sends only two things between the browsers: the tick number the host is showing and the host's optional note. Anyone who has the session code can join, so share it only with people you trust and keep personal information out of notes. The PeerJS broker is a free service run by the PeerJS project; we could not find a privacy policy for it, so we can't tell you how long it keeps its logs.

Battlefield AR: camera, location and motion

After you press "Start Battlefield AR", the AR page asks your browser for permission to use your rear camera, your location and your device's motion/orientation (compass) sensor. The camera picture is shown on your screen only; it is not recorded or uploaded. Your location is used inside your browser to work out the distance and direction to landmarks, and the compass aims the overlay. The page's only network request is for this site's own landmark data, so none of it is sent to us or to anyone else. You can withdraw permission at any time in your browser or phone settings; nothing else on the site needs it.

Tools that work only on your device

Cinematic Export records the map view into a video file on your device; nothing is uploaded. Immersive Mode makes its sound in your browser. Ask the Historian searches this site's own data in your browser, and what you type is not sent anywhere.

What we don't do

No account creation, no email collection, no advertising trackers or advertising cookies, and no analytics service other than Netlify's own hosting statistics and monitoring and the usage reports built into Mapbox's map software, both described above. We don't sell your data.

Cookies

This site does not set its own cookies. Netlify's monitoring script sets none, and when we last checked (September 30, 2026) Mapbox's map software set none on this site. Google (Street View and Google Maps) and the National Park Service may set their own cookies if you open their frames or pages, under their own policies linked above.

Children's privacy

This site is often used in classrooms. It has no accounts or sign-up and does not ask any visitor, including students, for personal information. Like any website, though, the services described above receive IP addresses and browser details when a page or feature contacts them. Teachers may want to remind students not to type personal information into Watch Together notes, which everyone in a live session can see.
