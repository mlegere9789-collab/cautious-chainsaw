Standard of Care

Last reviewed: October 4, 2026. Status: candidate.

These are eight commitments the project proposes to make to every visitor. They are a goal, not a description of the site today. Under each one, this page says what holds now and what does not.

The wording follows the proposal in docs/pitch/PITCH.md (“The Standard of Care”, marked there as design intent), with the first person plural changed to the neutral voice.

Today, 0 of the 8 hold fully, 2 hold in part and 6 do not yet hold.

  1. 1. Every number is a ladder. Not yet in place

    The page shows who said what, and when, and why one value is shown first.

    Today. The Casualty Dashboard shows one total, 46,163, while the site’s own data holds three bases (45,928, 46,163 and 51,049). The ladder exists on the Sources and Methods page, not on the map.

    What would make it true. The same ladder behind every figure on the map, with the evidence labels visible.

  2. 2. Every quotation has a receipt. Not yet in place

    The page, the scan, the highlight.

    Today. The live stories carry no page citations. The plan records zero citations in the Historian today. A text index of the Official Records, with page and scan citations on every passage, is built and tested and not live.

    What would make it true. The index live, its citations checked against the scans, and every quotation in a story linked to its page.

  3. 3. Every model is labelled. Partly in place

    Documented, measured, computed, modelled, contested or unknown.

    Today. Several pages carry a plain-language note: the Casualty Dashboard says its curves are modelled, the What-If Sandbox says it is not history, and Aftermath says it is an overview. The six labels themselves are not shown anywhere on the map.

    What would make it true. The six labels on every card, number and answer.

  4. 4. Every disagreement is shown. Not yet in place

    Nothing is quietly averaged or hidden.

    Today. The Terms say discrepancies are noted rather than silently resolved, yet the Casualty Dashboard shows one casualty total although the data holds three. The Sources and Methods page shows the disagreements it can compute: casualty figures and the time anchors.

    What would make it true. A register of disagreements with every value, its source and the reason one is shown first.

  5. 5. Every person named is sourced, and every unknown is honoured. Not yet in place

    The dead are named, and the page says how many cannot be.

    Today. The site lists regiments, not individual soldiers. Of 3,283 data records, 60 carry a source string.

    What would make it true. A source on every named person and a count of the unknown.

  6. 6. Every date is a range when it is one. Not yet in place

    Photographs, reports and newspapers are never made more precise than their evidence.

    Today. Moments print as clock times. All 33 proposed time anchors are unreviewed, and release R1c notes record that clock labels are hours off in places.

    What would make it true. Times shown as “about”, ranges where sources disagree, and a historian’s signed decision behind any exact time.

  7. 7. Every correction is logged, Not yet in place

    publicly, with a date and a reason.

    Today. The Corrections page and its log format are drafted. The log has no entries: an entry is written only after a correction is live.

    What would make it true. The Corrections page published, with a working address and a first logged entry.

  8. 8. Nothing the Historian says is generated. Partly in place

    Every sentence is a quotation, a template over a sourced row, or a stated limitation.

    Today. The first half holds: Ask the Historian runs in the browser and reads only this site’s own data files; it calls no model. The second half does not yet hold: answers are not all quotations with sources, templates over sourced rows, or stated limitations.

    What would make it true. Every answer type built from a cited quotation, a sourced template or a stated limit, checked on every build.

How this page is kept honest

A commitment moves to “In place” only when the evidence for it exists in the project and a test or a file shows it. It moves back if the evidence is withdrawn. The proposal does not ask visitors to trust the site; it gives them what they need to check it.
