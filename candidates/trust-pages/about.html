About

Last reviewed: October 4, 2026. Status: candidate.

The Gettysburg Interactive Map follows the Battle of Gettysburg, July 1–3, 1863, on a map. It places 465 regiments and 104 batteries (569 units) on the field and moves them through 675 moments across the three days.

What it holds

Beside the units, the map holds 24 written stories, a chronology of 137 events, 30 named places on the ground, and a 16-stop guided tour. Extra pages let you search the records, compare two moments, read the casualty figures corps by corps, and ask questions of the site’s own records.

What it is not

It is a reconstruction. Positions are reconstructed from published sources. Where a regiment’s exact place at a given time is not recorded, the map shows the best-supported position. In the data behind the map, 1,804 position rows cover 188 of the 675 moments, and 217 of the 465 regiments have positions of their own. The Sources and Methods page says what each kind of statement rests on.

Times are approximate. Sources disagree by half an hour or more, and the map plays as a time-lapse. The proposed clock rests on 33 time anchors; none of them has yet been checked by a historian.

This is an independent project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the National Park Service, the Gettysburg Foundation, the American Battlefield Trust, Mapbox or Google. [OWNER TO CONFIRM: the sentence that this is an independent project is true]

It lists regiments, not individual soldiers. To find a soldier by name, search the National Park Service’s Soldiers and Sailors System.

Who made it

The site’s credit line reads “Created by Mike Legere.”

I’m Mike Legere, and I built the Gettysburg Interactive Map for the love of history. It’s free, with no ads or accounts, and I maintain it on my own. Everything here is a reconstruction, so I try to show what the record documents, what is estimated, and where the sources disagree. If you spot a mistake, please tell me: gettysburginteractivemap@gmail.com.

How to check it

Nothing on this site asks to be taken on trust. These pages show what the map rests on and how to challenge it.

  • Sources and Methods: what the data holds, which works it names, where the sources disagree.
  • Standard of Care: eight commitments the project is working toward, and which of them hold today.
  • Corrections: how to report a mistake and where corrections are logged.
  • Accessibility Statement: what was tested, the results and the gaps.
