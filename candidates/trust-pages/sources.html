Evidence labels

This page uses six labels, taken from the project’s proposal for a visible evidence vocabulary. The map itself does not yet show them; they describe the data here.

The six evidence labels
LabelWhat it promises
DocumentedA verbatim passage or a figure printed in a named source, with page and scan.

Methods

How positions are made

  • The order of battle lists the units. Position rows give a place at a moment for some of them: 1,804 rows on 188 of 675 moments.
  • Between recorded positions, release R1c shows a unit at its latest recorded position at or before the moment you view. It does not move a unit smoothly between two recorded places.
  • A regiment with no positions of its own is shown with its brigade where the data has one. The release R1c text says: “This regiment has no recorded positions. It moves with its brigade.”
  • Positions are reconstructed from published sources. Where a regiment’s exact place at a given time is not recorded, the map shows the best-supported position.

How the clock works

  • The map has 675 moments. The data files declare that each moment is five minutes; an audit measured that declaration as wrong, so this project’s facts file carries no step length.
  • A proposed clock ties the moments to 33 anchors. All 33 are in the state “proposed”: not reviewed, confirmed or signed by a historian. 5 are marked as needing a decision.
  • The release R1c notes record that clock labels are hours off in places, and that release changes only the date.
  • Times are approximate. Sources disagree by half an hour or more, and the map plays as a time-lapse.

How casualties are counted

Casualties are men killed, wounded, and captured or missing, added together. A rate is casualties divided by the men who went into the battle, to one decimal.

The source register behind Ask the Historian

The project holds a register of 1,714 entries from a list called Gettysburg Sources v2 (generated September 30, 2026). The numbers are computed from the register file and agree with its review report. They are about what a list can add to Ask the Historian; they are not a bibliography of the map.

93.3% of the list is the Official Records, which the project’s own text index already holds. Those entries add an independent index and links, not new text.

The project’s text index of Official Records volume 27 holds 588 of the 589 numbered reports and 8,232 citable passages. It is built and tested and is not part of the live site.

The review report found that 1,940 of 2,046 links in the list go to two hosts that refuse automated clients (94.8%). Those links were not opened; they were checked only by shape against the Internet Archive scans.
