FAQ

Last reviewed: October 4, 2026. Status: candidate.

Frequently asked questions. Each answer is checked against a file of this project. Where the answer is “not yet” or “not known”, it says so.

What is the Gettysburg Interactive Map?

An interactive map of the Battle of Gettysburg, July 1–3, 1863. It places 569 units on the field and moves them through 675 moments, with written stories and a chronology. The About page has more.

Is it free, and do I need an account?

It is free, there is no account system, and there is no payment of any kind, according to the site’s Privacy page.

Does Ask the Historian use AI?

No. Ask the Historian runs in your browser and looks up text in this site’s own data files. It does not call an AI model, and its page says it is a search of the site’s records, not an AI.

How exact are the positions?

Positions are reconstructed from published sources. Where a regiment’s exact place at a given time is not recorded, the map shows the best-supported position. The data holds 1,804 position rows on 188 of 675 moments, and 217 of 465 regiments have positions of their own.

How exact are the times?

Times are approximate. Sources disagree by half an hour or more, and the map plays as a time-lapse. The moments are not five minutes apart everywhere, and the proposed clock rests on 33 time anchors, none of which has been checked by a historian yet.

Why do casualty totals differ between pages?

The site’s own data holds three bases for the combined total: 45,928, 46,163 and 51,049. Casualties are men killed, wounded, and captured or missing, added together. The Sources and Methods page lists the figures by source.

Can I find an individual soldier or ancestor?

Not here. This site lists regiments, not individual soldiers. Find Your Ancestor searches regiments by state or commander. To find a soldier by name, search the National Park Service’s Soldiers and Sailors System.

Which browsers and devices work?

Every test of release R1c ran in Chrome on a desktop computer, and touch was emulated there. Safari, Firefox, Samsung Internet and real phones have not been tested. A phone layout exists from release R1b.

Can I use it with a keyboard or a screen reader?

The map has keyboard shortcuts, listed on the Accessibility Statement page. Automated checks pass on the map page in release R1c. No conformance to WCAG is claimed, and no test with a screen reader is on record.

Can I use the content in class, on my own site or in print?

The site’s Terms welcome personal, educational and classroom use, including linking to the site, embedding its widget and citing it in student work, and they ask that the data files not be republished as someone else’s dataset. What do “Documented”, “Computed” and the other labels mean?

They are the six evidence labels used on the Sources and Methods page: documented, measured, computed, modelled, contested and unknown. The map itself does not show them yet.

Who checked the history?

No historian has reviewed the reconstruction yet. See the About page for the reviewer’s credit once there is one.

Who made the map?

Mike Legere built the map for the love of history, in his own words, and maintains it on his own. The About page has his own text.

How do I report a mistake?

Use the Corrections page. The Contact page has the address.
