Corrections

Last reviewed: October 4, 2026. Status: candidate.

The map reconstructs three days of 1863 from published sources, so it will contain mistakes. When one is found, it is corrected in the open: the date, what the page said, what it says now, and the source.

Report a mistake

Write to the corrections address. You do not need to give your name. Say where you saw it, what you think is wrong, and your source (author, title, page) if you have one.

[OWNER TO PROVIDE: corrections email address]

If your email program does not open, copy this template into a message:

Page address:
What I saw:
What I think is wrong, and why:
My source (author, title, page), if I have one:
May I be credited in the corrections log? (yes or no)

What happens next

  1. The report is received at the corrections address.
  2. The target is to acknowledge it within 3 days. This is a target, not a promise. [OWNER TO CONFIRM: the response targets (acknowledge in 3 days, fix or explain in 30 days) are the ones you will keep]
  3. The claim is checked against the sources. A historical correction is checked by a named historical reviewer once one is named; until then it is marked “Unreviewed reconstruction”.
  4. The target is to fix the mistake, or explain why it stands, within 30 days. [OWNER TO CONFIRM: the response targets (acknowledge in 3 days, fix or explain in 30 days) are the ones you will keep]
  5. A fix that is live is entered in the log below.

Where historians disagree, the page shows the range and the sources instead of picking a winner. The Sources and Methods page shows the disagreements that are known today.

What counts as a correction

  • A wrong fact: a unit, a place, a date, a time, a figure, a quotation or a name.
  • A missing or wrong source.
  • Text that says more than the evidence shows.
  • A broken link or a page that prints a code word instead of text.

An accessibility barrier is not a correction; report it through the Accessibility Statement page, which uses the same address and the same targets.

The public log

The log is newest first. Each entry has these fields, and the same fields are kept in a machine-readable file next to this page (corrections.json).

Fields of a log entry
FieldMeaning
idA stable number for the entry.
dateThe day the correction went live: year first in the file, month name first on the page.
whereThe page, moment or record that was wrong.
wasWhat it said, quoted.
nowWhat it says now, quoted.
sourceAuthor, title, edition and page for the change.
reviewerWho checked the historical part, or “unreviewed”.
credit_consentWhether the person who reported it agreed to be credited.
