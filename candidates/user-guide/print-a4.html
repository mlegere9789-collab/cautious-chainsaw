1. About this guide

This guide describes release R1c of the Gettysburg Interactive Map, the build the project calls Tranche 0. That release has not been released: it is built and tested locally and is not deployed. The guide documents what that release does, not what is planned.

The map follows the Battle of Gettysburg, July 1–3, 1863. It places 465 regiments and 104 batteries on the field and moves them through 675 moments, with 24 written stories and a chronology of 137 events.

2. Start here

Open the map in a web browser. On a computer you see the whole toolbar and the playback bar. On a phone or a touch tablet up to 834 pixels wide, and on a phone held sideways, you see the phone layout instead. Since R1b The layout is chosen automatically. Add ?layout=desktop or ?layout=mobile to the address to force one.

  1. Play. Press Space on a computer, or the play button on a phone. The map plays through time and the units move.
  2. Pause. Press Space again, or the play button.
  3. Step. Press the Right arrow to move forward one moment and the Left arrow to move back one. On a phone use the two step buttons.
  4. Jump to a day. Press 1, 2 or 3 to go to the start of July 1, 2 or 3. On a phone use the three day buttons.
  5. Look at a unit. Click it, or tap it, to see its details.

The Help panel lists the keys. Press ? on a computer, or open Help from the toolbar or the Tools sheet.

A moment is not a fixed length of time. The map has 675 moments. They are steps through the story, and the time between two moments is not the same everywhere. The section About the clock explains what that means for the times you see.

7. About the clock

The map plays as a time-lapse. It covers quiet stretches faster and the fighting more slowly, so one moment is not a fixed number of minutes.

  • The map has 675 moments. The data files declare that each moment is five minutes; an audit measured that declaration as wrong.
  • The clock the project proposes rests on 33 time anchors. All 33 are unreviewed: no historian has checked them yet.
  • Release R1c changes only the date. Times can be hours off in places. Use the order of events, and the text of a story, rather than the clock.
  • Times are approximate. Sources disagree by half an hour or more, and the map plays as a time-lapse.

There were no standard time zones in 1863. The proposed clock uses local mean time at Gettysburg.

9. Known differences and limits

These are the places where release R1c and its own text disagree, or where something is missing. They are listed so that you are not surprised.

  • The Help panel and some pages still say tick and five minutes, and call 12 moments one hour. Moments are not five minutes apart everywhere; read the moment as a place in the story, not a fixed length of time.
  • Clock labels can be hours off in places. Release R1c changes only the date (July 1, 2 and 3 instead of dates up to July 9); the clock itself is waiting on a decision about the time model.
  • The Monuments button is in the toolbar, but it shows nothing in this release. The star layer that appeared when it was pressed was removed from the site in production, and restoring it properly is on the owner’s list.
  • The heatmap key and the in-map sound switch were removed in release R1a. Immersive Mode is the page that plays sound.
  • Some labels on screen use older names, for example the menu is called Extra Features and one page is called Battle Reports. This guide uses the names the style guide settles on and gives the on-screen label beside the first mention.
  • Ask the Historian searches this site’s records. The count shown on its page is computed from the loaded files.
  • Everything in this guide was checked against the release’s text, key handler and tests. Release R1c has been run only in Chrome on a desktop computer (touch was emulated). Safari, Firefox, Samsung Internet and real phones have not been tested.

10. Using the map with a keyboard or a screen reader

  • Release R1b added a skip link, landmarks, labelled controls, a timeline that works as a slider with its value in words, and dialogs that keep keyboard focus. These are a bridge, not a claim of conformance to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines.
  • To choose a unit by keyboard, open Search with S, type in the field, press Down arrow to move into the list and Enter to choose a unit. Focus then moves to the unit’s details.
  • Esc closes the topmost panel or card first.
  • Space pauses the playback.

The Accessibility Statement lists what was tested, the results and the known gaps, including that no screen reader test is on record.
